Scenario-based practice

The PCP-AI examination is composed entirely of scenario-based multiple-choice questions. Certuvo's practice mirrors that format — realistic situations that ask for the best course of action — so the exam feels familiar, not surprising.

Study approach

How to structure your preparation

Certuvo works best when preparation is treated like a small project: baseline first, targeted effort next, verification last. Because the platform is self-paced, the sequence is yours to control — and the sequence matters more than the total hours.

Baseline before you study

Attempt a set of scenario questions cold, before opening the study materials. The score is not a verdict — it is a map of which areas of the body of knowledge deserve the most time.

Study one domain at a time

Work through each knowledge area, then go straight to its question bank while the material is fresh. Practising immediately after studying converts reading into judgement.

Little and often

Short, regular sessions beat occasional marathons. Scenario judgement builds through repetition, and a fully online platform makes twenty useful minutes easy to find.

Finish under exam conditions

Close with full-length timed mocks, sat in one block without notes, so the real exam format holds no surprises in pace or pressure.

Readiness signals

Knowing when you are ready to book

Progress tracking tells you what you scored; deciding to sit the exam means reading those numbers with judgement. Before you book, look for four signals:

  • Consistency across domains — a strong average can hide a weak area, and the examination samples the whole blueprint.
  • Stability across mocks — one good result can be luck; two or three at a comfortable margin are evidence.
  • Time in hand — finishing timed mocks with minutes to spare rather than scrambling through the final questions.
  • Reasoning, not recall — you can explain why each wrong option is wrong, not merely recognise the right one.

The common pitfall is over-familiarity: repeat the same question bank often enough and scores rise because you remember answers, not because judgement has improved. Vary your practice sets, and treat a score that survives unfamiliar questions as the one that counts.
