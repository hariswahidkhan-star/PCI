Why PCI.

Project controls deserves a focused professional standard for the AI era.

Project controls is changing — judgment matters more than ever

Schedules, forecasts, cost reports, dashboards and risk models are increasingly supported by digital tools and AI. Technology does not remove accountability. Projects still need professionals who can challenge assumptions, validate data, interpret results, explain trade-offs and support decisions with integrity.

Five reasons PCI exists

01

A distinct professional capability

Project controls is not just reporting. It combines planning, cost, risk, forecasting, contracts, project finance, communication and governance.

02

AI is changing the work

AI can accelerate analysis but can also create false confidence. Professionals must know how to validate outputs.

03

Employers need consistency

Organisations need a shared language for project-controls capability across teams, projects and regions.

04

Professionals need clarity

Project-controls professionals need a clear pathway to demonstrate skills and grow into leadership roles.

05

Better decision support

Good project controls helps leaders make earlier, better and more transparent decisions.

What it means for professionals and employers

  • For professionals. A credential that recognises the whole controls discipline — not one technique — plus the governed use of AI, and that travels with you across employers, sectors and borders.
  • For employers. A verifiable, impartial signal of competence you can trust without re-testing, a way to build team capability against a recognised standard, and a connection to a wider professional community.
  • For the profession. A shared standard and a professional home for a discipline that has long lacked both — raising the bar, and defining what good looks like in the AI era.

An honest position

PCI does not pretend to be something it is not. It is a institute, building its credential and community in the open, designed around ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles, and candid about what is in place today versus what is still being established.

That candour is deliberate. For a body whose entire value rests on trust, refusing to overstate recognition, outcomes or status is not a weakness — it is the foundation everything else is built on.

Common questions

Why does PCI exist?

To give project controls something it has long lacked: a shared standard, an independent credential and a professional home — built for how the discipline is practised now, with AI governance at its core.

Is the credential recognised yet?

PCI is building toward formal accreditation rather than claiming it. The credential and its processes are designed around the ISO/IEC 17024 framework. We state our status plainly and do not imply recognition we do not yet hold — that honesty is part of how a credible certification body earns trust.

How is PCI different from existing credentials?

It certifies the whole controls discipline as one integrated competence and treats AI governance as a first-class part of it, rather than fragmenting the field into separate certificates. PCI does not claim equivalence with other bodies.

Why should I trust PCI?

By holding us to what we actually claim. We state our status plainly, design our processes around an international standard, and refuse to overstate recognition or results — and the credential itself is independent, scenario-based and genuinely hard to fake.

Why this matters

This matters because a credential earns its value from substance, not marketing — clear standards, fair process, transparent governance and honesty about status. Everything here is written to that test: useful to professionals and employers, and never claiming more than is true today.

PCI builds in the open. That means being candid about what is in place and what is still developing, designing around the ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles, and never implying recognition it does not yet hold. That honesty is part of how trust is earned.

Deciding for yourself

How to test the case against your own work

The argument for a focused standard is easier to judge against your own working week than in the abstract. Try a short exercise.

  • List the deliverables you personally owned last month — schedules, cost reports, forecasts, risk registers, dashboards.
  • Mark the ones that required integrating more than one discipline: a forecast that needed cost and schedule together, or a report that combined finance and risk.
  • Mark the ones where a tool or model produced the first draft, and your real contribution was deciding whether to trust it.

If the second and third marks dominate, you are already practising the integrated, AI-assisted discipline the PCI certifications assess — and the body of knowledge will show you where your gaps sit. If your work lives inside a single specialism and you want to go deeper there, a specialist credential may serve you better. PCI would rather say that plainly than enrol the wrong candidate.

What good looks like

The capability the standard describes

Behind the reasons above sits a concrete picture of capability. A professional working at the standard PCI describes can do four things consistently.

Explains, not just reports

States the assumptions behind a forecast and what would have to change for it to be wrong — rather than simply presenting the number.

Traces the thread

Follows a variance from the dashboard back through the cost model to the schedule logic — and to the contract clause that drives it.

Governs the machine

Can say what an AI tool was asked to do, what data it saw, how the output was validated and who owns the result.

Speaks decision language

Frames controls information as options and consequences for the people who must decide, not as raw status.

These criteria are deliberately qualitative. Competence in project controls shows itself in judgment, and that is what the scenario-based examination is built to observe.
