Overview

Common questions

Is the Qatar chapter active yet?

It is being established. PCI builds in the open; chapter registration is open, so you can be part of it from the start.

Do I need to be certified to take part?

No. Chapters are open to everyone with an interest in project controls — practising professionals, people moving into the field, and students alike. You do not need to hold the PCP-AI credential, or even to be working towards it, to take part. Chapters are about community, learning and connection; certification is a separate, independent process, and many members get involved in a chapter long before they ever sit the exam.

What will the Qatar chapter do?

Build community, share knowledge, run events, and connect members to the global standard and credential.

How chapters work

A chapter in formation, stated plainly

PCI chapters are member-led communities, not franchises. Each begins as a chapter in formation: a founding group of local professionals, a named coordinator, and a simple charter under PCI’s governance framework. We list a chapter as established only when it has held its first sessions and adopted the charter — we do not publish member counts or activity we cannot evidence.

What a chapter in Qatar will do

Peer sessions on real project-controls problems; study circles for PCP-AI candidates; honest conversation about how governed AI is changing planning, cost and risk work locally; and a bridge between practitioners and employers in Qatar. Sessions may be in person, online, or both — the founding group decides.

Help found it

If you work in project controls in Qatar and want to shape the chapter, contact us with a short note on your background. Founding contributors are acknowledged on the chapter page once it is established. See the chapter framework and governance framework for how chapters relate to the Institute.

Practising in Qatar

How controls work is organised on Qatari programmes

Controls roles in Qatar tend to sit in one of three settings, and the daily discipline differs in each. Knowing which side of the table you are on — and what the other side needs from you — is half the job.

Owner-side teams

Guard the integrated baseline. The work is consolidation: aligning many contractor schedules to one programme, running independent forecasts rather than accepting submitted numbers at face value, and briefing decision-makers who expect evidence rather than optimism.

Contractor and JV teams

Progress measurement and record-keeping under EPC and construction contracts, often inside joint ventures where two corporate reporting systems must reconcile. Disciplined trend management and clean change records protect both the project and your employer's commercial position.

Consultancy and PMC roles

Sitting between owner and contractor, translating raw delivery data into decisions. Independence and visible method matter most here — your analysis is trusted only as far as your workings can be inspected.

Your pathway

Working towards the PCP-AI from Doha

You do not need to wait for the chapter to be fully established to start. The assessment is delivered online with remote invigilation, so candidates in Qatar sit the same exam as everyone else, without travelling. A sensible sequence:

  • Map your current role against the domains of the body of knowledge. Gas, rail and real-estate work each cover some domains deeply and leave gaps in others — find yours early.
  • Check the eligibility requirements before you plan study time, so you know which route applies to you.
  • Use real project material as revision. Reworking a genuine progress claim or period forecast as an earned-value exercise teaches more than reading alone.
  • Confirm your setup for remote proctoring well ahead of exam day — a quiet room and a stable connection are the practical hurdles, not the technology.

Once the chapter holds its first sessions, study circles will give candidates in Doha a peer group. Until then, the global materials and the wider PCI community serve the same purpose.
