How to join

Three routes into PCI

There are three ways to be recognised by the Project Controls Institute. The examined PCP-AI credential is always earned the same way — by passing the examination — whichever route brings you in.

Standard

Open to everyone. Enrol, activate your student membership, pay the exam fee and sit the PCP-AI examination. Pass, and the credential is yours. Enrol now →

Founding member

A limited founding cohort, joined by invitation during the founding stage. Founding members receive membership, study and examination access and are recognised as early builders of the standard — the credential is still earned by passing the exam. About founding membership →

Honorary Fellow (PCI)

A board-conferred recognition of distinguished contribution to the profession. It involves no examination and is conferred by the board, not applied for — a separate recognition that is never the examined PCP-AI credential. About honorary recognition →

Common questions

What is the Project Controls Institute?

PCI is a professional certification institute focused on project controls in the AI era. It sets a standard for the discipline, certifies professionals through the PCP-AI credential, and is building a global community — with the governed use of AI at its core.

What is the PCP-AI credential?

It certifies demonstrated competence across the integrated controls discipline — planning, cost, forecasting, risk and more — with AI governance assessed as a competency in its own right, through an independent, scenario-based assessment.

Is PCI accredited?

PCI is building toward formal accreditation rather than claiming it. The credential and its processes are designed around the ISO/IEC 17024 framework. We state our status plainly and do not imply recognition we do not yet hold — that honesty is part of how a credible certification body earns trust.

How is AI involved?

AI runs through everything PCI certifies, under the principle AI proposes, the professional disposes — a competent human validates, explains and owns every AI-assisted output. The credential proves you can govern AI, not just use it.

Who is it for?

Project-controls professionals — planners, schedulers, cost engineers, risk and controls managers — and the employers, universities and chapters that support them.

Choosing carefully

Questions to ask any certifying body

Before you invest in any professional credential — this one included — a few questions reliably separate substance from marketing. We publish our own answers openly, and we encourage you to put the same questions to every body you consider.

  • Who makes the certification decision, and is it independent of whoever sold you the training?
  • Can an employer verify the credential directly, rather than relying on a certificate file?
  • Is the body's recognition status stated plainly, or implied through logos and borrowed authority?
  • Is there a published standard describing what the credential actually assesses?
  • When things go wrong, are retakes, appeals and complaints governed by published policy?

PCI's answers are on this site: the certification decision is kept independent of preparation providers, the framework is developed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles, and our accreditation status is described honestly at every stage — including what we do not yet hold.
