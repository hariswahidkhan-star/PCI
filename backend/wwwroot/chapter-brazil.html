Overview

Brazil delivers capital projects at continental scale — pre-salt offshore oil and gas, large-scale mining, a power mix spanning hydro, wind and solar, and a wave of infrastructure, ports and logistics concessions. The PCI Brazil chapter is being established to connect project-controls professionals and share local practice under one global PCL-AI standard.

Common questions

Is the Brazil chapter active?

Chapters are in formation; PCI describes its status honestly and does not overstate local presence.

Do I need to be certified to take part?

No. Chapters are open to everyone with an interest in project controls — practising professionals, people moving into the field, and students alike. You do not need to hold a PCI credential, or even to be working towards it, to take part. Chapters are about community, learning and connection; certification is a separate, independent process, and many members get involved in a chapter long before they ever sit the exam.

How do I get involved?

Register your interest and PCI will keep you informed as chapters, events and opportunities develop in your area. Much of this is being built now, and early participants genuinely help shape how it takes form. You can take part as a member, as a volunteer in a local chapter, or simply by staying connected and joining activities as they are announced.

Why a chapter in Brazil

Long-duration, capital-intensive projects — many privately financed or run as concessions — need reliable schedules, transparent cost control and honest forecasting. The chapter exists to raise that capability across the country and link practitioners to a global standard.

The PCI Brazil chapter is intended to support exactly that: connecting practitioners across employers and projects, surfacing the challenges specific to the region, and linking local professionals to the global body of knowledge and the PCI certifications. It is built on the same principle as everything PCI does — capability over pedigree, governed AI throughout, and honesty about where the institute stands today.

  • A professional network for project controls in Brazil
  • Shared standards and exchange of practice
  • A clear route toward the recognised credential
  • A local voice into a global body of knowledge
How chapters work

A chapter in formation, stated plainly

PCI chapters are member-led communities, not franchises. Each begins as a chapter in formation: a founding group of local professionals, a named coordinator, and a simple charter under PCI’s governance framework. We list a chapter as established only when it has held its first sessions and adopted the charter — we do not publish member counts or activity we cannot evidence.

What a chapter in Brazil will do

Peer sessions on real project-controls problems; study circles for PCL-AI candidates; honest conversation about how governed AI is changing planning, cost and risk work locally; and a bridge between practitioners and employers in Brazil. Sessions may be in person, online, or both — the founding group decides.

Help found it

If you work in project controls in Brazil and want to shape the chapter, contact us with a short note on your background. Founding contributors are acknowledged on the chapter page once it is established. See the chapter framework and governance framework for how chapters relate to the Institute.

Local practice

What practising in Brazil asks of a controls professional

Project controls in Brazil has a distinct texture. Contracts on long-duration work are commonly indexed for inflation, so cost engineers must keep a clean separation between genuine scope or productivity movement and price adjustment when they report variances — a trend line that mixes the two misleads everyone. Environmental licensing and permitting milestones often sit on the critical path long before construction begins, which makes early-phase schedule risk analysis unusually valuable here. And because many major assets are delivered under concession or project-finance structures, controls reporting serves lenders and regulators as well as the delivery team — the numbers must stand up outside the project office.

Indexation discipline

Separate escalation and currency movement from real scope and productivity variance, so forecasts reflect performance rather than price mechanics.

Licensing on the critical path

Treat environmental and regulatory approvals as scheduled, risk-assessed activities with named owners — not as background assumptions that quietly slip.

Remote-site logistics

Offshore, mining and interior sites carry long supply chains. Progress measurement has to reflect transport and access constraints, not just installation rates.

Common questions

Language, experience and the global standard

Practitioners in Brazil often ask how a global credential sits alongside local working language and local contract practice. The honest answers are below.

Will chapter activity be in Portuguese?

That is for the founding group to decide, and Portuguese is the natural working language for local sessions. PCI's published standards, the body of knowledge and the examination are maintained in English, so most Brazilian practitioners work bilingually — discussing practice in Portuguese while keeping fluency in the English terminology the credential assesses. Study circles that move between the two are a practical way to prepare.

Does experience on Brazilian projects count towards eligibility?

Yes. Eligibility is assessed on relevant professional experience wherever it was gained. Controls work on Brazilian oil and gas, mining, energy or infrastructure projects counts on the same terms as experience anywhere else — see the eligibility requirements for what to prepare.

How does a global standard fit local contract practice?

The PCL-AI assesses transferable method — planning, cost, risk, earned value and governed AI — not any one country's contract forms. Applying that method within Brazilian contractual and regulatory practice is exactly the ground a local chapter exists to work through together.
