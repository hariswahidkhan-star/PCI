About PCI

An independent home for a discipline that didn't have one.

The Project Controls Institute exists to certify the integrated discipline of project controls, cost engineering and project finance — to a single, modern, AI-ready standard.

Our mission

Make project controls a recognised profession.

Project management has a global standard. Finance has several. Project controls — the discipline that actually keeps projects to plan, cost and cash flow — has had none. PCI is built to change that.

We define the body of knowledge, set the examination, and award a credential that employers can trust, with artificial intelligence treated as part of the discipline rather than a bolt-on.

Independence & impartiality

We set the standard. We don't sell the prep.

PCI develops and owns the standard and the examination. Preparation and training are provided separately by Certuvo, our official partner. This deliberate separation supports the impartiality expected of a certifying body developed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles.

What the institute actually does

PCI exists to do a small number of things well, and to be judged on them.

Set the standard.

We define what competent project controls looks like — the twelve-competency model, with AI governance as a first-class part of it — and keep it current as practice and technology change.

Certify professionals.

We assess individuals independently against that standard through the PCP-AI credential, keeping any training strictly separate from the certification decision, so a pass is a credible, verifiable signal of competence.

Build the profession.

We connect practitioners through membership and local chapters, give the discipline a professional home, and help people develop across a career rather than a single exam.

Research and inform.

We study how the discipline is genuinely practised and how AI is changing it, and feed that back into the standard, the body of knowledge and our guidance.

How PCI is different

Most credentials in this space either predate the arrival of AI in everyday controls work or treat it as a bolt-on. PCI was built the other way round — around the reality that AI now shapes how schedules, costs, risk and forecasts are produced, and that the decisive skill is governing it well: AI proposes, the professional disposes.

The institute is also deliberately honest about where it stands. It is developing its framework with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles, and it does not claim accreditation or recognition it has not yet earned. For a body whose entire value is trust, that candour is a feature, not a weakness.

Common questions

What makes the PCP-AI different from existing credentials?

It treats AI governance as a core competency rather than an add-on, and assesses the whole controls discipline as an integrated model. It is built for how the work is done now — using AI tools while keeping judgement and accountability with a competent professional.

Who is behind PCI?

PCI is an independent certification body, owned by no vendor, employer or training provider. That independence is deliberate: it is what allows the credential to be impartial and to mean the same thing regardless of who you work for or trained with.

Does PCI provide the training as well as the certification?

The certification decision is kept strictly separate from any training, so passing reflects assessed competence rather than attendance on a course. This separation is a core principle of credible, ISO/IEC 17024-referenced certification.

Is PCI accredited?

PCI is building toward formal accreditation rather than claiming it. The credential and its processes are designed around the ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles. We state our status plainly and do not imply recognition we do not yet hold — that honesty is part of how a credible certification body earns trust.

How can I trust a institute?

By holding us to what we actually claim. PCI states its status plainly, designs its processes around an international standard, and refuses to overstate its recognition or its results. Trust is earned through that consistency — and through a credential that is genuinely hard to fake.

Due diligence

How to judge a certifying body — including this one

A credential is only as good as the body behind it. Before committing time or money to any certification — ours included — apply the same short test. A credible certifying body should answer each of these questions in writing, on a public page, without hedging.

Is the standard published?

You should be able to read what is assessed — the competency model, the examination blueprint and how the pass mark is set — before you apply, not after.

Who profits from the training?

If the examiner also sells the preparation, the incentive to pass paying students corrodes the credential. Look for genuine separation between training and the certification decision.

Can decisions be challenged?

Fair process means published appeals and complaints routes, handled by people who were not involved in the original decision.

Can results be verified?

An employer should be able to confirm a credential with the issuing body directly, rather than taking a certificate at face value.

Is the status honest?

New bodies should state plainly what they hold and what they are still building. Vague claims of recognition are a warning sign anywhere.

Our own answers live in our published policies and documents. We would rather be examined against this list than trusted on assertion.

Reading the record

Where to start with what we publish

PCI publishes its governing documents deliberately and in full. If you are new to the institute, the sensible reading order depends on who you are.

  • Candidates should begin with the candidate handbook, which sets out the rules of the process, then move to the body of knowledge to see what the examination actually assesses.
  • Employers get the most value from the certification scheme description and the impartiality and integrity policies — the documents that explain what a pass means and how that meaning is protected.
  • Sceptics should go straight to founding status, which lists in plain terms what exists today and what is still in development.

Everything that governs a certification decision is published before anyone sits the examination. If you find a gap or an ambiguity, tell us — closing it improves the standard for everyone who comes after you.
