# Domain 9 — Agile, Scrum & Adaptive Delivery for Project Controls

## KA 9.5 — Agile cost control, forecasting & earned value in adaptive delivery

KA 8.6.4 established the central fact this KA now builds on: adaptive delivery does not abandon cost control
and forecasting, it **inverts** which corner of the scope–time–cost relationship is fixed and which is
forecast. Domain 6 measures a fixed scope (BAC) and forecasts what it will cost and when it will finish;
adaptive delivery fixes the funded run-rate and release length and forecasts how much of a deliberately
variable backlog that funding will buy. This KA is where that inversion is made fully operational for a
project controls professional: how an agile team or product is actually funded (9.5.1); how PV, EV, AC and
the full CV/SV/CPI/SPI/EAC vocabulary of KA 6.1–6.3 are reused — with **explicitly stated, scope-variable
assumptions** — against a story-point backlog, an adaptation this Body of Knowledge refers to descriptively
as **AgileEVM** (9.5.2); how a velocity-based release forecast and an AgileEVM cost forecast reconcile
(9.5.3); how backlog growth is handled without either ignoring it or panicking over it (9.5.4); and how
IFRS 15 recognises revenue on an agile delivery contract (9.5.5). Continuity runs on the **Field Progress &
Punch-list App** introduced in KA 8.6.4: a 200-point backlog, a three-person team funded at a fixed
$15,000 (SAR 56,250 at an illustrative **SAR 3.75 = USD 1**) per two-week sprint, with Sprint 1–3 actual
velocity of 16, 19 and 21 points (cumulative 56) exactly as established there. This KA continues that same
data set to Sprint 5 and beyond. Every classical symbol — PV, EV, AC, BAC, CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC, ETC, VAC —
is reused **identically** to KA 6.1–6.3; nothing here redefines them, it only states what must be assumed to
apply them to a variable-scope backlog.

### 9.5.1 Funding adaptive delivery: fixed-capacity budgets, rolling-wave funding and the inverted triangle

**Definition & purpose.** **Fixed-capacity funding** is the agile funding model in which a team is funded at
a stable, pre-agreed cost per iteration — its **run-rate** — for a fixed period, regardless of how many
backlog points that iteration ultimately delivers. The organisation is buying a slice of team *capacity* for
a fixed span of time, not a fixed unit of output; this is the funding mechanism that makes KA 8.6.4's
"inverted iron triangle" (time and cost fixed, scope variable) operationally real rather than aspirational.
**Rolling-wave (progressive) budgeting** — a technique recognised in PMI's *PMBOK Guide* — releases that
funding in successive waves (e.g. release-by-release) rather than authorising the whole programme's cost at
once, with each wave gated on a review of the value the prior wave actually delivered.

**The underlying principle.** Classical budgeting (KA 3.1) fixes scope via a WBS-derived BAC and lets time
and cost be forecast against it; fixed-capacity funding fixes the run-rate and lets scope be the forecast
output. Contingency also relocates: rather than (or in addition to) a funded contingency added to BAC
(KA 3.1, KA 12.3), adaptive delivery absorbs performance variance primarily through the **scope** corner —
re-prioritising or trimming lower-value backlog items — with a funded reserve of a few extra sprints as the
closest agile analogue to management reserve, released only if a wave-gate review shows genuine need.

**Formula.**
`R` = funded cost per sprint ($); `V` = velocity (points/sprint); `SP` = story points.
`n(planned) = SP(planned) / V(planned)` (planned release length, sprints)
`BAC = n(planned) × R = SP(planned) × CPP`
`CPP = R / V(planned) = BAC / SP(planned)` ($/point — the **cost-per-point conversion factor**)

**Example 9.5.1 — Setting the Field App's funding baseline.**

*Scenario.* Meridian's PMO funds the Field Progress & Punch-list App at a fixed run-rate of R = $15,000
(SAR 56,250) per two-week sprint. At release inception, the Product Owner and team estimate the full backlog
at SP(planned) = 200 points and set a planned/target velocity of V(planned) = 20 points/sprint.

*Required.* Planned release length, BAC, and CPP.

*Working.*
`n(planned) = 200 / 20 = 10 sprints`
`BAC = 10 × 15,000 = 150,000`
`CPP = BAC / SP(planned) = 150,000 / 200 = 750` (check: `R / V(planned) = 15,000 / 20 = 750` ✓)

*Answer.* **BAC = $150,000** (SAR 562,500); planned release length **10 sprints**; **CPP = $750/point**.

*Interpretation.* This triple — BAC, V(planned), CPP — is the funding baseline 9.5.2's AgileEVM measures
actual performance against, exactly as KA 6.1.2's approved time-phased budget is the baseline classical PV is
read from — except here the baseline is a **capacity plan**, not a WBS-derived cost curve.

**Mini-case — the rolling-wave gate.** Meridian's PMO does not release the full $150,000 up front; it
authorises two waves of 5 sprints each ($75,000/wave), with a go/no-go review at the Sprint-5 gate before
wave 2 is funded. The AgileEVM figures computed at that exact gate in Example 9.5.2 (CV an adverse $7,500;
EAC $166,667 against a BAC of $150,000) are precisely the evidence a wave-gate review would weigh — a
rolling-wave structure gives the PMO a natural, funded stopping point to act on an adverse AgileEVM signal
before committing the second $75,000, rather than discovering an overrun only after all ten sprints are spent.

**Common pitfalls.**
- Assuming fixed-capacity funding buys a fixed *outcome* — a client paying $15,000/sprint has not thereby
  purchased a guaranteed 20 points of value that sprint; it has purchased two weeks of the team's capacity.
- Confusing rolling-wave funding with contingency — rolling-wave paces *authorisation*; contingency absorbs
  *known risk*. A programme can (and often should) use both.
- Setting V(planned) arbitrarily rather than from a comparable team's historical velocity or a calibration
  sprint (cross-reference KA 9.3) — an ungrounded planned velocity makes every downstream CPP, PV and CPI
  figure in 9.5.2 unreliable from the outset, exactly as an un-resourced PV baseline invalidates classical
  EVM (KA 6.1.2).

**AI in this topic.** A financial-planning assistant can draft a rolling-wave funding schedule and a
plausible V(planned) from a team's own historical sprints or an organisational benchmark (cross-reference
KA 13.5), speeding up baseline-setting. It cannot judge whether that benchmark team is genuinely comparable in
skill, domain and tooling to the Field App team — that comparability judgement, and the funding decision built
on it, remain the PMO's. **AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable.**

**Cross-references.** KA 3.1 (fixed-scope budgeting, contingency, management reserve); KA 6.1.2 (PV baseline
integrity, the classical parallel); KA 8.6.4 (the inverted triangle, first introduced); KA 9.6 (agile
governance gates); KA 12.3 (the contingency parallel).

### 9.5.2 AgileEVM: mapping points and sprint cost to PV, EV and AC

**Definition & purpose.** This Body of Knowledge uses **AgileEVM** as a descriptive label — as the term is
used informally in agile and project-controls practitioner literature — for reusing EVM's PV/EV/AC/CV/SV/
CPI/SPI/EAC vocabulary against a story-point-based, variable-scope backlog. It is not a formal international
standard in the way IFRS 15 or PMI's *Practice Standard for Earned Value Management* are; different
organisations and tools implement the mapping below with minor variations, which is exactly why its
assumptions must be **stated explicitly alongside every figure reported**, not left implicit.

**The underlying principle.** Classical EVM (KA 6.1) measures physical progress against a fixed BAC using a
measurement method chosen for the nature of the work. AgileEVM instead measures physical progress — story
points delivered — against the funding baseline set in 9.5.1, using a single conversion factor (CPP) to turn
points into currency. Because that conversion factor, and BAC itself, rest on a *planning-time* assumption
about total backlog size, every AgileEVM figure is conditional on that assumption holding — the moment the
backlog changes materially (9.5.4), the figures must be explicitly revisited, not silently trusted.

**The scope-variable assumptions (state these every time AgileEVM is reported).**
1. **BAC is a funding target, not a fixed contractual scope baseline.** It is set from a planning-time
   estimate (`SP(planned) × CPP`, 9.5.1) and is revisited when the backlog changes materially (9.5.4) — unlike
   classical BAC, which changes only through formal, documented change control (KA 5.4, KA 6.1.2).
2. **CPP is held at its original planning basis** (`CPP = BAC / SP(planned)`) unless a formal rebase is
   applied. Recalculating it silently against a shifted backlog changes the yardstick mid-measurement.
3. **PV is built from the funding schedule** (`PV(t) = t × R`, sprints elapsed × run-rate), which is
   consistent with `t × V(planned) × CPP` only because CPP was itself derived to make the two agree
   (`CPP × V(planned) = R`, 9.5.1). If planned velocity and the funded run-rate are ever set inconsistently,
   the funding schedule — what is actually authorised to be spent — governs PV, exactly as classical PV is
   the *approved* time-phased budget, not an independently derived physical-progress assumption.
4. **EV is a value measure, not a cost measure.** `EV(t) = SP(actual, t) × CPP` — story points themselves have
   no currency value; only once multiplied by CPP do they become one (the "points as cost" pitfall below).
5. **AC is the actual cost incurred**, which equals the funded run-rate cumulative (`t × R`) only if the team
   draws exactly its funded rate each sprint — a special case, not a law (the mini-case below breaks it).

**Formula.** `PV(t) = t × R`; `EV(t) = SP(actual, t) × CPP`; `AC(t)` = actual cumulative cost incurred; then,
identically to KA 6.2–6.3: `CV = EV − AC`; `SV = EV − PV`; `CPI = EV / AC`; `SPI = EV / PV`; and the three EAC
analogues `EAC = AC + (BAC − EV)`, `EAC = BAC / CPI`, `EAC = AC + (BAC − EV)/(CPI × SPI)`.

**Example 9.5.2 — AgileEVM at the Sprint-5 gate: PV, EV, AC, CV, SV, CPI, SPI and EAC.**

*Scenario.* Continuing 8.6.4's data (Sprint 1–3: 16, 19, 21 points; cumulative 56), the Field App delivers a
further 18 points in Sprint 4 and 16 points in Sprint 5 (cumulative 56 + 18 + 16 = **90 points**). The team
drew exactly its funded $15,000 each sprint (no extra spend). From 9.5.1: R = $15,000/sprint; CPP = $750/
point; BAC = $150,000; V(planned) = 20 points/sprint.

*Required.* PV, EV, AC, CV, SV, CPI, SPI at the Sprint-5 data date, and EAC under all three method analogues.

*Working.*
`PV(5) = 5 × 15,000 = 75,000`
`AC(5) = 5 × 15,000 = 75,000` (funded rate drawn exactly)
`EV(5) = 90 × 750 = 67,500`
`CV = EV − AC = 67,500 − 75,000 = −7,500`
`SV = EV − PV = 67,500 − 75,000 = −7,500`
`CPI = EV / AC = 67,500 / 75,000 = 0.90`
`SPI = EV / PV = 67,500 / 75,000 = 0.90`

| Method | Formula | EAC ($) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — remaining points at planned rate | `AC + (BAC − EV)` = 75,000 + 82,500 | **157,500** |
| 2 — cost-per-point efficiency continues | `BAC / CPI` = 150,000 / 0.90 | **166,667** |
| 3 — cost & schedule pressure continue | `AC + (BAC − EV)/(CPI × SPI)` = 75,000 + 82,500/0.81 | **176,852** |

Using Method 2 as the reporting basis: `ETC = 166,667 − 75,000 = 91,667`; `VAC = 150,000 − 166,667 = −16,667`
(SAR (62,501), rounded).

*Answer.* **CV = SV = an adverse $7,500; CPI = SPI = 0.90.** EAC ranges **$157,500–$176,852**; on Method 2,
**ETC = $91,667, VAC = an adverse $16,667.**

*Interpretation.* **CV and SV, and CPI and SPI, are numerically identical here — a structural feature of
fixed-capacity AgileEVM, not a coincidence.** Because AC and PV both reduce to `sprints elapsed × R` whenever
the team draws exactly its funded rate, EV is being compared against the *same number twice*. Algebraically,
`CPI = EV/AC = (SP(actual) × CPP)/(t × R) = V(avg) × CPP / R = V(avg)/V(planned)` (since `CPP × V(planned) = R`);
here `18/20 = 0.90`, matching exactly. This means CPI and SPI are **not independently diagnostic** of "cost"
versus "schedule" performance under pure fixed-capacity funding with no extra spend — both simply read
whether actual velocity is tracking planned velocity. This is materially different from classical EVM
(KA 6.2), where AC and PV are always independent measures, and it is a limitation a professional must
recognise before reading an agile CPI/SPI pair as if it carried the same two-dimensional information as
Pipeline Segment 4B's (KA 6.2.5).

**Mini-case — when AC decouples from the funding schedule.** Suppose, in Sprint 5, Meridian's PMO approved a
$3,000 top-up to bring in a specialist tester and unblock a QA bottleneck, so `AC(5) = (4 × 15,000) + 18,000
= 78,000`, while PV and EV are unchanged ($75,000 and $67,500).
`CV = 67,500 − 78,000 = −10,500` (worse than the base case's −7,500)
`SV = 67,500 − 75,000 = −7,500` (unchanged)
`CPI = 67,500 / 78,000 = 0.8654 → 0.87`; `SPI = 67,500 / 75,000 = 0.90` (unchanged)
The moment AC decouples from the pure funded-rate schedule, CV separates from SV and CPI separates from SPI —
AgileEVM behaves exactly like classical EVM again, with cost and schedule genuinely independent dimensions.

- **Treating points as cost directly** — a story point has no intrinsic $ value; it acquires one only through

  and BAC still reflect the current backlog (forward-reference 9.5.4).

point's estimate is itself trustworthy, or why CPI has moved — those remain the professional's diagnosis,
exactly as in classical EVM (KA 6.2).

### 9.5.3 Forecasting completion: velocity-based ranges and reconciling with the AgileEVM EAC

**Definition & purpose.** A **velocity-based forecast** projects the number of further sprints (and their
cost) needed to clear the remaining backlog directly from observed velocity, typically expressed as an
optimistic/likely/pessimistic range rather than a single figure. This topic works that forecast in full and
reconciles it with 9.5.2's AgileEVM EAC — the same underlying trend read through two different lenses.

**The underlying principle.** A velocity forecast answers "how many *whole* sprints" (a discrete planning
reality — a team cannot deliver a fractional sprint); an AgileEVM EAC answers "what will it cost in total" as
a continuous ratio extrapolation. They reconcile exactly once the same velocity figure underlies both and the
whole-sprint rounding convention is accounted for separately.

**Formula.** `SP(remaining) = SP(planned or current backlog) − SP(actual, t)`; `sprints(needed) =
SP(remaining) / V(assumed)`, rounded **up** to a whole sprint; `total sprints = t + sprints(needed)`;
`forecast cost = total sprints × R`.

**Example 9.5.3 — Velocity-based range forecast at the Sprint-5 gate, reconciled with the AgileEVM EAC.**

*Scenario.* Continuing 9.5.2: SP(remaining) = 200 − 90 = 110 points. Sprint-by-sprint velocity so far: 16,
19, 21, 18, 16 (5-sprint average V(avg) = 90/5 = 18.0 points/sprint). Sprint 3 achieved the team's best single
sprint (21 points); the team lead has flagged two team members' planned annual leave in the coming quarter,
historically cutting velocity by roughly 17%, i.e. to about 15 points/sprint.

*Required.* Optimistic, likely and pessimistic sprint-count and cost forecasts; the continuous (unrounded)
equivalent of the "likely" case; and a reconciliation with 9.5.2's Method 2 EAC ($166,667).

*Working.*

| Scenario | V (points/sprint) | Sprints needed (110/V, rounded up) | Total sprints | Forecast cost ($) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Optimistic (best sprint, Sprint 3) | 21 | 110/21 = 5.24 → 6 | 11 | 11 × 15,000 = **165,000** |
| Likely (5-sprint average) | 18 | 110/18 = 6.11 → 7 | 12 | 12 × 15,000 = **180,000** |
| Pessimistic (leave-adjusted) | 15 | 110/15 = 7.33 → 8 | 13 | 13 × 15,000 = **195,000** |

Continuous (unrounded) equivalent of the likely case: `sprints(needed) = 6.111`; `total sprints = 11.111`;
`forecast cost = 11.111 × 15,000 = 166,667` — **exactly** 9.5.2's Method 2 EAC (`BAC/CPI = 150,000/0.90 =
166,667`).

*Answer.* **Range: $165,000–$195,000 (11–13 total sprints); likely $180,000 (12 sprints); the continuous
figure underlying "likely," $166,667, is identical to the AgileEVM EAC.**

*Interpretation.* The two forecasts are the same calculation in different units: algebraically,
`BAC/CPI = BAC/(V(avg)/V(planned)) = (SP(planned) × CPP) × V(planned)/V(avg) = (SP(planned)/V(avg)) × R` —
i.e. total sprints (continuous) × R, provided the *same* velocity figure drives both. The $180,000 practical
figure exceeds the $166,667 continuous figure only because a real sprint plan must commit to whole sprints:
`12 − 11.111 = 0.889` of an unneeded sprint, costing `0.889 × 15,000 ≈ 13,333` — matching the gap
(`180,000 − 166,667 = 13,333`) to the dollar. For sponsor reporting, disclose **both**: the continuous
$166,667 (which ties directly to the classical EAC method) and the practical $180,000 (what will actually be
authorised and spent) — directly paralleling KA 6.3.1's top-down/bottom-up ETC reconciliation discipline.

### 9.5.4 Handling scope change and backlog growth in the forecast

**Definition & purpose.** **Backlog growth** is the addition of new points to an agile backlog mid-release —
new stories discovered, requirements refined. Unlike classical change control, which requires a formal
re-baseline of PV before EVM continues to be relied on (KA 5.4, KA 6.1.2), agile scope was never fixed, so no
re-baseline *event* is technically required. That does **not** mean growth is free: it must be explicitly
reforecast and either funded or traded off, never silently absorbed within the standing run-rate.

**The underlying principle — the "no free lunch" corollary.** If time and cost stay fixed (9.5.1) and scope
grows, exactly one of three things must happen: (a) velocity rises (rarely controllable on demand); (b) the
funded release length/budget is explicitly extended (a sponsor-approved decision — itself a form of
re-baseline, even though BAC was never fixed in the classical sense); or (c) an equivalent amount of
lower-priority backlog is removed, a lever unique to adaptive delivery via the Product Owner's
re-prioritisation.

**Formula.** `SP(remaining, revised) = SP(backlog, revised) − SP(actual, t)`; reforecast sprints and cost
exactly as in 9.5.3, substituting the revised remaining points.

**Example 9.5.4 — Reforecasting after a 36-point backlog addition.**

*Scenario.* At the Sprint-5 gate, Meridian's site-engineering team requests two new backlog items — a
weather-delay tagging feature and a subcontractor sign-off workflow — each estimated at roughly one sprint's
average output (18 points), adding 36 points. Revised backlog: `200 + 36 = 236` points.

*Required.* Revised remaining points, revised likely forecast (at V(avg) = 18), and the funding gap against
the pre-growth likely forecast ($180,000) and the original BAC ($150,000).

*Working.*
`SP(remaining, revised) = 236 − 90 = 146`
`sprints(needed) = 146 / 18 = 8.11 → 9`
`total sprints = 5 + 9 = 14`
`forecast cost = 14 × 15,000 = 210,000`

| | Sprints (total) | Forecast cost ($) |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-growth likely (9.5.3) | 12 | 180,000 |
| Post-growth likely | 14 | **210,000** |
| Delta | +2 | **+30,000** |

*Answer.* Growth of 36 points (18.0% of the original backlog) adds **2 sprints and $30,000** to the likely
forecast, and **$60,000 (40.0%)** to the original $150,000 BAC.

*Interpretation.* Meridian's PMO now faces an explicit choice at the wave-2 gate (9.5.1): fund the extra two
sprints (taking Wave 2 from $75,000 to $105,000), or ask the Product Owner to descope roughly 36 points of
lower-priority backlog elsewhere to hold the release at its original 12-sprint, $180,000 likely forecast.
Either is a legitimate adaptive response; attempting to deliver 236 points within the original $150,000/
10-sprint envelope, unremarked, is not.

**Common pitfalls.**
- Treating backlog growth as automatically an adverse variance to be "corrected" — validated new scope is a
  normal, healthy part of backlog refinement (KA 9.1, KA 9.3); the actual problem is *ungoverned* growth with
  no reforecast or sponsor visibility, not growth itself.
- Holding CPP fixed at its original value when reforecasting without checking whether the original BAC
  assumption it was derived from still holds once the backlog is materially larger — CPP is a conversion
  factor from a specific planning basis, not a constant.
- Confusing backlog *growth* (new points added) with backlog *refinement* (existing items re-estimated, net
  points roughly flat) — the forecast impact and the governance conversation differ.

**AI in this topic.** A burnup/velocity tool can flag automatically when cumulative backlog size crosses a
materiality threshold and recompute the range instantly (cross-reference KA 13.5). It cannot decide whether
the sponsor should fund the growth or the Product Owner should descope elsewhere — a commercial and product
decision that remains the professional's and the Product Owner's, not the tool's.

**Cross-references.** KA 5.4 (classical change control, the contrast); KA 6.1.2 (re-baselining discipline,
the contrast); KA 9.5.1 (the rolling-wave gate where this decision is actually made); KA 9.6 (agile
governance and change control when change is expected).

### 9.5.5 The IFRS 15 revenue view for agile delivery

**Definition & purpose.** IFRS 15's five-step model (KA 2.2, in full — not repeated here) applies to any
contract with a customer, agile delivery included. This topic addresses only Step 5's application to adaptive
delivery: the over-time-versus-point-in-time test (KA 2.2.6) and how story-point progress maps to a progress
measure (KA 2.2.7).

**The underlying principle.** IFRS 15 does not care whether delivery is predictive or adaptive — it cares
about the facts of control transfer and payment terms. Meridian's own **in-house** Field App team (KA 8.6.4)
generates no external revenue: IFRS 15 applies only where there is a contract with a customer. To illustrate
agile revenue recognition, consider the same backlog, velocity and sprint economics delivered instead by an
external agile vendor, **Vertex Software Partners LLC**, to a customer, under two common agile contract
structures that reach different — both defensible — treatments.

**Scenario A — capped time & materials, the right-to-invoice practical expedient.** Vertex bills its customer
$15,000 per sprint as delivered. The arrangement satisfies over-time criterion (a) (KA 2.2.6): the customer
simultaneously receives and consumes the benefit of Vertex's development effort as it is performed, typical of
a services arrangement. Because the invoiced amount corresponds directly to the value of that sprint's
performance to the customer, this is a case for IFRS 15's **right-to-invoice practical expedient** — revenue
recognised each period equals the amount the entity has a right to invoice for that period.

*Working.* Cumulative revenue at Sprint 5 = `5 × 15,000 = 75,000`; cumulative amount billed = `75,000`
(invoiced sprint by sprint, matching delivery). `Contract asset/(liability) = 75,000 − 75,000 = 0`.

*Interpretation.* Under a clean per-sprint right-to-invoice arrangement, cumulative revenue and cumulative
billing march together by construction, so **no contract asset or liability arises** — unlike Route 9's
mobilisation-heavy billing schedule (KA 2.2.11), where the two diverged materially.

**Scenario B — a fixed-price agile release, the output method.** Suppose instead Vertex prices the *same*
200-point backlog at a fixed **$180,000** (a $30,000, 20.0% margin over its own $150,000 budgeted delivery
cost — Vertex's own internal AgileEVM BAC from 9.5.1, now the cost basis underlying a customer-facing price).
This satisfies over-time criterion (c) (KA 2.2.6): the bespoke software has no alternative use to Vertex, and
the contract entitles it to payment for work completed to date. Progress is measured with an **output method**
(KA 2.2.7): `% complete = cumulative points delivered / total points in the current backlog`.

*Working.* `% complete = 90 / 200 = 45.0%`; `Cumulative revenue = 45.0% × 180,000 = 81,000`. Suppose a
front-loaded milestone billing schedule has invoiced $85,000 to date: `Contract asset/(liability) = 81,000 −
85,000 = (4,000)` → a **contract liability of $4,000** (SAR 15,000).

*Interpretation.* Story points delivered, divided by the current total backlog, is a legitimate IFRS 15
**output** progress measure — the direct agile analogue of "units delivered/total units" (KA 2.2.7) and of
EVM's "percentage complete (units of measure)" method (KA 6.1.4) — but it must never be confused with the
$750/point AgileEVM CPP used in 9.5.2, which is a *cost* basis, not a *revenue* basis (here, $900/point =
180,000/200). Were the backlog to grow to 236 points (9.5.4), the output-method denominator would need
updating — `90/236 = 38.1%` — unless the transaction price is also amended under a contract modification
(KA 2.2.10), exactly mirroring the input method's requirement that its total-estimated-cost denominator stay
current (KA 2.2.7's pitfall).

| Scenario | Over-time criterion | Progress measure | Cumulative revenue ($) | Billed ($) | Contract asset/(liability) ($) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — capped T&M, right-to-invoice | (a) | Amount invoiced | 75,000 | 75,000 | 0 |
| B — fixed-price, output method | (c) | Points delivered / current backlog | 81,000 | 85,000 | (4,000) |

**Common pitfalls.**
- Assuming the input (cost-to-cost) method is the only valid over-time measure for agile delivery — points
  delivered, correctly denominated against the *current* backlog, is a legitimate output measure.
- Assuming all agile delivery is automatically "over time" without testing the three criteria against the
  actual facts (KA 2.2.6) — a fixed-price agile project delivering a single product with genuine alternative
  use and no interim payment right could still be point-in-time.
- Conflating the delivery-cost CPP ($750/point, an internal AgileEVM planning figure) with a customer-facing
  revenue-per-point figure ($900/point in Scenario B) — they are built on different bases (cost vs. price) and
  must never be used interchangeably.

**AI in this topic.** Contract-clause classifiers can flag likely right-to-invoice-eligible fee structures and
auto-compute an output-method % complete from a connected backlog tool (cross-reference KA 13.4, KA 13.5). They
cannot determine whether the invoiced amount genuinely "corresponds directly" to value delivered, or whether
"no alternative use" truly holds — both are accounting judgements requiring the professional's sign-off.

**Cross-references.** KA 2.2.6, KA 2.2.7, KA 2.2.11 (over-time criteria, progress measures, contract asset/
liability, in full); KA 7.1 (T&M, capped T&M and fixed-price contract types); KA 7.5 (the operational
over-/under-billing view, forward reference); KA 9.5.1–9.5.2 (the CPP and BAC reused here); KA 9.6
(contracting for agile delivery).

### Key terms

- **Fixed-capacity funding** — an agile funding model in which a team is funded at a stable run-rate per
  iteration, regardless of points delivered; the mechanism behind the inverted iron triangle.
- **Rolling-wave (progressive) budgeting** — releasing funding in successive, gated waves rather than in full
  at inception.
- **Run-rate (R)** — the funded cost per sprint ($).
- **Velocity (V)** — story points completed per sprint; `V(planned)` is the planning-time target,
  `V(actual)`/`V(avg)` the observed rate.
- **Cost per point (CPP)** — `BAC / SP(planned)` or equivalently `R / V(planned)`; the conversion factor from
  story points to currency, held fixed to its planning basis unless formally rebased.
- **AgileEVM** — this Body of Knowledge's descriptive term for reusing PV/EV/AC/CV/SV/CPI/SPI/EAC against a
  story-point backlog, with the scope-variable assumptions of 9.5.2 stated explicitly.
- **CPI = SPI = V(avg)/V(planned)** — the structural identity that holds under fixed-capacity funding when AC
  tracks the funded run-rate exactly; breaks once AC decouples from that schedule.
- **Backlog growth** — points added to an agile backlog mid-release; requires explicit reforecasting and a
  fund/descope decision, not silent absorption.
- **Right-to-invoice practical expedient** — an IFRS 15 method recognising revenue at the amount the entity
  has a right to invoice, where that amount corresponds directly to value delivered to date.
- **Output method (agile application)** — `cumulative points delivered / total points in the current backlog`
  as an IFRS 15 progress measure, distinct from an input (cost-to-cost) method.

### Sample MCQs

**MCQ 9.5.1 [topic 9.5.1, level: recall]**
In a fixed-capacity agile funding model, what is held fixed and what becomes the variable that is forecast?
A. Scope is fixed; time and cost are forecast
B. **Time and cost are fixed (the run-rate and release length); scope is variable and is forecast** ✔
C. Nothing is fixed — agile has no budget
D. Story points are fixed permanently at contract signature
*Rationale:* the inverted iron triangle (KA 8.6.4, KA 9.5.1) fixes the funded run-rate and release length and
treats the deliverable scope as the forecast output. A describes classical, fixed-scope funding; C and D
misrepresent fixed-capacity funding as either absent or scope-fixed.

**MCQ 9.5.2 [topic 9.5.1, level: application — numerical]**
A team is funded at $15,000/sprint. The full backlog is planned at 240 points with a target velocity of 20
points/sprint. What is the funded BAC?
A. $150,000
B. **$180,000** ✔
C. $200,000
D. $240,000
*Rationale:* `n(planned) = 240/20 = 12 sprints`; `BAC = 12 × 15,000 = 180,000`. A is the Field App's own BAC,
not this scenario's; C and D mistake the point count or a wrong rate for the budget.

**MCQ 9.5.3 [topic 9.5.2, level: application — numerical]**
Cumulative story points delivered are 90, and the cost-per-point conversion factor (CPP) is $750/point. What
is the Earned Value (EV)?
A. **$67,500** ✔
B. $75,000
C. $90,000
D. $120,000
*Rationale:* `EV = 90 × 750 = 67,500`. B is this scenario's AC, not EV — a common EV/AC mix-up. C mistakes the
90-point count directly for a dollar figure, the classic "points as cost" error. D is unsupported by the data.

**MCQ 9.5.4 [topic 9.5.2, level: application — numerical]**
Using EV = $67,500 and AC = $75,000, what is the CPI?
A. 1.11
B. **0.90** ✔
C. −$7,500
D. 1.00
*Rationale:* `CPI = 67,500/75,000 = 0.90`. A inverts the ratio; C is the CV value mislabelled as CPI, a
unit-confusion error; D wrongly assumes on-target cost performance.

**MCQ 9.5.5 [topic 9.5.3, level: application — numerical]**
The remaining backlog is 110 points, average velocity is 18 points/sprint, and the run-rate is $15,000/sprint.
Rounding up to a whole sprint, what is the forecast cost of the **sprints still needed** (not the total
project cost)?
A. $90,000
B. **$105,000** ✔
C. $110,000
D. $165,000
*Rationale:* `110/18 = 6.11 → 7 sprints`; `7 × 15,000 = 105,000`. A rounds down instead of up; C mistakes the
point count for a dollar figure; D is the *total* forecast cost including sprints already spent, not the
remaining cost alone.

**MCQ 9.5.6 [topic 9.5.5, level: application — numerical]**
Under IFRS 15's output method, cumulative points delivered are 90 out of a current total backlog of 200
points, and the fixed contract price is $180,000. What is cumulative revenue recognised?
A. $67,500
B. $75,000
C. **$81,000** ✔
D. $85,000
*Rationale:* `% complete = 90/200 = 45.0%`; `Revenue = 45.0% × 180,000 = 81,000`. A is the AgileEVM EV
(a *cost*-basis figure, wrongly used as revenue); B is the AC from the T&M scenario; D is the amount billed,
not revenue recognised.

### Self-check questions (answers in Appendix C)

1. Using the base-case Sprint-5 AgileEVM figures (EV $67,500; AC $75,000; PV $75,000; BAC $150,000), compute
   CV, SV, CPI and SPI, and explain why CV = SV and CPI = SPI in this particular case.
2. A team's backlog grows from 200 to 230 points at the Sprint-5 checkpoint (90 points delivered so far,
   average velocity 18 points/sprint, run-rate $15,000/sprint). Forecast the additional sprints and the total
   forecast cost needed to complete the revised backlog (round up to a whole sprint).
3. Explain, in your own words, why a capped time-and-materials agile contract using the right-to-invoice
   practical expedient typically generates no contract asset or liability, while a fixed-price agile release
   measured by an output method usually does.

**Answers**

1. `CV = EV − AC = 67,500 − 75,000 = −7,500` (adverse). `SV = EV − PV = 67,500 − 75,000 = −7,500` (adverse).
   `CPI = 67,500/75,000 = 0.90`; `SPI = 67,500/75,000 = 0.90`. They match because, under fixed-capacity
   funding drawn exactly at the funded run-rate, AC and PV both reduce to the same figure (sprints elapsed ×
   run-rate) — EV is being compared against that one number twice.
2. `SP(remaining) = 230 − 90 = 140`; `sprints(needed) = 140/18 = 7.78 → 8`; `total sprints = 5 + 8 = 13`;
   `total forecast cost = 13 × 15,000 = 195,000` (additional cost for the 8 further sprints alone =
   `8 × 15,000 = 120,000`).
3. Right-to-invoice ties revenue recognised each period directly to the amount invoiced for that period's
   effort, so cumulative revenue and cumulative billing move together by construction (provided invoicing is
   not itself front- or back-loaded), leaving no gap. A fixed-price release instead recognises revenue by an
   output measure (% complete × total price) that is independent of the negotiated milestone billing
   schedule, so the two normally diverge — creating a contract asset or liability exactly as on a classical
   fixed-price construction contract (KA 2.2.11).
