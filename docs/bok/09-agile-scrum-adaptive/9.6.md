# Domain 9 — Agile, Scrum & Adaptive Delivery for Project Controls

## KA 9.6 — Hybrid delivery & agile governance

KA 9.1–9.4 established agile foundations, the Scrum framework, backlog/metrics mechanics and Kanban/Lean/
scaling; KA 9.5 built AgileEVM — translating velocity and burn-rate into cost forecasts, CPI/SPI/EAC and
IFRS 15 revenue recognition for a variable-scope engagement. This closing KA of Domain 9 answers a different
question from either of those: not "how does an agile team plan and measure its own work" (9.1–9.5) and not
"which development approach fits this scope" (KA 8.6), but **how does an organisation that is fundamentally
governed by stage gates, milestones and a PMO actually *govern* an agile workstream living inside it, and
report its status upward in language that governance is built to understand?** KA 8.6.3 already named the
resulting pattern — **hybrid delivery**, a predictive spine with agile pockets — and worked through *when*
and *why* Meridian Rail Contracting LLC's programme takes that shape. This KA does not repeat that ground; it
picks up where 8.6.3 stops and builds the governance mechanics: designing the interface between gate cadence
and sprint cadence, tailoring how much governance weight a given pocket actually needs, funding and
quality-gating an agile pocket without destroying its adaptiveness, translating its velocity and burnup into
the RAG/EVM language a predictive-minded steering board already reads (cross-reference KA 4.3, KA 9.5), and
maintaining assurance over it. Continuity runs on the same two illustrations used throughout: **Meridian**'s
predictive **Pipeline Segment 4B** (BAC $600,000 / SAR 2,250,000 at an illustrative **SAR 3.75 = USD 1**;
KA 6.1–6.3) and its agile pocket, the **Field Progress & Punch-list App** (KA 8.6.1, KA 8.6.4). This KA
adopts, as its concrete funding structure, the capped scenario KA 8.6.4 floated illustratively — a
$180,000 / 12-sprint not-to-exceed cap for the app's initial 200-point MVP release, one release within the
app's longer overall engagement (KA 8.6.3's ≈33-sprint span) — and continues the sprint-by-sprint data KA
8.6.4 began (Sprints 1–3: 16, 19, 21 points) through to Sprint 6.

### 9.6.1 What hybrid governance means: the predictive envelope with agile delivery inside

**Definition & purpose.** Where KA 8.6.3 defined **hybrid delivery** as a programme, **hybrid governance** is
the specific control architecture that makes it work: a fixed, predictive **envelope** — stage gates,
milestone dates, funding authority, quality sign-off — wrapped around one or more agile **pockets**, each
running its own internal sprint cadence, that report status upward through the envelope's cadence rather than
their own. The envelope does not dictate *how* the pocket plans its sprints; the pocket does not dictate *when*
the envelope's gates fall. Designing the interface between the two — what crosses it, and how often — is this
KA's subject.

**The underlying principle.** A governance architecture has exactly two layers to design, and the entire
discipline of this KA lives in keeping them separate: an **outer layer** of fixed decision rights (gate
reviews, funding release, milestone dates — set by the programme, not the team) and an **inner layer** of
adaptive execution (sprint planning, backlog re-prioritisation, the Daily Scrum — owned by the team, not the
programme). The interface between them should carry **status and funding decisions**, never day-to-day
planning control. Get this wrong in either direction and the pocket fails: push the envelope's cadence and
formality down into the pocket (a gate review demanded every sprint) and adaptiveness is destroyed before it
can deliver any benefit (developed as *water-scrum-fall*, 9.6.6); let the pocket ignore the envelope entirely
and the programme loses its ability to fund, sequence or rely on the pocket's output at all.

**Example 9.6.1 — locating the interface on Meridian's programme.**

*Scenario.* Meridian's 24-month, seven-gate predictive spine (KA 8.6.3, KA 10.1) runs Design & route
engineering through Reinstatement & handover. The Field App's agile pocket spans Months 3–18 in roughly
two-week sprints; its first release — the 200-point MVP (photo-capture, punch-list assignment, offline sync;
KA 8.6.1) — is funded as a capped T&M engagement, $180,000 / 12 sprints (KA 7.1.5), released in three
$60,000 tranches gated on PMO acceptance of the demoed increment (9.6.3).

*Required.* Identify what crosses the envelope/pocket interface, and what stays inside the pocket.

*Working/Answer.* **Crosses the interface:** the tranche-release decision (every four sprints); the monthly
roll-up status feeding Meridian's existing programme report (KA 8.6.3, KA 4.3); the MVP's overall completion
forecast against the 12-sprint cap. **Stays inside the pocket:** which specific backlog items are pulled into
which sprint; the team's own Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum and Sprint Retrospective; the Definition of Done
applied story by story.

*Interpretation.* The PMO never asks "what is in Sprint 7's backlog" — that question is answered inside the
pocket. It does ask "is the MVP still tracking to land within $180,000 and 12 sprints" — that question is
answered at the interface, translated into the board's own language (9.6.4).

**Common pitfalls.**
- Designing the interface once and never revisiting it — a pocket's materiality or risk profile can change
  (9.6.2), and the interface should be reviewed at each tranche gate, not fixed for the engagement's life.
- Assuming a single interface design suits every pocket in a programme — a safety-critical pocket and a
  low-risk internal tool warrant materially different interfaces (9.6.2).
- Confusing "the interface is thin" with "there is no governance" — a thin interface is a deliberately
  designed control, not an absence of one (developed fully in 9.6.5).

**AI in this topic.** An AI-based programme-structuring assistant can read a set of work-package descriptions
and propose a first-draft interface — which elements look like funding/milestone decisions versus
team-internal planning — from precedent across an organisation's past hybrid programmes (cross-reference
KA 13.4, KA 13.5). It cannot weigh this programme's specific contractual, safety or organisational-risk
context; the interface design remains an accountable governance decision for the PMO and the controls
professional, not a template applied unread.

**Cross-references.** KA 8.6.3 (hybrid delivery patterns, in full — this topic builds on it, not repeats it);
KA 8.6.4 (the inverted iron triangle underlying the pocket's funding logic); KA 9.1–9.4 (Scrum mechanics
operating inside the pocket); KA 7.1.5 (capped T&M, the contract basis adopted here).

### 9.6.2 Tailoring the governance approach to context

**Definition & purpose.** Not every agile pocket in a programme warrants the same governance weight. This
topic applies PMI's **tailoring** principle (introduced at development-approach level in KA 8.6.3) to the
*design of the interface itself* — how much formality, reporting frequency and sign-off a given pocket's
governance regime should actually carry.

**The underlying principle.** Governance intensity should track **materiality and risk**, not habit. A pocket
that is small relative to the programme, carries low safety/regulatory exposure, and sits with a co-located,
trusted in-house team can be governed lightly — a monthly roll-up and a tranche gate, as in 9.6.1. A pocket
that is large relative to the programme, touches safety-critical or regulated functionality, or is delivered
by a third party warrants a heavier interface — more frequent gates, independent QA sign-off, tighter
reporting. Over-governing a low-risk pocket wastes the team's capacity on reporting instead of delivery and
erodes exactly the adaptiveness the approach was chosen for; under-governing a high-risk pocket leaves the
programme blind to a genuine exposure until it is expensive to fix.

**Tailoring-factor table.**

| Factor | Favours a lighter-touch interface | Favours a heavier-touch interface |
|---|---|---|
| Materiality to overall programme cost/schedule | Low (small % of programme value) | High |
| Safety/regulatory exposure | Low | High (e.g. touches a safety case or certified system) |
| Team structure | Co-located, in-house, established track record | Dispersed, third-party, new relationship |
| Contract basis (KA 7.1) | Capped T&M with the client's own PMO | Target-cost or fixed-milestone third-party agreement |
| Organisational agile maturity | Established (recognised ceremonies, tooling, history) | Immature (first agile pocket in the organisation) |

**Example 9.6.2 — tailoring two Meridian-adjacent pockets.**

*Scenario.* Compare the Field App (low materiality — under 3% of programme value; low regulatory exposure; a
trusted three-person in-house team) against a hypothetical second pocket: safety-interlock firmware for a
level-crossing controller, delivered by a specialist third party under an iterative development process.

*Required.* Recommend an interface weight for each.

*Working/Answer.* The Field App scores toward the light-touch column on every factor in the table — a monthly
roll-up and a four-sprint tranche gate (9.6.1) is proportionate. The firmware pocket scores toward the
heavy-touch column on every factor — materiality is higher, the exposure is safety-critical, the relationship
is new and third-party — and would warrant sprint-level QA evidence review, more frequent (e.g. two-sprint)
gates, and independent technical sign-off before any release, even though both pockets sit at the same point
on KA 8.6's development-approach spectrum.

*Interpretation.* "Adaptive" as a *development approach* choice (KA 8.6.5) and "how heavily governed" as a
*governance-design* choice are independent decisions — the firmware pocket remains genuinely agile in its
delivery mechanics while carrying materially more governance weight than the Field App.

**Common pitfalls.**
- Applying one governance template to every agile pocket in a portfolio regardless of materiality — the most
  common source of both over-governance and under-governance in practice.
- Treating "heavier governance" as meaning "less agile" — added QA evidence and gate frequency can sit
  entirely at the interface (9.6.1) without touching the team's internal sprint mechanics at all.
- Setting the interface weight once at kick-off and never re-testing it as the pocket's materiality changes
  (e.g. scope grows to touch a regulated feature partway through).

**AI in this topic.** A portfolio-level AI assistant can flag where an agile pocket's actual materiality (spend
run-rate, feature scope) has drifted from the risk profile assumed when its governance weight was first set
(cross-reference KA 13.5) — a genuinely useful early-warning scan across many pockets at once. It cannot make
the risk-appetite judgement itself; re-weighting a pocket's governance is a decision for the PMO and the
accountable controls professional.

**Cross-references.** KA 8.6.2, KA 8.6.5 (the suitability filter — a related but distinct judgement, on
development approach rather than governance weight); KA 12.1 (risk appetite); KA 7.1 (contract basis as a
tailoring factor); KA 11.3 (internal control intensity, an analogous tailoring judgement in a different
context).

### 9.6.3 Governing agile within a stage-gate/PMO world: milestones, funding tranches and quality gates

**Definition & purpose.** This topic works the specific mechanics that sit at the envelope/pocket interface
identified in 9.6.1: how a **milestone** (a fixed, external commitment the programme relies on) differs from
a **sprint boundary** (an internal planning cadence); how an agile pocket is **funded by increment** rather
than a single upfront sum; and how a sprint-level **quality gate** (the Definition of Done) differs from a
programme-level one (PMO acceptance).

**The underlying principle.** A milestone and a sprint boundary answer different questions. A milestone asks
"what capability, by what date, can the programme and its stakeholders rely on?" — it is set at a **release**
boundary (a cluster of sprints delivering something genuinely usable) and, once set, is a fixed external
commitment like any predictive milestone (KA 8.2, KA 10.1). A sprint boundary asks "what can this team
usefully pull into the next two weeks?" — it is an internal planning device, re-set every iteration, and
setting a *milestone* at every sprint boundary collapses the pocket's ability to re-plan (9.6.6). **Funding by
increment** follows the same logic on the money side: rather than releasing the pocket's entire budget upfront
(which removes any funding-side incentive to demonstrate value) or micromanaging spend sprint by sprint (which
re-imposes predictive control inside the pocket), funding is released in **tranches** tied to *release*
boundaries, each gated on evidence that the prior tranche's increment was actually accepted — directly
mirroring rolling-wave budgeting (KA 3.1) applied to a variable-scope engagement.

**Milestone vs sprint boundary vs quality gate — comparison table.**

| Element | Set/owned by | Cadence | What crosses to governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sprint boundary | The team | Every sprint | Nothing by default — internal re-planning |
| Definition of Done (DoD) | The team (KA 9.2) | Every story, every sprint | Evidence retained, reviewed on sample (9.6.5) |
| Release milestone | Programme/PMO, agreed with the team | Every N sprints (a release) | A fixed, external commitment |
| Tranche-release gate | PMO, funding authority | Aligned to release milestones | The funding decision itself |
| Stage gate | Programme governance (KA 10.1) | Per the predictive spine | The pocket's rolled-up status, only |

**Example 9.6.3 — funding the Field App's MVP release by tranche.**

*Scenario.* The MVP release (200 points, $180,000 cap, 12 sprints — 9.6.1) is contracted as capped T&M with a
not-to-exceed ceiling (KA 7.1.5): the rate ($15,000/sprint, SAR 56,250) is fixed, but total sprints drawn
against the cap is the client's — Meridian's PMO's — cost-control lever. Funding is released in three $60,000
tranches, each covering four sprints, with tranche *n+1* gated on PMO acceptance, at the Sprint Review, of the
increment tranche *n* was meant to deliver.

*Required.* State the funding position at the Sprint-6 data date (mid-way through tranche 2) and identify what
gated each release so far.

*Working/Answer.* Tranche 1 ($60,000, Sprints 1–4) was released at kick-off and fully drawn; its release gate
— PMO acceptance of the photo-capture and punch-list-assignment increment demoed at the Sprint 4 Review — was
met. Tranche 2 ($60,000, Sprints 5–8) is open and two of its four sprints drawn (Sprints 5–6); cumulative draw
to date = 6 sprints × $15,000 = **$90,000**, exactly half the $180,000 cap. Tranche 3's release will in turn
be gated on PMO acceptance of tranche 2's increment at the Sprint 8 Review.

*Interpretation.* Note what the tranche gate actually tests: **an accepted, demoed increment**, not a document
review of a plan — the quality gate here is empirical (does the working software do what was promised)
rather than documentary (does the design conform to a specification), which is the core distinction between a
predictive stage gate and an agile release gate even though both serve the same funding-control purpose.

**Common pitfalls.**
- Releasing the full $180,000 cap upfront "to reduce administration" — this removes the very incentive
  structure the tranche gate exists to create, and converts a controlled, incremental funding model into an
  uncontrolled lump-sum draw against a variable-scope engagement.
- Setting a milestone at every sprint boundary rather than at release boundaries — this is the specific
  mechanism by which "hybrid governance" collapses into water-scrum-fall (9.6.6): the team ends up re-planning
  nothing, because every two weeks is treated as an external, fixed commitment.
- Treating the Definition of Done (a team-level "this story is genuinely finished") as if it were equivalent to
  the tranche-release gate (a programme-level "this increment is accepted and paid for") — they are related but
  distinct checks, at different altitudes, and conflating them either weakens funding control or duplicates it
  unnecessarily.

**AI in this topic.** Workflow-automation tools can track each tranche's release conditions against sprint-tool
evidence (accepted stories, DoD completion, Sprint Review notes) and flag when a tranche is ready for the PMO's
acceptance decision (cross-reference KA 13.5) — a genuine efficiency gain in preparing the gate review. They
cannot authorise the release themselves; accepting an increment as fit for funding release is a judgement about
whether the demoed capability genuinely meets the programme's need, which remains the PMO's and the
professional's call.

**Cross-references.** KA 7.1.5 (capped T&M/NTE, the contract mechanics); KA 7.1.6 (target-cost with agile
delivery, an alternative for larger or higher-risk pockets); KA 3.1 (rolling-wave/incremental budgeting, the
predictive analogue); KA 9.2 (Definition of Done, Sprint Review); KA 5.4 (change control — largely superseded
inside the pocket by backlog re-prioritisation, KA 8.6.4).

### 9.6.4 Reporting agile progress to a predictive-minded steering board: translating velocity and burnup into RAG/EVM

**Definition & purpose.** A steering board fluent in BAC, CV, CPI, SPI and RAG (KA 4.3, KA 6.1–6.3) should
not be required to learn burndown-chart literacy to understand an agile pocket's health, any more than the
pocket's team should be required to abandon velocity and burnup to satisfy the board. This topic is the
**translation layer**: mapping the pocket's story points and fixed sprint run-rate into the same
$-denominated PV/EV/AC/CPI/SPI/EAC/VAC vocabulary, and the same seven-block anatomy and RAG rule (KA 4.3.3,
KA 4.3.4), already used for Pipeline Segment 4B — so the board reads one consistent report, not two
incompatible ones.

**The underlying principle.** AgileEVM (KA 9.5, in full) reuses the classical EV formulae under an explicit,
stated assumption set. Applied to a **fixed-capacity, capped-T&M** pocket like the Field App's MVP release,
the mapping is direct: treat the funding cap as the AgileEVM BAC, treat cumulative story points delivered
against the total backlog as the scope-completion fraction, and value both the planned and actual positions in
$ by applying that fraction to the BAC:

`PV = (planned cumulative points ÷ total backlog points) × BAC` — `EV = (actual cumulative points ÷ total
backlog points) × BAC` — `AC = sprints elapsed × cost per sprint`

**The sharpest translation caution.** Because the sprint run-rate is fixed by the capped-T&M contract, AC
tracks exactly against the planned duration — `AC = PV` at every data point, provided the team's staffing stays
stable (KA 8.6.4's caution about verifying that stability applies here directly). This means CPI and SPI are
**numerically identical** under this funding model, and a naive reading of "CPI and SPI both Green" can wrongly
be taken as "no risk" by a board used to reading it that way on a fixed-scope contract. It is not: a
fixed-capacity pocket can never show a cost overrun in the AC sense (the team physically cannot spend more than
the sprint rate), so CPI/SPI here are really a **scope-realisation index** — the true risk signal for the board
is whether the *forecast* (EAC against the cap, VAC, headroom) shows the backlog landing inside the funding
envelope, not whether spend is on track, which under this model it always trivially is.

**Example 9.6.4 — translating the Field App's Sprint-6 position for Meridian's steering board.**

*Scenario.* MVP release: total backlog 200 points; funding cap (AgileEVM BAC) $180,000 (SAR 675,000),
12 sprints at $15,000/sprint (SAR 56,250), planned evenly at 200/12 = 16.7 points/sprint. Continuing KA
8.6.4's actuals (Sprint 1–3: 16, 19, 21 points), Sprints 4–6 deliver 20, 22 and 23 points.

*Required.* (a) Cumulative actual points and average velocity to Sprint 6; (b) PV, EV, AC, CV, SV, CPI, SPI
and the board's RAG status; (c) EAC, VAC and the forecast completion point against the 12-sprint cap.

*Working.*

(a) Cumulative points `= 56 + 20 + 22 + 23 = 121`. Average velocity `= 121 / 6 = 20.2 points/sprint`.

(b) Planned cumulative points at Sprint 6 `= 6 × 16.7 ≈ 100` (equivalently `(6/12) × 200`).
`PV = (100/200) × 180,000 = 90,000`. `AC = 6 × 15,000 = 90,000` (confirming `AC = PV`, as the underlying
principle predicts). `EV = (121/200) × 180,000 = 108,900`.
`CV = EV − AC = 108,900 − 90,000 = 18,900` (favourable). `SV = EV − PV = 108,900 − 90,000 = 18,900`
(favourable — identical to CV, since AC = PV).
`CPI = EV/AC = 108,900/90,000 = 1.21`. `SPI = EV/PV = 108,900/90,000 = 1.21` (identical, for the same reason).
Applying KA 4.3.4's bands (Green ≥ 0.95): both indices Green; worst-of → **overall Green**.

(c) `EAC = BAC/CPI = 180,000/1.21 ≈ 148,760` (SAR 557,850) — equivalently, reprojecting the actual
cost-per-point rate (`AC ÷ actual points = 90,000/121 ≈ 743.80/point`) across the full 200-point backlog:
`200 × 743.80 ≈ 148,760`, the same figure by construction.
`VAC = BAC − EAC = 180,000 − 148,760 ≈ 31,240` (SAR 117,150) favourable — **17.3% headroom** against the cap.
Cross-checking against whole-sprint forecasting (KA 8.6.4's method): remaining backlog `= 200 − 121 = 79`
points; further sprints `= 79/20.2 ≈ 3.9 → 4 sprints` (rounded up); total sprints `= 6 + 4 = 10`, i.e. **two
sprints inside the 12-sprint cap**, at a discrete forecast cost of `10 × 15,000 = 150,000` — close to, but
`$1,240` above, the smooth $148,760 EAC, the gap explained entirely by the fact that sprints, unlike the
smooth EAC formula, can only be bought whole.

*Answer.* **Board-facing summary: RAG Green (CPI 1.21, SPI 1.21); scope 60.5% complete (121/200 points)
against 50.0% of the funding cap drawn ($90,000/$180,000); forecast completion in 10 total sprints
(4 sprints/8 weeks remaining) at a forecast total cost of $148,760–$150,000 — $30,000–$31,240 (17–17.3%) inside
the $180,000 cap.**

*Interpretation.* A predictive board reads this exactly like a favourable Pipeline Segment 4B report: Green,
comfortably inside budget, ahead of the planned burn. The professional's added narrative line — the one a board
must be told explicitly, because nothing in the numbers alone signals it — is that AC will *always* equal PV
here while the team's staffing stays stable, so "Green cost/schedule" is a structural feature of this funding
model, not itself evidence of health; the evidence of health is the **scope-forecast headroom** (VAC/EAC
against the cap), which the board should treat as the pocket's real leading indicator, not the CPI/SPI reading.

**Common pitfalls.**

- Computing CPI/SPI from this fixed-capacity model and treating them as comparable, on a like-for-like basis,

### 9.6.5 Assurance and controls over agile delivery

**Definition & purpose.** Agile delivery does not remove internal control; it **relocates** it. Where a
predictive gate review produces a periodic, document-heavy sign-off (KA 4.3.6), an agile pocket's controls are
continuous and embedded in its own ceremonies and tooling — but they must still satisfy the same control
objectives (authorisation, completeness, accuracy, segregation of duties, KA 11.3) that any other spend and
delivery activity is held to.

**The underlying principle.** Every classical control has a working agile equivalent; the discipline is making
sure the equivalent is actually configured and evidenced, not assuming agile tooling captures it automatically.

**Control-mapping table.**

| Classical control (predictive) | Agile equivalent | What it evidences |
|---|---|---|
| Formal change request form (KA 5.4) | Logged backlog re-prioritisation with a stated rationale | Authorised change, not silent scope drift |
| Milestone sign-off document | Sprint Review acceptance recorded in the sprint tool | The increment was reviewed and accepted |
| Segregation of duties on approval | Product Owner ≠ developer ≠ tranche-gate approver | No one accepts/pays for their own work |
| Report assurance trail (KA 4.3.6) | Sprint-tool audit log (who moved a story to "done," when) | A traceable record of who did what, and when |
| Independent review before sign-off | Independent QA/PMO review of Definition of Done evidence | The "done" claim is checked, not self-certified |

**Example 9.6.5 — auditing the Field App's tranche-2 release.**

*Scenario.* Ahead of releasing tranche 3 (Sprint 8's PMO gate, 9.6.3), an internal reviewer checks tranche 2's
control evidence: Definition of Done checklists for each of the 43 points accepted in Sprints 5–6 (of which 22
belong to Sprint 6); the Sprint Review notes recording the Product Owner's (not a developer's) acceptance of
each story; and the sprint tool's audit log confirming no story was marked "done" by the same person who coded
it.

*Required.* State what the reviewer is testing and the conclusion if all three checks pass.

*Working/Answer.* The reviewer is testing **authorisation** (was each story genuinely accepted by an
accountable party, not self-certified), **completeness** (does DoD evidence exist for every point claimed as
delivered) and **segregation of duties** (is the "done" decision independent of the person who did the work) —
the same three objectives KA 11.3 applies to any O2C/P2P control. If all three pass, the tranche-2 evidence
supports releasing tranche 3 on schedule; if any fails (for example, a developer self-marking their own story
"done" with no PO review), the gap must be closed — additional evidence gathered or the story re-reviewed —
before the tranche-3 release decision is made, exactly as an unreconciled figure holds a classical report
(KA 4.3.6, Example 4.3.6).

**Common pitfalls.**
- Assuming "agile" itself means "no audit trail" — modern sprint tooling retains a full, timestamped history by
  default; the actual failure mode is an organisation not configuring or reviewing that history, not agile
  methodology lacking one.
- A Product Owner acting as the sole approver of their own team's work with no independent PMO or QA check — a
  segregation-of-duties gap that is easy to miss because "the PO accepts the story" looks, superficially, like
  a control, when in fact it may be the *only* check.
- Treating the Definition of Done as sufficient evidence on its own, without independent sampling — DoD is the
  team's own quality bar; assurance requires an independent party to test that the bar was genuinely met.

**AI in this topic.** AI-based audit and process-mining tools can mine sprint-tool logs at scale for control
gaps — stories marked "done" by their own author, missing DoD evidence, backlog changes with no logged
rationale (cross-reference KA 11.3, KA 13.5) — surfacing exceptions far faster than manual sampling. They
cannot conclude whether a flagged exception is a genuine control failure or an explainable edge case (for
example, a one-person specialist team where strict segregation is genuinely impractical and a compensating
control has been agreed); that investigation and conclusion remain the reviewer's and the professional's.

**Cross-references.** KA 11.3 (segregation of duties and the audit trail, in full — this topic applies it to
agile artefacts, not repeats it); KA 4.3.6 (the predictive-side assurance trail this mirrors); KA 9.2 (Sprint
Review, Definition of Done — the artefacts being evidenced); KA 5.4 (change control, the predictive analogue
to logged backlog re-prioritisation).

### 9.6.6 The controls professional's role: bridging the two worlds

**Definition & purpose.** Across 9.6.1–9.6.5, one role recurs at every interface point: the project controls
professional, who is neither the pocket's Scrum Master nor a classical cost engineer applying predictive method
unmodified, but the person accountable for making the two governance languages meet without either one
distorting the other.

**The underlying principle — three concrete bridging responsibilities.** (1) **Design the interface** (9.6.1,
9.6.2) so gate cadence and sprint cadence do not collide, tailored to each pocket's actual materiality and
risk. (2) **Produce the translated report** (9.6.4) on the board's cadence and in the board's vocabulary,
without imposing that cadence or vocabulary on the team doing the work. (3) **Maintain assurance** (9.6.5)
using agile-native evidence, without recreating predictive-style paperwork inside the pocket "just to be safe."
Two named failure modes show what happens when this bridging role is done badly, in opposite directions.

**Failure mode 1 — water-scrum-fall.** Ceremonies survive; behaviours do not. A pocket holds daily stand-ups
and calls its two-week cycles "sprints," but the envelope has been allowed to impose gate-style, document-heavy
sign-off at every sprint boundary (9.6.3's pitfall), leaving no room for genuine backlog re-prioritisation. The
team pays adaptive delivery's overhead (constant reporting) without receiving its benefit (the ability to
change direction cheaply, KA 8.6.2) — this is the governance-design mirror of KA 8.6.3's "agile-in-name-only,"
caused specifically by an interface that leaked the envelope's cadence down into the pocket.

**Failure mode 2 — governance theatre.** The interface exists on paper — tranche gates, RAG dashboards, an
assurance trail (9.6.5) — but nobody actually uses it to decide anything: gates are rubber-stamped regardless
of the evidence, RAG dots sit unread in a report nobody escalates from, and the translation exercise (9.6.4)
becomes a compliance artefact rather than a genuine decision input. Where water-scrum-fall over-governs,
governance theatre under-*acts* on governance that formally exists — both leave the programme no better
informed than if no interface had been designed at all.

**Example 9.6.6 — a short diagnostic for the Field App's governance.**

*Scenario.* At the Sprint 8 tranche-3 gate, the PMO asks whether the MVP's governance is actually working, not
merely present.

*Required.* Three diagnostic questions, applied.

*Working/Answer.* (1) *Has any sprint boundary been treated as an external milestone, forcing a re-plan
outside the team's control?* No — only the four-sprint tranche boundaries carry funding decisions (9.6.3); the
team re-planned its backlog freely within each tranche. (2) *Has the RAG/EVM translation (9.6.4) changed any
actual decision* — a tranche release, an escalation, a scope conversation? Yes — the Sprint-6 Green/17.3%
headroom reading directly supported releasing tranche 3 on schedule with no additional review. (3) *Is the
assurance evidence (9.6.5) being sampled and acted on, or merely filed?* The tranche-2 audit (Example 9.6.5)
was actually performed and its result used as an input to the release decision, not filed unread.

*Answer.* **All three answers indicate a working hybrid governance regime — neither water-scrum-fall (the
team retained real re-planning freedom) nor governance theatre (the evidence and translated reporting were
genuinely used to decide, not just produced).**

*Interpretation.* This three-question diagnostic — is re-planning genuinely happening; is the translated report
genuinely used; is the evidence genuinely sampled — is the fastest practical check a professional can run
against any hybrid pocket's governance, and is worth applying at every tranche gate, not only when something
has visibly gone wrong.

**Common pitfalls.**
- Assuming water-scrum-fall and governance theatre are opposite problems requiring opposite fixes — both are
  actually the same underlying failure (an interface, 9.6.1, that was designed but never genuinely operated as
  intended) and the same three-question diagnostic catches either.
- Measuring governance success by the existence of artefacts (a RAG dashboard, an assurance log) rather than by
  whether they changed a decision — an unused artefact is governance theatre by definition, however complete
  it looks.
- Assuming the bridging role is a one-off design task completed at kick-off, rather than a standing
  responsibility exercised at every tranche gate and report cycle for the life of the pocket.

**AI in this topic.** Across Domain 9's adaptive-delivery workflows, AI tools genuinely assist the bridging
role: backlog drafting and story splitting, sprint/velocity forecasting, flow-anomaly detection, retrospective
analysis, and — as shown throughout this KA — automated agile-to-EVM reporting (cross-reference KA 13.4,
KA 13.5). None of them can design the envelope/pocket interface for a specific programme's risk and contractual
context, judge whether governance is being genuinely operated versus merely performed, or stand accountable
when a tranche-release or escalation decision is wrong. Every one of those judgements is, and remains, the
project controls professional's — consistent with the principle running through this entire Body of Knowledge:
**AI proposes; the professional disposes.**

**Cross-references.** KA 8.6.3 (agile-in-name-only, the development-approach-level analogue of water-scrum-fall
here); KA 9.6.1–9.6.5 (each bridging responsibility, in full); KA 13.6 (AI governance and sign-off, applied
more broadly across the Body of Knowledge).

### Key terms

- **Hybrid governance** — the control architecture, distinct from hybrid *delivery* (KA 8.6.3), that governs
  an agile pocket inside a predictive envelope: fixed decision rights outside, adaptive execution inside.
- **Envelope / pocket** — the outer predictive layer (gates, milestones, funding authority) and the inner
  adaptive layer (sprint planning, backlog re-prioritisation) of a hybrid governance model.
- **Interface** — the defined set of status and funding decisions that cross between envelope and pocket;
  everything else stays pocket-internal.
- **Release milestone** — a fixed, external commitment set at a release (cluster-of-sprints) boundary, distinct
  from an internal sprint boundary.
- **Funding by increment (tranche funding)** — releasing an agile pocket's budget in stages, each gated on
  evidence that the prior increment was accepted, rather than upfront or sprint-by-sprint.
- **Tranche-release gate** — the funding decision point, aligned to a release milestone, tested by an accepted,
  demoed increment rather than a document review.
- **AgileEVM translation (fixed-capacity case)** — mapping story points and a fixed sprint run-rate to
  $-denominated PV/EV/AC/CPI/SPI/EAC/VAC so a predictive-minded board can read an agile pocket's status in its
  own vocabulary (KA 9.5).
- **AC = PV caution** — under fixed-capacity funding, actual cost tracks planned value by construction, so
  CPI/SPI become a scope-realisation index; the leading risk indicator is EAC/VAC headroom against the cap,
  not the CPI/SPI reading itself.
- **Water-scrum-fall** — ceremonies survive, adaptive behaviours do not, because the envelope's cadence and
  formality have leaked into the pocket (e.g. a milestone set at every sprint boundary).
- **Governance theatre** — the interface, gates and assurance trail formally exist but are not genuinely used
  to make decisions.
- **Segregation of duties in agile** — ensuring the Product Owner, the developer and the tranche-gate approver
  are not the same person, mirroring classical segregation-of-duties controls (KA 11.3).

### Sample MCQs

**MCQ 9.6.1 [topic 9.6.1, level: recall]**
In a hybrid governance model, which of the following should cross the envelope/pocket interface?
A. The specific backlog items pulled into next sprint
B. The team's Daily Scrum discussion
C. **A tranche-release funding decision, gated on an accepted increment** ✔
D. The Definition of Done wording used by the team
*Rationale:* the interface carries funding and status decisions (9.6.1), not day-to-day planning detail. A, B
and D are all pocket-internal matters that stay with the team.

**MCQ 9.6.2 [topic 9.6.3, level: application]**
A programme sets a fixed, external milestone at every two-week sprint boundary for an agile pocket, requiring
formal sign-off before the next sprint can begin. What is the most likely consequence?
A. Improved adaptiveness, since sprints are reviewed more often
B. **Water-scrum-fall — the team loses the ability to re-plan, while still carrying agile's ceremonial
   overhead** ✔
C. A stronger tranche-funding control, with no downside
D. No effect, since milestones and sprint boundaries are the same thing
*Rationale:* per 9.6.3 and 9.6.6, treating every sprint boundary as an external milestone collapses the team's
re-planning freedom — the defining symptom of water-scrum-fall. A and C mistake added formality for added
control; D conflates two deliberately distinct concepts (9.6.3).

**MCQ 9.6.3 [topic 9.6.4, level: application — numerical]**
An agile pocket is funded at a fixed $20,000 per sprint under a capped T&M contract. At Sprint 5 of a planned
10-sprint, 150-point release, the team has delivered a cumulative 90 points. What are AC and PV at this data
date (BAC = $200,000)?
A. AC = $200,000; PV = $90,000
B. **AC = $100,000; PV = $100,000** ✔
C. AC = $100,000; PV = $120,000
D. AC = $90,000; PV = $100,000
*Rationale:* `AC = 5 sprints × 20,000 = 100,000`. Planned cumulative points at Sprint 5 of 10 `= 5/10 × 150 =
75`; `PV = (75/150) × 200,000 = 100,000` — equivalently `(5/10) × BAC`. Under fixed-capacity funding, `AC = PV`
by construction (9.6.4), which only option B reflects.

**MCQ 9.6.4 [topic 9.6.4, level: analysis]**
Continuing MCQ 9.6.3's data (AC = PV = $100,000; EV computed from 90/150 points against the $200,000 BAC),
CPI and SPI both compute to 1.20 and the board reads "Green." What is the most important caution the controls
professional should add?
A. None — a Green RAG confirms the pocket carries no risk
B. **Under fixed-capacity funding, AC will always equal PV while staffing is stable, so CPI/SPI here is a
   scope-realisation index, not a spend-control signal — the real risk indicator is the EAC/VAC headroom
   against the cap** ✔
C. CPI and SPI should be averaged into a single blended score for the board
D. The figures should be withheld until a variance actually appears
*Rationale:* this is 9.6.4's central teaching point. A overstates what a structurally-always-on-track cost
figure actually proves; C repeats an error KA 4.3.4 and KA 6.2 both warn against; D is neither necessary nor
consistent with timely reporting (KA 4.3.5).

**MCQ 9.6.5 [topic 9.6.6, level: analysis]**
A programme maintains a full RAG dashboard, an assurance log and quarterly tranche gates for an agile pocket,
but no gate has ever withheld funding or changed a decision regardless of the evidence presented. This is best
described as:
A. Water-scrum-fall
B. **Governance theatre** ✔
C. A well-functioning hybrid governance regime
D. Agile delivery, correctly applied
*Rationale:* the artefacts of governance are present but never actually used to decide anything — precisely
9.6.6's definition of governance theatre, the mirror-image failure to water-scrum-fall (A), which instead
over-imposes envelope control on the pocket's internal planning.

### Self-check questions (answers in Appendix C)

1. In your own words, distinguish water-scrum-fall from governance theatre, and explain why the same
   three-question diagnostic in 9.6.6 (Example 9.6.6) can detect either.
2. Using the Field App's MVP release (BAC $180,000, 12 sprints, 200-point backlog, $15,000/sprint), suppose
   that by Sprint 8 the team has delivered a cumulative 158 points. Compute PV, AC, EV, CPI and SPI, and state
   the resulting RAG status under KA 4.3.4's bands.
3. Explain why AC equals PV at every data point in the Field App's capped-T&M example, and why a board should
   not read a consistently Green CPI/SPI reading, on its own, as evidence that the pocket carries no risk.

**Answers**

1. **Water-scrum-fall** is over-governance in the wrong direction: the envelope's predictive cadence and
   formality (a milestone or sign-off at every sprint boundary) is imposed down into the pocket, so the team
   loses its ability to genuinely re-plan while still carrying agile's ceremonial overhead. **Governance
   theatre** is the opposite symptom with the same root cause: the interface, gates and assurance trail are
   designed and formally present, but nobody actually uses them to make a decision — funding is released and
   reports are filed regardless of the evidence. The 9.6.6 diagnostic (is re-planning genuinely happening; is
   the translated report genuinely used; is the evidence genuinely sampled) catches both, because both are, at
   root, the same failure: an interface that was designed but is not actually being operated as intended.
2. Planned cumulative points at Sprint 8 of 12 `= (8/12) × 200 = 133.3`; `PV = (133.3/200) × 180,000 = 120,000`
   (equivalently `(8/12) × 180,000 = 120,000`). `AC = 8 × 15,000 = 120,000` (confirming `AC = PV`). `EV =
   (158/200) × 180,000 = 142,200`. `CPI = EV/AC = 142,200/120,000 = 1.185`. `SPI = EV/PV = 142,200/120,000 =
   1.185` (identical to CPI, as expected under fixed-capacity funding). Applying KA 4.3.4's bands, both indices
   are `≥ 0.95` → **Green**, worst-of → **overall Green**.
3. AC equals PV because the sprint rate is fixed by the capped-T&M contract and the planned burnup was set at
   an even rate across the same 12 sprints the funding cap assumes — so however many sprints have elapsed, the
   money actually spent (`sprints elapsed × rate`) exactly equals the money the plan expected to have spent by
   that point, regardless of how much scope has actually been delivered. A consistently Green CPI/SPI therefore
   only confirms that spend is on its structurally-guaranteed track, not that the pocket is healthy; the
   genuine risk — whether the remaining backlog will fit inside the remaining cap — is visible only in the
   EAC/VAC headroom, which is why 9.6.4 treats headroom, not the ratio, as the board's real leading indicator.
