# Domain 9 — Agile, Scrum & Adaptive Delivery for Project Controls

## KA 9.6 — Hybrid delivery & agile governance

KA 9.1–9.4 established agile foundations, the Scrum framework and backlog/flow metrics; KA 9.5 builds
AgileEVM — translating velocity and burn-rate into cost forecasts, CPI/SPI/EAC and IFRS 15 revenue
recognition for a variable-scope engagement. This closing KA of Domain 9 answers a different question from
either: not "how does an agile team plan and measure its own work" (9.1–9.5), and not "which development
approach fits this scope" (KA 8.6), but **how does an organisation governed by stage gates, milestones and a
PMO actually *govern* an agile workstream living inside it, and report its status upward in language that
governance already understands?** KA 8.6.3 named the resulting pattern — **hybrid delivery**, a predictive
spine with agile pockets — and worked through *when* and *why* Meridian Rail Contracting LLC's programme takes
that shape; this KA does not repeat that ground. It builds the governance mechanics: the interface between
gate cadence and sprint cadence, tailoring how much governance weight a pocket needs, funding and
quality-gating a pocket without destroying its adaptiveness, translating its velocity and burnup into the
RAG/EVM language a predictive-minded steering board already reads (cross-reference KA 4.3, KA 9.5), and
maintaining assurance over it. Continuity runs on **Meridian**'s predictive **Pipeline Segment 4B** (BAC
$600,000 / SAR 2,250,000 at an illustrative **SAR 3.75 = USD 1**; KA 6.1–6.3) and its agile pocket, the
**Field Progress & Punch-list App** (KA 8.6.1, KA 8.6.4). This KA adopts, as the app's initial 200-point MVP
release's concrete funding structure, the capped scenario KA 8.6.4 floated illustratively: a $180,000,
12-sprint not-to-exceed cap (KA 7.1.5), released in three tranches. Its worked mini-case (9.6.4) reports the
pocket's status at the Sprint-8 tranche gate, using the cumulative burnup the team's tracked velocity history
produces (KA 9.3), without repeating that chapter's sprint-by-sprint mechanics.

### 9.6.1 What hybrid governance means: the predictive envelope with agile delivery inside

**Definition & purpose.** Where KA 8.6.3 defined **hybrid delivery** as a programme pattern, **hybrid
governance** is the control architecture that makes it work: a fixed, predictive **envelope** — stage gates,
milestone dates, funding authority, quality sign-off — wrapped around one or more agile **pockets**, each
running its own sprint cadence, that report status upward through the envelope's cadence rather than their
own. Designing the interface between the two — what crosses it, and how often — is this KA's subject.

**The underlying principle.** A governance architecture has exactly two layers to design: an **outer layer**
of fixed decision rights (gate reviews, funding release, milestone dates — owned by the programme) and an
**inner layer** of adaptive execution (sprint planning, backlog re-prioritisation, the Daily Scrum — owned by
the team). The interface should carry **status and funding decisions only**, never day-to-day planning
control. Push the envelope's cadence down into the pocket (a gate review demanded every sprint) and
adaptiveness is destroyed before it delivers any benefit (*water-scrum-fall*, 9.6.6); let the pocket ignore
the envelope entirely and the programme loses its ability to fund, sequence or rely on the pocket's output.

**Example 9.6.1 — locating the interface on Meridian's programme.**

*Scenario.* Meridian's 24-month, seven-gate predictive spine (KA 8.6.3, KA 10.1) runs Design & route
engineering through Reinstatement & handover. The Field App's agile pocket spans Months 3–18; its first
release — the 200-point MVP (photo-capture, punch-list assignment, offline sync; KA 8.6.1) — is funded as
capped T&M, $180,000 / 12 sprints (KA 7.1.5), in three $60,000 tranches gated on PMO acceptance of the
demoed increment.

*Required/Answer.* **Crosses the interface:** the tranche-release decision (every four sprints); the monthly
roll-up status feeding Meridian's existing programme report (KA 8.6.3, KA 4.3); the MVP's completion forecast
against the cap. **Stays inside the pocket:** which backlog items are pulled into which sprint; Sprint
Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Retrospective; the Definition of Done applied story by story.

*Interpretation.* The PMO never asks "what is in Sprint 7's backlog" — that stays inside the pocket. It does
ask "is the MVP still tracking to land within $180,000 and 12 sprints" — answered at the interface, translated
into the board's own language (9.6.4).

changes.

accountable decision for the PMO and the controls professional.

### 9.6.2 Tailoring the governance approach to context

**Definition & purpose.** Not every agile pocket warrants the same governance weight. This topic applies
PMI's **tailoring** principle (introduced at development-approach level in KA 8.6.3) to the *design of the
interface itself*.

**The underlying principle.** Governance intensity should track **materiality and risk**, not habit.
Over-governing a low-risk pocket wastes the team's capacity on reporting instead of delivery, eroding the
adaptiveness the approach was chosen for; under-governing a high-risk pocket leaves the programme blind to a
genuine exposure until it is expensive to fix.

**Tailoring-factor table.**

| Factor | Favours a lighter-touch interface | Favours a heavier-touch interface |
|---|---|---|
| Materiality to overall programme value | Low | High |
| Safety/regulatory exposure | Low | High (e.g. a safety case or certified system) |
| Team structure | Co-located, in-house, established track record | Dispersed, third-party, new relationship |
| Contract basis (KA 7.1) | Capped T&M with the client's own PMO | Target-cost or fixed-milestone third-party deal |
| Organisational agile maturity | Established | Immature (first agile pocket) |

**Example 9.6.2 — tailoring two Meridian-adjacent pockets.**

*Scenario.* Compare the Field App (low materiality; low regulatory exposure; a trusted in-house team) against
a hypothetical safety-interlock firmware pocket for a level-crossing controller, delivered by a specialist
third party.

*Working/Answer.* The Field App scores toward the light-touch column on every factor — a monthly roll-up and
a four-sprint tranche gate (9.6.1) is proportionate. The firmware pocket scores toward the heavy-touch column
throughout, and warrants sprint-level QA evidence review, more frequent gates and independent technical
sign-off, even though both pockets sit at the same point on KA 8.6's development-approach spectrum.

*Interpretation.* "Adaptive" as a *development-approach* choice (KA 8.6.5) and "how heavily governed" as a
*governance-design* choice are independent — the firmware pocket remains genuinely agile while carrying
materially more governance weight.

**Common pitfalls.** Applying one governance template to every pocket regardless of materiality. Treating
heavier governance as meaning "less agile," when added evidence and gate frequency can sit entirely at the
interface without touching sprint mechanics. Setting the weight once at kick-off and never re-testing it as
materiality changes mid-project.

**AI in this topic.** A portfolio-level AI assistant can flag where a pocket's actual materiality has drifted
from the risk profile assumed when its governance weight was set (cross-reference KA 13.5). It cannot make the
risk-appetite judgement itself — re-weighting a pocket's governance remains a PMO and professional decision.

**Cross-references.** KA 8.6.2, KA 8.6.5 (the suitability filter — a related but distinct judgement); KA 12.1
(risk appetite); KA 7.1 (contract basis); KA 11.3 (internal control intensity, an analogous tailoring
judgement).

### 9.6.3 Governing agile within a stage-gate/PMO world: milestones, funding tranches and quality gates

**Definition & purpose.** This topic works the mechanics at the interface: how a **milestone** (a fixed,
external commitment) differs from a **sprint boundary** (internal planning cadence); how a pocket is
**funded by increment**; and how a sprint-level **quality gate** (the Definition of Done) differs from a
programme-level one (PMO acceptance).

**The underlying principle.** A milestone is set at a **release** boundary (a cluster of sprints delivering
something usable) and, once set, is a fixed commitment like any predictive milestone (KA 8.2, KA 10.1). A
sprint boundary is an internal planning device, re-set every iteration; setting a *milestone* at every sprint
boundary collapses the pocket's ability to re-plan (9.6.6). **Funding by increment** follows the same logic on
the money side: rather than releasing the whole budget upfront (removing the incentive to demonstrate value)
or micromanaging spend sprint by sprint (re-imposing predictive control inside the pocket), funding is
released in **tranches** tied to release boundaries, each gated on evidence the prior tranche's increment was
accepted — mirroring rolling-wave budgeting (KA 3.1) for a variable-scope engagement.

**Milestone vs sprint boundary vs quality gate.**

| Element | Owned by | Cadence | Crosses to governance? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sprint boundary | The team | Every sprint | No — internal re-planning |
| Definition of Done (DoD) | The team (KA 9.2) | Every story | Evidence retained, sampled (9.6.5) |
| Release milestone | Programme/PMO, agreed with the team | Every release | Yes — a fixed, external commitment |
| Tranche-release gate | PMO, funding authority | Aligned to release milestones | Yes — the funding decision |
| Stage gate | Programme governance (KA 10.1) | Per the predictive spine | Yes — rolled-up status only |

**Example 9.6.3 — funding the Field App's MVP release by tranche.**

*Scenario.* The MVP release (200 points, $180,000 cap, 12 sprints) is capped T&M with an NTE ceiling
(KA 7.1.5): the rate ($15,000/sprint, SAR 56,250) is fixed; total sprints drawn is the PMO's cost-control
lever. Funding releases in three $60,000 tranches (Sprints 1–4, 5–8, 9–12), each gated on PMO acceptance, at
the Sprint Review, of the prior tranche's demoed increment.

*Required/Answer.* At the Sprint-6 data date (mid tranche 2), cumulative draw `= 6 × $15,000 = $90,000` —
exactly half the $180,000 cap. Tranche 1's release gate (PMO acceptance of the Sprint-4 increment) has already
been met; tranche 3's release will be gated on PMO acceptance of tranche 2's increment at the Sprint-8 Review.

*Interpretation.* The tranche gate tests an **accepted, demoed increment**, not a document review of a plan —
an empirical quality gate, not a documentary one, even though it serves the same funding-control purpose as a
predictive stage gate.

**Common pitfalls.** Releasing the full cap upfront "to reduce administration," removing the incentive
structure the tranche gate exists to create. Setting a milestone at every sprint boundary — the specific
mechanism by which hybrid governance collapses into water-scrum-fall (9.6.6). Treating the Definition of Done
(team-level "finished") as equivalent to the tranche-release gate (programme-level "accepted and paid for") —
related but distinct checks at different altitudes.

**AI in this topic.** Workflow-automation tools can track each tranche's release conditions against sprint-
tool evidence and flag when a tranche is ready for the PMO's acceptance decision (cross-reference KA 13.5).
They cannot authorise the release — accepting an increment as fit for funding release remains the PMO's and
the professional's call.

**Cross-references.** KA 7.1.5 (capped T&M/NTE); KA 7.1.6 (target-cost with agile delivery, for larger or
higher-risk pockets); KA 3.1 (rolling-wave budgeting, the predictive analogue); KA 9.2 (Definition of Done,
Sprint Review); KA 5.4 (change control, largely superseded inside the pocket by backlog re-prioritisation).

### 9.6.4 Reporting agile progress to a predictive-minded steering board: translating velocity and burnup into RAG/EVM

**Definition & purpose.** A steering board fluent in BAC, CV, CPI, SPI and RAG (KA 4.3, KA 6.1–6.3) should not
need to learn burndown-chart literacy to understand an agile pocket's health. This topic is the **translation
layer**: mapping story points and a fixed sprint run-rate into the same $-denominated PV/EV/AC/CPI/SPI/EAC/VAC
vocabulary, and the same report anatomy and RAG rule (KA 4.3.3, KA 4.3.4), already used for Pipeline Segment
4B.

**The underlying principle.** AgileEVM (KA 9.5, in full) reuses the classical EV formulae under a stated
assumption set. Applied to a fixed-capacity, capped-T&M pocket, the mapping is direct:

`PV = (planned cumulative points ÷ total backlog points) × BAC` — `EV = (actual cumulative points ÷ total
backlog points) × BAC` — `AC = sprints elapsed × cost per sprint`

**The sharpest translation caution.** Because the run-rate is fixed by the capped-T&M contract, `AC = PV` at
every data point, provided staffing stays stable (KA 8.6.4's caution applies directly). CPI and SPI are
therefore **numerically identical** — and a board used to reading "CPI/SPI both Green" as "no risk" on a
fixed-scope contract can misread it here. A fixed-capacity pocket can never show a cost overrun in the AC
sense (the team physically cannot spend more than the sprint rate), so CPI/SPI here is really a
**scope-realisation index**; the true risk signal is whether the *forecast* (EAC, VAC, headroom) shows the
backlog landing inside the funding cap.

**Example 9.6.4 — translating the Field App's Sprint-8 position for Meridian's steering board.**

*Scenario.* MVP release: 200-point backlog; funding cap (AgileEVM BAC) $180,000 (SAR 675,000), 12 sprints at
$15,000/sprint, planned evenly at 16.7 points/sprint. At the Sprint-8 data date — the tranche-2/tranche-3 gate
point — the team's tracked burnup (KA 9.3) shows a cumulative **150 points** delivered.

*Required.* (a) PV, AC, EV, CV, SV, CPI, SPI and the board's RAG status; (b) EAC, VAC and the forecast
completion against the 12-sprint cap.

*Working.*

(a) Planned cumulative points at Sprint 8 of 12 `= (8/12) × 200 ≈ 133`. `PV = (8/12) × 180,000 = 120,000`.
`AC = 8 × 15,000 = 120,000` (confirming `AC = PV`). `EV = (150/200) × 180,000 = 135,000`.
`CV = EV − AC = 135,000 − 120,000 = 15,000` (favourable). `SV = EV − PV = 15,000` (favourable — identical to
CV, since `AC = PV`). `CPI = EV/AC = 135,000/120,000 = 1.125`. `SPI = EV/PV = 1.125` (identical). Applying
KA 4.3.4's bands (Green ≥ 0.95): both **Green**; worst-of → **overall Green**.

(b) `EAC = BAC/CPI = 180,000/1.125 = 160,000` (SAR 600,000) — equivalently, the actual cost-per-point rate
(`120,000/150 = 800/point`) reprojected across the full 200-point backlog: `200 × 800 = 160,000`.
`VAC = BAC − EAC = 180,000 − 160,000 = 20,000` (SAR 75,000) favourable — **11.1% headroom**. In whole-sprint
terms: remaining backlog `= 200 − 150 = 50`; average velocity to date `= 150/8 = 18.75 points/sprint`; further
sprints `= 50/18.75 ≈ 2.7 → 3` (rounded up, since a fractional sprint cannot be delivered); total sprints
`= 8 + 3 = 11`, at a discrete forecast cost of `11 × 15,000 = 165,000` — **exactly the release's actual
outcome (KA 9.4)** — one sprint inside the 12-sprint cap.

*Answer.* **Board-facing summary: RAG Green (CPI 1.13, SPI 1.13); scope 75.0% complete (150/200 points)
against 66.7% of the cap drawn ($120,000/$180,000); forecast completion in 11 total sprints at $160,000–
$165,000, $15,000–$20,000 (8–11%) inside the $180,000 cap.**

*Interpretation.* A predictive board reads this exactly like a favourable Pipeline Segment 4B report — Green,
inside budget, ahead of plan. The professional's added narrative line, which nothing in the numbers alone
signals, is that AC will *always* equal PV here while staffing stays stable, so "Green cost/schedule" is a
structural feature of fixed-capacity funding, not itself evidence of health; the evidence of health is the
**scope-forecast headroom**, which the board should treat as the pocket's real leading indicator.

### 9.6.5 Assurance and controls over agile delivery

**Definition & purpose.** Agile delivery does not remove internal control; it **relocates** it. Where a
predictive gate review produces a periodic, document-heavy sign-off (KA 4.3.6), a pocket's controls are
continuous and embedded in its ceremonies and tooling — but must still satisfy the same objectives
(authorisation, completeness, accuracy, segregation of duties, KA 11.3).

**The underlying principle.** Every classical control has a working agile equivalent; the discipline is making
sure it is actually configured and evidenced, not assumed.

**Control-mapping table.**

| Classical control | Agile equivalent | What it evidences |
|---|---|---|
| Formal change request form (KA 5.4) | Logged backlog re-prioritisation with a stated rationale | Authorised change, not silent drift |
| Milestone sign-off document | Sprint Review acceptance recorded in the sprint tool | The increment was reviewed and accepted |
| Segregation of duties on approval | Product Owner ≠ developer ≠ tranche-gate approver | No one accepts/pays for their own work |
| Report assurance trail (KA 4.3.6) | Sprint-tool audit log (who moved a story to "done," when) | A traceable record of who did what, when |
| Independent review before sign-off | Independent QA/PMO review of DoD evidence | The "done" claim is checked, not self-certified |

**Example 9.6.5 — auditing tranche 2 ahead of the Sprint-8 gate.**

*Scenario.* Ahead of releasing tranche 3 (9.6.3), an internal reviewer checks tranche 2's (Sprints 5–8)
control evidence: Definition of Done checklists for every story accepted in that span; Sprint Review notes
recording the Product Owner's — not a developer's — acceptance of each; the sprint tool's audit log confirming
no story was marked "done" by the person who coded it.

*Required/Answer.* The reviewer is testing **authorisation** (was each story genuinely accepted, not
self-certified), **completeness** (does DoD evidence exist for every point claimed) and **segregation of
duties** (is the "done" decision independent of the doer) — the same three objectives KA 11.3 applies to any
O2C/P2P control. If all three pass, the evidence supports releasing tranche 3 on schedule; if any fails — for
example, a developer self-marking their own story "done" with no PO review — the gap must be closed before the
release decision is made, exactly as an unreconciled figure holds a classical report (KA 4.3.6).

**Common pitfalls.** Assuming "agile" means "no audit trail" — sprint tooling retains a full timestamped
history by default; the failure mode is not configuring or reviewing it. A Product Owner acting as the sole
approver of their own team's work with no independent PMO or QA check — an easily missed segregation-of-duties
gap. Treating the Definition of Done as sufficient evidence on its own, without independent sampling.

**AI in this topic.** AI-based audit and process-mining tools can mine sprint-tool logs at scale for control
gaps — self-authored "done" marks, missing DoD evidence, unlogged backlog changes (cross-reference KA 11.3,
KA 13.5). They cannot conclude whether a flagged exception is a genuine control failure or an explainable edge
case; that investigation remains the reviewer's and the professional's.

**Cross-references.** KA 11.3 (segregation of duties and the audit trail, in full); KA 4.3.6 (the predictive-
side assurance trail this mirrors); KA 9.2 (Sprint Review, Definition of Done); KA 5.4 (change control, the
predictive analogue).

### 9.6.6 The controls professional's role: bridging the two worlds

**Definition & purpose.** Across 9.6.1–9.6.5, one role recurs at every interface point: the project controls
professional, accountable for making the two governance languages meet without either distorting the other.

**The underlying principle — three bridging responsibilities.** (1) **Design the interface** (9.6.1, 9.6.2) so
gate cadence and sprint cadence do not collide, tailored to each pocket's materiality and risk. (2) **Produce
the translated report** (9.6.4) on the board's cadence and vocabulary, without imposing it on the team. (3)
**Maintain assurance** (9.6.5) using agile-native evidence, without recreating predictive-style paperwork
inside the pocket. Two failure modes show what happens when this is done badly, in opposite directions.

**Failure mode 1 — water-scrum-fall.** Ceremonies survive; behaviours do not. A pocket holds daily stand-ups
and calls its cycles "sprints," but the envelope has been allowed to impose gate-style sign-off at every
sprint boundary (9.6.3), leaving no room for genuine re-prioritisation — the governance-design mirror of
KA 8.6.3's "agile-in-name-only," caused by an interface that leaked the envelope's cadence down into the
pocket.

**Failure mode 2 — governance theatre.** The interface exists on paper — tranche gates, a RAG dashboard, an
assurance trail — but nobody uses it to decide anything: gates are rubber-stamped regardless of evidence, RAG
dots sit unread, and the translation exercise (9.6.4) becomes a compliance artefact. Where water-scrum-fall
over-governs, governance theatre under-*acts* on governance that formally exists — both leave the programme no
better informed than if no interface had been designed.

**Example 9.6.6 — a short diagnostic for the Field App's governance.**

*Scenario.* At the Sprint-8 tranche-3 gate, the PMO asks whether the MVP's governance is actually working, not
merely present.

*Working/Answer.* (1) *Has any sprint boundary been treated as an external milestone?* No — only the
four-sprint tranche boundaries carry funding decisions; the team re-planned freely within each tranche.
(2) *Has the RAG/EVM translation (9.6.4) changed any decision?* Yes — the Sprint-8 report (Green, $20,000/
11.1% headroom) directly supported releasing tranche 3 on schedule. (3) *Is the assurance evidence (9.6.5)
sampled and acted on, or merely filed?* The tranche-2 audit (Example 9.6.5) was performed and used as an input
to the release decision.

*Answer.* **All three answers indicate a working hybrid governance regime — neither water-scrum-fall nor
governance theatre.**

*Interpretation.* This three-question diagnostic — is re-planning genuinely happening; is the translated
report genuinely used; is the evidence genuinely sampled — is worth applying at every tranche gate, not only
when something has visibly gone wrong.

**Common pitfalls.** Assuming water-scrum-fall and governance theatre need opposite fixes, when both are the
same underlying failure (an interface designed but not genuinely operated) and the same diagnostic catches
either. Measuring governance success by the existence of artefacts rather than whether they changed a
decision. Treating the bridging role as a one-off design task rather than a standing responsibility.

**AI in this topic.** Across Domain 9's adaptive-delivery workflows, AI tools genuinely assist the bridging
role: backlog drafting and story splitting, sprint/velocity forecasting, flow-anomaly detection, retrospective
analysis, and — as shown throughout this KA — automated agile-to-EVM reporting (cross-reference KA 13.4,
KA 13.5). None of them can design the envelope/pocket interface for a specific programme's risk and
contractual context, judge whether governance is genuinely operated versus merely performed, or stand
accountable when a release or escalation decision is wrong. Every one of those judgements is, and remains, the
project controls professional's — consistent with the principle running through this Body of Knowledge:
**AI proposes; the professional disposes.**

**Cross-references.** KA 8.6.3 (agile-in-name-only, the development-approach-level analogue of water-scrum-

### Key terms

- **Hybrid governance** — the control architecture, distinct from hybrid *delivery* (KA 8.6.3), that governs
  an agile pocket inside a predictive envelope.
- **Envelope / pocket** — the outer predictive layer (gates, milestones, funding authority) and the inner
  adaptive layer (sprint planning, backlog re-prioritisation).
- **Interface** — the defined set of status and funding decisions crossing between envelope and pocket;
  everything else stays pocket-internal.
- **Release milestone** — a fixed, external commitment set at a release boundary, distinct from an internal
  sprint boundary.
- **Funding by increment (tranche funding)** — releasing a pocket's budget in stages, each gated on evidence
  the prior increment was accepted.
- **Tranche-release gate** — the funding decision point, tested by an accepted, demoed increment rather than a
  document review.
- **AgileEVM translation (fixed-capacity case)** — mapping story points and a fixed sprint run-rate to
  $-denominated PV/EV/AC/CPI/SPI/EAC/VAC (KA 9.5).
- **AC = PV caution** — under fixed-capacity funding, actual cost tracks planned value by construction, so
  CPI/SPI is a scope-realisation index; the leading risk indicator is EAC/VAC headroom against the cap.
- **Water-scrum-fall** — ceremonies survive, adaptive behaviours do not, because the envelope's cadence has
  leaked into the pocket.
- **Governance theatre** — the interface, gates and assurance trail formally exist but are not genuinely used
  to decide.
- **Segregation of duties in agile** — ensuring the Product Owner, the developer and the tranche-gate approver
  are not the same person (KA 11.3).

### Sample MCQs

**MCQ 9.6.1 [topic 9.6.1, level: recall]**
In a hybrid governance model, which of the following should cross the envelope/pocket interface?
A. The specific backlog items pulled into next sprint
B. The team's Daily Scrum discussion
C. **A tranche-release funding decision, gated on an accepted increment** ✔
D. The Definition of Done wording used by the team
*Rationale:* the interface carries funding and status decisions (9.6.1), not day-to-day planning detail. A, B
and D are pocket-internal matters that stay with the team.

**MCQ 9.6.2 [topic 9.6.3, level: application]**
A programme sets a fixed, external milestone at every two-week sprint boundary for an agile pocket, requiring
formal sign-off before the next sprint can begin. What is the most likely consequence?
A. Improved adaptiveness, since sprints are reviewed more often
B. **Water-scrum-fall — the team loses the ability to re-plan, while still carrying agile's ceremonial
   overhead** ✔
C. A stronger tranche-funding control, with no downside
D. No effect, since milestones and sprint boundaries are the same thing
*Rationale:* treating every sprint boundary as an external milestone collapses re-planning freedom — the
defining symptom of water-scrum-fall (9.6.3, 9.6.6). A and C mistake added formality for added control; D
conflates two deliberately distinct concepts.

**MCQ 9.6.3 [topic 9.6.4, level: application — numerical]**
An agile pocket is funded at a fixed $20,000 per sprint under a capped T&M contract. At Sprint 5 of a planned
10-sprint, 150-point release (BAC = $200,000), the team has delivered a cumulative 90 points. What are AC and
PV at this data date?
A. AC = $200,000; PV = $90,000
B. **AC = $100,000; PV = $100,000** ✔
C. AC = $100,000; PV = $120,000
D. AC = $90,000; PV = $100,000
*Rationale:* `AC = 5 × 20,000 = 100,000`. Planned cumulative points at Sprint 5 of 10 `= 5/10 × 150 = 75`;
`PV = (75/150) × 200,000 = 100,000`. Under fixed-capacity funding, `AC = PV` by construction (9.6.4), which
only option B reflects.

**MCQ 9.6.4 [topic 9.6.4, level: analysis]**
Continuing MCQ 9.6.3's data, CPI and SPI both compute to 1.20 and the board reads "Green." What is the most
important caution the controls professional should add?
A. None — a Green RAG confirms the pocket carries no risk
B. **Under fixed-capacity funding, AC will always equal PV while staffing is stable, so CPI/SPI here is a
   scope-realisation index, not a spend-control signal — the real risk indicator is the EAC/VAC headroom
   against the cap** ✔
C. CPI and SPI should be averaged into a single blended score for the board
D. The figures should be withheld until a variance actually appears
*Rationale:* this is 9.6.4's central teaching point. A overstates what a structurally-always-on-track cost
figure proves; C repeats an error KA 4.3.4 and KA 6.2 both warn against; D is neither necessary nor consistent
with timely reporting (KA 4.3.5).

**MCQ 9.6.5 [topic 9.6.6, level: analysis]**
A programme maintains a full RAG dashboard, an assurance log and quarterly tranche gates for an agile pocket,
but no gate has ever withheld funding or changed a decision regardless of the evidence presented. This is best
described as:
A. Water-scrum-fall
B. **Governance theatre** ✔
C. A well-functioning hybrid governance regime
D. Agile delivery, correctly applied
*Rationale:* the artefacts of governance are present but never used to decide anything — precisely 9.6.6's
definition of governance theatre, the mirror-image failure to water-scrum-fall (A).

### Self-check questions (answers in Appendix C)

1. In your own words, distinguish water-scrum-fall from governance theatre, and explain why the same
   three-question diagnostic in 9.6.6 can detect either.
2. Using the Field App's MVP release (BAC $180,000, 12 sprints, 200-point backlog, $15,000/sprint), suppose
   that by Sprint 10 cumulative delivered points stand at 185. Compute PV, AC, EV, CPI and SPI, and state the
   resulting RAG status under KA 4.3.4's bands.
3. Explain why AC equals PV at every data point in the Field App's capped-T&M example, and why a board should
   not read a consistently Green CPI/SPI reading, on its own, as evidence that the pocket carries no risk.

**Answers**

1. **Water-scrum-fall** is over-governance in the wrong direction: the envelope's predictive cadence and
   formality is imposed down into the pocket, so the team loses the ability to genuinely re-plan while still
   carrying agile's ceremonial overhead. **Governance theatre** is the opposite symptom with the same root
   cause: the interface, gates and assurance trail are formally present, but nobody uses them to make a
   decision. The 9.6.6 diagnostic (is re-planning genuinely happening; is the translated report genuinely
   used; is the evidence genuinely sampled) catches both, because both are, at root, the same failure: an
   interface designed but not genuinely operated as intended.
2. Planned cumulative points at Sprint 10 of 12 `= (10/12) × 200 ≈ 167`; `PV = (10/12) × 180,000 = 150,000`.
   `AC = 10 × 15,000 = 150,000` (confirming `AC = PV`). `EV = (185/200) × 180,000 = 166,500`. `CPI = EV/AC =
   166,500/150,000 = 1.11`. `SPI = EV/PV = 1.11` (identical, as expected under fixed-capacity funding).
   Applying KA 4.3.4's bands, both indices `≥ 0.95` → **Green**, worst-of → **overall Green**.
3. AC equals PV because the sprint rate is fixed by the capped-T&M contract and the planned burnup is spread
   evenly across the same 12 sprints the funding cap assumes — so however many sprints have elapsed, the money
   actually spent (`sprints elapsed × rate`) exactly equals the money the plan expected to have spent by that
   point, regardless of how much scope has actually been delivered. A consistently Green CPI/SPI therefore only
   confirms that spend is on its structurally-guaranteed track, not that the pocket is healthy; the genuine
   risk — whether the remaining backlog fits inside the remaining cap — is visible only in the EAC/VAC
   headroom, which is why 9.6.4 treats headroom, not the ratio, as the board's real leading indicator.
