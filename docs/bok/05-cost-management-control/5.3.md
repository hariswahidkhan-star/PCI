# Domain 5 — Cost Management & Cost Control

## KA 5.3 — Cost breakdown & control accounts

Domain 1 (KA 1.5) introduced the chart of accounts, the cost breakdown structure (CBS), project cost coding
and the control account as the WBS × OBS intersection — using Meridian Rail Contracting LLC's Rail
Signalling Contract (Project 07) as its worked entity. Domain 6 has since used a control-account structure
throughout its Pipeline Segment 4B earned-value examples (BAC $600,000 / SAR 2,250,000 at an illustrative
SAR 3.75 = USD 1) without stopping to show how that structure was actually built. This Knowledge Area closes
that gap. It treats the **cost breakdown structure (CBS)** and the **code of accounts** as first-class
structures in their own right (not merely a restatement of KA 1.5's coding mechanics); formalises the
**control account** as the genuine intersection of the WBS and the organisational breakdown structure
(OBS), and introduces the **Control Account Manager (CAM)** — the single named individual accountable for
it; distinguishes the **work package** from the **planning package**, the two levels of detail a control
account's scope can sit at; explains **rolling wave planning**, the disciplined process that converts one
into the other as a project matures; extends cost coding to show how a project's own control-account
structure gives full **traceability** from a single invoice to the project budget; and closes by showing
precisely how control accounts **roll up to the project budget** and become the level at which Domain 6's
earned value management is actually measured and reported — without repeating Domain 6's formulae. Every
worked figure in this chapter is built from, and reconciles exactly to, the Pipeline Segment 4B basis of
estimate already established in KA 3.2.4 and the control-account structure already established in KA 6.4.3,
so Domains 1, 3, 5 and 6 read as one continuous, arithmetically consistent worked example.

### 5.3.1 The cost breakdown structure (CBS) and the code of accounts

**Definition and purpose.** The **cost breakdown structure (CBS)**, as used throughout AACE International's
Total Cost Management (TCM) Framework, is a hierarchical breakdown of total project cost into **cost
elements** — labour, materials, equipment/plant, subcontract, indirect/overhead — organised by the *nature*
of the cost rather than by the deliverable it produced. The **code of accounts** is AACE TCM's term for the
numbering system that structures and identifies each position in the CBS, so that an estimate line, a
budget line and an actual-cost posting can all be summarised, compared and reported consistently across a
project's life — from estimate (KA 3.2), through budget (KA 3.1, KA 3.3), to actual cost and forecast
(KA 6.1–6.3). It is a distinct concept from the entity-wide financial **chart of accounts (CoA)** built in
KA 1.5.1 — the CoA is the accountant's list of natural accounts for statutory reporting; a code of accounts
is the cost engineer's coding scheme for structuring a CBS — though, as this topic shows, a well-designed
project can usefully build its code of accounts directly on top of the CoA's natural-account blocks rather
than inventing a parallel one.

**The underlying principle.** The WBS (KA 1.5.3) and the CBS are two independent, complementary lenses on
the same dollar. The WBS answers *"what deliverable does this cost belong to?"*; the CBS answers *"what kind
of cost is it?"* Neither alone is sufficient for cost engineering: a CBS-only report ("the project spent
$334,000 on labour") says nothing about which deliverable that labour built; a WBS-only report ("Trenching
& Earthworks cost $200,000") says nothing about whether that was mostly labour, materials or equipment. A
controls professional needs both simultaneously — which is exactly why the WBS × CBS intersection, captured
once as a coded transaction (the segmented cost code introduced in KA 1.5.2), matters: one posting, cut two
ways, both totals reconciling to the same figure.

**Structure.** Extending Meridian's entity-wide chart of accounts (KA 1.5.1) directly as Pipeline Segment
4B's code of accounts, the project's cost is classified into three cost elements actually used on this
scope: 510 Direct project cost/materials, 520 Labour cost, and 540 Equipment/plant cost.

**Example 5.3.1 — Building Pipeline Segment 4B's CBS from its work packages.**

*Scenario.* Pipeline Segment 4B's $600,000 base cost estimate was built work package by work package in
KA 3.2.4. Meridian's cost engineer now reclassifies that same estimate by cost element instead of by work
package. WP1's 800 engineering hours are wholly labour; WP2's supplier-quotation pricing is wholly
materials. WP3's $80/m labour-and-equipment build-up (KA 3.2.4) split $2,400/day crew cost against $800/day
excavator cost — a 75:25 ratio — giving labour $60/m and equipment $20/m; its $15/m bedding/backfill
materials stay as materials, and its $5/m consumables are grouped with equipment/plant as minor plant cost
($20 + $5 = $25/m equipment). WP4's resource-based cost is allocated 70% labour / 30% equipment, reflecting
the crew-intensive nature of pipe-laying and welding work. WP5's supervision cost is wholly labour.

*Required.* The CBS by cost element, and confirmation that both the work-package and cost-element cuts
reconcile to $600,000.

*Working.*

| WP | Description | Budget ($) | 520 Labour ($) | 510 Materials ($) | 540 Equipment ($) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WP1 | Design & Engineering | 80,000 | 80,000 | — | — |
| WP2 | Pipe & Valve Procurement | 150,000 | — | 150,000 | — |
| WP3 | Trenching & Earthworks | 200,000 | 120,000 (60×2,000) | 30,000 (15×2,000) | 50,000 (25×2,000) |
| WP4 | Pipe-laying & Welding | 120,000 | 84,000 (70%) | — | 36,000 (30%) |
| WP5 | Site Supervision | 50,000 | 50,000 | — | — |
| **Column total** | | **600,000** | **334,000** | **180,000** | **86,000** |

*Answer.* CBS = Labour (520) **$334,000** (55.7%) + Materials (510) **$180,000** (30.0%) + Equipment (540)
**$86,000** (14.3%) = **$600,000**, exactly matching the WBS/work-package total already established in
KA 3.2.4 and KA 6.1.4.

*Interpretation.* Two clean, mutually reconciling cuts of the same $600,000 — one by work package (KA 3.2.4,
KA 6.1.4), one by cost element (here) — using exactly the same reconciliation discipline as KA 1.5's
WBS × cost-type matrix (Example 1.5.3), now applied to a second Meridian project. Notice this project has no
530 Subcontract cost line at all — unlike Project 07's Rail Signalling Contract, coded in KA 1.5.2 with a
$70,000 Subcontract line for signalling installation, Pipeline Segment 4B is delivered entirely through
Meridian's own labour, plant and procured materials. A code of accounts inherited unchanged from one project
should never be assumed to fit another without checking which cost elements the new project actually uses.

**Mini-case — orphan costs foreshadowed.** Suppose a $2,000 site-office rental cost, genuinely part of WP5's
supervision scope, is posted to natural account 540 (Equipment) because the invoice was miscoded by an
inexperienced clerk unfamiliar with Pipeline Segment 4B's code of accounts. The CBS column totals would
still sum to $600,000 (the same total, wrongly distributed: Equipment $88,000, Labour $332,000), but WP5's
own reported cost would understate its true labour/overhead nature by $2,000 — a **mismatched WBS/CBS**
error, structurally identical to the WBS-phase miscoding in KA 1.5's Example 1.5.4b, now occurring on the
cost-element axis instead of the WBS-phase axis. Section 5.3.5 develops the fuller "orphan cost" scenario, where a
transaction has no valid control-account code at all, not merely the wrong one.

**Common pitfalls.** (1) Treating the CBS as identical to the WBS because, on a simple project, their
top-level groupings can coincidentally look similar — they answer different questions and must be coded
independently. (2) A **mismatched WBS/CBS** posting, where a transaction is coded to the wrong cost element
for its work package (the mini-case above) — individually small, cumulatively distorting every cost-element
report. (3) Carrying a code of accounts unchanged from a dissimilar prior project without checking it fits
the new project's actual resourcing model (the Project 07 subcontract contrast above).

**AI in this topic.** AI-assisted classification tools, of the kind already described in KA 1.5.6, can
propose a cost-element code for a new transaction from its free-text description and can benchmark a
proposed code of accounts against similar past projects to flag likely omissions (e.g. a cost element that
appears in most comparable projects but is entirely absent here). Such tools cannot judge whether a genuine
allocation assumption — such as WP3's 75:25 labour/equipment split or WP4's 70:30 split used above — is
itself correct; that remains a cost-engineering judgement grounded in the resource build-up, not a
classification problem.

### 5.3.2 Work packages and planning packages

**Definition and purpose.** A **work package** (KA 1.5.3) is the lowest WBS component: a defined scope,
small enough to estimate, schedule, cost and assign to a single owner, that — once placed under a control
account (section 5.3.3) — also carries a specific earned value measurement method (KA 6.1.4). A **planning
package**, in PMBOK's terminology, is a component *within* a control account that carries known scope and an
assigned budget but has **not yet** been decomposed into individual work packages, detailed schedule
activities, or an EV measurement method — because the detail needed to plan it fully is not yet available,
or not yet needed this early in the project.

**A necessary disambiguation.** A planning package is not the same thing as **Undistributed Budget (UB)**,
introduced in KA 3.3.5. UB is authorised budget **not yet assigned to any control account at all** — it sits
above the control-account structure entirely. A planning package, by contrast, already sits **inside** a
named control account, already has a named CAM accountable for it (section 5.3.3); only its internal work-package
detail remains to be elaborated. The two are adjacent points on the same spectrum of planning maturity, not
synonyms.

**The underlying principle.** Not all scope benefits from being planned to full work-package detail at the
same time. Planning a control account in exhaustive detail long before it is needed risks wasted effort if
design, ground conditions or scope shift before execution — the detail is simply redone. Leaving a control
account undetailed until execution is imminent risks starting work without a defensible schedule or EV
basis. **Progressive elaboration** — adding detail as more becomes known and as execution approaches — is
the discipline that navigates between these two failure modes; section 5.3.4 develops the formal process (rolling
wave planning) that governs *when* a planning package must convert into work packages. This topic defines
the two building blocks that process acts on.

**Structure.**

| Attribute | Work package | Planning package |
|---|---|---|
| Scope definition | Detailed, deliverable-specific | Known at a summary level only |
| Budget basis | Bottom-up estimate (KA 3.2.4) | Typically analogous or parametric (KA 3.2.2–3.2.3) |
| Schedule | Specific start/finish dates, activities tied to the IMS | Placeholder duration/window only |
| EV measurement method | Assigned (0/100, 50/50, % complete, weighted milestones, LOE — KA 6.1.4) | Not yet assignable |
| Owner | The control account's CAM (section 5.3.3) | The same CAM — the package already belongs to their account |

**Example 5.3.2 — WP4 as a planning package, then decomposed.**

*Scenario.* At Pipeline Segment 4B's initial baseline, before the detailed pipe-laying and welding sequence
was finalised, WP4 existed as a single **$120,000 planning package**, sized using a Class 3 parametric
estimate tied to the 2,000 m pipe length (the same cost-estimating-relationship logic developed in
KA 3.2.3). Once detailed design was complete, ahead of site mobilisation, Meridian's estimator decomposed it
into the four weighted-milestone work packages already used throughout KA 6.1.4: Prep $12,000, Lay $48,000,
Weld $36,000, Test $24,000.

*Required.* Confirm the decomposition conserves the planning package's total budget.

*Working.* `12,000 + 48,000 + 36,000 + 24,000 = 120,000`

*Answer.* **$120,000 = $120,000** — the planning package's budget and the sum of its four resulting work
packages are identical.

*Interpretation.* Decomposition redistributes *detail*, never *total budget* — the same "coding is
relabelling" principle KA 1.5's Example 1.5.1 demonstrated for natural-account coding, now applied to
scope/schedule detail. Only once WP4 existed as four discrete work packages could it receive the
weighted-milestone EV method used throughout KA 6.1.4 and confirmed schedule-derived in KA 6.4.4 — a single,
undecomposed $120,000 planning package cannot meaningfully be assigned a measurement method at all.

**Mini-case — the cost of decomposing too early.** Suppose, instead, Meridian had decomposed WP3 (Trenching
& Earthworks) into ten detailed 200 m work packages at the project's initial baseline (Month 0) — before the
utility-conflict risk identified in KA 3.2.6's risk register had been surveyed and resolved. Once the survey
located an unmapped utility crossing at approximately the 1,100 m mark, several of those ten work packages
would need to be re-sequenced and re-planned around a new avoidance detail — detail planned in full at Month
0 turns out to be wasted. Leaving WP3 as a single planning package until the survey was complete, then
decomposing it around the confirmed utility position, avoids this rework entirely. Read together with
section 5.3.4's later mini-case (decomposing too *late*), this shows progressive elaboration is a genuine trade-off
in both directions, not a bias toward either extreme.

**Common pitfalls.** (1) Decomposing a control account's scope into work packages before the underlying
design or survey information is stable (the mini-case above). (2) Leaving a planning package undecomposed
until execution is imminent, forcing a rushed, poorly-derived work breakdown (section 5.3.4 develops this failure
mode in full). (3) Confusing a planning package with Undistributed Budget — reporting a planning package's
budget as though it were still unassigned to any control account, when it already has a named CAM
accountable for it.

**AI in this topic.** A GenAI tool can draft a first-cut decomposition of a planning package into candidate
work packages by pattern-matching similar, already-decomposed control accounts from past projects — a
genuine drafting accelerant, in the same spirit as KA 3.2.4's AI note on cross-checking a bottom-up take-off.
It cannot itself judge whether the underlying design or ground-condition information is mature enough to
decompose responsibly right now, as opposed to next month — that timing call belongs to the CAM, informed by
the rolling wave process in section 5.3.4.

measurement methods, assignable only once decomposed); section 5.3.4 (rolling wave planning, the governing process).

### 5.3.3 The WBS × OBS intersection: control accounts and the Control Account Manager (CAM)

**Definition and purpose.** A **control account** (KA 1.5.4, PMBOK/EVM sense) is the management point where
the WBS (what work) and the organisational breakdown structure (OBS — who is accountable) intersect, and
where scope, schedule, budget and, once work begins, actual cost and earned value are integrated for
performance measurement. KA 6.4.3 built the full Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM) for Pipeline Segment
4B's six control accounts and is not repeated here. This topic adds what that matrix, by itself, does not
supply: the **Control Account Manager (CAM)** — the AACE TCM / PMBOK term for the single named individual
personally accountable for a control account's scope, schedule, budget, resources and EV measurement
method(s).

**The underlying principle.** Single-point accountability requires a named *person*, not merely a
department. An OBS unit such as "Construction" can hold several control accounts (KA 6.4.3 showed
Construction holding both CA-03 and CA-04); naming a CAM is what turns "Construction is accountable" into
"the Construction Manager – Installation is accountable for CA-04 specifically." Without a CAM, a control
account's budget, schedule and progress can be reported, but no one individual can be asked to explain a
variance, approve a trend, or defend the EV method chosen.

**Structure.** Extending KA 6.4.3's RAM with a CAM and a current planning-maturity status (section 5.3.2's terms):

| CA | WBS scope | OBS unit | Budget ($) | CAM | Status (section 5.3.2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CA-01 | WP1 Design & Engineering | Engineering | 80,000 | Head of Engineering | Work packages (Wave 1) |
| CA-02 | WP2 Procurement | Procurement | 150,000 | Procurement Manager | Work packages (Wave 1) |
| CA-03 | WP3 (Construction portion) | Construction | 190,000 | Construction Manager — Earthworks | Wave 2 (decomposed Month 1) |
| CA-04 | WP4 (Construction portion) | Construction | 112,000 | Construction Manager — Installation | Wave 2 (decomposed Month 1) |
| CA-05 | WP5 Site Supervision | Site Mgmt/PMO | 50,000 | Site/Project Manager | Work packages, LOE (Wave 1) |
| CA-06 | WP3+WP4 QA/HSE portions | Site Mgmt/PMO | 18,000 | QA/HSE Manager | Wave 2 (decomposed Month 1) |
| **Total** | | | **600,000** | | |

A CAM's accountability, per AACE TCM/PMBOK, covers four things for their control account: (1) owning the
WBS dictionary scope entry (KA 1.5.3); (2) holding the schedule dates and activities, tied to the Integrated
Master Schedule (KA 10.1); (3) holding the time-phased budget and monitoring actual cost against it
(KA 5.2, KA 6.1.3); and (4) selecting, applying and defending the EV measurement method(s) used, including
signing off physical progress (KA 6.1.4).

**Example 5.3.3 — A RACI matrix for CA-06, the cross-cutting QA/HSE account.**

*Scenario.* CA-06 is the clearest control account to examine because — as KA 6.4.3 showed — it does not
correspond to a single WBS row or a single department's natural scope; it is QA/HSE effort carved out of two
work packages. Beyond the *budget* (KA 6.4.3's RAM) and the *ownership* (the table above), Meridian defines
who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for four recurring CA-06 activities, using the

*Working/Answer.*

| Activity | QA/HSE Manager (CAM) | Site Inspector | Cost Engineer | Construction Manager | Site/Project Manager |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cost-code CA-06 transactions | A | — | R | I | — |
| Verify & sign off CA-06 physical progress (EV basis) | A | R | I | C | — |

*Interpretation.* The CAM is Accountable throughout — it is their control account — but is not always the
one performing the task (Responsible), and, notably, is **not** the one who finally Approves their own
account's monthly report: that Accountability sits one level up with the Site/Project Manager. This
separation of "produces the report" from "approves the report" is a basic segregation-of-duties control
(the same principle developed in full in Domain 11) applied at control-account level — it prevents a CAM
from being the sole authority over the reporting of their own performance.

**Mini-case — a control account with no named CAM.** Suppose CA-06 had never been assigned a named CAM, and
was instead reported simply as "QA/HSE effort, owned by Site Management/PMO" in general. When a $4,000
adverse cost variance appears on CA-06 at a monthly review, there is no single individual whose job it is to
investigate and explain it — the Site Management/PMO department can deflect to "whoever was on site that
week," and the variance risks going uninvestigated for a full reporting cycle. Naming the QA/HSE Manager as
CA-06's CAM (as in the table above) closes this gap: the variance is theirs to explain, by name, at the next
review.

**Common pitfalls.** (1) A control account reported only at department (OBS) level with no named CAM — the
mini-case above. (2) One individual named CAM for two control accounts with materially different technical
content (e.g. an Engineering CAM also covering QA/HSE) — authority without the specific competence needed to
catch technical issues in the second account. (3) Collapsing "Responsible" and "Accountable" into the same
person for every activity, defeating the segregation-of-duties value the RACI split is designed to provide
(the report-approval row in Example 5.3.3 above).

**AI in this topic.** AI tools can auto-draft a RAM or RACI matrix from a WBS and an organisation chart, and
flag WBS scope that has no assigned OBS owner at all — a genuine coding-gap check, as KA 6.4.3's AI note
already observed for the RAM. They cannot judge whether a *specific* proposed CAM assignment reflects
genuine competence and accountability fit (for example, whether QA/HSE really should report through Site
Management/PMO rather than through Construction) — that is an organisational-design decision for the
professional, not a pattern-matching exercise.

### 5.3.4 Integrating scope, cost and schedule at the control account; rolling wave planning

**Definition and purpose.** A control account is only genuinely "control-account-ready," in AACE TCM/PMBOK
terms, once it carries all four integration elements introduced in section 5.3.3: a WBS dictionary scope entry, a
schedule tied to the Integrated Master Schedule, a time-phased budget, and an EV measurement method. **Rolling
wave planning** is the disciplined, PMBOK-recognised form of progressive elaboration that governs *when*
each control account acquires that full detail: near-term work is planned to full work-package detail now;
work further out remains at planning-package level (section 5.3.2) until a later "wave" of planning rolls forward
to meet it, typically shortly before that scope's field execution begins.

**The underlying principle.** section 5.3.2 already established the two risks progressive elaboration manages —
wasted detail planned too early, execution risk from detail left too late — at the level of a single control
account. Rolling wave planning is the *project-wide schedule* for resolving that trade-off across every
control account at once: it names which accounts are planned in full now, which remain planning packages,
and the trigger point at which each wave's remaining detail must be added.

**Structure — Pipeline Segment 4B's two planning waves.**

| Wave | Control accounts | Budget ($) | Detail at Month-0 baseline | Field execution window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 | CA-01, CA-02, CA-05 | 80,000 + 150,000 + 50,000 = 280,000 | Full work-package detail from baseline | Months 1–6 (Engineering front-loaded; Procurement ordered early; Supervision spans throughout) |
| Wave 2 | CA-03, CA-06a, CA-04, CA-06b | 190,000 + 10,000 + 112,000 + 8,000 = 320,000 | Planning packages at baseline, decomposed by end of Month 1 | Months 2–6 (Trenching Months 2–5, per KA 6.4.2; Pipe-laying follows) |
| **Total** | | **600,000** | | |

*Check:* `280,000 + 320,000 = 600,000 = BAC.` Wave 1's scope (design, procurement, supervision) is
well-understood at project start and is planned in full immediately. Wave 2's scope (trenching, pipe-laying
and their QA/HSE effort) depends on ground conditions and design details that firm up closer to execution,
so it is deliberately held as planning packages until a **decomposition trigger** at the end of Month 1 —
roughly one month ahead of Wave 2's earliest field mobilisation, consistent with WP3's resource-loaded
schedule already fixed in KA 6.4.2 (Months 2–5).

**Example 5.3.4 — Confirming the wave schedule against the resource-loaded plan.**

*Scenario/Required.* Confirm Wave 2's decomposition trigger leaves enough lead time before CA-03/CA-06a's
Month-2 field mobilisation, and that the wave subtotals reconcile to BAC.

*Working.* Trigger at end of Month 1; mobilisation at start of Month 2 — approximately four weeks of lead
time, sufficient to complete the milestone-weight derivation shown in KA 6.4.4 (WP4's schedule-derived
10%/40%/30%/20% split) before work begins. `280,000 + 320,000 = 600,000`, matching BAC exactly.

*Answer.* The wave schedule is consistent with the already-established resource-loaded plan, and the two
wave subtotals reconcile exactly to Pipeline Segment 4B's $600,000 BAC.

*Interpretation.* Rolling wave planning is not a separate budget exercise — it is a *timing* discipline
layered over the same control-account structure and the same $600,000 total already fixed in section 5.3.3; no wave
adds or removes budget, it only sequences when detail is added.

**Mini-case — the cost of decomposing too late.** Suppose CA-04's decomposition slipped from its planned
end-of-Month-1 trigger to mid-Month 2, delayed by a resourcing gap in Meridian's estimating team. Because the
milestone weights KA 6.4.4 later showed to be schedule-derived (10% Prep / 40% Lay / 30% Weld / 20% Test,
from the activity's planned duration profile) can only be properly derived once the detailed schedule
exists, a decomposition rushed under pressure risks assigning weights by convention or habit instead —
precisely the **front-loading** risk KA 6.1.4 and KA 6.4.4 already flagged, now traced to its root cause: a
late rolling-wave decomposition, not an isolated modelling error. Read together with section 5.3.2's mini-case
(decomposing too early), this confirms progressive elaboration is a genuine two-sided trade-off, not a bias
toward either extreme.

### 5.3.5 Resource and cost coding for traceability

**Definition and purpose.** KA 1.5.2 built a four-segment cost code (`Project.WBS-phase.Discipline.Natural
account`) for Project 07, before that project had formally defined control accounts. Once control accounts
exist (section 5.3.3), each already encodes a WBS × OBS intersection in a single token — so Pipeline Segment 4B's
code of accounts can be compressed to a **three-segment code**:

`Cost code = Project.CA.NNN`

where **Project** = 4B (Pipeline Segment 4B), **CA** = the control account (01–06, from section 5.3.3), and **NNN**
= the natural account (510/520/540, from section 5.3.1's CBS, itself drawn from Meridian's entity-wide chart of
accounts, KA 1.5.1).

**The underlying principle.** A dollar still has several simultaneous descriptions (KA 1.5.2's founding
principle), but a code of accounts should evolve as the structures it encodes mature: once a control account
already carries the WBS-phase and OBS/discipline information (section 5.3.3), repeating those facts as separate
code segments is redundant, not additional traceability. Collapsing them into a single CA token is a
defensible simplification — not a loss of information — provided the CA-to-WBS/OBS mapping (section 5.3.3's table)
is itself kept current.

**Example 5.3.5 — Coding and tracing one transaction from invoice to BAC.**

*Scenario.* A supplier invoice for **$33,600** — welding-rig and crane hire supporting WP4's Lay and Weld
milestones — is submitted for work performed under CA-04.

*Required.* The cost code, and the full traceability chain from the invoice to the project budget.

*Working/Answer.* Code = **`4B.CA-04.540`** (Pipeline Segment 4B · CA-04, Construction–Installation ·
Equipment/plant cost). Traceability chain:

Invoice ($33,600) → cost code `4B.CA-04.540` → work package WP4, Pipe-laying & Welding, Lay/Weld milestones
(KA 6.1.4) → control account CA-04, Construction, CAM: Construction Manager — Installation, budget $112,000
(section 5.3.3) → CBS cost element 540 Equipment/plant, part of the project's $86,000 Equipment total (section 5.3.1) →
project BAC $600,000.

*Interpretation.* One code recovers five separate facts about the same $33,600 — the same traceability
KA 1.5 established with four segments, now anchored to a control account rather than to separate WBS-phase
and discipline segments, because the control account has absorbed that information.

**Mini-case — an orphan cost, and its resolution.** In Month 1, before CA-03/CA-06a had been decomposed from
their Wave 2 planning package (section 5.3.4), Meridian mobilised the trenching excavator early to secure a
favourable hire rate, incurring a **$9,500** mobilisation charge. Because no control-account code yet existed
for this scope, the transaction was captured with only its natural account, coded `4B.CA-00.540` — CA-00
being a temporary **suspense/clearing control-account code** reserved for exactly this timing gap. At Month
1 close, the cost ledger shows $9,500 sitting in CA-00: code-complete at the natural-account level, but not
yet traceable to any specific control account — an **orphan cost**. In Month 2, once CA-03/CA-06a were
established (the rolling-wave decomposition trigger, section 5.3.4), the $9,500 was reclassified in full: it is
trenching-specific mobilisation, not QA/HSE effort, so it moves entirely to CA-03.

| | Month 1 close | Month 2, after reclassification |
|---|---|---|
| CA-00 (suspense) | $9,500 | $0 |
| CA-03 (Trenching) | — | +$9,500 |

*Interpretation.* This scenario is only possible because section 5.3.4 permits a control account to remain a
planning package while cost is already legitimately being incurred against its future scope. The suspense
code is the governance device that prevents that timing gap from becoming a lost, uncoded cost — extending
KA 1.5.5's reconciliation logic from the natural-account level to the control-account level: a clean
reconciliation of coded detail to the general ledger would still balance throughout this episode (the $9,500
was always correctly coded to 540), but it would not, by itself, reveal that the cost was sitting in the
wrong *control account* until someone checked CA-00's balance specifically.

### 5.3.6 Control-account roll-up: from work package to BAC, and the foundation for EVM

**Definition and purpose.** This closing topic shows how the full set of control accounts, once established
(section 5.3.3) and integrated (section 5.3.4), rolls up exactly to the project's Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB,
KA 6.4.1) and hence to BAC — and how that same control-account structure becomes the level at which Domain
6's PV, EV and AC are actually measured and aggregated, once work is under way. It closes the loop from *how
cost is structured* (this KA) to *how performance is measured* (Domain 6), without repeating Domain 6's
formulae or re-deriving its variances and indices.

**The underlying principle.** A control-account structure is only useful for earned value management if two
things hold: its roll-up to BAC is exact, with no control account omitted and no cost double-counted; and
each control account can be independently measured, so a work package's performance can be disaggregated to
the specific control accounts — and the specific CAMs — accountable for it.

**Example 5.3.6a — Rolling up Pipeline Segment 4B's six control accounts to BAC.**

*Working.* `CA-01 80,000 + CA-02 150,000 + CA-03 190,000 + CA-04 112,000 + CA-05 50,000 + CA-06 18,000 =
600,000`

*Answer.* **$600,000 = BAC = PMB** (KA 6.4.1) — the same six control accounts established in section 5.3.3, now
formally shown summing to the project's full budget.

*Interpretation — BAC/PMB versus the total project budget.* This KA, like Domain 6, treats BAC = $600,000
as the PMB directly — the sum of the six control accounts above. KA 3.2.6 separately built a fuller cost
baseline for Pipeline Segment 4B: base cost $600,000 + escalation $3,000 (base estimate $603,000) +
contingency $30,000 = **$633,000**. Following KA 3.1.6's hierarchy (`Total project budget = Cost baseline +
Management reserve`) and KA 3.3.5's principle that reserve is held *outside* the PMB, that $33,000
escalation-and-contingency layer sits outside every control account in this KA's roll-up — it is not
distributed to any CAM, and no control account's budget in the table above includes any part of it. Pipeline
Segment 4B's total authorised project budget is therefore $633,000; its PMB/BAC for EVM purposes, and the
figure every control account above rolls up to, is $600,000.

**Example 5.3.6b — Splitting a work package's earned value across its control accounts.**

*Scenario.* At the Month-3 data date, KA 6.1.4 established WP3 (Trenching & Earthworks) at 45% physical
progress (900 of 2,000 m excavated) with EV = $90,000. WP3's budget spans two control accounts: CA-03
($190,000, Construction) and CA-06a ($10,000, QA/HSE). In this scenario, QA/HSE inspection of the trench is
verified metre-for-metre alongside excavation, so both accounts share the same 45% physical-progress basis.

*Required.* EV for CA-03 and CA-06a individually, and confirmation they sum to WP3's established total.

*Working.* `EV(CA-03) = 45% × 190,000 = 85,500` · `EV(CA-06a) = 45% × 10,000 = 4,500`

*Answer.* **EV(CA-03) = $85,500** (SAR 320,625); **EV(CA-06a) = $4,500** (SAR 16,875); combined
**$90,000**, matching KA 6.1.4's WP3 figure exactly.

*Interpretation.* Control accounts are the level at which EVM is actually reported to named CAMs (section 5.3.3): a
work package's blended EV figure, as KA 6.1.4 presented it for teaching purposes, is in practice
disaggregated across however many control accounts share that work package's scope, each independently
reportable.

**Mini-case — a cross-cutting account's progress rate should never be assumed.** Unlike Example 5.3.6b, a
cross-cutting control account's progress basis should never simply be *assumed* equal to its "parent" work
package's rate without independent verification — precisely the duration-elapsed-versus-verified-quantity
caution KA 6.4.4 raised. Suppose QA/HSE inspection genuinely lagged excavation, with only 6 of 20 planned
checkpoints complete (30%, not 45%): `EV(CA-06a) = 30% × 10,000 = 3,000`, and the combined WP3-level EV would
then be `85,500 + 3,000 = 88,500` — $1,500 below the blended $90,000 figure KA 6.1.4 used for teaching
purposes. This divergence is not an error to be reconciled away; it is exactly the extra precision
control-account-level measurement is designed to provide over a single blended work-package figure, and it
is why section 5.3.3 assigned CA-06 its own CAM in the first place.

### Key terms

- **Cost breakdown structure (CBS)** — AACE TCM's hierarchical breakdown of project cost by cost element/
  nature (labour, materials, equipment, subcontract, indirect), independent of the WBS.
- **Code of accounts** — AACE TCM's coding/numbering system for the CBS; distinct from, but here built upon,
  the financial chart of accounts (KA 1.5.1).
- **Control account (PMBOK/EVM sense)** — the WBS × OBS management point where scope, budget, schedule and
  actual cost/earned value are integrated (KA 1.5.4).
- **Control Account Manager (CAM)** — the single named individual accountable for a control account's scope,
  schedule, budget, resources and EV measurement method.
- **Organisational breakdown structure (OBS)** / **Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM)** — cross-reference
  KA 1.5.4, KA 6.4.3.
- **RACI** — a responsibility-assignment technique (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) used here
  to define individual duties within a control account, distinct from the RAM's dollar-value mapping.
- **Work package** — the lowest WBS component: defined scope, budget, schedule and (once under a control
  account) an EV measurement method (KA 1.5.3).
- **Planning package** — a control-account component with known scope and assigned budget, not yet
  decomposed into work packages, detailed schedule activities or an EV measurement method.
- **Undistributed Budget (UB)** — authorised budget not yet assigned to any control account (KA 3.3.5);
  distinct from a planning package, which already sits inside a named control account.
- **Rolling wave planning** — progressive elaboration in which near-term scope is planned to full detail and
  far-term scope is held as planning packages until closer to execution.
- **Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB)** — the time-phased budget produced by integrating scope,
  schedule and cost; its cumulative endpoint equals BAC (KA 6.4.1).
- **Orphan cost** — cost captured with a valid natural account but no current, correct control-account
  traceability.
- **Suspense/clearing control-account code** — a temporary placeholder code (e.g. CA-00) for cost incurred
  before its true control account has been established.
- **Management reserve / contingency reserve / total project budget** — cross-reference KA 3.1.6.

**MCQ 5.3.1 [topic 5.3.1, level: recall]**
Which statement correctly distinguishes the cost breakdown structure (CBS) from the work breakdown structure
(WBS)?
A. The CBS and WBS are two names for the same structure
B. The WBS decomposes deliverables/scope; the CBS classifies cost by nature (labour, materials, equipment,

C. The CBS is defined only for indirect/overhead cost, never for direct project cost
D. The WBS is a finance-only structure with no project management use

*Rationale:* B states the AACE TCM/PMBOK distinction developed in section 5.3.1. A collapses two genuinely distinct,
complementary structures into one; C and D misstate what each structure actually covers.

**MCQ 5.3.2 [topic 5.3.2, level: application — numerical]**
A control account's WP4 planning package is budgeted at $120,000. Once decomposed into four work packages,
three are budgeted at $12,000 (Prep), $48,000 (Lay) and $24,000 (Test). What must the fourth work package
(Weld) be budgeted at for the decomposition to conserve the planning package's total budget?
A. $24,000
B. $30,000
C. $36,000 ✅
D. $48,000

*Rationale:* `120,000 − (12,000 + 48,000 + 24,000) = 120,000 − 84,000 = 36,000` (Example 5.3.2);

B is an unsupported intermediate figure.

**MCQ 5.3.3 [topic 5.3.3, level: application]**
Under the RACI matrix applied to Pipeline Segment 4B's CA-06 (QA/HSE) in section 5.3.3, who is Accountable for
cost-coding CA-06's transactions?
A. The Cost Engineer, because they perform the coding
B. The Construction Manager, because CA-06 draws on Construction's WBS scope
C. The QA/HSE Manager, as CA-06's Control Account Manager (CAM) ✅

*Rationale:* C reflects the RACI matrix in section 5.3.3 — the CAM is Accountable for their own control account even

**MCQ 5.3.4 [topic 5.3.4, level: analysis]**
Why does rolling wave planning decompose CA-03 and CA-04 into full work-package detail only shortly before
their field execution starts, rather than at the project's initial baseline?
A. Because PMBOK forbids planning any control account before execution begins
B. Because detailed planning this early would be wasted effort if design or ground conditions still change
   before execution, while planning too late risks starting work without a defensible schedule and EV
   basis ✅
C. Because control accounts cannot legally hold a budget until they are fully decomposed
D. Because Engineering and Procurement are not permitted to be planned in detail

*Rationale:* B is the progressive-elaboration trade-off developed in section 5.3.4. A, C and D state constraints
that do not exist in PMBOK/AACE practice — control accounts can and do hold budget as planning packages
(section 5.3.2), and Wave 1 accounts (CA-01, CA-02, CA-05) are deliberately planned in full detail early.

**MCQ 5.3.5 [topic 5.3.5, level: application — numerical]**
A $9,500 mobilisation cost is coded `4B.CA-00.540` in Month 1 because CA-03 has not yet been decomposed from

A. $9,500; a permanent, correctly coded cost
B. $0; an orphan cost resolved once its true control account existed ✅
C. $9,500; an unresolved reconciliation break
D. $4,750; the cost was split evenly between CA-00 and CA-03

*Rationale:* B matches the mini-case in sections 5.3.5–CA-00 is a temporary suspense code; once CA-03 exists, the

**MCQ 5.3.6 [topic 5.3.6, level: application — numerical]**

B. $85,500 ✅

### Self-check questions (answers in Appendix C)

1. In your own words, explain why a project needs both a WBS and a CBS, and what a coded transaction (their
   intersection) tells you that neither structure alone can.
2. A control account contains a planning package budgeted at $50,000. Explain what must happen before that
   budget can be reported against a specific EV measurement method, and name the process that governs when
   this should occur.
3. Using section 5.3.3's RACI matrix for CA-06, explain why the QA/HSE Manager can be Accountable for CA-06's
   monthly report while the Site/Project Manager, not the QA/HSE Manager, gives final approval of it.
4. Explain how the $9,500 orphan-cost mini-case in section 5.3.5 could have been avoided entirely, and why Meridian
   nonetheless chose to allow it to happen temporarily (via a suspense code) rather than delaying the
   excavator's mobilisation.

**Answers**

1. A WBS answers "what deliverable/scope"; a CBS answers "what kind of cost" (nature: labour, materials,
   equipment). Neither alone tells a controls professional both facts about one dollar; the coded
   transaction (project/control-account/natural-account, section 5.3.5) carries both simultaneously, so cost can be
   reported by deliverable and by nature from the same captured data, exactly as KA 1.5's WBS × cost-type
   matrix first demonstrated.
2. The planning package must be decomposed into one or more work packages, each with a defined scope,
   schedule dates tied to the IMS, and its own EV measurement method (0/100, 50/50, % complete, weighted
   milestones or LOE) — only a work package, not a planning package, can be measured for earned value. This
   decomposition is governed by rolling wave planning (section 5.3.4), which times the conversion to shortly before
   the work's field execution begins.
3. The CAM (QA/HSE Manager) is Accountable for the content and accuracy of their own control account's
   report — they own the scope, budget and progress being reported. Final approval sits one level up with
   the Site/Project Manager as a basic segregation-of-duties control, preventing a CAM from being the sole
   authority who both produces and approves their own account's performance report (cross-reference Domain
   11's internal-control theme).
4. It could have been avoided by completing CA-03/CA-06a's rolling-wave decomposition before any cost was
   incurred against that scope — i.e. finishing the conversion before, not after, mobilisation. Meridian
   allowed it temporarily because delaying the excavator's mobilisation to wait for full control-account
   decomposition would have delayed the trenching schedule itself; a suspense code (CA-00) let the cost be
   captured immediately and completely (avoiding an omission, cross-reference section 1.5.5) while still being
   fully reclassified once the true control account existed a period later.
