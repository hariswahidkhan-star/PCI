## SLIDE 5 — The honest limit

**Headline**
## Four registers. An exam reaches three.

**Table, large**
1 · Recall — **yes**
2 · Application — **yes**
3 · Judgement — **partly**
4 · Stewardship — **no**

**Body**
Stewardship is setting the method for others and answering for the system. No examination can establish it. Ours included.

**Visual**
Four stacked bands; the fourth outlined rather than filled.

**Caption note for designer:** This is the trust slide. A certification body naming the limit of its own instrument is the most credible thing in the deck. Do not soften it.

## SLIDE 7 — The column most frameworks omit

## SLIDE 8 — The averaging error

**Headline**
## Eight cost engineers cannot run a schedule risk analysis.

**Body**
Competencies are not substitutes. Yet almost every organisation aggregates them into an average — the one statistic guaranteed to hide the gap.

**Pull quote in gold**
> Capability is set by coverage, not by average.

**Visual**
Two teams with identical average scores. One flat, one spiky with three holes. Same mean, different organisations.

## SLIDE 9 — The metric

**Headline, centred**
# CI = L3+ ÷ projects

**Legend**
Professionals at Level 3 or above in a competency, divided by concurrent projects requiring it.

**Scale**
≥ 1.0 covered · 0.5–1.0 shared · 0.2–0.5 thin · **< 0.2 absent**

**Visual**
A horizontal gauge with the four bands, needle sitting in the "thin" zone.

## SLIDE 10 — What it found

**Headline**
## One risk specialist. Nine projects.

**Body**
An EPC controls function of 24, regarded internally as strong. Assessed properly:

Cost management **CI 0.89**
Project risk **CI 0.11**

**Callout in gold**
Eight of nine projects were setting contingency by percentage, not by quantified risk. The average said "stretched but functioning".

**Visual**
Fourteen bars sorted descending. The bottom three in an alert tone.

---

## SLIDE 11 — Why the gap formed

**Headline**
## Competence investment follows competence.

**Body**
Four consecutive years of cost training — in the function's already-strongest competency. The internal trainers were cost engineers. The shared vocabulary was cost. Risk had no advocate.

**Body, second beat**
Organisations train where they are strong, and the gap widens.

**Visual**
Two diverging lines over four years — the strong cluster rising, the weak one flat.

**Headline**
## Judgement was always a by-product of volume.

**Body**
Build the artefact a hundred times, meet the cases where the method strains, and pattern recognition accumulates. That is how Level 3 was made.

AI removes much of that volume.

**Visual**
Two pathways from L1 to L4. The historical band wide; the current one a thread, with the judgement node faded.

## Publishing notes

**Post as a document (PDF), not as images.** Same reasoning as Series 01 — the document viewer earns dwell time.

**Slide 5 is the differentiator.** Every competency framework on LinkedIn claims completeness. This one names what its own examination cannot measure. If the deck is trimmed, protect slide 5 before slide 11.

**Slide 12 is the shareable one.** The Apprenticeship Gap is the idea most likely to be quoted onward, because it is the argument nobody in the sector is currently making. Consider it as an alternate opener if the deck underperforms.

**Accessibility.** Alt text for every slide in the body or first comment. Gold on navy passes contrast at the specified weights; do not drop the accent below 500.

**No certification pitch on any slide.** The framework is the argument.
