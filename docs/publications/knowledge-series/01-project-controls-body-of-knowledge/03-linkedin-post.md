## The post

**A project controls team I would call excellent once reported a cost performance index of 0.957.**

The real figure was 0.887.

Nobody had falsified anything. The ledger balanced. The variance analysis was thorough, the report was well written, and the review meeting was well attended. The number was simply built on invoiced cost, and 12.4 million of subcontract work had been performed but not yet invoiced.

On a 420 million programme at month 14 of 30, that one gap moved the estimate at completion from 438.9 million to 473.3 million.

The difference — 34.4 million — was larger than the entire 21 million contingency.

The board had been told the project was uncomfortable but contained. It was neither.

---

**Here is what makes that case worth studying: the forecasting was not wrong.**

Every earned value formula was applied correctly. The EAC arithmetic was textbook. The analysis layer — the part organisations buy software for, hire specialists for, and put on the improvement plan — was working exactly as designed.

The failure happened before any of it, at the point where physical and commercial reality enters the record.

And it propagated silently through every downstream calculation, because that is what an input error does. It does not produce an obviously wrong report. It produces a confident, internally consistent, plausible report that is wrong.

Those are the expensive ones.

---

**A control system performs six conversions.**

Reality becomes a record. The record acquires structure. Structure becomes a position — where are we now. Position becomes a forecast — where will we end. The forecast becomes a decision. The decision becomes reality again.

Six steps, a closed loop.

Now look at where the money goes. Dashboards, EVM engines, forecasting tools, integrated controls platforms — nearly all of it improves conversions three and four. The middle of the loop. The visible, purchasable part.

Conversions one and six are the two that touch reality. How cost genuinely enters the system. Whether a decision genuinely lands in the baseline.

They are the least governed and the most damaging when they fail. They are also almost never what gets funded.

---

**There is a second pattern underneath this, and it is more uncomfortable.**

Control effectiveness is multiplicative, not additive.

Fidelity — is the number right. Timeliness — does it arrive while the decision is still open. Authority — is there a named person who can act on it, and did they.

Multiply the three.

Take a function that is honest with itself: good numbers but not perfect, say 0.9. Reporting that lands late on about a fifth of decisions, 0.8. And half of its variances carry no named owner, 0.5.

Effectiveness: 0.36.

Now run the improvement options.

Fund the data quality programme and lift fidelity from 0.9 to 0.95 — expensive, technical, highly visible, and the one that usually gets approved. Effectiveness moves to 0.38. Two points.

Instead, require that every open variance carries a named individual and a due date. That costs a governance decision and a column in a register. Effectiveness moves to 0.65. Twenty-nine points.

This is not an argument against data quality. When fidelity is genuinely low, fix it first — that was precisely the failure in the case above. It is an argument for diagnosing which term is binding before committing the budget, because the binding term is very often the cheapest one to fix and the least interesting one to present.

---

**One more thing that case teaches, about AI.**

An anomaly-detection model comparing goods-received-not-invoiced against booked accruals, control account by control account, would have flagged that gap in month 9 rather than month 14.

Five months earlier, with sixteen months of programme still to run.

That is a genuine, unglamorous, entirely real use of the technology — and notice what it does not do. It does not book the accrual. It does not sign the forecast. It shortens the distance between an error occurring and a professional seeing it.

Which raises the sequencing question most organisations get backwards. That model has to be trained on the organisation's historical ledger. If the ledger carries years of missed accruals and progress claimed by opinion, the model learns to reproduce that organisation's historical optimism — with more confidence and better formatting.

Clean the record first. Then model it. The order is not negotiable.

AI proposes. The professional disposes.

---

**The published research on this is seventy years deep and consistent.** Cost forecasting accuracy in infrastructure has not measurably improved across the entire period — a span that includes critical path method, earned value management, ERP and cloud analytics.

Every one of those was a real advance in capability.

None of them moved the aggregate outcome, because the constraint was never analytical capability. It was decision latency.

---

I would genuinely like to hear from people running these functions:

**When did you last score a forecast?** Take one issued six months ago, compare it to actuals, and see how it did. Very few organisations do this routinely, and the ones that do improve.

**What proportion of your open variances have a named owner and a date?** Not a team. A person.

**And can you name one decision from last quarter that a controls forecast actually caused** — and show where it landed in the baseline?

If that third one has no answer, the function is describing the past rather than steering the project, whatever the tooling says.

No judgement in that. It is the normal state, and it is fixable with governance rather than capital.

## Publishing notes

**Hook.** The post opens on a specific number and a contradiction. Do not soften the first line — the gap between 0.957 and 0.887 is what stops the scroll.

**Formatting for the feed.** Keep the short paragraphs and the visual breaks. LinkedIn truncates around 200 characters; the first two lines must survive alone.

**Comment strategy.** Post the link to the full publication in the first comment as well as the body. Answer the three closing questions yourself in the thread if engagement is slow — the discussion questions are genuine, not rhetorical.

**Do not add a certification call to action.** The credential is not mentioned in this post by design. Trust is the objective; the credential is discoverable by anyone who wants it.
