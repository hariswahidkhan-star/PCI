0Document control and how to review this

You asked for the SRS first and then a phase-by-phase review rather than the whole system in one delivery. This document is built for exactly that: it is the only artefact produced before any code, and it is numbered so each later phase can be signed off against the clauses it claims to implement.

0.1What this document is, and is not

It is the complete specification of the target system: architecture, schema, agent contracts, governance, APIs, screens, tests, deployment and rollout. Everything a delivery team needs to cost and build the platform without re-litigating the design.

It is not code, and it deliberately contains no implementation. Section 18 sets out six build phases; each has an entry gate that names the clauses it must satisfy and an exit gate that must be demonstrated before the next begins.

0.2How to review it

  1. Read §1, §2 and §7 first. Those three settle what the system is, where its rules come from, and what it is never allowed to do without a human. If any of the three is wrong, everything downstream is wrong.
  2. Then §6 — the agent roster. Sixteen agents, each with a mission, typed inputs and outputs, tools, memory, schedule and approval class. This is where you will have the strongest opinions.
  3. Then Annex D — open decisions. Eleven questions that need your answer before Phase 1 can start. Nine are business decisions, not technical ones.
  4. Everything else can be reviewed lazily, at the start of the phase that implements it.

0.3Honest scope assessment

Read this before costing anything

The system specified here is roughly 3.5–4.5 engineer-years of work: two senior backend, one frontend, one platform/DevOps, plus a part-time designer, over about twelve months to a hardened multi-tenant release. A single engineer building it alone should expect two to three years, and the agent layer will drift out of date faster than they can finish it.

The recommendation in §18 is therefore a thin vertical slice first: one tenant, four agents, three connectors, the full approval spine. That is a 10–14 week build for two engineers and it produces something PCI can genuinely run on. The remaining twelve agents are then additive rather than architectural.

0.4Two constraints that shaped the whole design

These are not preferences. They come from the source workbook itself and from platform terms of service, and they rule out the most obvious version of this product.

Constraint 1 · No automated LinkedIn actions Hard

The workbook's Golden Rule 5 reads: “No bulk sending, no automation tools, no bots, no scraping. LinkedIn restricts accounts for this and it destroys reply rates.” LinkedIn's User Agreement independently prohibits automated access and scraping.

The LinkedIn Outreach agent (§6.9) therefore drafts and queues; a human sends. It never holds a LinkedIn session, never automates a click, and never scrapes a profile. It composes a personalised note from data the operator has already captured, the operator reviews and sends it inside LinkedIn, and the platform records what was sent. Any design in which the agent transmits on LinkedIn's behalf would breach both PCI's own rules and LinkedIn's terms, and would put at risk the accounts the entire commercial strategy depends on.

Constraint 2 · Models never compute a KPI Hard

An independent numerical audit of the workbook found that every serious defect in it was a silent arithmetic error — revenue-this-week reading 2.5× revenue-to-date, acceptance rates above 100%, meetings erased by their own success. None of them threw an error; all of them would have been quoted to a board.

Language models are worse at this than spreadsheets, and they fail less visibly. So the platform splits hard: every number is computed in SQL against an immutable event log (§5), and agents may only read those numbers. An agent that needs a figure calls the metric API; it is never permitted to derive one from raw rows in its context window. Judgment, drafting, prioritisation and classification are the model's job. Arithmetic is the database's.

1Purpose, scope and non-goals

1.1Problem statement

PCI runs its entire marketing and commercial function out of a 42-sheet workbook covering 133 platforms, 11 campaign objectives, 7 brands, 5,683 article briefs and a scored daily operating rhythm for every employee. The workbook works — it is internally consistent and independently audited — but it has four ceilings that no amount of spreadsheet engineering removes:

  • It cannot act. It records work after the fact. Nothing in it drafts an article, proposes next week's schedule, or notices at 09:00 that follow-ups are overdue.
  • It cannot be trusted concurrently. One shared file, several people, no row-level locking. Sorting a shared log moves other people's rows under them.
  • It has a hard capacity. 1,006 daily rows and 1,203 leads. Beyond that, data is silently invisible to every report.
  • It does not compound. Nothing learns. Last quarter's evidence about what worked is prose in a playbook, not a policy the system applies.

1.2Product definition

PCI Workforce OS is a multi-tenant enterprise SaaS platform in which a coordinated workforce of AI agents runs the operating model the workbook defines. The agents plan, research, draft, schedule, measure and report. Humans set intent, approve anything consequential, and do the work that must legitimately be done by a person. The platform keeps the record, computes the truth, and remembers what worked.

1.4Explicit non-goals

Naming these now prevents scope drift later.

  • Not a general chatbot. There is a conversational surface, but it is a control plane over typed workflows, not an open-ended assistant.
  • Not a CRM replacement. The workbook itself concedes that at sustained volume the lead log belongs in a CRM. Workforce OS owns activity, campaign and content; it integrates with a CRM rather than becoming one.
  • Not a scraper or automation harness. See §0.4. Official APIs only, or a human in the loop.
  • No model fine-tuning in v1. Learning is retrieval plus explicit versioned policy (§10). Fine-tuning is a Phase 6 option, not a foundation.
  • No autonomous spend. Every currency-denominated action requires human approval, without exception and without a bypass flag.

3.3The four planes

Every request belongs to exactly one plane, and the planes have different guarantees. Confusing them is the most common way platforms like this become unsafe.

PlaneContainsGuaranteeMay cause a side effect?
RecordActivity events, leads, content, deals — the immutable logAppend-only, auditable, replayableNo — it is the record
TruthMetric service, derived views, data-health checksDeterministic. Same inputs → same output, foreverNo — read-only by construction
JudgmentAgent runs, model calls, proposals, draftsNon-deterministic, fully logged, replayable, never trustedNo — emits proposals only
EffectAction executors, connectors, notificationsIdempotent, retryable, authorised by an approval recordYes — and only here

The single most important architectural invariant in this document: the judgment plane cannot reach the effect plane directly. Every path between them passes through the policy and approval engine (§7), which is ordinary deterministic code with its own tests.

4.2Multi-tenancy: shared schema with row-level security

Decision. One database, one schema, tenant_id uuid not null on every tenant-owned table, PostgreSQL RLS enforcing isolation. Not schema-per-tenant, and not database-per-tenant.

Why. The target is thousands of organisations. Schema-per-tenant multiplies migration time by tenant count and exhausts connection pooling; database-per-tenant multiplies infrastructure cost and operational surface. RLS puts isolation in the engine rather than in application code, where a forgotten WHERE clause becomes a cross-tenant data breach.

-- every tenant table follows this exact pattern
ALTER TABLE activity_event ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;
ALTER TABLE activity_event FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;   -- applies to the owner too

CREATE POLICY tenant_isolation ON activity_event
  USING      (tenant_id = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid)
  WITH CHECK (tenant_id = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid);

-- the API sets this once per connection checkout, from the validated JWT claim.
-- No application query ever names tenant_id in a filter; forgetting it is now
-- impossible rather than merely discouraged.

Escape hatch, deliberately narrow. A single platform_admin role may set app.bypass_rls for cross-tenant operations. Every such session is logged with the operator's identity, the reason, and the statements executed. Enterprise tenants who require physical separation are offered a dedicated deployment of the same image — a commercial decision, not an architectural fork.

CREATE TABLE activity_event (
  id                bigint GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY,

  minutes           int         NULL CHECK (minutes  BETWEEN 0 AND 960),

  evidence_url      text        NULL,

  created_at        timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(),
  PRIMARY KEY (tenant_id, occurred_on, id),
  CONSTRAINT not_future  CHECK (occurred_on <= current_date + 1),
  CONSTRAINT has_actor   CHECK (actor_user_id IS NOT NULL OR actor_agent_id IS NOT NULL)
) PARTITION BY RANGE (occurred_on);

-- monthly partitions via pg_partman; 24 months hot, then compressed archive.
CREATE INDEX ON activity_event (tenant_id, occurred_on DESC, activity_type_id);
CREATE INDEX ON activity_event (tenant_id, actor_user_id, occurred_on DESC);
CREATE INDEX ON activity_event (tenant_id, platform_id, occurred_on DESC);

Five constraints, five audit findings

  • occurred_on date NOT NULL — a text date landed in cumulative totals and in no week. The type makes it impossible.
  • not_future — one wrong-year typo multiplied every cumulative figure and drove platform ages negative.
  • quantity ≤ 2000, minutes ≤ 960 — 100000 in a quantity field read as 1,250,200% of target and printed “On target”.
  • objective_id, brand_id NOT NULL — untagged rows fell through every bucket and vanished from the total.
  • has_actor — a row with minutes and no name counted for the team and for nobody, so the per-person block could never reconcile.

5Deterministic KPI and rules engine

This clause is the truth plane. It exists so that no number anyone acts on has ever passed through a language model.

5.1Metric definition as data

Metrics are not scattered SQL strings. Each is a row in metric_definition: a stable key, a human definition, the SQL expression, its grain, its unit, its dependencies, and the version it was introduced at. The metric service compiles definitions into parameterised queries; nothing else in the platform may compute a KPI.

{
  "key": "revenue_recorded_total",
  "label": "Total revenue recorded",
  "definition": "Certification revenue with a real purchase date, plus enterprise deal
                 value with a real contract-signed date. A typed note is not a sale.",
  "unit": "currency", "grain": ["tenant","period","brand","objective"],
  "sql": "SELECT coalesce(sum(l.revenue),0) FROM lead l
            WHERE l.purchase_date BETWEEN @from AND @to
          UNION ALL
          SELECT coalesce(sum(d.value),0) FROM deal d
            WHERE d.contract_signed_date BETWEEN @from AND @to",
  "supersedes": null, "introduced_in": "1.0"
}

One definition per concept — enforced, not encouraged

The audit's headline defect was two revenue definitions in adjacent dashboard tiles, which made revenue-this-week read 2.5× revenue-to-date. The registry makes that structurally impossible: a metric key resolves to exactly one expression, dashboards reference metrics by key, and a CI check fails the build if any UI component computes a figure locally. Superseding a definition requires a new key and an explicit supersedes link, so historical reports stay reproducible.

5.2Period arithmetic

Every windowed metric resolves its period through one service, never inline. Rules, all of them fixes for real defects:

  • Week start is tenant_settings.week_start_dow — Sunday for Gulf teams, Monday otherwise. The workbook hard-coded Monday and quietly bucketed every Sunday of Gulf output into the previous week.
  • Windows are half-open: [start, end). A closed upper bound on a bare date excluded everything logged on the last day of the period with a time of day.
  • Trailing averages use completed periods only, and divide by periods actually elapsed since the programme start. A 28-day window ending mid-week divided by a hard 4 read 20% low on a Monday and correct on a Friday.
  • Targets multiply by person-days — distinct (person, day) pairs with logged work — never by headcount × calendar days. That single error understated attainment 2.9× and produced “behind target” verdicts against an inflated bar.

5.3Containment rules for ratios

Any rate whose numerator and denominator come from independent columns is computed with a guarded denominator: rate = num / greatest(den, num). Separately, the contradiction is counted and surfaced rather than smoothed away — because the underlying event is a logging failure a manager must fix, not a rounding artefact.

In the workbook, marking a lead accepted without logging the connection request pushed acceptance to 123% and earned that person +32.5 points on a 100-point score. The sheet rewarded the single commonest logging slip. Guarded denominators cap the rate; dh_accepted_without_request (§5.6) names the rows responsible.

5.4Progressive states, not terminal flags

A meeting is counted from meeting_date IS NOT NULL, never from a status field. Status is single-valued and moves forward, so counting meetings by status erased every meeting that progressed to application or sale — success deleted the evidence, and the cumulative tile read lower than the weekly one. The general rule: a lifecycle fact is recorded as a dated event, and counted from that date. Status is for showing where something is now, never for counting what has happened.

5.5The 23 techniques and 8 targets as executable policy

Targets become target_policy rows the engine evaluates per person per period: 30 leads researched, 20 connection requests, 15 first messages, 10 follow-ups, 2 posts, 3 community answers, 5 partnership contacts, 15 engagements — each per person per working day, with the weekly figure derived from working_days_per_week. Attainment, streaks and shortfalls are metrics like any other.

The growth techniques become a registry with structure: preconditions, steps, cadence, the KPI that proves it, and the log it writes to. Technique 15 — what not to do — inverts into a denylist the policy engine consults: no engagement pods, no automated LinkedIn tooling, no article-directory spam, no bulk PDF submission. An agent proposal that matches a denylist entry is rejected before a human ever sees it, with the technique cited as the reason.

6The agent workforce

Sixteen agents. Each has one mission, a typed input contract, a typed output contract, an explicit tool allowlist, a defined memory scope, a schedule, and a fixed approval class. An agent that cannot state its mission in one sentence is two agents.

6.1Universal agent contract

Every agent, without exception, obeys these six rules. They are enforced by the runtime, not by prompt instruction alone.

  1. Emits proposals, never effects. The only writes an agent can perform are proposed_action rows and its own run record.
  2. Cites or abstains. Every factual claim carries a citation — a metric key with a period, or a document ID with a span. An uncited claim is stripped by the output validator, and a proposal that loses its supporting claim is rejected.
  3. Never computes. Numbers come from the metric API. The runtime blocks the sequence “raw rows in context → arithmetic in output”; §16.4 tests it adversarially.
  4. Declares an expected effect. A prediction with a metric, a direction and a horizon. §10 scores it. An agent whose predictions are unreliable loses autonomy.
  5. Runs inside a budget. Tokens, wall-clock, tool calls and currency, per run and per tenant per day. Exceeding any of them fails the run cleanly and notifies.
  6. Is reproducible. Prompt version, model, parameters, resolved inputs and full transcript are persisted. Any run can be replayed and diffed.

7.3The approval experience

An approval that is tedious gets rubber-stamped, and a rubber-stamped approval is worse than none because it manufactures a false audit trail. So the queue is designed for a decision in under thirty seconds:

  • The exact payload, rendered as it will appear — the actual post, the actual message, not a summary of it.
  • Why, in the agent's own words, with its citations resolvable in one click.
  • What it expects to happen — the prediction, so the approver can disagree with the reasoning rather than only the wording.
  • What changed if this is a revision, as a diff.
  • Inline amendment. Editing then approving is one action, not a rejection followed by a new proposal.
  • Batch where safe. Twelve drafts for one platform can be approved together; twelve outreach messages cannot, and the interface does not offer it.

8.2Calendar correctness

All scheduling is evaluated in the tenant's timezone against the tenant's week definition. Requirements: week_start_dow configurable (Sunday for Gulf teams); holiday calendars per tenant with per-region overrides; DST transitions must not double-fire or skip — schedules anchor to local wall-clock time and a skipped hour fires once on recovery; cadence cycle lengths in days (daily 1, 3× weekly 7/3, weekly 7, fortnightly 14, monthly 30.44); a window whose end precedes its start produces no plan and raises a validation error rather than a negative count.

8.3Idempotency

Every enqueued unit carries a deterministic key — {tenant}:{agent}:{period}:{input_digest} for runs, and the client-supplied or derived idempotency_key for actions. Redis holds the key for 24 hours; PostgreSQL holds a unique constraint permanently. A duplicate is a no-op that returns the original result. This is what makes “retry safely” true rather than aspirational, and it is the reason a connector timeout cannot double-publish.

10The learning loop

"Learns from prior runs" has to mean something falsifiable. Here it means four specific mechanisms, each measurable, and none of them requiring model training.

10.1Prediction scoring

Every proposal carries an expected_effect: a metric key, a direction, a magnitude and a horizon. The evaluation worker scores it when the horizon closes, producing a per-agent calibration record. Calibration then drives autonomy: an agent whose predictions hold for eight consecutive weeks may have a configured action type relaxed by one risk step — but only within the global floor of §7.2, so outreach.send and spend.commit can never be relaxed at all. An agent whose calibration degrades tightens automatically.

10.2Amendment mining

When a human edits a proposal before approving, the diff is the most valuable signal in the platform: it is a domain expert stating precisely what was wrong, at no extra cost. Amendments are clustered by action type and recurring patterns become candidate policy rules, surfaced to the owner as: “the last 14 outreach drafts were all edited to remove the phrase X — add it to the banned-phrase list?” Accepting writes a versioned policy rule; the platform's behaviour changes without anyone touching a prompt.

10.3Experiments as a first-class framework

The workbook's Experiments sheet becomes a real A/B framework: hypothesis, variants, the metric being judged, sample sizes, an explicit significance test, and a decision. A significant result promotes into the playbook as a versioned rule with its evidence attached; the promotion is what closes the loop. Insignificant results are retained — the workbook's own instruction to record the disappointing outcomes is what makes the corpus honest, and an agent that can see what failed proposes better than one that only sees what worked.

10.4Retrieval that improves

The knowledge index is scored against a labelled question set. Retrieval quality is a tracked metric, chunking and ranking changes are evaluated against it, and documents contradicted by newer verified facts are flagged rather than silently outranked. Agents cite what they retrieved, so a bad answer is traceable to a bad chunk.

10.5What is deliberately excluded from v1

No fine-tuning, no reinforcement from implicit signals, no autonomous prompt rewriting. Each would make behaviour harder to explain and to roll back, and none is necessary to get the compounding effect the four mechanisms above already deliver. Prompt changes are code changes: versioned, reviewed, tested, deployable and revertible.

Nothing an agent does is on the critical path of a human logging their work.

16.2Isolation testing as a standing suite

Two tenants are seeded with recognisably distinct data. Every endpoint, every agent tool and every retrieval path is then exercised as tenant A attempting to reach tenant B's data — by ID, by search, by export, by report, and by asking an agent for it in natural language. Any leak fails the build. This runs on every pull request, not at audit time, because a tenant-isolation bug found in production is a company-ending event for a B2B platform.

16.3The workbook's defects as regression tests

Each of the nine defects in §2.2 becomes an executable test that injects the exact adversarial input and asserts the corrected behaviour: the lead accepted without a logged request, the date typed as text, the wrong-year typo, the meeting that progressed past its status, the schedule whose end precedes its start, the minutes logged under a misspelled name, the deal signed with a typed note, the content published without a date, the negative value pasted past validation. These are the highest-value tests in the suite because each one represents a real failure that reached a real report.

16.4Testing the no-arithmetic invariant

An adversarial suite hands agents raw rows and asks for totals, ratios and comparisons in prompts designed to tempt computation. The assertion is that the agent calls the metric API or declines — and that any number appearing in its output resolves to a metric response in the same run's transcript. An unsourced figure in agent output fails the test. This is the mechanical enforcement of §0.4's second constraint.

16.5Provider drift detection

A nightly job runs the golden-set agent tasks against live providers and compares behaviour to the recorded baseline: schema adherence, refusal behaviour, citation discipline, cost and latency. A model updated under us shows up here rather than in a customer's board pack. Drift beyond threshold opens a ticket with the diff attached.

16.6Load profile

Test against a realistic shape, not a flat average: a 09:00 logging spike as the team starts, a Monday 06:00–08:00 report burst, continuous agent runs through the day, and a month-end reporting peak. Assert §14.1 targets under the peak, verify the per-tenant agent cap prevents one tenant starving others, and confirm graceful degradation when a provider is removed mid-run.

17Deployment

17.1Images and topology

Four images from one multi-stage Dockerfile: api, orchestrator, workers, web (static, CDN-served). Non-root, distroless runtime, read-only filesystem, health and readiness probes, SBOM and signature per build. Phase 1–2 run on a single Docker Compose host with managed PostgreSQL and Redis; Phase 3 onward on Kubernetes with HPA on queue depth for workers and on request rate for the API. The scheduler is a leader-elected singleton — two schedulers means double-firing, which for outreach.send means contacting a person twice.

17.2Pipeline and release discipline

On pull request: lint, typecheck, unit, metric-truth, integration, agent-contract, isolation, E2E, container scan. On merge to main: build, sign, push, deploy to staging, run smoke and drift suites. Production is a manual promotion of a staging-verified image. Migrations are expand–contract in separate releases so a rollback never needs a down-migration against live data. Feature flags per tenant let an agent be enabled for one organisation before all.

Prompts are versioned artefacts. A prompt change is a code change: reviewed, tested against the golden set, deployed with the release, and revertible independently. A prompt edited in a database by a support engineer is an unversioned production change to business logic, and the platform provides no such path.

18.1Why this order

The record and the truth come before any agent because an agent reasoning over untrustworthy numbers produces confident nonsense, and because P1 alone already beats the workbook on concurrency, capacity and integrity. The approval spine comes before any agent that can act, so the safety property is established while it is cheap to change. The four P2 agents are deliberately incapable of external effect — the spine gets exercised with real work before anything can reach the outside world. Publishing precedes outreach because a bad post can be deleted and a bad message cannot. The orchestrator comes last, not first, because it needs real agents with real calibration histories to arbitrate between; built first it would be a scheduler with pretensions.

18.2Migration from the workbook

The workbook stays authoritative until the P1 exit gate is signed. It is then imported — reference data as a versioned seed, transactional rows through the validated bulk endpoint, with a rejection report for every row that fails the constraints in §4.3. Those rejections are valuable: they are the file's accumulated data-quality debt, made explicit for the first time. A four-week dual-run follows, in which the platform and the workbook are reconciled weekly and any divergence is explained before the workbook is retired to read-only archive. No cutover event, no data-loss risk, and a real reconciliation record at the end of it.

AAnnex A · Agent prompt contract

Every agent prompt is assembled from the same seven blocks, in this order, so that behaviour is comparable across agents and a change to shared policy propagates everywhere. Blocks 1–4 are stable and cache-eligible; 5–7 vary per run.

[1] IDENTITY      Agent key, version, mission in one sentence, and the named
                  human role accountable for its output.

[2] INVARIANTS    The six universal rules of §6.1, verbatim and identical across
                  all agents. Includes: you emit proposals, never effects; you
                  cite or you abstain; you never compute a number.

[3] POLICY       Tenant golden rules, technique denylist, claims policy, brand
                  voice, character limits, jurisdiction constraints. Injected from
                  the database — never hard-coded in the prompt file.

[4] TOOLS        Typed tool definitions, allowlisted for this agent. Each carries
                  its schema, its cost, and whether its result is trusted or
                  untrusted input.

[5] CONTEXT      Resolved inputs for this run: metric values (with keys and
                  periods), retrieved documents (with source and span), prior-run
                  memory, and the current data-health state.

[6] TASK         The specific instruction, and the output schema it must satisfy.

[7] OUTPUT       Required shape: proposals[], each with payload, rationale,
                  citations[], expected_effect. Plus abstentions[] — what it chose
                  not to propose and why, which is often the more useful half.

Block 7's abstentions array is not decoration. An agent that says “I did not propose a schedule for Pinterest because its native scheduler caps at 30 days and the requested window is 90” is far more useful than one that silently proposes nothing, and it makes the reasoning reviewable. Abstentions are surfaced in the run detail view and mined in §10.2 alongside amendments.

Output validation is mechanical and runs before a human ever sees the result: schema conformance, every citation resolvable, every number matching a metric response in the same transcript, no policy-violating content, expected_effect present and well-formed. A response failing validation is retried once with the violations fed back, then failed. It is never partially accepted.

D.1Decisions needed before Phase 1 starts

Nine of these eleven are business decisions rather than technical ones. Each has a recommendation so none of them needs to be a long conversation.

#DecisionRecommendation
D1Is Workforce OS a PCI-internal platform, or a product sold to other institutes?Build multi-tenant, run single-tenant. Costs ~15% more in P1 and avoids a rewrite if the answer becomes “sell it”.
D2Does the LinkedIn constraint in §0.4 stand?Yes, without exception. It is your own Golden Rule 5. Automating it risks the accounts the whole strategy rests on.
D3Which CRM is authoritative for leads at volume?Decide now even if adoption is later — it changes whether lead is a source of truth or a projection.
D4Who holds owner, and who deputises when they are unavailable?Two named owners. A single approver becomes the bottleneck that gets designed around.
D5Monthly AI spend ceiling per tenant?Start at $400/month for PCI's team size; the metering makes it adjustable with evidence.
D6Which three AI providers, and any residency constraints?Two at launch (one primary, one for cross-vendor critique), third as fallback. Gulf residency may constrain the choice.
D7Do executives and board members get platform logins, or PDFs?PDFs in P1–P4; the executive surface in P5. Read-only board access is a support burden before the numbers are stable.
D8Retention period for lead personal data?24 months from last contact, then anonymise. Set it before import, not after.
D9Does the workbook remain a supported export?Yes for 12 months. It is the fallback if the platform is unavailable and it eases adoption.
D10Compliance regime to design against — GDPR, UK GDPR, Gulf laws?Design to the strictest applicable (GDPR) and configure down. Retrofitting is far more expensive.
D11Build team shape?Two senior backend, one frontend, one platform, part-time design. One engineer cannot hold §7 and §9 safely.

The one sentence to take from this document

The value of this platform is not that AI agents can do marketing work — plenty of tools claim that. It is that every number it shows you is computed in SQL and traceable to its definition, and every action it takes in the world was signed off by a named human whose decision is on the record. Those two properties are what make it safe to run a business on, and §3.3, §5 and §7 exist to make them structurally true rather than merely intended.
