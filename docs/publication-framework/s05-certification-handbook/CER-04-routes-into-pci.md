# Routes into PCI: standard, founding, honorary

> Three ways in, and a straight answer about which of them ends in an examination.

**In one paragraph.** There are three public routes into PCI. The standard route is application, payment
and examination. The founding route is an invitation-only cohort entered with a code that waives the fee
and bundles membership, study and examination access — the examination itself is unchanged. The honorary
route confers the designation Honorary Fellow (PCI) by board decision and requires no examination, awards
no certification, and grants no post-nominal in the credential suite. This document states what each route
changes and what no route changes.

**Who this is for.** Prospective candidates deciding how to enter; employers and sponsors funding a
cohort; anyone who has been offered a founding code and wants to know what it is worth; and anyone
considering an honorary nomination, for themselves or for someone else.

---

## 1. The one rule that survives every route

**No route awards a credential without an examination.** The standard route and the founding route both
end in the same sitting, marked to the same standard. The honorary route does not end in a credential at
all.

That sentence is the whole document in one line, and it is written first because the alternative is a
reader who works it out in month three. A route changes **what you pay** and **how you were invited**. It
never changes what you have to demonstrate.

---

## 2. The three public routes at a glance

| | Standard | Founding | Honorary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who may enter | Anyone meeting the eligibility rule | Invited holders of a valid founding code | Anyone may apply; the board decides |
| Examination required | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** |
| What is awarded on success | PCL-AI, PFL-AI or PML-AI | The same credential, on the same terms | The designation **Honorary Fellow (PCI)** |
| Eligibility rule | Three years, any field | Three years, any field, self-declared with evidence | Distinguished contribution, judged by the board |
| What you pay | The examination fee | Waived by the code, within its window | No examination fee, because there is no examination |
| Who decides | Assessment, then the certification decision | Assessment, then the certification decision | The board |
| Post-nominal in the credential suite | Yes | Yes | **No** |

## 3. The standard route

### 3.1 What it is

Apply online, meet the eligibility rule of three years' professional experience in any field, satisfy the
booking conditions, pay the examination fee, book inside your window, sit under proctoring, and earn the
credential by passing.

### 3.2 What it costs

A single examination fee in US dollars, which opens a scheduling window rather than buying a credential.
Fees are route-dependent and discountable, and the amount that binds you is the amount shown at checkout.
The figure itself — including an unresolved conflict between two published amounts — is owned by
`CER-05` §4, together with the window, refunds and retakes.

### 3.3 Why it is the reference route

Every other route is described by how it differs from this one. If you are reading a claim about PCI
certification and cannot tell which route it describes, assume the standard route and check.

---

## 4. The founding route

### 4.1 What it is

An **invitation-only founding cohort**, entered with a valid founding code. One code grants **membership,
study access and examination access together**: a full fee waiver layered over the standard flow, valid
inside the window set on the code.

### 4.2 What it changes

Money and access, and nothing else. A founding candidate:

- declares the same eligibility — three years of professional experience in any field — and supports the
  declaration with evidence at the point of application;
- sits the same examination, of the same duration, marked to the same standard;
- receives the same credential, with the same validity, subject to the same recertification obligation.

Founding applications are reviewed. An application is recorded as approved or pending review, and may be
rejected or, later, revoked. Approval of a founding application is approval of entry to the cohort — not
of the credential.

### 4.3 What it does not change

A founding candidate is not certified by being founding. The credential is still earned by passing the
examination. Nothing about founding status confers a result, softens a mark, or shortens a requirement.

### 4.4 Availability

The founding cohort exists inside a defined window. `[CONFIRM: whether the founding-route window is
currently open and the date on which it closes]` — check the current published position before planning
around it, and do not assume a code you were sent last quarter is still live.

### 4.5 Why the Institute runs a founding route at all

*PCI interpretation.* A founding-stage certification body needs candidates before it has a track record,
and candidates need a reason to go first. The founding route pays for that asymmetry with a fee waiver
rather than with a lowered standard, because a lowered standard would devalue the credential of exactly
the people who took the risk. Founding candidates are the cohort with the strongest interest in the
examination being hard.

## 5. The honorary route

### 5.1 State it plainly

**The honorary route requires no examination.** It confers the designation **Honorary Fellow (PCI)** — a
board-conferred recognition of distinguished contribution to the discipline. It does **not** award PCL-AI,
PFL-AI or PML-AI. An honorary award is recorded separately from examined credentials, under its own award
reference series, precisely so that the two can never be confused by a reader, an employer or a
verification check.

### 5.2 How it works

Anyone may apply; the board reviews and decides. An application asks for your professional history,
qualifications, certifications and a summary of the contribution being put forward, with supporting
documents. The decision is the board's, and the citation — the board's stated reason — is recorded with
the award and shown on verification.

An honorary award is **revocable**. It can be withdrawn for cause, and the record shows when and why.

### 5.3 What it is not

- It is **not a certification**. No examination has been passed, so no assessed competence is attested.
- It is **not a post-nominal in the credential suite**. An honorary recipient does not write PCL-AI,
  PFL-AI or PML-AI after their name.
- It is **not a membership grade**. Membership grades — Student Member (SPCI), Associate Member (APCI),
  Professional Member (MPCI) and Fellow (FPCI) — are a separate ladder governed by membership rules.
  Whether an honorary award interacts with a membership grade is a membership question, not a
  certification one, and this handbook does not decide it.

### 5.4 Describing an honorary award honestly

If you hold one, describe it as what it is: an honorary recognition conferred by the Institute's board.
Do not describe it as certification, do not list it among certifications on a professional profile, and do
not let a reader infer an examination that did not happen. The Institute records honorary awards
separately for the same reason.

## 6. The administrative routes

The platform also supports routes that are not marketed as public entry points but exist because real
programmes need them: a **sponsored** route where a third party funds the candidate, a **complimentary**
route, **full** and **partial fee waivers**, and a non-public **test** route used for platform
verification.

Every one of them is a **billing arrangement**. They determine who pays and how much. None alters
eligibility, the examination, the pass standard, the certification decision or the recertification
obligation. A candidate entered under a sponsorship sits exactly the examination a self-funding candidate
sits.

Two consequences worth stating for employers:

1. **Sponsorship buys an attempt, not an outcome.** A sponsoring organisation is buying its people the
   opportunity to be assessed. If it needs a guaranteed number of passes, it is buying the wrong thing
   from the wrong kind of body.
2. **The certification decision is not the sponsor's.** The credential belongs to the individual, and the
   assessment result is theirs. A sponsor is not a party to the certification decision.

---

## 7. How this goes wrong

- **Assuming honorary is a fast route to the credential.** It is not a route to the credential at all.
  §5.1.
- **Listing an honorary designation among certifications.** It reads as a certification the holder does
  not have, and it is the kind of error a hostile reader finds first. §5.4.
- **Treating a founding code as a certificate.** A code grants access. Nothing else. §4.3.
- **Letting a founding code's window lapse.** The waiver lives inside a window; the window closes whether
  or not you booked. §4.4.
- **Sponsors expecting influence over the result.** §6.
- **Comparing route fees without checking route conditions.** A waiver bundled with membership and study
  access is not the same product as a discount on an examination fee, and the two do not compare on price
  alone.
