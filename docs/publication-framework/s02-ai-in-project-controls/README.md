# S02 — AI in Project Controls Guide

**12 documents · prefix `AIG` · [registry](../00-framework/ASSET-REGISTRY.md#s02--ai-in-project-controls-guide-12--prefix-aig)**

The Institute's governed position on AI in a controls function. Domain 13 of the Body of Knowledge is the
source; this series is the practitioner-facing treatment.

## The position, stated once

**AI proposes; the professional disposes.** Every output is *explainable, validated and owned by a
competent human*. That is not a slogan bolted on at the end — it is the load-bearing claim, and it is what
makes the rest of the series coherent.

## What makes this series different

The register is **governed professional scepticism**: neither boosterism nor doomerism. The audience has
been sold AI several times already and has watched at least one pilot quietly disappear. What earns their
attention is being specific about where the technology genuinely helps, blunt about where it does not, and
clear about who signs the number.

The series is deliberately ordered so governance precedes procurement: `AIG-08` (governing AI on a
project) ships before `AIG-11` (evaluating tools). The reverse order is how organisations end up writing a
policy for a tool they already bought.

## Cautions specific to this series

- **Never name a commercial AI product** as an endorsement or as a failure. Describe capability classes.
- **Never invent** adoption rates, accuracy benchmarks, productivity percentages or market statistics.
  This series attracts that temptation more than any other; there is no figure worth the credibility.
- The decision boundary in `AIG-10` is the most consequential content in the series: what AI may propose
  versus what a competent human must decide and sign — baseline change, contingency release, extension-of-
  time entitlement, any forecast submitted to a board.
- Confidentiality of commercially sensitive project data is a real risk, not a compliance footnote —
  `AIG-08` and `ETH-05` own it between them.

## Reading order

`AIG-01` and `AIG-02` set the frame. `AIG-03` is the unglamorous prerequisite — most AI failures in
controls are data failures. `AIG-04` to `AIG-07` are the applied method documents. `AIG-08` to `AIG-10`
are the governance core. `AIG-11` and `AIG-12` cover buying the tools and building the people.
