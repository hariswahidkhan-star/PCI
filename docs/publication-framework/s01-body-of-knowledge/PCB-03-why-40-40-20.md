# Why 40/40/20 — the weighting and what it claims

> The argument for putting forty per cent of a controls credential on finance and twenty per cent on AI.

**In one paragraph.** A certification weighting is an argument about a job, not an allocation of pages.
This document sets out why the PCL-AI puts forty per cent of the examination on project accounting and
finance, forty per cent on project management principles and twenty per cent on AI — with a worked example
showing how a controls professional's forecast decision moves reported revenue — and states plainly what
evidence would change the weighting. A reader who disagrees afterwards will at least be disagreeing with a
stated position rather than an unexplained number.

**Who this is for.** Practitioners deciding whether the framework describes their job, heads of controls
functions judging whether it describes their team's, and anyone who looked at the split and thought the
finance share was too high or the AI share too low.

---

## 1. What a weighting is a claim about

Three groups carry the framework: project accounting and finance at forty per cent, project management
principles at forty per cent, and AI knowledge and practical approach at twenty per cent. The domains inside
each group are mapped in `PCB-02 — The thirteen domains at a glance`.

Those three numbers are the most consequential editorial decision in the credential: a weighting determines
which competence a pass certifies and which it merely brushes. Publish one and you have committed to a view
of the job that can be tested against practice and, if wrong, held against you.

The Institute's position, labelled as the Institute's: **project controls, cost engineering and project
finance are one discipline, and the boundary between them is where projects are lost.** The 40/40/20 split
is that claim expressed as an examination.

## 2. Why finance is forty per cent

The usual objection is that controls professionals do not do accounting; accountants do. True, and beside
the point. The controls professional does not *record* the entry — they *originate the input* that
determines it. On a long-term contract accounted for over time by the cost-to-cost input method, the
percentage complete driving recognised revenue is costs incurred against total expected costs. Total
expected costs is the estimate at completion. The estimate at completion is a controls output. That is not a
corner case; it is how revenue arises in project-based businesses, and it means the forecast defended in a
monthly review lands, largely unaltered, in the profit the board reports.

The second reason is symmetrical: without the finance half, the controls half cannot be checked. Actual cost
is a ledger extract, and knowing whether it is complete requires knowing whether the accruals were raised
and the commitments captured. Cost variance computed on incomplete actuals is not a small error — it is a
*favourable* one, which is the most dangerous kind.

## 3. Worked example — one forecast decision, two revenue numbers

*Illustrative figures.* One fixed-price contract, accounted for over time under the cost-to-cost input
method. Currency stated as USD; data date end of month 9. Money to the nearest whole unit, ratios to two
decimal places, percentages to one — all computed from unrounded values.

**Given.** Contract price 12,000,000 · Budget at Completion (`BAC`) 10,000,000 · Planned Value (`PV`)
6,300,000 · Earned Value (`EV`) 6,000,000 · Actual Cost (`AC`) 5,700,000 · certified and billed to date
6,400,000.

**Step 1 — the controls position.** Under budget, behind plan.

`CPI = EV ÷ AC = 6,000,000 ÷ 5,700,000 = 1.05`
`SPI = EV ÷ PV = 6,000,000 ÷ 6,300,000 = 0.95`
`CV = EV − AC = 6,000,000 − 5,700,000 = 300,000` favourable
`SV = EV − PV = 6,000,000 − 6,300,000 = (300,000)` adverse

**Step 2 — the index-based forecast, and what it does to revenue.**

`EAC = BAC ÷ CPI = 10,000,000 ÷ 1.0526… = 9,500,000`
`VAC = BAC − EAC = 10,000,000 − 9,500,000 = 500,000` favourable
`Progress (cost-to-cost) = AC ÷ EAC = 5,700,000 ÷ 9,500,000 = 60.0 %`
`Revenue recognised to date = 60.0 % × 12,000,000 = 7,200,000`
`Contract asset (unbilled) = 7,200,000 − 6,400,000 = 800,000`

`AC ÷ EAC` came out at exactly the same 60.0 per cent as `EV ÷ BAC = 6,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000`. That is not a
coincidence of these figures but an identity: where the estimate at completion is the index-based
`BAC ÷ CPI`, then `AC ÷ EAC = AC ÷ (BAC × AC ÷ EV) = EV ÷ BAC`, whatever the numbers. The accountant's
percentage complete and the controls professional's percentage complete are one calculation that two
professions have been taught to name differently.

**Step 3 — the forecast the practitioner actually defends.** The index-based forecast assumes past cost
performance continues. Suppose the control account managers' bottom-up estimate to complete says the
remaining work will cost 4,500,000, because the favourable variance came from early scope now finished.

`EAC = AC + ETC = 5,700,000 + 4,500,000 = 10,200,000`
`Progress = AC ÷ EAC = 5,700,000 ÷ 10,200,000 = 55.9 %`
`Revenue recognised to date = 55.9 % × 12,000,000 = 6,705,882`
`Contract asset (unbilled) = 6,705,882 − 6,400,000 = 305,882`

**The consequence.** Substituting a bottom-up estimate to complete for the index-based forecast moved
recognised revenue from 7,200,000 to 6,705,882 — a reduction of 494,118, or `494,118 ÷ 12,000,000 = 4.1 %`
of the contract price. Nothing about the work changed. One professional judgement about the cost of the
remaining scope changed the number the board reports.

**Assumptions the answer depends on.** Progress measured by the cost-to-cost input method; a single
performance obligation satisfied over time; no variable consideration or constraint in play; transaction
price unchanged at 12,000,000; the two estimates at completion the only variable between the cases. Which
method to use is itself a judgement, treated in `BPG-09 — Estimate at completion — choosing and defending a
method`.

## 4. Why project management is forty per cent and not sixty

Eight of the thirteen domains sit in the project management group — cost control, earned value, contracts,
lifecycle, agile, scheduling, process cycles and risk — and on knowledge-area count the group is much the
larger, thirty-five of sixty-one. It carries the same forty per cent, and this is the harder half of the
argument.

The delivery group is weighted for what the professional must *originate*: the schedule logic, the measured
progress, the committed cost, the quantified risk. The finance group is weighted for what they must be able
to *stand behind* — which requires comparable depth because the consequences are less visible and arrive
later. A schedule error announces itself. A recognition error waits for an audit. Sixty per cent on delivery
would reproduce a credential the market already has several of.

## 5. Why AI is twenty per cent, and not a chapter

Twenty per cent is deliberately uncomfortable in both directions.

**Against a token chapter.** AI now sits in the tooling of estimating, forecasting, document review, risk
identification and reporting, and a professional who cannot judge an AI-produced forecast will either
rubber-stamp it or refuse it — both failures of competence. AI proposes; the professional disposes, and
disposing requires knowing what the model was given, what its output is sensitive to, what it cannot know
and what a human must verify before anything is built on it. That is examinable knowledge, and a single
chapter cannot carry a competence exercised weekly.

**Against a larger share.** Twenty per cent is also a ceiling, set there because AI in project controls is a
*governed application* of the other twelve domains, not a replacement for them. Someone who prompts fluently
but cannot compute an estimate at completion by hand has no basis on which to disagree with the machine. The
group is weighted at the level of judgement it requires, not the level of attention it attracts.

Two structural points follow. The twenty per cent is measured across the whole AI surface — every non-AI
domain carries embedded coverage of governed AI use in its subject, and knowledge area 13.5 carries the
systematic per-domain treatment. And this weighting will age faster than the other two, as it should.

## 6. Why the weights attach to groups and not to domains

The Institute publishes three weights, not thirteen. Weighting each domain would imply a precision the
evidence does not support: no defensible study has established that scheduling is worth 6.5 per cent of a
controls professional's competence and process cycles 3.5 per cent. Group weights claim only what can be
defended — that these three bodies of knowledge matter in roughly this proportion — and they protect
candidates from a false optimisation, because there is no domain small enough to skip safely and no
per-domain passing requirement to game.

## 7. What would change the weighting

A position that cannot be falsified is not worth publishing. Three things would move this one.

**A job-task analysis that contradicts it.** A formal job-task analysis with practising professionals will
validate the blueprint's sampling against the work the credential attests. If practitioners' actual task
frequencies and criticality do not support a forty per cent finance group, the weight changes and the reason
is published. Timing: `[CONFIRM: date the job-task analysis reports, and whether it confirms the 40/40/20
group weights]`.

**Evidence that the finance share is not discriminating.** If item analysis after the first sittings shows
the finance group failing to separate competent from not-yet-competent candidates, that is evidence it is
testing recall rather than the judgement described in §2 — and the fix is item quality before it is
weighting.

**A material change in what AI does.** If the judgement a professional must exercise over AI outputs
broadens, the share rises; if verification becomes routine rather than skilled, it falls. Either way the
change is published with its reason, and candidates are assessed only against the edition in force for their
sitting.

## 8. How this goes wrong

**Reading a weight as a study budget.** The weights describe the paper, not your gaps. A planner with strong
schedule logic and weak accounting should spend well over forty per cent of revision on Domains 1–4.

**Reading a weight as a statement of workplace value.** Forty per cent examination weight is not forty per
cent of a controls professional's week. Sampling weights the knowledge a decision depends on, not the hours
a task consumes; using it to size a team misapplies it. The correct employer use is `PCB-06 — Using the Body
of Knowledge: a guide for employers`.

**Inferring a per-domain requirement.** There is no minimum score per domain and no published per-domain
percentage. Results carry a domain breakdown for diagnostic value; the pass decision is made on the whole
paper against a single criterion-referenced standard.

**Assuming the AI share means AI questions.** Governed-AI judgement appears inside items sitting in any
group, because that is where it appears at work.
