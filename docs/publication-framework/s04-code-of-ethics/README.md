# S04 — Code of Ethics

**6 documents · prefix `ETH` · [registry](../00-framework/ASSET-REGISTRY.md#s04--code-of-ethics-6--prefix-eth)**

Professional conduct, and the casebook that makes it real.

## What makes this series different

`ETH-01` is a **governance instrument, not an essay**: numbered clauses to citable depth, so a decision can
point at `§4.2` and mean something. It supersedes and expands
`docs/downloads/code-of-professional-conduct.md` and must not contradict it.

The rest of the series exists because a code nobody can apply is decoration. `ETH-03` is the heart of it —
a casebook of realistic dilemmas, each explicitly fictitious, each ending not with a moral but with what a
professional actually says in the room: the forecast a director wants softened before a board meeting, the
accrual quietly deferred to protect a monthly indicator, the float hidden to preserve a completion date,
the progress measured generously at year-end.

That is the difference between an ethics document that gets cited and one that gets filed.

## Cautions specific to this series

- **Jurisdiction neutrality is the largest risk here.** Employment protection, whistleblowing rights and
  company law vary enormously. Describe the principle; state that legal protection varies and that a
  person should take local advice. Never present one country's rule as universal.
- **Every case is fictitious and labelled as such.** No real company, project, person or dispute — no
  exceptions, and no "based on a real project" framing either.
- **Do not invent sanctions or appeal timescales.** Where the Institute's process is not yet published,
  that is a `[CONFIRM: …]` placeholder, not a plausible guess.
- The register is a professional body stating obligations plainly. Not sermonising, not hedging.
- The principle set derives from the predecessor document. Do not assert a different number of principles
  than that document supports; if the count is unclear, structure by theme and say so.

## Where this series meets the others

`ETH-05` (ethical use of AI and data) shares a boundary with `AIG-08` (governing AI on a project) — ethics
owns the duty, governance owns the mechanism. `ETH-03` shares a boundary with `BPG-14` (monthly reporting)
and `BPG-09` (estimate at completion): those documents explain how to produce an honest number, this one
explains what to do when someone senior would prefer a different one.

`ETH-06` names the governance bodies that handle concerns — the Appeals Panel and the Certification and
Impartiality Committee.
