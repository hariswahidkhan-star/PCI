# PML-AI Professional Laws — PCI Project Management Leader – AI

**Status:** Certification Laws for the **PML-AI** credential (Project Management Leader – AI),
drafted under and subordinate to the binding specification in [`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md).
Twenty-four laws, anchored to the sixteen-domain PML-AI Body of Knowledge
(`docs/books/pml-ai/`).

> PCI Professional Laws are professional certification rules and standards of conduct established by
> PCI Global. They are not legislation, regulatory requirements or substitutes for applicable law,
> contractual obligations or authoritative professional standards. Where any applicable law,
> regulation, contract or authoritative professional standard imposes a stricter requirement, that
> requirement governs.

---

## How to read these laws

- **The drafting specification governs.** Definition, hierarchy, normative language, the mandatory
  field template, external-reference rules, the visual system and stable identifiers are all defined
  in [`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md). These laws conform to it; where any doubt arises, that
  specification prevails.
- **Identifiers are stable.** Each law is cited as `PML-LAW-DD-NN`, where `DD` is the two-digit
  Body-of-Knowledge domain of primary anchorage and `NN` a sequence within that domain. Cite laws by
  ID, never by page number. A law that spans domains anchors to the domain that teaches it and lists
  the others under *Related book content*.
- **These laws sit under the Foundational Laws.** `PCI-LAW-F-01` to `PCI-LAW-F-14`
  ([`PCI_FOUNDATIONAL_LAWS.md`](PCI_FOUNDATIONAL_LAWS.md)) bind every PCI credential holder,
  including every PML-AI holder. The laws below add what leading projects, programmes and
  portfolios specifically requires; they never reduce a foundational obligation.
- **Normative language is deliberate.** `must` / `must not` / `shall` / `shall not` state mandatory
  professional rules; `should` states recommended practice from which departure needs a recorded
  reason; `may` is discretionary. Text is labelled, every time, as **Mandatory PCI Law**,
  **Mandatory PCI Rule**, **Recommended PCI Practice**, **External requirement**,
  **Illustrative example** or **Jurisdictional requirement** — never mixed.
- **External references are characterised, never reproduced.** Real instruments are named and
  described in PCI's own words; the official publication always governs. No clause numbers or
  editions are asserted unless verified.
- **The suite principle** applies to every law in this set, in its one approved formulation:

> **AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable.**

## Domain 1 — The Project Leadership Profession

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-01-01 — Leadership Accountability

**Rule.** A credential holder who leads a project, programme or portfolio shall remain personally
accountable for the delivery decisions taken under their authority — including decisions informed,
drafted or recommended by an AI system, an adviser, a supplier or a committee — and shall not
transfer, dilute or disclaim that accountability by reference to any tool, process, body or third
party.

**Purpose.** Delivery leadership is an accountable role, not a coordinating one. Every other law in
this set assumes there is an identifiable person who answers for the decision; where accountability
is diffused across a governance diagram, a delivery partner and an analytics platform, no one
answers and the project drifts unowned.

**Scope.** All credential holders exercising project, programme or portfolio leadership, in every
delivery model — predictive, adaptive or hybrid — and irrespective of whether the work is delivered
in-house, through partners or through an automated toolchain.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Accept the leadership accountability in a written instrument
(charter, appointment or terms of reference) that names the individual; know which decisions are
personally reserved and which are delegated; understand the material basis of any decision issued
under one's authority; record each significant decision with its options, rationale, dissent and
date; correct and notify errors in issued decisions promptly on discovery.

**Required evidence.** The charter or appointment naming the accountable leader; the delegation
schedule; decision-log entries carrying options considered, rationale, recorded dissent and the
accountable name; correction and notification records for decisions later found defective.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder as accountable project, programme or portfolio leader,
for decisions within their authority; the sponsor for decisions reserved above it.

**Independent review.** Required at each stage gate as part of the assurance arrangements, whenever
leadership accountability is split across organisations or contracts, and whenever accountability
for a delivery decision is disputed.

**Escalation trigger.** Any material decision that has no identifiable accountable person; any
instruction to act beyond or outside the recorded authority; discovery that a decision was issued
in the leader's name without their knowledge or review.

**Prohibited practice.** Presenting a committee, a model, a methodology or a supplier as the
accountable party for a decision; accepting a leadership title without the corresponding decision
rights while allowing others to believe accountability is discharged; signing a charter one does
not intend to honour.

**AI application.** AI may assemble decision packages, summarise options and trade-offs, maintain
and age the decision log, and flag decisions recorded without a named accountable owner.

**AI restriction.** AI must not hold, share or discharge accountability, be recorded as the decision
maker or approver of record, or be cited as the reason a decision was taken; no configuration,
disclaimer or contract removes the credential holder's accountability.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-assembled decision material
against its sources before the decision is taken, must test the decision log periodically for
entries lacking a named owner or rationale, and must be able to demonstrate the basis of any
decision issued under their authority.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including how accountabilities are distributed between the governing body, the sponsor and the
project manager. ISO 21505 (international standard) — governance of projects, programmes and
portfolios, which locates delivery accountability within an organisation's governance
arrangements. ISO/IEC 42001 (international standard) — management of artificial intelligence,
including the assignment of organisational roles and responsibilities for AI use. OECD AI
Principles (voluntary framework) — accountability of human actors for AI-influenced outcomes; not
legislation. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Directors' duties, statutory delivery, safety and planning roles, and
professional negligence law determine legal responsibility independently of this professional rule;
obtain local legal advice on the statutory roles attaching to a specific project and entity.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-03; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-01-02; PML-LAW-03-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 1, KA 1.2 — The project leader's accountability (1.2.1
accountability and responsibility; 1.2.2 the obligation set; 1.2.3 the professional standard of
care); KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI (1.4.3 the leader's AI accountability);
Domain 3, KA 3.2 — Sponsorship and steering; Domain 14, KA 14.4 — Human accountability and the AI
use register.

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenario items in which a delivery decision goes wrong
and responsibility is contested between a leader, a governance body, a delivery partner and an
analytics tool, requiring the candidate to locate accountability and state what the accountable
leader must do. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-01-02 — Human Decision Authority

**Rule.** Every delivery decision that commits money, time, scope, people, safety or reputation
shall be taken by an identified, competent and authorised human being; a credential holder shall
not permit an AI system, an automated rule or an unattended workflow to take, approve or trigger
such a decision, and shall not adopt an AI recommendation without independent professional
judgement applied to it.

**Purpose.** Automation can prepare a decision far faster than a leader can consider it, and a
recommendation adopted without judgement is a decision made by the tool. This law fixes the point
at which authority attaches: AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains
accountable.

**Scope.** All credential holders and all delivery workflows — manual, automated, assisted or
agentic — that produce or execute commitments, approvals, prioritisation, resourcing, escalation,
acceptance or go-live decisions.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify, for every decision point in the delivery workflow,
the human who decides and the authority under which they do so; ensure that person has the
information, competence and time to consider the decision rather than to endorse it; record the
human judgement applied to any AI recommendation adopted, including what was checked; design
automated pipelines so that decision-grade outputs are held for human decision rather than acted
on automatically.

**Required evidence.** The decision-authority map linking each decision type to a human role and
threshold; decision records showing the human reasoning applied, not merely the recommendation
accepted; workflow documentation identifying every automated step and its human decision point;
the AI use register for the delivery environment.

**Decision owner.** The named human decision maker for each decision type; the accountable project
leader for the design of the decision workflow.

**Independent review.** Required whenever automation is introduced or extended into a decision path,
whenever an AI system is used to support a decision above the leader's own authority threshold, and
periodically to test that human decisions are substantive rather than pass-through.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of a commitment, approval or escalation that took effect without a
human decision; a pattern of AI recommendations being adopted without recorded consideration; a
tool or supplier configuration that removes a human decision point without governance approval.

**Prohibited practice.** Configuring systems to auto-approve decision-grade outputs; recording an AI
agent, service account or "the system" as the decision maker; adopting a recommendation whose basis
the decision maker does not understand; describing a decision as human when the human role was to
click through.

**AI application.** AI may generate options, model consequences, rank alternatives against declared
criteria, prepare the decision paper, and check that a decision is routed to the correct authority.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide, approve, commit, prioritise, escalate or accept on behalf of
the credential holder, and must not be recorded as the authority for any decision.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must independently test the reasoning and
inputs behind any AI recommendation before adopting it, must sample decisions periodically to
confirm that human consideration is real, and must audit automated pipelines for undeclared
auto-decision paths.

**External references.** OECD AI Principles (voluntary framework) — human-centred values, human
agency and oversight in AI-influenced decisions; not legislation. ISO/IEC 42001 (international
standard) — management of AI use, including human oversight arrangements. ISO/IEC 38507
(international standard) — governance implications for a governing body of an organisation's use of
AI. NIST AI Risk Management Framework, United States National Institute of Standards and Technology
(voluntary framework) — a function-based approach to managing AI risk; not a standard and not a
regulation. The European Union AI Act is named as an example of a risk-tiered regulatory approach,
binding only within its own jurisdiction. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Some jurisdictions restrict automated decision-making that affects
individuals, or require a human review right; obtain local legal advice for any workflow that
touches personal data, employment decisions or statutory determinations.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-02; PCI-LAW-F-03; PCI-LAW-F-04; PML-LAW-01-01; PML-LAW-14-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 1, KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI (1.4.2 the
responsible-AI principle applied; 1.4.3 the leader's AI accountability); Domain 14, KA 14.3 — AI use
across the lifecycle, prompting and verification (14.3.4 the plausible wrong number); KA 14.4 —
Explainability, bias, human accountability (14.4.3 human accountability and the AI use register);
Domain 3, KA 3.2 — Decision authorities and thresholds.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting automated or AI-assisted delivery workflows in
which a decision effectively takes itself, testing whether the candidate can identify the missing
human decision point and reconstruct a compliant workflow. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 2 — Strategy, Selection and Business Alignment

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-02-01 — Business-Case Integrity

**Rule.** A credential holder shall ensure that any business case they prepare, endorse, maintain or
rely upon states its scope, options, assumptions, benefit claims, costs, risks and exclusions
honestly and traceably, and shall not present, sustain or allow reliance on a business case they
know to be optimistic, superseded, selectively evidenced or reverse-engineered to a predetermined
answer.

**Purpose.** The business case is the instrument by which an organisation decides to spend its money
and its people. A business case shaped to secure approval rather than to inform a decision
transfers the failure to delivery, where it costs far more to discover.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare, appraise, approve, update or deliver against a
business case for a project, programme or portfolio investment, at any stage of maturity.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Record the option set actually considered, including the do
nothing or do minimum option; state each material assumption with its source, owner and sensitivity;
keep costs, benefits and risk allowances on a consistent basis and price date; refresh the business
case when its material assumptions change; state honestly where evidence is thin rather than
presenting an estimate as a fact.

**Required evidence.** The business case with its options appraisal; the assumption and dependency
register with named owners; the basis of cost and benefit estimates; sensitivity or scenario
analysis; the version history showing refreshes and the decisions each supported.

**Decision owner.** The sponsor owns the business case and its approval; the credential holder
leading delivery owns its factual accuracy and its currency in respect of delivery information.

**Independent review.** Required before investment approval, at each stage gate, and whenever a
material assumption fails, a cost or benefit moves outside the stated tolerance, or the case is
re-presented for further funding.

**Escalation trigger.** Instruction or pressure to adjust benefits, costs, timescales or risk
allowances to reach an approval threshold; discovery that an approved case rests on an assumption
already known to be false; a case being relied on after its evidence has expired.

**Prohibited practice.** Reverse-engineering a case to a required return; omitting a known cost,
dependency or disbenefit; presenting a single-point figure as certain when the underlying range is
wide; carrying forward a superseded case because updating it would reopen a decision.

**AI application.** AI may draft business-case narrative, structure option comparisons, test
internal arithmetic consistency, surface unstated assumptions, and compare a case against
organisational templates and historical outturns.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide the investment, select the preferred option, set benefit or
cost values used for approval, or certify a business case as sound.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-drafted figures and
comparisons against the underlying estimates, benefit evidence and delivery data; every number that
enters an approval paper must be traceable to a source a human has checked.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including the role of the business case and benefits within project practice. ISO 21504
(international standard) — portfolio management guidance, in which investment selection depends on
comparable and current case information. AACE International, referenced for the existence and
purpose of its cost-estimate classification progression within the Total Cost Management framework
(professional guidance) — estimate maturity governs what an early business case can honestly claim.
The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Public-sector appraisal rules, subsidy conditions, procurement
regulations and listed-company disclosure obligations may impose specific business-case content,
appraisal methods or approval routes; obtain local advice before treating a PCI-compliant case as
a compliant statutory submission.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-10; PML-LAW-02-02; PML-LAW-03-03; PML-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 2, KA 2.2 — The business case and selection (2.2.1 the business
case as a decision instrument; 2.2.2 options and appraisal; 2.2.3 selection and prioritisation
models); KA 2.3 — Benefits, value and sustainability (2.3.4 assumptions and dependencies); KA 2.1 —
Strategic context and alignment (2.1.3 alignment as a repeated test); Domain 15, KA 15.2 — Benefits
and portfolio balancing.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a business case must be defended, refreshed or
withdrawn under approval pressure, testing whether the candidate can identify the compromised
element and the professional response. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-02-02 — Benefits Ownership

**Rule.** Every benefit claimed to justify an investment shall have a named owner outside the
delivery team who accepts responsibility for realising it, a defined measure, a baseline and a
measurement date; a credential holder shall not allow a benefit to be claimed in a business case,
a portfolio total or a report without those four elements, and shall not accept ownership of
benefits they have no authority to realise.

**Purpose.** Outputs are delivered by projects; benefits are realised by operations. A benefit with
no owner in the receiving organisation is an assertion, not a plan, and the double counting of
unowned benefits is how portfolios come to promise more value than the organisation can absorb.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading or supporting projects, programmes and portfolios whose
approval or continuation depends on claimed benefits, financial or non-financial.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a benefits register in which each benefit carries an
owner, measure, baseline, target, measurement date and the change in working that must occur to
realise it; obtain the named owner's acceptance; test each claimed benefit for double counting
across the portfolio; carry disbenefits in the same register as benefits.

**Required evidence.** The benefits register with named owners and their recorded acceptance; the
benefits map linking outputs to outcomes to benefits; baseline measurements taken before change;
the double-count check across the portfolio; the benefits measurement plan.

**Decision owner.** The sponsor for the benefit set as a whole; the named benefit owner in the
receiving business for each individual benefit.

**Independent review.** Required at investment approval, at each gate where benefits are restated,
and whenever a benefit is transferred between owners or the receiving operation is reorganised.

**Escalation trigger.** A benefit carried in an approved case with no owner or no measure; a named
owner declining or being unable to accept the benefit; the same benefit appearing in more than one
case or portfolio line.

**Prohibited practice.** Assigning benefit ownership to the project team, to a governance body or
to a vacant role; inflating benefit values to close a business-case gap; claiming a benefit whose
baseline was never measured; quietly deleting a failed benefit rather than reporting it.

**AI application.** AI may build benefit maps from case documentation, detect probable double
counting across registers, check measures for testability, and monitor benefit-owner acceptance
status.

**AI restriction.** AI must not assign benefit ownership, set or approve benefit values, or declare
a benefit realisable.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-detected double counts and
AI-drafted measures with the named owners, and must confirm that each baseline exists and was
recorded before the change took effect.

**External references.** ISO 21503 (international standard) — programme management guidance, in
which benefit realisation is treated as an owned, managed outcome rather than a by-product of
delivery. ISO 21504 (international standard) — portfolio management guidance, including the
comparability of benefit claims across a portfolio. ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance
on project management, including the relationship between outputs, outcomes and benefits. The
official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Where claimed benefits support grant funding, regulated pricing or
public accountability statements, local rules may govern how benefits are defined, evidenced and
reported; obtain local advice before relying on a PCI-compliant register for a statutory return.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PML-LAW-02-01; PML-LAW-16-02; PML-LAW-11-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 2, KA 2.3 — Benefits, value and sustainability (2.3.1 benefits
mapping; 2.3.2 measures and baselines); Domain 1, KA 1.3 — Systems thinking and value (1.3.2
outputs, outcomes, benefits and value); Domain 15, KA 15.2 — Benefits and portfolio balancing
(15.2.1 the portfolio benefits register and the four double counts); Domain 16, KA 16.4 — Benefits
measurement.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting a benefits register with unowned, unmeasured
or double-counted entries, requiring the candidate to identify the defect and state the corrective
action before approval. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 3 — Governance, Organisation and Decision Rights

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-03-01 — Governance Before Delivery

**Rule.** A credential holder shall not commence or continue delivery of a project or programme
without governance arrangements that are recorded, resourced and actually operating — a named
sponsor with authority, a defined governing body, stated decision rights, an escalation route and
a decision record — and shall report the absence of any of these as a delivery risk until it is
remedied.

**Purpose.** Governance is what converts activity into accountable delivery. Projects that begin
without a functioning sponsor and decision route do not avoid governance; they defer it to the
point of crisis, when decisions are taken under pressure and without record.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading or supporting projects, programmes and portfolios in any
organisational form — functional, matrix, project-based, joint venture or partner-delivered — and
in predictive, adaptive or hybrid delivery.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Record the governance arrangement in a governance map naming
the sponsor, governing body, assurance provider and the leader's own authority; confirm the sponsor
is available and empowered to decide within stated timescales; establish the decision record before
the first decision; review the arrangement when the organisation, contract or delivery approach
changes.

**Required evidence.** The governance map and terms of reference; the sponsor appointment; meeting
records showing the governing body convening and deciding; the decision record; the escalation
route with response times.

**Decision owner.** The accountable executive or governing body that establishes governance; the
credential holder for reporting its absence, decay or ineffectiveness.

**Independent review.** Required at project or programme initiation, at each stage gate, and
whenever governance changes materially — a new sponsor, a merged governing body, a change of
delivery partner or a move between delivery approaches.

**Escalation trigger.** A sponsor who cannot be reached or cannot decide; a governing body that has
not met or has met without deciding while decisions await it; delivery proceeding against decisions
that were never recorded.

**Prohibited practice.** Operating a governance structure that exists only on paper; substituting
informal approval by a senior individual for the decision of the body that holds the right;
allowing a governance body to receive assurance from the team it is assuring without disclosure.

**AI application.** AI may draft governance maps and terms of reference, track decision latency and
outstanding decisions, prepare governance packs, and flag decisions taken outside the recorded
route.

**AI restriction.** AI must not constitute governance, hold a seat, cast a decision, or serve as the
governing body's assurance of its own arrangements.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm that the governance arrangement
described in documents matches what actually occurs — that the named bodies meet, decide and record
— and must verify AI-generated governance reporting against meeting records.

**External references.** ISO 21505 (international standard) — governance of projects, programmes
and portfolios, including the relationship between the governing body and delivery management.
ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, including governance and
sponsorship practices. ISO/IEC 38507 (international standard) — governance implications for a
governing body of an organisation's use of AI, relevant where governance is itself
digitally supported. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Public bodies, regulated industries and listed entities may be subject
to mandated governance structures, approval routes and reporting duties; obtain local advice before
treating a PCI-compliant governance map as satisfying a statutory or regulatory governance
requirement.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-03-02; PML-LAW-03-03; PML-LAW-01-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 3, KA 3.1 — Governance models (3.1.1 what governance is for; 3.1.2
structures across organisational forms; 3.1.3 governance in agile and hybrid environments); KA 3.2 —
Sponsorship and steering (3.2.1 the sponsor role; 3.2.2 steering committees that work); Domain 15,
KA 15.4 — Transformation governance and strategic reporting.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting absent, decorative or decayed governance —
an unavailable sponsor, a board that never meets, decisions taken in corridors — requiring the
candidate to identify the governance failure and the leader's obligation. No live examination
content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-03-02 — Decision Rights and Thresholds

**Rule.** A credential holder shall exercise only the decision rights delegated to them, at the
thresholds recorded in the delegation schedule, and shall not split, aggregate, re-time or
recharacterise a decision to bring it within their own authority or to avoid a governing body;
decisions above the threshold shall be referred, with a recommendation, to the authority that holds
them.

**Purpose.** Thresholds exist so that the size of a commitment matches the seniority of the person
making it. A leader who quietly stays under the limit by dividing the decision has not respected
the threshold; they have defeated it, and removed the organisation's chance to say no.

**Scope.** All credential holders holding delegated authority over scope, cost, schedule,
contingency, resources, procurement, acceptance or release, whether the delegation is contractual,
organisational or programme-level.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Hold a current written delegation schedule; test each decision
against the cumulative as well as the individual threshold; refer decisions above authority with a
clear recommendation and the information needed to decide; record every referral and its outcome;
seek re-delegation formally rather than acting and regularising afterwards.

**Required evidence.** The delegation schedule with thresholds and cumulative tests; decision-log
entries showing the authority relied upon; referral papers and the deciding body's response;
records of any emergency decision taken outside authority, with the immediate notification made.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for decisions within their delegation; the governing body
or sponsor for decisions above it.

**Independent review.** Required where a series of related decisions approaches a cumulative
threshold, following any decision taken outside authority, and periodically to test whether
delegations still match the risk profile of the work.

**Escalation trigger.** A decision required above the holder's authority with no available deciding
body; discovery that related decisions have been divided to remain within a threshold; instruction
to approve something the holder has no authority to approve.

**Prohibited practice.** Splitting a change, a purchase or a drawdown to stay under a limit;
back-dating an approval; treating silence from a governing body as consent; exercising authority
that has lapsed or was never granted.

**AI application.** AI may check proposed decisions against the delegation schedule, detect
threshold-splitting patterns in change and procurement data, and route items to the correct
authority.

**AI restriction.** AI must not grant, vary or interpret authority, approve a decision at any
threshold, or determine that a decision falls below a limit.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-generated authority checks
against the current delegation schedule, and must periodically test samples of approved decisions
for correct authority, including cumulative effects.

**External references.** ISO 21505 (international standard) — governance of projects, programmes and
portfolios, including the delegation of authority from the governing body. ISO 21502 (international
standard) — guidance on project management, including the project manager's authority and its
limits. PMI *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional
guidance) — established practice on change authority and escalation paths. The official publications
govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Financial delegations in public bodies, regulated entities and companies
are frequently set by statute, constitution or board resolution, and exceeding them can carry
personal legal consequences; obtain local legal and financial advice on the binding delegation
instrument.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-03-01; PML-LAW-04-01; PML-LAW-10-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 3, KA 3.2 — Sponsorship and steering (3.2.3 decision authorities
and thresholds); KA 3.3 — Assurance, gates and escalation (3.3.3 escalation design); Domain 4,
KA 4.4 — Integrated change control (4.4.3 the change board and the decision log); Domain 8, KA 8.3 —
Responses, reserves and governance (8.3.2 reserves and their authority).

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items on threshold-splitting, lapsed delegations and decisions
taken in the absence of a deciding body, testing whether the candidate can identify the authority
breach and the compliant route. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-03-03 — Gate Evidence

**Rule.** A credential holder shall present at every stage gate the evidence the gate requires,
complete and unedited as to substance, including evidence adverse to proceeding, and shall not
recommend, secure or accept a gate decision obtained through incomplete, selectively presented,
out-of-date or retrospectively assembled evidence.

**Purpose.** A gate is the organisation's opportunity to stop, redirect or fund with knowledge. A
gate passed on curated evidence does not remove the problem; it removes the decision, and converts
an informed investment into an uninformed one.

**Scope.** All credential holders preparing, assuring, chairing or relying on stage gates, phase
reviews, investment decision points, readiness reviews and equivalent checkpoints in any delivery
approach.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Agree the gate's evidence requirements before the gate;
present the current position rather than the position expected by the gate date; state open items,
failed criteria and dissenting assurance opinions explicitly; record any conditional pass with its
conditions, owners and dates, and track them to closure.

**Required evidence.** The gate criteria and evidence pack; assurance reports including adverse
findings; the open-items list with owners and dates; the gate decision record including conditions;
closure evidence for each condition.

**Decision owner.** The governing body or gate authority takes the gate decision; the credential
holder is accountable for the completeness and currency of the evidence placed before it.

**Independent review.** Required for every gate that authorises material expenditure or an
irreversible commitment, and whenever a gate is passed with conditions, deferred criteria or a
dissenting assurance opinion.

**Escalation trigger.** Instruction to remove, soften or defer adverse evidence; a gate scheduled
before its evidence can exist; conditions from a previous gate still open at the next one.

**Prohibited practice.** Presenting an evidence pack that omits a known failed criterion; passing a
gate on the promise that evidence will follow; assembling gate evidence after the decision; using
a conditional pass as a way of avoiding a stop decision.

**AI application.** AI may assemble and index evidence packs, check them for completeness against
the gate criteria, track conditions and open items, and flag evidence that has aged beyond its
useful life.

**AI restriction.** AI must not take a gate decision, declare a criterion satisfied, or certify an
evidence pack as complete.

## Domain 4 — Integration and Delivery Architecture

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-04-01 — Integrated Change Control

**Rule.** A credential holder shall route every change to an approved baseline — scope, schedule,
cost, quality, resources or contractual obligation — through the authorised change process:
identified, impact-assessed across all baselines, approved at the correct authority, and only then
incorporated; unapproved change shall not be implemented, concealed or regularised after the event.

**Purpose.** Baselines are the only stable reference against which performance, entitlement and
forecast mean anything. Change that enters without assessment destroys that reference silently, and
the project loses the ability to say what it agreed to deliver.

**Scope.** All credential holders who raise, assess, approve, reject or incorporate change on
projects and programmes, including contract variations, internal transfers, design development and
changes arising from adaptive delivery within a fixed envelope.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a single change register from identification to
closure; assess each change for its effect on scope, schedule, cost, risk, benefits and contract
before approval; match approval to the delegation schedule; update every affected baseline and
forecast only after approval and as a set; record rejected changes and the reason.

**Required evidence.** The change register; impact assessments covering all baselines; approval
records at the correct authority; the baseline change log; correspondence for contract-related
change; the configuration record showing what the current baseline is.

**Decision owner.** The change authority or governing body approves within its threshold; the
credential holder leading delivery is accountable for assessment quality and for incorporation
discipline.

**Independent review.** Required for change above defined materiality, for cumulative change
approaching contingency or benefit tolerance, for any retrospective approval, and where change
alters the project's stated benefits.

**Escalation trigger.** Work proceeding without approved change; an unassessed change backlog
distorting the forecast; a change whose true impact exceeds the authority under which it was
approved; a pattern of small changes eroding the baseline without governance visibility.

**Prohibited practice.** Doing the work first and papering the change afterwards without disclosure;
assessing cost impact while ignoring schedule, benefit or contract impact; absorbing change into
existing budget so that it never becomes visible; approving change one lacks authority to approve.

**AI application.** AI may detect scope drift in documents and delivery data, draft change requests
and impact assessments, model cross-baseline consequences, and monitor register ageing and
cumulative effect.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve or reject a change, set the authority required, decide that
a change need not be raised, or update a baseline without human approval.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-drafted impact assessments
against the schedule model, cost basis and contract, and must confirm each period that the register
is complete against project records and correspondence.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including integrated change control across project baselines. PMI *A Guide to the Project
Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional guidance) — established practice on
integrated change control and configuration management. FIDIC suite of standard forms of contract

**Jurisdictional caution.** Contractual notice periods and condition-precedent provisions can
extinguish entitlement if missed, and their effect varies by governing law; obtain contract-law
advice on time bars and notice validity in the applicable jurisdiction.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PML-LAW-03-02; PML-LAW-05-01; PML-LAW-06-01; PML-LAW-07-01.

## Domain 5 — Scope, Requirements and Value Definition

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-05-01 — Scope Integrity

**Rule.** A credential holder shall maintain a defined, documented and current scope baseline
stating what is included, what is excluded and on what assumptions, and shall not permit work to be
added, removed or substituted outside the change process, nor allow undocumented scope growth to be
presented as delivery within baseline.

**Purpose.** Scope creep is rarely a decision; it is an accumulation of small accommodations, each
defensible alone. Without a maintained boundary, the team is judged against a baseline the work no
longer resembles, and the honest conversation about cost and time never happens.

**Scope.** All credential holders accountable for scope on projects and programmes in any delivery
approach, including adaptive delivery where the envelope, not the item list, is the controlled
boundary.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Publish a scope statement with explicit exclusions,
assumptions and interfaces; hold scope, its breakdown structure and its acceptance criteria as one
controlled baseline; distinguish change from creep in the record; refuse informal scope additions
and route them to change control; report cumulative scope movement, not only individual changes.

**Required evidence.** The scope statement with exclusions and assumptions; the work or product
breakdown structure; the scope baseline version history; the change register entries for every
scope movement; the record of scope requests declined and the reason.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder leading delivery for maintaining the baseline; the change
authority or sponsor for any movement of it.

**Independent review.** Required at each gate, when cumulative approved change materially alters
the original scope, and where scope is defined across a contractual boundary between organisations.

**Escalation trigger.** Work being performed that no baseline authorises; a pattern of small
accommodations absorbed without change requests; a customer or stakeholder relying on scope the
baseline does not contain.

**Prohibited practice.** Accepting scope informally to preserve a relationship; describing new work
as clarification to avoid a change request; deleting scope quietly to protect a date; presenting a
scope baseline that no longer reflects agreed change.

**AI application.** AI may compare requirement, design and delivery artefacts against the scope
baseline, detect drift and unlisted work, draft scope statements and exclusions, and track
cumulative movement.

**AI restriction.** AI must not add, remove or reinterpret scope, decide that an item lies inside
the baseline, or approve a scope change.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-detected drift against the
controlled baseline and the change record, and must confirm that the published baseline matches the
sum of the original scope plus approved change.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including scope definition, control and the relationship between scope and deliverables. ISO 21500
(international standard) — context and concepts for project, programme and portfolio management,
within which scope is the agreed boundary of the temporary undertaking. PMI *A Guide to the Project
Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional guidance) — established practice on scope
baselines and scope validation. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Where scope is defined in a contract, its interpretation, the effect of
implied terms and the treatment of extra work are matters of the governing law; obtain contract-law
advice before treating a project scope baseline as the contractual scope.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PML-LAW-04-01; PML-LAW-05-02; PML-LAW-09-01; PML-LAW-13-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 5, KA 5.1 — Scope definition and the scope baseline (5.1.1 the

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-05-02 — Requirement Traceability

**Rule.** Every requirement a credential holder accepts into delivery shall be uniquely identified,
attributable to a named source, testable through stated acceptance criteria, and traceable forward
to the deliverable that satisfies it and the test that proves it; work shall not be accepted as
complete against a requirement that cannot be tested or traced.

**Purpose.** Traceability is what allows an organisation to prove it built what was asked for, to
find the consequences of a change, and to defend acceptance. Untestable requirements are disputes
waiting for a delivery date.

**Scope.** All credential holders accountable for requirements on projects and programmes, including
requirements expressed as user stories, service levels, regulatory obligations or performance
specifications, and requirements generated or refined with AI assistance.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a traceability matrix from source through requirement
to design, deliverable, test and acceptance; write or require acceptance criteria that state an
observable condition; record requirement changes with their impact; close the loop at acceptance by
demonstrating each requirement satisfied or formally waived.

**Required evidence.** The requirements register with unique identifiers and named sources; the
traceability matrix; acceptance criteria for each requirement; test and verification results;
records of waived or deferred requirements with the approving authority.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder leading delivery for traceability discipline; the sponsor,
product owner or customer authority for the acceptance or waiver of a requirement.

**Independent review.** Required before acceptance of a deliverable that carries regulatory, safety
or contractual requirements, and where requirements have been substantially generated or
consolidated by AI.

**Escalation trigger.** A requirement accepted into delivery without a testable criterion; a
delivered item with no traceable requirement; a requirement satisfied in test but not traceable to
its original source; requirements silently dropped between baselines.

**Prohibited practice.** Accepting vague or aspirational requirements into a baseline; writing
acceptance criteria after the deliverable to fit what was built; closing a requirement on assertion
rather than evidence; allowing AI-generated requirements into the baseline without a named human
source and review.

**AI application.** AI may elicit and draft candidate requirements, detect ambiguity, duplication
and untestable wording, propose acceptance criteria, and maintain and reconcile the traceability
matrix.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a requirement into the baseline, declare a requirement
satisfied, waive a requirement, or be recorded as the source of a requirement in place of a human
stakeholder.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm AI-drafted requirements and
criteria with the human stakeholder who owns the need, and must verify that every traceability link
reported as closed rests on test or verification evidence that has been examined.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including requirements and the definition of deliverables. ISO 9001 (international standard) — the
certifiable requirements standard for quality management systems, including the determination and
review of requirements for products and services. PMI *A Guide to the Project Management Body of
Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional guidance) — established practice on requirements
documentation and traceability. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Regulatory, safety-critical and accessibility requirements are set by
law in many jurisdictions and may carry mandatory evidence and certification regimes; obtain local
regulatory advice before treating project traceability records as regulatory compliance evidence.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-02; PCI-LAW-F-05; PML-LAW-05-01; PML-LAW-09-01; PML-LAW-16-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 5, KA 5.2 — Requirements elicitation, analysis and traceability

## Domain 6 — Planning, Scheduling and Delivery Flow

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-06-01 — Schedule Credibility

**Rule.** A credential holder shall lead, endorse and report only schedules that are logically
sound, resource-feasible and consistent with the scope and cost baselines, whose durations and
dates rest on a stated basis; a schedule known to be unachievable shall not be published, defended
or used as the basis of a commitment.

**Purpose.** A schedule is a commitment made on behalf of many people. A date produced by working
backwards from a required end, or held together by constraints and negative float, misinforms every
downstream decision and transfers the failure to the people who must deliver against it.

**Scope.** All credential holders accountable for planning and delivery flow on projects and
programmes, in predictive, adaptive and hybrid approaches, including forecasts derived from
iteration throughput.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Build schedules on stated logic rather than imposed dates;
record the basis of durations, calendars, resource assumptions and constraints; keep the critical
path visible and explicable; reconcile the schedule to the scope breakdown and the cost baseline;
state the confidence and range attached to a date rather than a single number alone; report
achievability honestly when the required date is not supported.

**Required evidence.** The schedule model with its logic, constraints and calendars; the basis of
schedule; the resource profile and any levelling assumptions; the critical-path analysis; the
baseline and its change history; forecast and scenario records.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder leading delivery owns schedule integrity; the sponsor or
governing body owns the acceptance of a date as a commitment.

**Independent review.** Required before a schedule becomes a baseline or an external commitment,
after significant compression or recovery replanning, and where an imposed date drives the plan
rather than the logic.

**Escalation trigger.** Instruction to publish a date the schedule does not support; widespread
negative float or constraint-driven dates masking infeasibility; a resource profile that cannot be
staffed; a recovery plan whose assumptions have already failed.

**Prohibited practice.** Removing logic or inserting constraints to produce a required date;
reporting progress against a superseded baseline without disclosure; compressing durations without
recording the cost, risk and quality consequence; presenting an AI-generated or vendor-generated
schedule as a plan without professional review.

**AI application.** AI may generate candidate networks, check logic and network quality, identify
resource conflicts, run scenario and compression analyses, and compare durations against
historical outturn data.

**AI restriction.** AI must not set or approve a delivery date, declare a schedule achievable,
authorise a baseline, or determine the acceptable level of schedule risk.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must independently test AI-produced logic,
durations and critical-path results against the delivery method and resource reality, and must
trace any published date to a schedule model a human has reviewed.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including schedule development, control and the relationship between schedule and other baselines.
AACE International, referenced for the existence and purpose of its total cost management treatment
of planning and scheduling within the cost-engineering discipline (professional guidance). PMI *A
Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional guidance) —
established practice on schedule development and control. The official publications govern; verify
current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Where a programme or schedule is a contract document, its status, the
effect of float ownership and the requirements for delay analysis are governed by the contract and
its applicable law; obtain contract-law advice before relying on a schedule for entitlement.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-04-01; PML-LAW-07-01; PML-LAW-13-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 6, KA 6.1 — Planning levels and logic networks (6.1.2 dependencies

## Domain 7 — Cost, Resources and Commercial Awareness

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-07-01 — Cost and Resource Stewardship

**Rule.** A credential holder shall steward the funds, people and assets entrusted to a project as
resources belonging to the organisation and its stakeholders, shall keep the cost baseline,
commitments, actuals and forecast reconcilable at all times, and shall not conceal overspend,
consume contingency without authority, or apply resources to purposes other than those approved.

**Purpose.** A delivery leader spends other people's money and other people's time. Stewardship
means the organisation can see, at any point, what has been committed, what has been consumed and
what remains — and that the answer does not change when someone asks a difficult question.

**Scope.** All credential holders accountable for budgets, commitments, resource assignment,
contingency drawdown or supplier expenditure on projects, programmes and portfolios.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a cost baseline reconciled to commitments and actuals
each reporting period; forecast to completion on a stated basis and revise it when evidence
changes; draw contingency only through the recorded authority and record the reason; account for
resource time honestly, including borrowed and shared resources; report an emerging overspend when
it emerges, not when it completes.

**Required evidence.** The cost baseline and its change history; the commitment register;
period reconciliations of budget, commitment, actual and forecast; contingency drawdown records
with authority; the resource plan and actual utilisation; the basis of the current forecast.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder leading delivery for budget management within delegation;
the sponsor or governing body for budget increases, contingency release above threshold and
reallocation between projects.

**Independent review.** Required when the forecast moves outside tolerance, before any request for
additional funding, when contingency consumption outpaces risk retirement, and at each gate.

**Escalation trigger.** A forecast overspend beyond delegated tolerance; contingency drawn without
authority or to cover scope growth rather than risk; commitments made outside the approved budget;
resource assumptions in the plan that the organisation cannot supply.

**Prohibited practice.** Delaying disclosure of a known overspend to a later report; moving cost
between control accounts, periods or projects to obscure a variance; using contingency as a
discretionary budget; booking project resource time to another cost centre to protect a position.

**AI application.** AI may reconcile commitments to actuals, detect anomalous or duplicated
transactions, produce forecast ranges from performance data, model resource scenarios and cash
profiles, and flag contingency consumption against risk retirement.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve expenditure, release contingency, set the forecast used for
governance reporting, or determine that a variance need not be reported.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-generated reconciliations and
forecasts against source cost records and the risk register, and must be able to explain the
movement between the previous forecast and the current one.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Capitalisation, cost allocation, grant eligibility and tax treatment of

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-03-02; PML-LAW-04-01; PML-LAW-08-01.

## Domain 8 — Risk, Uncertainty and Resilience

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-08-01 — Risk Escalation

**Rule.** A credential holder shall escalate, without delay and in writing, any risk or issue whose
potential consequence exceeds the tolerance delegated to them — including risks to safety, legal
compliance, benefits, solvency, reputation or public interest — and shall not suppress, downgrade,
re-score or defer a risk to keep it within their own authority or to protect a report.

**Purpose.** Escalation is not an admission of failure; it is the mechanism by which an organisation
places a decision where the authority to take it sits. Risks that are managed quietly below the
threshold are risks the organisation never chose to accept.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading or supporting delivery, for threats and opportunities
arising from any source — technical, commercial, resource, external, behavioural or AI-related —
in any delivery approach.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a risk register with owners, current assessment,
responses and tolerance thresholds; assess against the delegated tolerance rather than personal
comfort; escalate in writing with the facts, the assessment, the options and a recommendation;
record the receiving authority's decision, including a decision to accept; re-escalate when an
escalated risk is not decided.

**Required evidence.** The risk and issue registers with assessment history; the tolerance
thresholds and their source; escalation records with dates, recipients and recommendations; the
deciding authority's response; the contingency and reserve position linked to retained risk.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for risks within tolerance; the sponsor or governing body
for risks above it; the risk owner named in the register for the response.

**Independent review.** Required for any risk that could threaten life, statutory compliance, the
business case or the organisation's standing, for quantitative risk analyses used to size
contingency, and whenever an escalated risk is accepted rather than treated.

**Escalation trigger.** Any risk exceeding the delegated tolerance; a material issue realised; an
escalation not acknowledged or decided within the stated response time; pressure to lower a score
or remove an entry from the register.

**Prohibited practice.** Re-scoring a risk downward without new evidence; splitting a risk to keep
each part below the threshold; describing a realised issue as a risk to defer disclosure; escalating
orally only, so that no record exists of the warning.

**AI application.** AI may scan delivery, supplier and external data for emerging risk signals,
propose register entries, model aggregate exposure and contingency adequacy, and track escalation
ageing and response times.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide that a risk falls within tolerance, accept a risk on the
organisation's behalf, set contingency, or determine that escalation is unnecessary.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must test AI-generated risk signals and
quantitative outputs against evidence and expert judgement before they enter the register or a
governance report, and must confirm that risks flagged for escalation were in fact escalated.

**External references.** ISO 31000 (international standard) — risk management principles and
guidelines, including risk criteria, treatment and the escalation of risk beyond an established
appetite. ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, including risk
management within the delivery lifecycle. ISO/IEC 23894 (international standard) — guidance on risk
management for artificial intelligence, relevant where AI systems are themselves a source of
delivery risk. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Duties to report safety, environmental, financial-crime, data-breach and
consumer risks to regulators or authorities are set by law and may carry personal liability and
strict time limits; obtain local legal advice on mandatory reporting obligations, which override
internal escalation routes.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-11; PCI-LAW-F-07; PML-LAW-03-02; PML-LAW-07-01; PML-LAW-11-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 8, KA 8.1 — Threats, opportunities and identification (8.1.2

## Domain 9 — Quality, Assurance and Continuous Improvement

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-09-01 — Quality Acceptance

**Rule.** A credential holder shall accept a deliverable only against its defined acceptance
criteria, on evidence of verification, and with any nonconformance formally dispositioned by the
authority entitled to decide it; acceptance shall not be granted on schedule pressure, partial
evidence, verbal assurance or an intention to remedy later without a recorded and owned commitment.

**Purpose.** Acceptance is the moment quality either holds or is quietly traded away. A deliverable
accepted without evidence transfers unresolved defects to operations, to users and often to safety,
where the cost of correction is highest and the leader is no longer present.

**Scope.** All credential holders who accept, recommend acceptance of, or rely on the acceptance of
deliverables, increments, services or works, from internal teams or suppliers, in any delivery
approach.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Ensure acceptance criteria exist and are testable before work
starts; require verification evidence proportionate to consequence; record every nonconformance and
its disposition — accept, rework, repair, regrade or reject — with the deciding authority; track
concessions and deferred remediation to closure; keep acceptance decisions separate from the
production of the work being accepted.

**Required evidence.** Acceptance criteria; inspection, test and verification records; the
nonconformance register with dispositions and authorities; concession and waiver records with owners
and dates; the signed acceptance record naming the accepting authority.

**Decision owner.** The named accepting authority — customer, sponsor, product owner or technical
authority — for the acceptance decision; the credential holder for ensuring the process is followed
and the evidence is genuine.

**Independent review.** Required for acceptance carrying safety, regulatory, security or
irreversible consequences, for acceptance under concession, and where the accepting authority sits
inside the delivering organisation.

**Escalation trigger.** Pressure to accept without evidence or before completion; a nonconformance
dispositioned by someone without authority; a growing backlog of deferred remediation; acceptance
criteria altered after the deliverable was produced.

**Prohibited practice.** Accepting to preserve a milestone date; splitting a deliverable so the
defective part is not the part accepted; recording verification that did not occur; permitting the
producing team to accept its own work where the process requires an independent authority.

**AI application.** AI may check evidence packs against acceptance criteria, detect missing or
inconsistent test records, classify and cluster nonconformances, and support root-cause analysis.

**AI restriction.** AI must not accept a deliverable, disposition a nonconformance, grant a
concession, or certify that acceptance criteria have been met.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify that AI-assessed evidence
corresponds to actual inspection and test records, and must confirm that every criterion reported as
satisfied is supported by evidence a human has examined.

**External references.** ISO 9001 (international standard) — the certifiable requirements standard
for quality management systems, including control of nonconforming outputs and the release of
products and services. ISO 9000 (international standard) — quality management fundamentals and
vocabulary, which fixes the meaning of terms such as nonconformity and concession. ISO 10006
(international standard) — quality management in projects, addressing quality processes within a
project context. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory certification, building control, type approval, clinical or
product-safety regimes may impose their own acceptance and evidence requirements and reserve the
decision to a licensed person; obtain local regulatory advice before treating project acceptance as

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-09-02 — Organisational Learning

**Rule.** A credential holder shall capture, record and transfer the lessons arising from delivery —
including failures, near misses and the lessons that reflect poorly on the leader — in a form that
another team can act on, and shall not close a project, suppress a review finding, or repeat a known
failure without recording it.

**Purpose.** An organisation that does not learn pays for the same failure repeatedly, and each
repetition is more expensive than the review that would have prevented it. Lessons that only
flatter, or that sit unread in a closed folder, are not learning; they are administration.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading projects and programmes, at every stage — not only at
closure — including delivery through partners, where lessons cross organisational boundaries.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Run reviews during delivery as well as at closure; record each
lesson with its context, cause, consequence and the specific action or standard change it implies;
assign an owner for the change the lesson requires; place lessons where the next team will
encounter them, not only in a repository; check the lesson store before planning comparable work.

**Required evidence.** Review records including participants and dissenting views; the lessons
register with causes, actions and owners; evidence that lessons changed a process, standard,
estimate or template; the search of prior lessons undertaken during planning.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for capture and honesty; the process, capability or PMO
owner for adopting the change a lesson requires.

**Independent review.** Required after a significant failure, a safety or security event, a
terminated project, or where the review's findings implicate the leader conducting it.

**Escalation trigger.** A review prevented, curtailed or edited to remove uncomfortable findings; a
recurring failure already recorded as a lesson; lessons closed without any change to practice.

**Prohibited practice.** Recording only successes; anonymising a lesson so that no cause can be
identified; attributing systemic failure to individuals to close a review; deferring the review
until the team has dispersed.

**AI application.** AI may cluster lessons across projects, surface prior lessons relevant to a
current plan, detect recurrence patterns in defect and delay data, and draft review summaries.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine the cause of a failure, decide what may be recorded, close
a lesson, or replace the participation of the people who did the work.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-generated cause clusters and
summaries against the underlying records and participant testimony, and must confirm that summaries
have not removed adverse findings.

**External references.** ISO 9001 (international standard) — the certifiable requirements standard
for quality management systems, including corrective action and continual improvement. ISO 10006
(international standard) — quality management in projects, including learning within and across
projects. ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, including the
capture and use of lessons across the lifecycle. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Investigation records, safety reports and post-incident reviews may be
subject to disclosure obligations, legal privilege rules or protected-reporting regimes that differ
by jurisdiction; obtain local legal advice before recording or circulating findings concerning an
incident.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-12; PCI-LAW-F-13; PML-LAW-09-01; PML-LAW-12-02;
PML-LAW-16-02.

## Domain 10 — Procurement, Contracts and Supply Networks

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-10-01 — Procurement Fairness

**Rule.** A credential holder involved in procurement shall treat every bidder equally, apply only
the evaluation criteria and weightings published before bids were received, protect the
confidentiality of bid information, and declare any interest that could affect impartiality; the
evaluation model shall not be changed after bids are opened to favour or exclude a party.

**Purpose.** Procurement decisions commit large sums and confer advantage. Fairness is not only an
ethical obligation but the source of the competitive tension that produces value; a process
believed to be steered destroys both the outcome and the organisation's ability to attract bidders
in future.

**Scope.** All credential holders who specify, market-test, evaluate, negotiate, recommend or award
contracts, and those who influence such decisions through requirements, scoring or supplier
relationships, in public and private settings.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Fix requirements, criteria, weightings and scoring rules
before issuing the invitation; give all bidders the same information at the same time; record
scores with reasoned justification; declare interests and step aside where impartiality could
reasonably be doubted; keep bid information confidential and separate from any competing internal
bid.

**Required evidence.** The published evaluation model with weightings; clarification questions and
answers issued to all bidders; individual and consensus score sheets with reasoning; the conflict
register and declarations; the award recommendation and approval record; the debrief record.

**Decision owner.** The award authority or governing body for the award decision; the credential
holder for process integrity and for the accuracy of the evaluation record.

**Independent review.** Required for high-value or strategically significant awards, for
single-source or direct awards, where an incumbent supplier assisted in specification, and where
any evaluator has declared an interest.

**Escalation trigger.** Pressure to adjust scores, criteria or weightings after bids are received;
disclosure of one bidder's information to another; an undeclared relationship between an evaluator
and a bidder; an award recommendation that the evaluation record does not support.

**Prohibited practice.** Writing a specification around a preferred supplier's product without
disclosure; running a competition where the outcome is already decided; accepting hospitality,
employment discussion or benefit from a bidder during a live procurement; sharing pricing between
bidders.

**AI application.** AI may screen bids for completeness and compliance, check arithmetic and
normalise pricing, summarise responses against criteria, and detect scoring inconsistency or
unexplained divergence between evaluators.

**AI restriction.** AI must not score qualitative bid content as the decision of record, select a
winner, exclude a bidder, or approve an award recommendation.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must review AI-produced summaries against the
bidders' actual submissions before scoring, and must confirm that each score rests on reasoning
recorded by a named human evaluator.

**External references.** FIDIC suite of standard forms of contract (contract framework) — the
tendering and contract structures within which fair evaluation and award operate in engineering and
construction. NEC suite of contracts (contract framework) — a collaborative contracting approach
whose procedures depend on transparent and consistent commercial administration. ISO 21502
(international standard) — guidance on project management, including procurement within project
delivery. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Public procurement is regulated in most jurisdictions, with mandatory
notice, standstill, transparency and remedy provisions, and competition, anti-bribery and

or altering a competitive process.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-09; PCI-LAW-F-10; PCI-LAW-F-13; PML-LAW-03-02; PML-LAW-11-01.

## Domain 11 — Stakeholders, Communication and Influence

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-11-01 — Stakeholder Transparency

**Rule.** A credential holder shall report delivery status, forecasts and risks to stakeholders
truthfully, completely enough for their decisions, and in time for those decisions to be made;
status shall not be softened, aggregated, delayed or differently framed for different audiences in
order to manage impressions rather than inform judgement.

**Purpose.** Stakeholders act on what they are told. A report that conceals a slipping date or a
failing assumption does not buy time; it removes the organisation's opportunity to respond while
response is still cheap, and it destroys the leader's credibility precisely when it is needed.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare, approve, present or distribute status reports,
dashboards, board papers, assurance summaries or public communications relating to delivery.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Report against the approved baseline with the date and age of
the data stated; present forecast as a range with its basis where uncertainty is material; disclose
material risks, issues and failed assumptions in the same report as performance; keep one version
of the truth across audiences, tailoring depth and language but never substance; correct a report
found to be materially wrong, to the same audience that received it.

**Required evidence.** Issued reports with data dates and baselines identified; the underlying data
extracts; the risk and issue positions reported; correction notices; the distribution record showing
who received what and when.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder leading delivery owns the accuracy of the report; the
sponsor or governing body owns onward communication decisions within the organisation.

**Independent review.** Required for reports supporting investment, gate or public decisions, where
a report contradicts the assurance opinion, and where reporting is substantially automated or
AI-generated.

**Escalation trigger.** Instruction to alter, remove or delay adverse content; discovery that a
distributed report was materially wrong; a stakeholder making a decision on information the leader
knows to be superseded.

**Prohibited practice.** Reporting a status colour that the underlying data does not support;
presenting a milestone as achieved when only part of it was; giving a governing body a different
substantive picture from the delivery team's; using aggregation to make a failing component
invisible.

**AI application.** AI may assemble reports from delivery data, summarise for different audiences,
check consistency between narrative and data, flag stale data, and translate communications for
multilingual stakeholders.

**AI restriction.** AI must not set a reported status, decide what is material enough to disclose,
approve a report for issue, or generate stakeholder communications that are issued without human
review.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-generated reports and
summaries against the source data and the risk position before issue, must confirm that the
narrative matches the numbers, and must check translated or audience-tailored versions for
substantive equivalence.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including stakeholder engagement and the reporting of project performance. ISO 21505 (international
standard) — governance of projects, programmes and portfolios, in which transparent information
flow to the governing body is a governance condition. PMI *A Guide to the Project Management Body
of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional guidance) — established practice on communications
management and performance reporting. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Disclosure to markets, regulators, funders and the public may be
governed by securities law, freedom-of-information regimes, funding conditions and consultation
duties; obtain local legal advice before making or withholding an external statement about delivery
status.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-04; PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-08-01; PML-LAW-03-03;
PML-LAW-14-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 11, KA 11.2 — Executive communication and reporting (11.2.2 the

## Domain 12 — Leadership, Teams and Organisational Behaviour

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-12-01 — Leadership Conduct

**Rule.** A credential holder shall lead by the standard they impose on others: honest in
communication, fair in the allocation of work, credit and blame, respectful of the dignity and
safety of every person affected by the project, and consistent between what they say in public and
what they instruct in private.

**Purpose.** A delivery leader's conduct sets the behavioural baseline for everyone on the project.
Teams comply with what leaders tolerate and reward, not with what policies state; a leader who
takes credit, shifts blame or applies rules selectively creates the conditions in which every other
law in this set is quietly broken.

**Scope.** All credential holders exercising leadership over people, whether employed, contracted,
seconded, supplied or voluntary, and in all interactions where they represent the project or PCI.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Apply the same standard to oneself as to the team; attribute
work accurately; address performance and conduct issues directly and privately rather than through
proxies or public criticism; refuse instructions that require others to act unethically, and record
the refusal; protect the wellbeing and safety of people under delivery pressure; keep private
instructions consistent with public statements.

**Required evidence.** Records of decisions affecting individuals with their stated basis; the team
charter or agreed ways of working; recorded refusals of improper instructions; grievance,
whistleblowing and duty-of-care records handled through proper channels; recognition and attribution
records.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder personally, for their own conduct; the employing
organisation's people or ethics function for formal conduct proceedings.

**Independent review.** Required where a conduct concern is raised about the leader, where delivery
pressure is producing sustained excessive hours or unsafe practice, and where a person reports
retaliation.

**Escalation trigger.** An instruction to mislead, coerce, discriminate against or endanger any
person; a pattern of excessive working hours or unsafe practice on the project; an allegation of
retaliation against someone who raised a concern.

**Prohibited practice.** Claiming another person's work; blaming subordinates for decisions the
leader took; applying rules to some team members and not others; using AI-generated content to
communicate a message about a person that the leader has not personally reviewed and stands behind;
retaliating against a person who escalates.

**AI application.** AI may help prepare and rehearse difficult conversations, check written
communication for unclear or intemperate wording, and analyse anonymous, consented team-climate
data for patterns.

**AI restriction.** AI must not assess, rank or discipline individuals, generate performance
judgements used as the basis of a decision about a person, or issue communications about a person's
conduct or employment without human authorship and review.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must review any AI-assisted communication
concerning individuals before it is sent, must confirm that people-related analytics are consented
and lawful, and must never rely on a machine-generated judgement about a person without independent
human assessment.

**External references.** PMI *Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct* (professional guidance) —
responsibility, respect, fairness and honesty as professional obligations of project practitioners.
ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, including the leadership and
development of project teams. ISO 45001 (international standard) — occupational health and safety
management systems, within which working conditions on a project are managed. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Employment, equality, working-time, health-and-safety and
whistleblower-protection law varies widely and takes precedence over any project practice; obtain
local employment-law advice before acting on a conduct, performance or working-hours matter.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-13; PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-10; PML-LAW-12-02; PML-LAW-11-01.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-12-02 — Psychological Safety

**Rule.** A credential holder shall maintain conditions in which any team member can report bad
news, error, risk, dissent or concern without penalty, shall respond to such reports on their
substance, and shall not permit or practise retaliation, ridicule or exclusion against a person who
raises one.

**Purpose.** Every control in this set depends on information reaching the leader while there is
still time to act. Where people learn that raising a problem is punished, the reporting stops before
the problem does, and the leader is the last person on the project to know.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading teams, including distributed, multicultural, seconded and
supplier teams, and including reports raised anonymously or through third parties.

**Minimum professional requirement.** State explicitly how concerns are raised and what happens
next; respond to raised concerns with a recorded outcome; separate the reporting of a problem from
any assessment of the person reporting it; model the behaviour by reporting one's own errors and
uncertainty; watch for the practical signals of suppressed reporting — late surprises, unreported
overruns, silence in review meetings — and treat them as evidence of a safety failure.

**Required evidence.** The stated concern-raising route; the log of concerns raised and the recorded
response; retrospective and review records showing dissent captured; team-climate indicators where
lawfully and consensually collected; records of action taken on reported issues.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for the conditions within their team; the employing
organisation's designated function for formal whistleblowing and grievance matters.

**Independent review.** Required where retaliation is alleged, where a serious problem was known at
working level but not escalated, and where an anonymous route is the only one being used.

**Escalation trigger.** Any act of retaliation or exclusion following a report; a serious issue that
surfaces only after it becomes unavoidable; a person removed from a role shortly after raising a
concern.

**Prohibited practice.** Punishing the messenger; treating dissent recorded in a review as
disloyalty; requiring problems to be raised only through a line manager who is their cause;
labelling honest error as incompetence to close a discussion; using monitoring or analytics to
identify and target people who report concerns.

**AI application.** AI may support anonymised and consented sentiment or climate analysis, summarise
themes from retrospectives, and track whether raised concerns received a recorded response.

**AI restriction.** AI must not identify individuals from anonymous reports, score people on
attitude, dissent or engagement, or determine the outcome of a concern.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm that any people-analytics use is
consented, lawful and incapable of re-identifying anonymous reporters, and must test the concern log
periodically to confirm reports received a substantive human response.

**External references.** ISO 45003 (international standard) — guidelines on managing psychosocial
risk within occupational health and safety management, addressing the work conditions this law
depends on. ISO 45001 (international standard) — occupational health and safety management systems.
ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, including team development and
the working environment. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Whistleblower protection, works-council consultation, employee
monitoring and data-protection law govern how concerns and team data may be handled and vary
significantly by jurisdiction; obtain local legal advice before establishing monitoring, anonymous
reporting or investigation processes.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-11; PCI-LAW-F-13; PML-LAW-12-01; PML-LAW-08-01; PML-LAW-09-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 12, KA 12.2 — Motivation, team formation and psychological safety

## Domain 13 — Agile, Adaptive and Hybrid Delivery

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-13-01 — Governance of Adaptive Delivery

**Rule.** A credential holder using adaptive or hybrid delivery shall govern it on evidence of
working outcomes — a controlled envelope of cost, time and quality, an accountable product decision
right, a defined increment and honest flow measures — and shall not present iterative working as a
reason to dispense with baselines, decision records, acceptance criteria or escalation.

**Purpose.** Adaptive delivery changes what is fixed, not whether anyone is accountable. Where
"agile" is used to explain the absence of a plan, a scope boundary or a record, the organisation
loses both the discipline of predictive control and the feedback that adaptive working was supposed
to provide.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading or governing adaptive, iterative or hybrid delivery,
including scaled arrangements, adaptive work inside a predictive programme, and adaptive work
delivered under contract.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Fix the envelope — funding, time-box, quality and the outcome
sought — where scope is variable; name a single accountable product decision maker; maintain a
definition of done and acceptance criteria per increment; measure flow honestly and forecast from
observed throughput rather than aspiration; match governance cadence to iteration length so that
decisions do not become the constraint; report to predictive governance bodies in terms they can
act on without misrepresenting progress.

**Required evidence.** The delivery approach statement and its tailoring rationale; the controlled
envelope and its authority; the product decision-right record; the backlog with prioritisation
basis; the definition of done; flow and throughput data; increment acceptance records; the decision
log.

**Decision owner.** The named product decision maker for scope and priority within the envelope; the
governing body for the envelope itself and for any change to it; the credential holder for the
integrity of the approach.

**Independent review.** Required when adaptive delivery operates inside a fixed-price or
fixed-scope contract, when scaling across multiple teams, and where flow metrics are used to
support funding decisions.

**Escalation trigger.** Scope expanding beyond the envelope without decision; a product decision
right held by a committee or vacant; governance decision latency exceeding the iteration length; a
velocity or burn-down figure being reported as progress towards an outcome it does not measure.

**Prohibited practice.** Using iterative delivery to avoid recording decisions; declaring an
increment done without meeting the definition of done; manipulating story sizing, velocity or
work-in-progress limits to produce a favourable metric; contracting for fixed scope, fixed price and
fixed time while calling the delivery adaptive.

**AI application.** AI may forecast completion from throughput history, detect flow anomalies and
ageing work items, draft and refine backlog items, and prepare governance reporting that translates
flow data for a predictive audience.

**AI restriction.** AI must not own the product decision right, prioritise the backlog as the
decision of record, declare an increment done, or set the delivery envelope.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-generated forecasts against
actual throughput and known constraints, and must confirm that reported flow metrics correspond to
accepted, working outcomes rather than counted activity.

**External references.** *The Scrum Guide*, Schwaber and Sutherland (industry practice) — defines
one widely used iterative framework, including a single accountable product decision role and the
notion of a done increment. PMI *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)*
(professional guidance) — established practice on tailoring delivery approaches and on hybrid
governance. ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, which applies
across predictive, adaptive and hybrid delivery. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Adaptive delivery under public procurement, regulated change control or
capitalisation rules may face constraints on how scope, acceptance and expenditure are evidenced;
obtain local procurement, accounting and legal advice before adopting an adaptive contract model.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PML-LAW-03-01; PML-LAW-03-02; PML-LAW-05-01; PML-LAW-06-01;
PML-LAW-11-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 13, KA 13.3 — Scaling considerations and hybrid governance (13.3.2
governance latency against iteration length; 13.3.3 designing hybrid delivery honestly; 13.3.4
reporting flow to a predictive governance body); KA 13.1 — Agile principles and product ownership

## Domain 14 — Digital Delivery, Data and Responsible AI

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-14-01 — Responsible Data Use

**Rule.** A credential holder shall use project, personal and commercial data only for purposes for
which it was lawfully and knowingly obtained, shall keep the delivery information environment
controlled — with defined ownership, access, quality standards, security and retention — and shall
not disclose confidential or personal data to any system, supplier or tool, including an AI service,
without an authorised basis for doing so.

**Purpose.** Delivery now runs on shared information environments and third-party services, and a
single careless upload can breach confidence, contract or privacy law irreversibly. Data quality and
data protection are delivery constraints, not administrative overheads: decisions made on
uncontrolled data are unsafe even when nothing leaks.

**Scope.** All credential holders who collect, hold, share, publish or process delivery data —
including schedule, cost, supplier, workforce, safety, personal and model-training data — in common
data environments, dashboards, analytics platforms and AI services.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Establish named ownership and access control for each data
set; use the project's authorised information environment rather than personal or unapproved
services; check the lawful and contractual basis before sharing data with any external system;
apply the organisation's classification, minimisation and retention rules; state the quality
limitations of data used for a decision; secure the environment against the loss, alteration or
exposure of delivery records.

**Required evidence.** The information-management plan and data ownership record; access control and
classification records; approvals for external data sharing, including AI service use; data-quality
assessments for decision-critical data sets; retention and disposal records; incident records.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for delivery data within the project; the organisation's
information, security, privacy or legal function for the lawful basis, classification and approval
of external processing.

**Independent review.** Required before introducing a new data-sharing arrangement or external
service, before processing personal or safety-critical data at scale, and following any suspected
breach or loss of data integrity.

**Escalation trigger.** Confidential or personal data placed in an unapproved system; a data set
used for a decision whose quality is unknown; a supplier or tool retaining project data beyond the agreed
purpose or period; any suspected breach, loss or unauthorised alteration.

**Prohibited practice.** Pasting confidential, personal or commercially sensitive material into an
unapproved AI service or consumer tool; keeping shadow copies of project data outside the controlled
environment; presenting analysis from a data set known to be incomplete or stale without saying so;
retaining personal data because it may be useful later.

**AI application.** AI may profile data quality, detect duplicates, gaps and anomalies, classify
records, monitor access patterns for irregularity, and support the mapping of data flows.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine the lawful basis for processing, authorise data sharing or
disclosure, set retention rules, or classify data as non-sensitive for release purposes.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-produced data-quality and
classification outputs against sampled records, and must confirm before any external transfer that a
human-approved basis exists for the specific data and the specific recipient.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 27001 (international standard) — information-security management
systems. ISO/IEC 27701 (international standard) — privacy information management extending an
information-security management system. ISO 8000 (international standard) — data quality. ISO/IEC
25012 (international standard) — a data-quality model for software and systems. ISO 19650
(international standard) — organisation and digitisation of information about buildings and civil
engineering works, including the common data environment concept. The European Union General Data
Protection Regulation is named as an example of a rights-based data-protection regime, binding only
within its own jurisdiction. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Data-protection, data-localisation, confidentiality, state-secrecy and
sector-specific data rules differ sharply between jurisdictions and apply to cross-border transfers
including those made by AI services; obtain local legal advice before transferring, storing or
processing data outside the approved environment.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-14-02 — Responsible AI in Delivery

**Rule.** A credential holder shall govern the use of AI in delivery as a managed capability:
recorded use, stated purpose and limits, verified outputs proportionate to consequence, disclosed
material AI contribution, and attention to bias, explainability and failure modes; AI output shall
not be relied upon, issued or acted on as fact until a competent professional has verified it.

**Purpose.** AI is now embedded in estimating, scheduling, reporting, requirements, evaluation and
communication, and its most dangerous output is not an obvious error but a plausible wrong number
delivered with fluency. Governed use captures the benefit; ungoverned use imports invisible risk
into every decision the tool touches.

**Scope.** All credential holders using, commissioning, procuring or governing AI systems in
delivery — including general-purpose assistants, embedded vendor features, agents and predictive
models — and all outputs those systems produce for professional use.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain an AI use register recording each material use, its
purpose, its data, its human owner and its verification method; set verification effort
proportionate to the consequence of being wrong; ground outputs in identified sources and check
citations, arithmetic and assumptions; disclose material AI contribution where a recipient's
reliance depends on it; assess outputs affecting people or selection for bias and differential
error; know and state what the system cannot do.

**Required evidence.** The AI use register; prompts, sources and outputs retained for
decision-relevant uses; verification records naming the verifier and what was checked; disclosure
statements; bias or impact assessments where AI affects people; supplier assurance for embedded AI
features.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder using the output remains the decision owner; the
organisation's AI governance function for approval of the system, its data and its permitted uses.

**Independent review.** Required before AI is used in a decision-critical or people-affecting
process, when an AI system is materially changed or updated, and after any incident in which
AI-derived information proved wrong.

**Escalation trigger.** AI output found to be materially wrong after issue; an AI system used
outside its registered purpose; systematic differential error affecting a group; a supplier unable
to explain the basis of an AI-derived output relied on for a decision.

**Prohibited practice.** Issuing AI output as one's own analysis without verification; using AI in a
decision-critical process without registration or human ownership; concealing material AI
contribution from a recipient who is relying on the work; accepting an unexplainable output as the
basis of a decision that requires explanation; using AI-generated communications, images or evidence
in a way that misleads about their origin.

**AI application.** AI may draft, analyse, summarise, model, translate, monitor, check consistency
and generate options across the delivery lifecycle, wherever the other laws in this set permit it.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide, approve, accept, certify, evaluate people, or be recorded as
the source of professional judgement; no configuration or supplier assurance removes the credential
holder's obligation to verify and to remain accountable.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI output against independent
sources or method before professional use, at an effort proportionate to consequence; the
verification, the verifier and the date must be recorded for decision-relevant uses; unverified
output must be labelled as such and must not enter a governance report or a decision paper.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 42001 (international standard) — artificial-intelligence management
systems, including governance, roles and oversight of AI use. ISO/IEC 23894 (international
standard) — guidance on risk management for artificial intelligence. ISO/IEC 38507 (international
standard) — governance implications for a governing body of an organisation's use of AI. NIST AI
Risk Management Framework, United States National Institute of Standards and Technology (voluntary
framework) — a function-based approach to identifying and managing AI risk; not a standard and not a
regulation. OECD AI Principles (voluntary framework) — human-centred values, transparency,
robustness and accountability; not legislation. The European Union AI Act is named as an example of
a risk-tiered regulatory approach, binding only within its own jurisdiction. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** AI regulation is developing rapidly and differs by jurisdiction and by
use case, with stricter duties where AI affects employment, credit, safety, essential services or
fundamental rights; obtain local legal advice on the classification and obligations attaching to a
specific AI use, and verify current requirements before deployment.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-02; PCI-LAW-F-03; PCI-LAW-F-04; PCI-LAW-F-06;
PML-LAW-01-02; PML-LAW-14-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 14, KA 14.3 — AI use across the lifecycle, prompting and
verification (14.3.2 prompting, grounding and provenance; 14.3.3 verification-effort economics;
14.3.4 the plausible wrong number); KA 14.4 — Explainability, bias, human accountability (14.4.1
explainability as a decision requirement; 14.4.2 bias and differential error; 14.4.3 human
accountability and the AI use register); Domain 1, KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI;
Domain 9, KA 9.4 — AI-output quality and the verification that earns its cost.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items containing fluent but defective AI output — invented
sources, wrong arithmetic, misapplied method, biased selection — for the candidate to detect,
verify, disclose and handle. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 16 — Transition, Closeout and Benefits Realisation

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-16-01 — Transition Readiness

**Rule.** A credential holder shall recommend or authorise transition into live operation only when
every defined readiness condition is satisfied and evidenced — the product, the receiving
organisation, its people, data, support arrangements and the reversion route — and shall not present
a go-live as ready when any condition is unmet, deferred without owner or evidenced only by
assertion.

**Purpose.** Readiness is a conjunction: the deliverable can be technically complete while the
people, data or support are not, and the failure lands on users, patients, customers or the public.
A go-live decision taken to protect a date is a decision to transfer risk to those least able to
carry it.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading or advising on handover, commissioning, service
acceptance, go-live, cutover and operational transition, including phased, parallel and staged transitions.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Define readiness conditions and their evidence before the
decision point; test the receiving organisation's readiness — training, capacity, processes, data
migration, support and hypercare — not only the product's; hold a documented go/no-go decision with
a named authority; maintain a tested reversion or contingency route; state honestly any condition
being waived, with its owner, its risk and the authority accepting it.

**Required evidence.** The readiness criteria and assessment against each; commissioning, test and
data-migration records; training and competence records for operational users; the support and
hypercare arrangement; the go/no-go decision record; the reversion plan and evidence it was tested.

**Decision owner.** The receiving operational authority and the sponsor jointly take the go-live
decision; the credential holder is accountable for the completeness and honesty of the readiness
assessment placed before them.

**Independent review.** Required for transitions affecting safety, essential services, regulated
processes or the public, for irreversible cutovers, and where readiness is being declared with
conditions waived.

**Escalation trigger.** Pressure to go live with unmet readiness conditions; a reversion route that
does not exist or has never been tested; the receiving organisation stating it is not ready;
readiness evidence that rests on assertion rather than test.

**Prohibited practice.** Declaring readiness on planned rather than completed activity; transferring
an unresolved defect backlog into operations without disclosure and ownership; treating training
delivered as competence achieved; going live without a decision record naming the authority that
decided.

**AI application.** AI may consolidate readiness evidence, check completeness against criteria,
model cutover sequences and dependencies, monitor early operational signals during hypercare, and
track open defects into operational ownership.

**AI restriction.** AI must not declare readiness, take or approve the go-live decision, waive a
readiness condition, or determine that a reversion route is adequate.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-consolidated readiness
evidence against the underlying test, training and migration records, and must confirm personally
that any condition reported as satisfied has evidence behind it.

**External references.** ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management,
including transition of deliverables into use and project closure. ISO 9001 (international
standard) — the certifiable requirements standard for quality management systems, including the
release of products and services and the control of nonconforming output. ISO 10006 (international
standard) — quality management in projects. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory commissioning, safety-case acceptance, clinical or
building-occupation approvals and regulatory notifications may be preconditions to lawful operation
and can only be granted by the designated authority; obtain local regulatory and legal advice before
authorising entry into service.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-11; PML-LAW-03-03; PML-LAW-09-01; PML-LAW-05-02;
PML-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 16, KA 16.1 — Handover, commissioning and readiness (16.1.2
commissioning and service acceptance; 16.1.3 readiness as a conjunction of conditions; 16.1.4 the
go-live decision, hypercare and reversion); KA 16.2 — Operational transition and contract closeout;
KA 16.3 — Transferring knowledge to the run organisation; Domain 9, KA 9.3 — The acceptance
decision.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a go-live date is defended against incomplete
readiness — untested migration, untrained users, no reversion route — testing whether the candidate
can assemble an honest readiness position and route the decision. No live examination content is
exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PML-LAW-16-02 — Benefits Measurement

**Rule.** A credential holder shall ensure that the benefits used to justify an investment are
measured after delivery against their recorded baselines, on the stated measures and dates, with
attribution treated honestly; benefits shall not be reported as realised on the basis of outputs
delivered, forecasts restated as results, or comparisons chosen after the outcome is known.

**Purpose.** The claim that closed the business case must eventually be tested, or the organisation
learns nothing about the value of its investments and keeps funding the cases that promise most
rather than those that deliver. Measurement after the fact is also what makes the original claim
honest.

**Scope.** All credential holders leading projects, programmes and portfolios whose approval rested
on benefit claims, and those advising on post-implementation review, benefits reporting and the
retention of the records that support it.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a benefits measurement plan naming the measure,
source, baseline, method, date and owner for each benefit; ensure measurement continues after the
project organisation closes; state attribution and the comparison used, including known confounding
factors; report shortfalls and disbenefits with the same prominence as achievements; retain the
data, models and — where AI contributed materially to a decision — the record of that contribution,
for the period required.

**Required evidence.** The benefits measurement plan; pre-change baseline measurements; post-change
measurement results with method and comparison stated; the variance analysis against the business
case; the archive of decision, model and data records supporting the measurements; the retention
schedule applied.

**Decision owner.** The named benefit owner in the receiving organisation for realisation and
measurement; the sponsor for accepting and reporting the realised position; the credential holder
for establishing the plan and handing it over intact.

**Independent review.** Required where reported benefits support further investment, external
reporting, funding conditions or incentive payments, and where measured benefits diverge materially
from the business case.

**Escalation trigger.** Measurement quietly abandoned after closure; a benefit reported as realised
without a measurement against a baseline; a comparison or period selected after results were known;
records needed for measurement destroyed or made inaccessible.

**Prohibited practice.** Claiming realisation from output delivery; changing the measure, baseline
or date to produce a favourable result; attributing to the project outcomes caused by unrelated
factors; deleting an unfavourable benefit line rather than reporting it; disposing of supporting
data before the retention period expires.

**AI application.** AI may collect and reconcile measurement data, test for confounding factors and
seasonality, compare outturn against the business-case profile, and maintain the archive index and
retention schedule.

**AI restriction.** AI must not declare a benefit realised, choose the comparison or attribution
that determines the reported result, approve the closing benefits report, or authorise the disposal
of records.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-produced measurements and
attribution analyses against source data and the recorded baselines, and must confirm that the
method reported is the method that was planned before the results were known.

**External references.** ISO 21503 (international standard) — programme management guidance, in
which benefit realisation is measured and managed beyond delivery. ISO 21504 (international
standard) — portfolio management guidance, including the evaluation of realised portfolio
performance. ISO 21502 (international standard) — guidance on project management, including project
closure and post-project evaluation. ISO 15489 (international standard) — records management,
governing the retention and disposal discipline this law depends on. The official publications
govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Benefit claims used for grant acquittal, regulated pricing, tax relief
or public reporting are subject to local rules on evidence, audit and retention, and record
retention periods are set by law and contract; obtain local legal, accounting and records advice
before reporting or disposing.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-12; PML-LAW-02-01; PML-LAW-02-02; PML-LAW-09-02;
PML-LAW-14-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 16, KA 16.4 — Benefits measurement, responsible archive and
model/data retention (16.4.1 the benefits measurement plan; 16.4.3 attribution and the comparison
cohort; 16.4.4 responsible archive and retention; 16.4.5 model and data retention for AI-assisted
decisions); KA 16.3 — The post-project review; Domain 2, KA 2.3 — Measures and baselines; Domain 15,
KA 15.2 — The portfolio benefits register.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which realisation is claimed from outputs, measures are
changed after the fact, or measurement lapses at closure, testing whether the candidate can restore
an honest measured position. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PML-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.
