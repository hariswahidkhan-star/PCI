# PCL-AI Professional Laws — PCI AI Project Controls Leader

**Status:** Certification Laws for the **PCL-AI** credential (AI Project Controls Leader), drafted
under and subordinate to the binding specification in [`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md). Twenty laws,
anchored to the thirteen-domain PCL-AI Body of Knowledge (`../../bok/`).

> PCI Professional Laws are professional certification rules and standards of conduct established by
> PCI Global. They are not legislation, regulatory requirements or substitutes for applicable law,
> contractual obligations or authoritative professional standards. Where any applicable law,
> regulation, contract or authoritative professional standard imposes a stricter requirement, that
> requirement governs.

---

## How to read these laws

- **The drafting specification governs.** Definition, hierarchy, normative language, the mandatory
  field template, external-reference rules, the visual system and stable identifiers are all defined
  in [`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md). These laws conform to it; where any doubt arises, that
  specification prevails.
- **Identifiers are stable.** Each law is cited as `PCL-LAW-DD-NN`, where `DD` is the two-digit
  Body-of-Knowledge domain of primary anchorage and `NN` a sequence within that domain. Cite laws by
  ID, never by page number. A law that spans domains anchors to the domain that teaches it and lists
  the others under *Related book content*.
- **Normative language is deliberate.** `must` / `must not` / `shall` / `shall not` state mandatory
  professional rules; `should` states recommended practice from which departure needs a recorded
  reason; `may` is discretionary. Text is labelled, every time, as **Mandatory PCI Law**,
  **Mandatory PCI Rule**, **Recommended PCI Practice**, **External requirement**,
  **Illustrative example** or **Jurisdictional requirement** — never mixed.
- **External references are characterised, never reproduced.** Real instruments are named and
  described in PCI's own words; the official publication always governs. No clause numbers or
  editions are asserted unless verified.
- **No endorsement, affiliation or accreditation is claimed or implied.** Naming an external
  instrument means only that it exists and is relevant. No standards body, professional institute,
  government, supervisory authority or financial institution has reviewed, approved, endorsed or
  accredited these laws, the PCL-AI credential or PCI Global, and nothing here is a claim of
  conformity with, certification to, or accreditation under any instrument named. Where an
  instrument's status is known to be changing, that is noted at the point of use.
- **The suite principle** applies to every law in this set, in its one approved formulation:

> **AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable.**

## Domain 1 — Foundations of Accounting for Project Controls

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-01-01 — Cost Recognition and Cut-Off

**Rule.** A credential holder shall recognise project costs in the accounting period in which the
underlying work is performed or the goods or services are received, applying a documented period
cut-off, and shall not shift, accelerate, defer or reclassify cost between periods to alter reported
performance.

**Purpose.** Period-end cost figures drive earned value, forecasts, variance analysis and commercial
decisions; a manipulated or careless cut-off corrupts every downstream control.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare, process or approve project cost records, period-end
cost reports or cost feeds into performance measurement, on any project and any delivery model.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Apply a written cut-off procedure at each reporting date;
match recorded cost to evidence of work performed or receipt; review late-arriving invoices and
goods-received-not-invoiced items; document any judgemental allocations with their basis.

**Required evidence.** The cut-off procedure; period-end cost reconciliations to the ledger or cost
system; goods-receipt and work-performed records supporting recognised cost; a log of judgemental
allocations with basis and approver.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead for controls cost records; the responsible finance
controller for entries in the books of account.

**Independent review.** Required at least annually, and whenever period-end cost adjustments are
material to reported performance or a cut-off dispute arises.

**Escalation trigger.** Any instruction, request or pressure to move cost across a period boundary
without a matching change in when the work was performed or received.

**Prohibited practice.** Holding known costs out of a period, pre-booking cost for work not yet
performed, or reclassifying cost between periods or accounts to flatter a report.

**AI application.** AI may match invoices to receipts and commitments, flag anomalous period-end
entries, and draft candidate cut-off accruals for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide the period in which a cost is recognised, approve a
period-end adjustment, or certify a cut-off as complete.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-flagged matches and drafted
entries against source documents before posting, and must reconcile the closed period to the ledger.

**External references.** *Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting*, IFRS Foundation
(authoritative accounting standard) — establishes the accrual basis and faithful representation on
which period cost recognition rests. AACE International *Total Cost Management Framework*
(professional guidance) — describes the cost control cycle within which period cut-off operates.
The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory recognition, capitalisation and tax deductibility of project
cost follow local GAAP and tax law; obtain local accounting and tax advice before treating the
controls position as the statutory position.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-01-02; PCL-LAW-04-01; PCL-LAW-11-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 1, KA 1.3 — Accrual accounting and the matching concept; KA 1.5 —
Chart of accounts and cost coding for projects; Domain 5, KA 5.2 — The cost control cycle.

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenario items in which period-end entries are proposed
under schedule or reporting pressure and the candidate must identify the compliant treatment. No
live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-01-02 — Accrual Completeness

**Rule.** At each reporting date a credential holder shall ensure that accruals for work performed
but not yet invoiced are complete and supportable, and shall not omit a known or reasonably
estimable liability from the period's cost position, nor record an accrual without a documented
basis.

**Purpose.** Incomplete accruals understate actual cost, inflate cost performance and delay bad
news; unsupported accruals create hidden reserves. Both destroy the truthfulness of the cost
baseline position.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare or approve period-end cost positions, accrual
schedules or actual-cost feeds to earned value and forecasting.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain an accrual register reconciled to commitments,
subcontract progress and goods receipts; estimate each accrual from evidence of work performed;
reverse or true-up prior accruals promptly; distinguish accruals from provisions and disclose
estimation uncertainty.

**Required evidence.** The accrual register with basis of estimate per line; reconciliation to
commitments and subcontractor progress records; true-up history; approver sign-off for the period.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead, jointly with the responsible finance controller for
entries in the books of account.

**Independent review.** Required when accruals are material to the period's performance measurement,
and at least annually as part of controls assurance.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of a known liability deliberately or repeatedly excluded from the
accrual position, or an accrual with no supportable basis.

**Prohibited practice.** Suppressing accruals to protect a cost performance index, creating
unsupported "cushion" accruals, or netting unrelated over- and under-accruals to hide both.

**AI application.** AI may propose accrual candidates from commitment, timesheet and progress data,
detect gaps against historical patterns, and draft the accrual schedule.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine that the accrual position is complete, set the value of a
judgemental accrual, or approve the period position.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must test AI-proposed accruals against
subcontractor progress and receipt evidence, confirm completeness by reconciliation, and sign the
period position.

**External references.** IAS 37 *Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets*, IFRS
Foundation (authoritative accounting standard) — governs the boundary between accruals, provisions
and contingent liabilities that this law's records must respect. *Conceptual Framework for
Financial Reporting*, IFRS Foundation (authoritative accounting standard) — the accrual basis and
completeness as a component of faithful representation. The official publications govern; verify
current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Whether an item is an accrual, a provision or a contingent liability in
the statutory accounts is a local-GAAP determination; obtain local accounting advice for the books
of account.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-01-01; PCL-LAW-03-03; PCL-LAW-04-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 1, KA 1.3 — Accrual accounting and the matching concept; KA 1.4 —
Cost provisions and cost accruals (IAS 37); Domain 6, KA 6.1 — EVM fundamentals (actual cost).

**Examination relevance.** Scenario-based items presenting an incomplete or padded accrual position
for the candidate to identify, correct and escalate. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 3 — Budgeting & Forecasting

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-03-01 — Estimate Basis

**Rule.** Every estimate a credential holder prepares, endorses or relies upon for decision or
baseline purposes must carry a documented basis of estimate stating its scope, methodology, data
sources, assumptions, exclusions, classification and expected accuracy range; an estimate without a
stated basis shall not be presented as fit for decision.

**Purpose.** An estimate is only as good as its basis. Undocumented estimates cannot be reviewed,
classified, risk-adjusted or defended, and invite false precision.

**Scope.** All credential holders producing or approving cost or resource estimates at any maturity,
including AI-assisted and parametric estimates.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Prepare a basis-of-estimate document proportionate to the
estimate's class and use; state the estimate classification and accuracy range honestly; record
assumptions and exclusions explicitly; identify the pricing date and escalation treatment.

**Required evidence.** The basis-of-estimate document; the estimate model or calculation files; the
assumption and exclusion registers; classification rationale; reviewer and approver sign-off.

**Decision owner.** The estimating lead or Project Controls Lead who signs the estimate as fit for
its stated use.

**Independent review.** Required before an estimate is adopted as a funding or baseline figure, and
for any estimate supporting a sanction or contract-award decision.

**Escalation trigger.** A request to present an estimate at a higher confidence or tighter accuracy
range than its basis supports, or to remove stated exclusions without re-estimating.

**Prohibited practice.** Presenting a screening-level figure as a definitive estimate, quoting an
accuracy range unsupported by the classification, or concealing assumptions and exclusions.

**AI application.** AI may generate parametric estimates, retrieve historical benchmarks, test
assumption sensitivity and draft basis-of-estimate documentation.

**AI restriction.** AI must not assign the estimate classification, certify the accuracy range, or
approve the estimate for decision use.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must trace AI-produced figures to their data
sources, check benchmarks for relevance, recompute material lines, and confirm the stated basis
matches what was actually done.

**External references.** AACE International Recommended Practices 17R-97 and 18R-97 on cost
estimate classification (professional guidance) — define maturity-based estimate classes and
expected accuracy ranges that a basis of estimate should reference. AACE International *Total Cost
Management Framework* (professional guidance) — situates estimating within the total cost
management process. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Estimates supporting regulated procurement, tariff or public-funding
decisions may face statutory documentation requirements; obtain local legal advice where estimates
enter regulatory processes.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-03-02; PCL-LAW-12-01; PCL-LAW-13-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 3, KA 3.2 — Cost estimation; KA 3.1 — Budgeting fundamentals;
Domain 12, KA 12.3 — Contingency and management reserve.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which an estimate's stated class, range and basis are
inconsistent and the candidate must identify the professional response. No live examination content
is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-03-02 — Baseline Integrity

**Rule.** A credential holder shall establish the performance measurement baseline as an approved,
time-phased, scope-complete budget before performance is measured against it, and shall change it
only through authorised change control; the baseline must not be revised retrospectively to absorb
overruns or restate history.

**Purpose.** The baseline is the contract between plan and performance. If it moves without
authority, every variance, index and forecast measured against it is meaningless.

**Scope.** All credential holders who establish, maintain or approve cost or schedule baselines,
including the budget log of distributed budget, undistributed budget and management reserve.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Time-phase the approved budget against the schedule; hold a
baseline change log reconciling original budget to current baseline through authorised changes only;
freeze past-period baseline values except for authorised corrections, which must be disclosed.

**Required evidence.** The approved baseline document and its time-phasing; the baseline change log
with approvals; reconciliation of budget at completion from original to current; disclosure notes
for any authorised historical correction.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead maintains the baseline; the project sponsor or change
board owns approval of baseline change.

**Independent review.** Required at baseline establishment, at each re-baselining, and whenever
cumulative baseline change is material relative to the original budget.

**Escalation trigger.** Any change to baseline values without a change-board authorisation, or a
proposal to re-baseline in order to reset unfavourable variances without disclosure.

**Prohibited practice.** Rubber baselining, retrofitting the plan to actuals, moving budget between
control accounts to mask overruns, or eliminating variances by unauthorised re-baselining.

**AI application.** AI may check baseline coherence (scope–budget–schedule alignment), detect
unauthorised changes between snapshots, and draft baseline change documentation.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a baseline, authorise a baseline change, or decide that a
re-baselining is warranted.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must reconcile each period's baseline to the
prior period plus authorised changes, and verify AI-detected discrepancies against the change log
before reporting.

**External references.** ANSI/EIA-748 *Earned Value Management Systems* (industry practice) — the
widely adopted guideline set for establishing and controlling performance measurement baselines.
ISO 21508 *Earned value management in project and programme management* (international standard) —
international guidance on baseline establishment and maintenance. PMI *The Standard for Earned
Value Management* (professional guidance) — describes baseline discipline within EVM practice. The
official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Government contracting regimes may impose formal EVMS baseline-control
requirements with legal effect; obtain local contracting advice on any mandated system criteria.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-05-01; PCL-LAW-06-01; PCL-LAW-10-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 3, KA 3.3 — The time-phased budget / cost baseline (Planned
Value); Domain 6, KA 6.1 — EVM fundamentals; Domain 5, KA 5.4 — Change control and cost impact.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items involving pressure to re-baseline or shift budget between
control accounts, requiring the candidate to apply change-control discipline. No live examination
content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-03-03 — Forecast Honesty

**Rule.** A credential holder shall forecast cost, schedule and cash outcomes as an unbiased
professional expectation based on current performance, remaining work and known risks, and shall not
suppress, anchor or manage a forecast to a budget, target or prior forecast in order to avoid
disclosing a deteriorating position.

**Purpose.** Forecasts exist to inform decisions before outcomes are fixed. A forecast bent to the
budget hides the truth precisely when it is most needed.

**Scope.** All credential holders producing or approving estimates at completion, estimates to
complete, schedule forecasts and cash-flow forecasts, however derived.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Base each forecast on a stated method; reconcile top-down
(performance-factored) and bottom-up forecasts and explain material divergence; incorporate approved
and probable changes and risk exposure; record the forecast basis and movement from the prior
period.

**Required evidence.** The forecast file with method and inputs; reconciliation between forecasting
methods; forecast movement analysis; the risk-adjustment basis; approver sign-off.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead who signs the forecast; the project manager owns the
management position reported against it.

**Independent review.** Required when a forecast is overridden against the indicated performance
trend, and periodically for projects whose forecasts drive external commitments.

**Escalation trigger.** An instruction to hold a forecast at budget contrary to performance
evidence, or a management override of a forecast without a recorded, evidence-based rationale.

**Prohibited practice.** Reporting an estimate at completion below what performance data and known
exposures support; serial "hockey-stick" recovery assumptions with no credible recovery plan;
deleting risk exposure from the forecast to meet a target.

**AI application.** AI may compute performance-factored forecasts, run scenario and probabilistic
analyses, detect forecast bias patterns, and draft forecast commentary.

**AI restriction.** AI must not set the reported forecast, approve an override of an indicated
forecast, or decide which scenario is disclosed.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute or benchmark AI-generated
forecasts against at least one independent method, verify inputs, and record the rationale for the
adopted figure.

**External references.** ISO 21508 *Earned value management in project and programme management*
(international standard) — international guidance on estimate-at-completion practice. AACE
International *Total Cost Management Framework* (professional guidance) — forecasting within the
cost control cycle. PMI *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)*
(professional guidance) — forecasting within monitoring and controlling. The official publications
govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Forecasts that feed listed-company disclosures, loan covenants or
regulated reporting may carry legal disclosure duties; obtain local legal and accounting advice
before external use.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-01-02; PCL-LAW-04-01; PCL-LAW-06-01; PCL-LAW-12-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 3, KA 3.4 — Forecasting; KA 3.5 — Cash-flow forecasting; Domain 6,
KA 6.3 — Forecasting with EVM: the EAC family.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which performance data contradicts a directed forecast
and the candidate must determine the honest reportable position and escalation path. No live
examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 4 — Performance Management, Variance Analysis & Reporting

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-04-01 — Reporting Integrity

**Rule.** Every performance report a credential holder prepares or approves must be accurate,
complete, reconciled to its source data and free of presentation designed to mislead; adverse
information within the report's scope shall not be omitted, minimised or obscured.

**Purpose.** Reports are the instrument by which governance sees the project. A report that is
selectively accurate is collectively false.

**Scope.** All credential holders producing or approving periodic performance reports, dashboards,
variance analyses and management commentary, in any medium including AI-generated drafts and
visualisations.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Reconcile every reported figure to the controlled source
system; apply consistent definitions and cut-offs across periods; explain material variances with
cause, impact and corrective action; ensure visualisations use honest scales and complete data.

**Required evidence.** Source-to-report reconciliations; the variance analysis with cause and
action; the report approval record; prior-period comparatives showing consistent definitions.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead for the reported figures; the project manager for the
management commentary.

**Independent review.** Required for reports supporting sanction, re-sanction or external
commitments, and periodically as part of controls assurance.

**Escalation trigger.** A request to remove, soften or delay adverse content that the reporting
criteria require, or discovery of a reported figure that cannot be reconciled to source.

**Prohibited practice.** Cherry-picking metrics, truncated or dual axes chosen to disguise trend,
reporting stale data as current, or issuing commentary that contradicts the underlying figures.

**AI application.** AI may assemble report packs, reconcile figures across systems, draft variance
narratives and generate visualisations for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a report for issue, decide what adverse information is
omitted, or certify that figures reconcile.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-drafted narratives against the
reconciled figures, check visualisations for honest presentation, and sign the report before issue.

**External references.** *Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting*, IFRS Foundation
(authoritative accounting standard) — faithful representation and neutrality as qualities of useful
information, applied here by analogy to management reporting. COSO *Internal Control — Integrated
Framework* (voluntary framework) — reporting objectives and information quality within internal
control. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Reports feeding statutory, market or lender disclosures may attract
legal liability for misstatement; obtain local legal advice before external release.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-03-03; PCL-LAW-04-02; PCL-LAW-13-03.

**Related book content.** Domain 4, KA 4.2 — Variance analysis; KA 4.3 — Management reporting; KA
4.4 — Data visualisation and storytelling for controls.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting a report that is technically accurate but
presentationally misleading, for the candidate to detect and correct. No live examination content
is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-04-02 — Escalation

**Rule.** A credential holder shall escalate breaches of defined thresholds and material adverse
developments promptly to the accountable governance level through the established route, and shall
not delay, dilute or filter an escalation because it is unwelcome.

**Purpose.** Controls only protect the project if bad news travels upward faster than it compounds.
Escalation is a professional duty, not a discretionary courtesy.

**Scope.** All credential holders operating within a project's reporting and governance structure,
including matters discovered through AI-generated analysis.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Know the project's escalation thresholds and routes; escalate
in writing with the evidence, impact and recommended action; record the escalation and the response;
re-escalate to the next level if no adequate response follows within a reasonable, recorded period.

**Required evidence.** The escalation record (what, when, to whom, evidence attached); the response
and decision; the re-escalation trail where applicable.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder owns the duty to escalate; the receiving governance level
owns the decision on the escalated matter.

**Independent review.** Required where an escalation concerns the conduct of the person who would
normally receive it; the escalation then goes to the next independent level.

**Escalation trigger.** Threshold breaches on cost, schedule, risk or safety-relevant controls;
discovery of misstatement, suppressed information or control override; any instruction to breach a
PCI Professional Law.

**Prohibited practice.** Sitting on threshold breaches, escalating verbally only to avoid a record,
editing an escalation to remove its substance, or retaliating against a person who escalates.

**AI application.** AI may monitor thresholds continuously, draft escalation notices and assemble
the supporting evidence pack.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide whether a matter is escalated, suppress an alert, or be
treated as having discharged the professional's escalation duty.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-raised alerts against source
data before escalating, and must confirm that automated monitoring coverage matches the defined
thresholds.

**External references.** ISO 21502 *Project, programme and portfolio management — Guidance on
project management* (international standard) — governance and issue-management practice within
project direction. COSO *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (voluntary framework) —
information and communication as a component of control. The official publications govern; verify
current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Whistleblowing, employment and confidentiality law vary by
jurisdiction; obtain local legal advice before escalating outside the organisation.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-04-01; PCL-LAW-12-01; PCL-LAW-13-04.

**Related book content.** Domain 4, KA 4.1 — Performance management principles; KA 4.3 — Management
reporting; Domain 11, KA 11.3 — Internal control and segregation of duties.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items testing recognition of an escalation trigger, the correct
route and the response to a blocked escalation. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 5 — Cost Management & Cost Control

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-05-01 — Change Control

**Rule.** A credential holder shall process every change to scope, budget, schedule or contractual
obligation through the authorised change-control procedure — identified, estimated, assessed for
impact, approved at the proper authority and only then incorporated into the baseline — and shall
not implement, conceal or retrospectively regularise unapproved change.

**Purpose.** Uncontrolled change is the leading destroyer of baselines, entitlement and
accountability. Change control keeps the baseline honest and the commercial record defensible.

**Scope.** All credential holders who raise, assess, approve or incorporate changes, including
trends, variations, compensation-style contract events and internal transfers between control
accounts.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a single change register from identification to
close-out; estimate each change's cost, schedule and risk impact before approval; match approval to
the delegated authority level; update baseline, forecast and contract records only after approval.

**Required evidence.** The change register; impact assessments; approval records at the correct
authority; baseline change log entries; correspondence for contract-related changes.

**Decision owner.** The change board (or delegated authority holder) approves; the Project Controls
Lead ensures assessment and incorporation discipline.

**Independent review.** Required for changes above a defined materiality, for cumulative change
approaching contingency limits, and for any retrospective approval.

**Escalation trigger.** Work proceeding without approved change, a change split to evade an
authority threshold, or a backlog of unassessed changes distorting the forecast.

**Prohibited practice.** Doing the work first and papering the change later without disclosure,
burying change in existing budgets, or splitting changes to stay under approval thresholds.

**AI application.** AI may detect scope drift from documents and progress data, draft change
requests and impact estimates, and track register ageing.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a change, set the authority level required, or decide that a
change need not be raised.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-drafted impact estimates
against the estimate basis and schedule model, and confirm register completeness against project
records each period.

**External references.** FIDIC suite of standard forms of contract (contract framework) — provides
formal variation and claims mechanisms that project change control must mirror. NEC4 suite of
contracts (contract framework) — provides prospective assessment of change through its
compensation-event mechanism, illustrating disciplined forward pricing of change. PMI *A Guide to
the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)* (professional guidance) — integrated change
control practice. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Contractual notice periods and condition-precedent clauses can bar
entitlement if missed; obtain contract-law advice on time-bar effects in the governing
jurisdiction.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-03-02; PCL-LAW-07-01; PCL-LAW-12-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 5, KA 5.4 — Change control and cost impact; KA 5.3 — Cost breakdown
and control accounts; Domain 7, KA 7.2 — Contract management.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items on unapproved work, threshold-splitting and retrospective
change, testing the compliant sequence of assessment and approval. No live examination content is
exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 6 — Earned Value Management & Forecasting

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-06-01 — Earned Value Integrity

**Rule.** A credential holder shall measure earned value strictly in accordance with the
pre-defined measurement rules for each work package, keep planned value, earned value and actual
cost consistent in scope, coding and cut-off, and shall not manipulate any element of the earned
value data set to improve reported variances or indices.

**Purpose.** EVM is only decision-grade when its three data sets describe the same work on the same
basis. One flattered element falsifies every index and forecast built on it.

**Scope.** All credential holders operating, maintaining or approving earned value measurement,
including AgileEVM adaptations and hybrid delivery.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Assign an objective earned value technique to each work
package before work starts; credit EV only per those rules; align AC to the same scope and cut-off
as EV; investigate and explain index anomalies rather than adjusting data; route any measurement
correction through disclosed, authorised adjustment.

**Required evidence.** The EV technique assignment per work package; period EV calculation records;
PV–EV–AC reconciliation to the baseline and ledger; the log of authorised data corrections with
rationale.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead for the earned value system and its reported data.

**Independent review.** Required at system establishment, after any material data correction, and
periodically via data-quality surveillance.

**Escalation trigger.** EV credited outside the assigned rules, AC and EV cut-offs diverging
materially, or any request to adjust EV data to move an index.

**Prohibited practice.** Crediting EV for unperformed work, changing measurement technique
mid-period to gain EV, booking AC late while crediting EV on time, or retroactively editing past
periods without authorisation and disclosure.

**AI application.** AI may compute EV metrics, run data-quality checks across PV, EV and AC, flag
index anomalies and draft variance analysis.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine percent complete for judgement-based work packages, alter
measurement rules, or approve corrections to the EV data set.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-computed metrics against the
baseline and source progress records, and confirm each period that the three data sets share scope
and cut-off.

**External references.** ISO 21508 *Earned value management in project and programme management*
(international standard) — international EVM practice guidance. ANSI/EIA-748 *Earned Value
Management Systems* (industry practice) — the widely adopted EVMS guideline set including data
integrity expectations. PMI *The Standard for Earned Value Management* (professional guidance) —
EVM measurement technique practice. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Where EVMS compliance is a contract or government-procurement condition,

## Domain 7 — Contracts, Commercial Management, BoQ, Invoicing & Revenue

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-07-01 — Commercial Traceability

**Rule.** Every commercial position a credential holder prepares or approves — valuation,
application for payment, invoice, claim or entitlement assessment — must trace, item by item, to
the contract provisions, agreed rates or bills of quantities, and contemporaneous records that
support it; an untraceable commercial position shall not be certified or submitted.

**Purpose.** Commercial value that cannot be traced cannot be defended, audited or honestly
recognised as revenue. Traceability is what separates entitlement from assertion.

**Scope.** All credential holders performing measurement, valuation, invoicing, applications for
payment, claims support or revenue-recognition inputs on any contract form.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Map each valuation line to its contractual basis (rate, BoQ
item, variation or event); support quantities with measurement records; keep claim narratives
consistent with contemporaneous records; reconcile invoiced, certified and recognised amounts each
period.

**Required evidence.** The valuation build-up with contractual references; measurement and site
records; the variation and event registers; invoice-to-certificate-to-ledger reconciliations.

**Decision owner.** The commercial manager or quantity surveyor certifying the position; the
Project Controls Lead for the reconciliation to cost and revenue records.

**Independent review.** Required for material claims and final accounts, and for any valuation
where entitlement is contested.

**Escalation trigger.** A valuation or claim line with no contractual basis or supporting record,
or pressure to invoice ahead of entitlement.

**Prohibited practice.** Invoicing unperformed or unsubstantiated work, valuing variations on bases
the contract does not provide, drafting claim narratives contradicted by contemporaneous records,
or double-counting scope between base and change.

**AI application.** AI may extract contract obligations and rates, match measurement records to BoQ
items, assemble claim chronologies and flag unsupported lines.

**AI restriction.** AI must not certify a valuation, determine entitlement, or approve an invoice or
application for payment.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-extracted contract terms
against the executed contract, test matched quantities against source measurement, and sign the
traceability of every submitted position.

## Domain 10 — Project Scheduling

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-10-01 — Schedule Logic

**Rule.** A credential holder shall build and maintain schedules as complete logic-linked networks
in which every activity except the start and finish milestones has predecessors and successors, and
shall document and justify every constraint, lag and lead; logic shall not be replaced by
constraints, nor edited to force a desired date.

**Purpose.** Only a sound network can calculate dates, float and the critical path. Broken or
forced logic produces a picture of a plan, not a plan.

**Scope.** All credential holders who develop, update, review or approve project schedules at any
level, in any scheduling tool, including AI-assisted schedule generation.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain full network logic; minimise and justify hard
constraints, open ends, long lags and negative lags in a documented log; run schedule health checks
each update cycle; record the basis of schedule (calendars, assumptions, methodology).

**Required evidence.** The native schedule files; the basis-of-schedule document; the constraint
and open-end log with justifications; periodic schedule health-check results.

**Decision owner.** The planning lead or Project Controls Lead who signs the schedule as fit for
use.

**Independent review.** Required before baseline approval, after major logic revisions, and for
schedules underpinning contractual dates or extension-of-time positions.

**Escalation trigger.** Health-check results showing material logic degradation, or an instruction
to force dates by constraint or logic edits contrary to the plan of work.

**Prohibited practice.** Pinning activities with constraints to show a required completion date,
deleting logic to remove reported delay, hiding delay in artificial lags, or maintaining a
presentation schedule that diverges from the working schedule without disclosure.

**AI application.** AI may propose activity networks from scope documents, run logic health checks,
detect anomalous constraints and lags, and draft the basis of schedule.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve the schedule, decide that a constraint is justified, or
finalise logic that encodes the plan of work.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must walk AI-proposed logic against the
intended execution sequence with those who will do the work, and verify health-check exceptions
before sign-off.

**External references.** PMI *Practice Standard for Scheduling* (professional guidance) — good
practice for network development and maintenance. U.S. Government Accountability Office *Schedule
Assessment Guide* (professional guidance) — characteristics of a reliable schedule. The DCMA
14-point schedule assessment (industry practice) — widely used quantitative logic-health metrics.
The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Some contracts give the accepted programme contractual status with
formal update and acceptance procedures; obtain contract advice on the governing regime.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-10-02; PCL-LAW-10-03; PCL-LAW-03-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 10, KA 10.1 — Schedule development; KA 10.2 — Network analysis and

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-10-02 — Critical Path Verification

**Rule.** Before relying on a critical path for decisions, reporting, acceleration or delay
analysis, a credential holder shall verify that it is genuinely derived from the current network's
logic and progress, is credible against the physical sequence of work, and shall not assert a
critical path that the network analysis does not support.

**Purpose.** The critical path directs management attention, money and legal argument. An
unverified or asserted path misdirects all three.

**Scope.** All credential holders who report the critical path, analyse delay, plan compression or
advise on time-related entitlement.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Recalculate the network after each update; trace the driving
path end-to-end and confirm it reflects real dependencies; examine near-critical paths and float
consumption; investigate path changes between updates and record their causes.

**Required evidence.** The calculated network with the traced driving path; float and
near-critical-path analysis; the update-to-update path change log with causes; the verification
record before each reliance.

**Decision owner.** The planning lead or Project Controls Lead who certifies the critical path for
the stated use.

**Independent review.** Required before the critical path is used in extension-of-time or delay
claims, acceleration decisions, or external reporting.

**Escalation trigger.** A critical path that shifts without explained cause, is driven by
constraints or preferential logic rather than work sequence, or is contradicted by site reality.

**Prohibited practice.** Declaring criticality by assertion or seniority, engineering logic or
constraints to move the path onto or off particular activities, or presenting a superseded path as
current.

**AI application.** AI may trace and compare paths across updates, quantify float erosion, flag
constraint-driven criticality and draft path-change commentary.

**AI restriction.** AI must not certify the critical path, select the delay-analysis methodology,
or determine time entitlement.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must independently trace the driving path in
the native schedule, confront it with physical progress, and verify AI path analyses before any
reliance.

**External references.** PMI *Practice Standard for Scheduling* (professional guidance) — critical
path method practice. AACE International Recommended Practice 29R-03 *Forensic Schedule Analysis*
(professional guidance) — recognised methods for analysing delay against the network. U.S.
Government Accountability Office *Schedule Assessment Guide* (professional guidance) — tests of a
credible critical path. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Delay-analysis method acceptability differs across legal forums; obtain
local legal advice before deploying critical-path analysis in formal disputes.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-10-01; PCL-LAW-13-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 10, KA 10.2 — Network analysis and the Critical Path Method; KA

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-10-03 — Progress Measurement

**Rule.** A credential holder shall measure progress against objective methods and rules of credit
agreed before reporting begins, based on evidence of physical work performed, and shall not report
progress from opinion, effort expended or money spent where these do not represent physical
completion.

**Purpose.** Progress is the raw material of earned value, schedule status and payment. Subjective
or spend-based progress converts every downstream control into guesswork.

**Scope.** All credential holders who capture, calculate, review or approve progress on activities,
work packages or deliverables, including subcontractor-reported progress.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Define the measurement method and rules of credit per work
type before execution; support reported progress with quantifiable evidence; verify
subcontractor-reported progress before acceptance; keep the progress cut-off aligned with cost
cut-off.

**Required evidence.** The progress measurement procedure and rules of credit; period progress
returns with supporting evidence; verification records for third-party progress; the progress
audit trail per period.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead for the progress system; discipline or package
managers for the evidence behind their returns.

**Independent review.** Required periodically by walk-down or quantity check, and whenever reported
progress and independent indicators (quantities installed, milestones) diverge materially.

**Escalation trigger.** Reported progress unsupported by physical evidence, systematic optimism in
a package's returns, or divergence between progress and quantity records beyond tolerance.

**Prohibited practice.** "Percent complete by feel", crediting progress for staged materials where
rules do not allow it, accepting subcontractor progress without verification, or revising historical
progress without disclosure.

**AI application.** AI may compute progress from quantity and milestone data, reconcile returns
against evidence, analyse imagery or sensor data as supporting input, and flag optimism bias
patterns.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a progress return, override the agreed rules of credit, or
serve as sole evidence where the procedure requires physical verification.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-derived progress against the
agreed rules and sample it against physical evidence before the period is closed.

**External references.** AACE International *Total Cost Management Framework* (professional
guidance) — progress and performance measurement within the control cycle. ISO 21508 *Earned value
management in project and programme management* (international standard) — measurement of work
performed for EVM. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Where progress certifies payment under security-of-payment legislation,
statutory timing and certification rules may apply; obtain local legal advice.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-06-01; PCL-LAW-10-01; PCL-LAW-13-01.

## Domain 11 — Business Process Cycles & Control Environment

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-11-01 — Segregation of Duties

**Rule.** A credential holder shall ensure that incompatible duties — initiating, approving,
recording, executing payment or custody, and reconciling — are separated among different people or
roles within the controls and commercial processes they operate, and where separation is
impracticable shall implement and document compensating controls; a credential holder shall not
knowingly exercise, or design a process that concentrates, incompatible duties without such
controls.

**Purpose.** Concentrated duties enable both error and fraud to occur and remain hidden.
Segregation is the structural control on which most other controls depend.

**Scope.** All credential holders who design, operate or approve controls processes touching
commitments, valuations, payments, master data or system access — including AI agents and system
accounts granted process roles.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Map duties across the process cycles; identify and remediate
incompatible combinations, including those held by automated agents and shared accounts; review
access rights periodically; document compensating controls with their owners where separation is
impracticable.

**Required evidence.** The duty and access matrix; the incompatible-combination register with
remediation or compensating controls; periodic access-review records; approvals for documented
exceptions.

**Decision owner.** The process owner for each cycle; the Project Controls Lead for controls-side
processes.

**Independent review.** Required at process design, after organisational or system change, and
periodically through internal control review.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of an unremediated incompatible combination in use, an override
of a segregation control, or an AI agent operating across duty boundaries.

**Prohibited practice.** Approving one's own initiations, sharing credentials to bypass
separations, granting an automated agent end-to-end authority over commit-approve-pay, or leaving
known conflicts undocumented.

**AI application.** AI may analyse role and access data to detect incompatible combinations,
monitor for override patterns, and draft the duty matrix.

**AI restriction.** AI must not hold approval authority incompatible with its other process roles,
grant or certify access rights, or approve exceptions to segregation.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-detected conflicts against
actual system rights, and confirm that every documented compensating control is operating.

**External references.** COSO *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (voluntary framework) —
control activities including segregation of duties within an internal control system. ISO 21502
*Project, programme and portfolio management — Guidance on project management* (international
standard) — governance and organisational responsibility structures. The official publications
govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Corporate-governance and financial-controls legislation in some
jurisdictions imposes specific internal-control duties on companies; obtain local legal advice on
statutory control requirements.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-07-01; PCL-LAW-11-02; PCL-LAW-13-03.

**Related book content.** Domain 11, KA 11.3 — Internal control and segregation of duties; KA 11.1

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-11-02 — The Audit Trail

**Rule.** A credential holder shall ensure that every controls figure, decision and record they
produce or approve carries a retrievable audit trail — who did what, when, on what data and
authority — retained in accordance with the applicable retention requirements, and shall not
destroy, alter or fabricate records, nor operate a process whose outputs cannot be traced to their
inputs.

**Purpose.** A control that leaves no trail cannot be evidenced, audited or defended. The audit
trail is what converts professional work into professional evidence.

**Scope.** All credential holders and all controls processes, systems, models and AI tools whose
outputs enter reports, baselines, valuations or decisions.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Keep versioned source data, calculations and models; record
approvals with identity, date and basis; log material AI interactions that produced relied-upon
outputs (inputs, tool and version where available, and the verification performed); apply the
retention schedule; protect records from unauthorised alteration.

**Required evidence.** Version histories; approval logs; the AI-use log for relied-upon outputs;
retention schedule and disposal records; access and change logs for controlled systems.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead for controls records; system owners for the platforms
that hold them.

**Independent review.** Required when records support disputes, audits or investigations, and
periodically as part of records-management assurance.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of altered, missing or fabricated records, or a process producing
decision-grade outputs with no reconstructable trail.

**Prohibited practice.** Deleting or editing records to change history, back-dating approvals,
maintaining off-system "shadow" records for reported figures, or presenting an unlogged AI output
as evidenced work.

**AI application.** AI may automate capture of logs and version histories, detect gaps or anomalies
in trails, and assemble evidence packs for audit.

**AI restriction.** AI must not alter or dispose of records, certify a trail as complete, or
approve exceptions to retention requirements.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must periodically test that sampled reported
figures reconstruct end-to-end from retained records, including the AI-use log where AI
contributed.

**External references.** ISO 15489 *Information and documentation — Records management*
(international standard) — principles for creating, controlling and retaining authoritative
records. COSO *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (voluntary framework) — documentation and
information quality within control activities. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory retention periods, data-protection law and litigation-hold

## Domain 12 — Risk Management for Project Controls

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-12-01 — Risk and Contingency Governance

**Rule.** A credential holder shall identify, assess, own and manage risks through a documented
risk process, shall establish contingency and management reserve on an assessed and recorded risk
basis, and shall hold and draw them down only through authorised governance; contingency shall not
be hidden in line-item estimates, treated as budget available for scope, or used to mask overruns.

**Purpose.** Contingency is the priced expression of uncertainty. Ungoverned contingency either
disappears into padding or is silently consumed — and in both cases the risk position reported to
governance is false.

**Scope.** All credential holders who run risk processes, quantify risk exposure, set or administer
contingency and management reserve, or report risk-adjusted positions.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a live risk register with owners, assessments and
responses; derive contingency by a stated method proportionate to project size and risk; record
every drawdown against its causal risk or change with the approving authority; report remaining
contingency against remaining risk exposure each period.

**Required evidence.** The risk register and review records; the contingency derivation (method,
inputs, results); the drawdown log with approvals; the period reconciliation of remaining
contingency to remaining exposure.

**Decision owner.** The project manager or sponsor for contingency drawdown at the delegated level;
the Project Controls Lead for the risk-quantification and drawdown records.

**Independent review.** Required at sanction for the contingency basis, and whenever drawdown rate
materially outpaces risk retirement.

**Escalation trigger.** Contingency consumption without matched risk events or approved changes,
remaining contingency falling below assessed remaining exposure, or discovery of hidden padding.

**Prohibited practice.** Distributing contingency into work-package budgets to conceal it, drawing
down contingency to absorb performance overruns without disclosure, or reporting risk exposure
that the register does not support.

**AI application.** AI may propose risk candidates from project data, run quantitative risk and
contingency simulations, track drawdown against retirement, and draft risk reporting.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a drawdown, set the contingency level, or close a risk.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify AI-simulated contingency results
against the register inputs and method, and reconcile drawdowns to authorisations each period.

**External references.** ISO 31000 *Risk management — Guidelines* (international standard) —
principles and process for managing risk. AACE International Recommended Practices on risk analysis
and contingency determination (professional guidance) — recognised methods for deriving contingency
from assessed risk. PMI *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)*
(professional guidance) — project risk management practice. The official publications govern;
verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Public-sector and regulated-industry regimes may prescribe contingency
and reserve treatment in funding submissions; obtain local advice on mandated methods and
disclosures.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-03-03; PCL-LAW-04-02; PCL-LAW-05-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 12, KA 12.1 — The risk framework; KA 12.2 — The risk process; KA

## Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-13-01 — Data Lineage

**Rule.** A credential holder shall know and be able to demonstrate the lineage of the data behind
every controls deliverable and every AI system they rely upon — its source, ownership, currency,
transformations and quality checks — and shall not base a decision-grade output on data of unknown
or unverifiable lineage without explicit disclosure of that limitation.

**Purpose.** AI and analytics amplify their inputs. Without lineage, errors, stale data and
unauthorised sources flow silently into baselines, forecasts and reports with false authority.

**Scope.** All credential holders who assemble data for controls analysis, feed data to AI tools,
or rely on AI or analytical outputs built from project, commercial or financial data.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify the authoritative source system for each data

use; label outputs built on degraded or proxy data; control unofficial extracts and spreadsheets
that bypass source systems.

**Required evidence.** The data-source register; transformation and mapping documentation; data
quality-check results; lineage annotations on decision-grade deliverables.

**Decision owner.** The Project Controls Lead for controls data; the designated data owner for each
source system.

**Independent review.** Required when a new data pipeline or AI tool enters decision-grade use, and
after material changes to source systems or mappings.

**Escalation trigger.** A decision-grade output whose inputs cannot be traced to an authoritative
source, or discovery that a relied-upon feed has silently changed or degraded.

**Prohibited practice.** Reporting from untraceable spreadsheets as though from source, feeding
confidential or unlicensed data to tools without authority, or concealing a known data-quality
defect in a relied-upon output.

**AI application.** AI may map and document lineage, profile data quality, detect drift and
anomalies in feeds, and flag outputs built on degraded inputs.

**AI restriction.** AI must not designate the authoritative source, waive a data-quality check, or
certify lineage as verified.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify lineage for each decision-grade

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-13-02 — AI Verification

**Rule.** A credential holder shall verify every AI-generated output before professional reliance —
checking material figures by recomputation or independent comparison, checking asserted facts and
references against authoritative sources, and checking reasoning for applicability to the actual
project context — and shall not present an unverified AI output as professional work product.

**Purpose.** Generative and analytical AI produce fluent, confident output that can be wrong,
fabricated or subtly misapplied. Verification is the control that converts AI assistance into
professional work; the suite principle applies: AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and
remains accountable.

**Scope.** All credential holders using AI tools of any kind — generative, predictive, analytical
or agentic — whose outputs contribute to controls deliverables, advice or decisions.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Verify proportionately to consequence: recompute or
independently benchmark material numbers; trace cited standards, clauses and facts to the official
source before repeating them; test outputs against known cases; record what verification was
performed for decision-grade outputs.

**Required evidence.** The verification record for decision-grade AI outputs (checks performed,
sources consulted, corrections made); reconciliations or benchmark comparisons for material
figures.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder who adopts the output into their work owns its
verification.

**Independent review.** Required for AI outputs supporting sanction decisions, external submissions
or disputes, and for any newly adopted AI tool before decision-grade use.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of a fabricated reference, materially wrong figure or unsafe
recommendation in AI output already relied upon, or a tool exhibiting systematic error in a
decision-grade role.

**Prohibited practice.** Copying AI output into deliverables unread, citing standards or contract
clauses on an AI's say-so without checking the official text, or signing work whose AI-produced
content the signer has not verified.

**AI application.** AI may assist verification itself — cross-checking one output against another
method, locating sources and flagging inconsistencies — as an aid to, never a substitute for,
professional verification.

**AI restriction.** AI must not verify its own output as the sole check, declare an output correct,
or discharge the professional's verification duty.

**Verification requirement.** Verification must be performed by a professional competent in the
subject matter of the output, at a depth proportionate to the output's consequence, and recorded
for decision-grade use.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 42001 *Artificial intelligence — Management system* (international
standard) — organisational controls for responsible AI use. ISO/IEC 23894 *Artificial intelligence
— Guidance on risk management* (international standard) — managing risks arising from AI systems.
NIST *AI Risk Management Framework* (voluntary framework) — trustworthiness characteristics and
risk treatment for AI. OECD AI Principles (voluntary framework) — intergovernmental principles for
trustworthy AI; not legislation. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** AI-specific regulation is emerging and uneven across jurisdictions;
obtain local legal advice on obligations attaching to AI use in professional and regulated work.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-13-01; PCL-LAW-13-03; PCL-LAW-13-04.

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-13-03 — Human Sign-Off

**Rule.** Every controls deliverable, approval and decision within a credential holder's scope must
carry the recorded sign-off of an identified, competent and authorised human being; an AI system
shall not be the approver of record, and a credential holder shall not design or operate a workflow
in which AI output takes effect as a decision without human sign-off.

**Purpose.** Sign-off is where authority and accountability attach. If no identifiable human signs,
no one is accountable — and automation becomes an accountability laundering machine.

**Scope.** All credential holders and all workflows — manual, automated or agentic — that produce
baselines, forecasts, valuations, change approvals, payments, reports or advice.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Define the human approver for every decision point in each
workflow; ensure the signer has the competence, information and time to review meaningfully, not
merely click through; record identity, date and basis of each sign-off; design automated pipelines
so that decision-grade outputs are held for human approval.

**Required evidence.** The approval matrix mapping decisions to human approvers; sign-off records
with identity, date and basis; workflow design documentation showing human decision points in
automated pipelines.

**Decision owner.** The named human approver for each decision; the process owner for workflow
design.

**Independent review.** Required when automation is introduced or extended into a decision path,
and periodically to test that sign-offs are substantive rather than rubber-stamped.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of a decision that took effect without human sign-off, an
approver signing outside their authority or competence, or systematic pass-through approval of AI
output without review.

**Prohibited practice.** Configuring systems to auto-approve decision-grade outputs, recording an
AI agent or shared account as approver, signing work one has not reviewed, or lending one's
sign-off to another's unreviewed judgement.

**AI application.** AI may prepare complete decision packages, route items to the correct approver,
and check that approvals carry the required authority level.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve, certify or sign any deliverable or decision, nor be
recorded as the approver of record under any circumstances.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must periodically test sampled approvals for
substantive review — that the signer engaged with the content — and audit automated workflows for
undeclared auto-approval paths.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 42001 *Artificial intelligence — Management system* (international
standard) — human oversight within AI management. OECD AI Principles (voluntary framework) —
human-centred values and accountability for AI outcomes; not legislation. COSO *Internal Control —
Integrated Framework* (voluntary framework) — authorisation and approval as control activities.
The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Some jurisdictions regulate automated decision-making affecting
individuals and require human review by law; obtain local legal advice for workflows touching
personal data or statutory decisions.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-11-01; PCL-LAW-13-02; PCL-LAW-13-04.

**Related book content.** Domain 13, KA 13.6 — Governance, ethics, risk & assurance of AI; KA 13.7
— Building an AI-augmented project-controls capability; Domain 11, KA 11.3 — Internal control and
segregation of duties.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items on automated pipelines lacking human decision points and
on rubber-stamp approval, testing workflow design and sign-off discipline. No live examination
content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PCL-LAW-13-04 — Professional Accountability

**Rule.** A credential holder remains personally and fully accountable for every work product,
figure, recommendation and decision issued under their name or authority, regardless of the tools,
AI systems, vendors or delegates that contributed to it; accountability shall not be transferred to,
or diluted by reference to, a model, tool, supplier or automated process.

**Purpose.** This law closes the set: every other law depends on there being a professional who
answers for the work. "The AI produced it" is an explanation of method, never a defence of
substance.

**Scope.** All credential holders, for all work within their PCL-AI certification scope, in every
delivery model and every degree of automation.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Understand the material basis of any output issued under
one's authority; maintain the competence to challenge tool and AI output in one's discipline;
disclose material AI contribution where the recipient's reliance decisions depend on it; correct
and notify errors in issued work promptly on discovery.

**Required evidence.** Authorship and issue records tying work products to accountable
individuals; verification and sign-off records under PCL-LAW-13-02 and PCL-LAW-13-03; error
correction and notification records.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder — accountability under this law is personal and cannot be
reassigned.

**Independent review.** None as a standing requirement, because the law establishes personal
accountability itself; reviews required by other laws in this set apply to the underlying work.

**Escalation trigger.** An attempt — by the credential holder or applied to them — to attribute
responsibility for defective work to a tool, model or vendor in place of an accountable person, or
discovery of material error in issued work.

**Prohibited practice.** Blaming a tool, vendor or AI system to evade responsibility for issued
work, issuing work under one's name without understanding its basis, or contractually or
procedurally routing accountability to an entity that cannot bear it.

**AI application.** AI may support the accountable professional in every task the other laws
permit: analysis, drafting, checking, monitoring and evidence assembly.

**AI restriction.** AI must not be assigned, or be described as holding, accountability for any
professional work product or decision; no configuration, disclaimer or workflow removes the
credential holder's accountability.

**Verification requirement.** The credential holder must be able to demonstrate, on request within
PCI's quality and conduct processes, the basis of issued work and the verification and sign-off
records behind it.

**External references.** OECD AI Principles (voluntary framework) — accountability of human actors
for AI system outcomes; not legislation. ISO/IEC 42001 *Artificial intelligence — Management
system* (international standard) — assignment of organisational roles and responsibilities for AI.
The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Professional liability, negligence and consumer-protection law
determine legal responsibility independently of this professional rule; obtain local legal advice
on liability for AI-assisted work.

**Related PCI laws.** PCL-LAW-04-02; PCL-LAW-13-02; PCL-LAW-13-03.

**Related book content.** Domain 13, KA 13.6 — Governance, ethics, risk & assurance of AI; KA 13.7
— Building an AI-augmented project-controls capability; Domain 8, KA 8.4 — Monitoring &
Controlling.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which responsibility for a defective AI-assisted
deliverable is disputed, testing where accountability sits and what the accountable professional
must do. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PCL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.
