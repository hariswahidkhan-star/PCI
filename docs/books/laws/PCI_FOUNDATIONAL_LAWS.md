# PCI Foundational Laws

**Status:** Foundational law set of the PCI Professional Law System. These laws apply to **all three
PCI credentials** — PCL-AI (AI Project Controls Leader), PFL-AI (Project Finance Leader – AI) and
PML-AI (Project Management Leader – AI) — and to every candidate and credential holder within the
scope of their PCI-certified work. Drafted under the binding specification in
[`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md); IDs are stable (`PCI-LAW-F-NN`) and are cited by ID, never by page.

> PCI Professional Laws are professional certification rules and standards of conduct established by
> PCI Global. They are not legislation, regulatory requirements or substitutes for applicable law,
> contractual obligations or authoritative professional standards. Where any applicable law,
> regulation, contract or authoritative professional standard imposes a stricter requirement, that
> requirement governs.

## How to read these laws

Every law below follows the mandatory drafting format of `LAW_SYSTEM.md` §4, with no field omitted.
The normative language of §3 applies throughout: **must / must not / shall / shall not** mark
mandatory professional rules; **should** marks recommended practice; **may** marks discretion. Each
law is a **Mandatory PCI Law**; material labelled as guidance, external requirement, jurisdictional
requirement or illustrative example is identified as such in text and is never itself a law. The
whole set is governed by the suite principle, stated once and used identically everywhere:

> **AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable.**

External instruments are named and characterised in PCI's own words per `LAW_SYSTEM.md` §5 and the
shared source registry (`../registries/SOURCES.md`); their official publications govern; no external
text is reproduced. Certification-specific laws (`PCL-LAW-DD-NN`, `PFL-LAW-DD-NN`, `PML-LAW-DD-NN`)
are subordinate elaborations of this foundational set and must not conflict with it.

## Part I — Accountability and the governed use of AI

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-01 — Professional Accountability and the Suite Principle

**Rule.** A PCI credential holder or candidate is personally and non-delegably accountable for every
professional work product, judgement and decision within the scope of their PCI-certified work,
whether or not artificial intelligence assisted in its production, and shall apply the suite
principle — AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable — to all such
work.

**Purpose.** To establish that certification attaches accountability to a person, not to a tool, a
model, a vendor or a team, and to make the suite principle the single governing formulation for AI
use across all three credentials.

**Scope.** All candidates and credential holders of PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI, in all professional
work performed within a PCI certification scope, with or without AI assistance.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify the accountable professional for each work product
before it is relied upon; accept accountability explicitly when issuing, signing or submitting work;
never attribute responsibility for an error to an AI system, a software supplier or an unnamed
process.

**Required evidence.** Authorship and sign-off records identifying the accountable professional;
where AI materially assisted, the verification record required by PCI-LAW-F-03.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder or candidate performing the work. Accountability shall
never be assigned to an AI system or its vendor.

**Independent review.** Required where a work product carries organisational or third-party reliance
(for example a funding decision, a certified progress assessment or a published forecast), per the
organisation's assurance arrangements and PCI-LAW-F-05.

**Escalation trigger.** Any situation in which the professional cannot honestly accept
accountability for a work product — because it cannot be verified, exceeds their competence, or was
produced under improper pressure — triggers the duty in PCI-LAW-F-11.

**Prohibited practice.** Issuing work "as generated"; disclaiming responsibility on the ground that
an AI system, template or colleague produced the content; presenting unverified machine output as
one's own professional judgement.

**AI application.** AI may assist any stage of analysis, drafting, calculation checking,
summarisation or option generation, subject to the restrictions in this law set.

**AI restriction.** AI must not be named, treated or relied upon as the accountable party for any
professional work product, decision, approval or certification.

**Verification requirement.** Before relying on or issuing any work product, the accountable
professional must review it for correctness, completeness and fitness for its intended reliance, to
the standard they would apply to work produced entirely by hand.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 17024 (ISO/IEC) — international standard for bodies certifying
persons; PCI characterises it as the benchmark for competence-based, impartial certification of
individuals. OECD AI Principles (OECD) — voluntary intergovernmental framework promoting
human-centred, accountable AI; never legislation. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST) — public
voluntary framework for governing AI risk. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory professional-liability, delegation and signing regimes differ
by jurisdiction and profession; obtain local legal advice before relying on any delegation or
limitation of personal responsibility.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-02, PCI-LAW-F-03, PCI-LAW-F-04, PCI-LAW-F-13.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PML-AI KA 1.2 (the project leader's accountability) and PFL-AI KA 1.3 (ethics, fiduciary awareness
and responsible AI).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenario items testing where accountability sits when
AI-assisted work is wrong, incomplete or contested. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: examination invalidation, certification
suspension or revocation, referral to PCI's conduct process, and mandatory remediation conditions on
any reinstatement.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-02 — Verification of AI Output Before Professional Use

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall verify every AI-generated or AI-assisted output —
including analyses, forecasts, schedules, financial calculations, drafted documents, code and
summaries — for accuracy, completeness, relevance and freedom from fabrication before it is used,
relied upon, communicated or embedded in any professional work product.

**Purpose.** To prevent unverified machine output — including plausible fabrications, silent
omissions, stale data and miscalculation — from entering professional deliverables on which others
rely.

**Scope.** All AI-assisted work within a PCI certification scope, whatever the tool, vendor,
deployment model or apparent quality of the output.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Check AI output against authoritative sources, independent
recalculation or direct inspection proportionate to the reliance placed on it; confirm cited facts,
figures, references and quotations actually exist and say what is claimed; correct or discard output
that cannot be verified.

**Required evidence.** A verification record proportionate to the stakes: what was checked, against
what, by whom and with what result — for high-reliance outputs, retained with the work product per
PCI-LAW-F-12.

**Decision owner.** The professional who uses or issues the output; verification may be assisted but
its adequacy remains that professional's decision.

**Independent review.** Required before AI-assisted output supports a decision that is irreversible,
safety-relevant, financially material or externally reported; the reviewer must be independent of
the output's production.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery that unverified AI output has already entered a deliverable,
decision or external communication; or an AI tool producing systematically unreliable output in
continued organisational use.

**Prohibited practice.** Passing AI output onward unchecked; "verifying" by re-asking the same or
another AI system without independent grounding; deleting or bypassing verification steps under time
pressure without recording the departure and its justification.

**AI application.** AI may assist verification itself — cross-checking, reconciliation, anomaly
flagging, test generation — provided the final adequacy judgement is human.

**AI restriction.** AI must not be the sole verifier of AI output, and must not determine that
verification is unnecessary.

**Verification requirement.** Verification depth must scale with reliance: routine internal drafts
require professional review; externally relied-upon figures require independent recalculation or
source confirmation; certified or contractual statements require documented independent review.

**External references.** NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST) — public voluntary framework whose
measurement and management functions PCI characterises as the reference discipline for testing and
monitoring AI output. Federal Reserve SR 11-7 supervisory guidance on model risk management (US
Federal Reserve/OCC) — public supervisory guidance establishing model-validation discipline;
jurisdiction-specific, cited as guidance only. EU Artificial Intelligence Act (European Union) —
legislation in its jurisdiction imposing human-oversight expectations for certain AI systems; an
external requirement where applicable; verify current requirements. The official publications
govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Sector regulators (financial services, public procurement, safety) may
impose specific validation, audit or human-oversight obligations; obtain local regulatory advice
where AI output feeds regulated activity.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-03, PCI-LAW-F-06, PCI-LAW-F-07.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI (especially KA 14.3, AI use cases, prompting and verification); PFL-AI
Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance (especially KA 16.3, explainability,
validation, bias and model risk); also PML-AI KA 9.4 (data quality and AI-output quality) and PFL-AI
KA 6.4 (model checks, model audit and AI-assisted modelling controls).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios containing plausible but flawed AI output that
the candidate must detect, verify or reject. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: adverse conduct finding, certification
conditions requiring supervised practice or retraining, suspension or revocation where reliance harm
resulted, and examination invalidation where breached in assessment.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-03 — Human Decision Authority

**Rule.** Professional decisions, approvals and certifications within a PCI certification scope
shall be made only by an accountable human professional; an AI system must not decide, approve,
certify, sign, release or authorise any professional work product, commitment, payment, baseline
change or result, and automated recommendations shall take effect only upon explicit human
adoption.

**Purpose.** To keep decision authority — and therefore accountability — with people, and to
prevent automation from converting a recommendation pipeline into an unexamined decision pipeline.

**Scope.** All decisions, approvals, gate passages, releases, certifications and authorisations in
PCI-certified work, including those embedded in automated workflows, scripts and agentic tools.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify every point in a workflow where AI output could take
effect without human adoption and place an explicit human decision there; record who adopted each
material recommendation and on what basis; keep the authority to reject, amend or defer genuinely
exercisable, not a rubber stamp.

**Required evidence.** Decision records naming the human decision-maker, the inputs considered
(including AI inputs), the decision and its date; workflow documentation showing the human decision
points in any automated chain.

**Decision owner.** The professional holding the relevant decision right under the applicable
governance arrangement (for example sponsor, budget holder, certifying professional); never the AI
system.

**Independent review.** Required for the design of any automated workflow in which AI output feeds
directly toward commitments, payments or external statements, before the workflow enters use.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery that an AI-driven step has taken effect without human adoption; or
organisational pressure to remove human decision points for speed.

**Prohibited practice.** Configuring systems to auto-approve, auto-certify, auto-release or auto-pay
on AI output; ratifying AI decisions after the fact as a formality; presenting a human sign-off that
did not involve actual review.

**AI application.** AI may rank options, draft recommendations, pre-populate approval packages,
flag exceptions and route work to the right decision-maker.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide, approve or certify anything; it must not close the loop from
recommendation to effect without an accountable human act in between.

**Verification requirement.** Periodically test automated workflows to confirm human decision points
operate in practice — that rejection is possible, exercised where warranted, and recorded.

**External references.** OECD AI Principles (OECD) — voluntary framework emphasising human-centred
values and accountability; never legislation. EU Artificial Intelligence Act (European Union) —
legislation in its jurisdiction with human-oversight requirements for certain systems; an external
requirement where applicable; verify current requirements. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST)
— public voluntary framework addressing human-AI configuration and oversight. The official
publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Some jurisdictions and sectors regulate automated decision-making about
individuals (including rights to human review); obtain local legal advice before deploying any
automated decision flow affecting people.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-02, PCI-LAW-F-05, PCI-LAW-F-11.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI (especially KA 14.4, explainability, bias and human accountability); PFL-AI
Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance (especially KA 16.4, privacy,
cybersecurity, human approval and AI governance); also PML-AI KA 3.2 (decision authorities and
thresholds).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios asking where human decision points must sit in
automated project-controls, delivery and finance workflows. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, certification suspension or
revocation for permitting AI to exercise decision authority, and mandatory governance remediation as
a condition of reinstatement.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-04 — Disclosure of Material AI Assistance

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall not conceal material AI assistance: where AI
materially contributed to a professional work product, analysis or communication, that assistance
shall be disclosed to those entitled to rely on the work whenever they could reasonably regard it as
relevant to their reliance, and shall never be denied when asked.

**Purpose.** To preserve informed reliance and trust: those who act on professional work are
entitled to an honest account of how it was produced.

**Scope.** All professional work products and communications within a PCI certification scope,
including reports, forecasts, models, certifications, correspondence and examination submissions
where PCI rules require declaration.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Know and be able to state where AI materially assisted; answer
truthfully any question about AI involvement; follow any stricter disclosure rule of the client,
employer, contract or PCI examination conditions; record material AI assistance in the work
product's production record.

**Required evidence.** Production records or work-product notes identifying material AI assistance
and the verification applied to it (per PCI-LAW-F-02); disclosure statements where made.

**Decision owner.** The accountable professional issuing the work, applying the "reasonably relevant
to reliance" test honestly and, in doubt, disclosing.

**Independent review.** Required where an organisation adopts a standing policy on AI-assistance
disclosure, at policy level; individual disclosures do not require independent review.

**Escalation trigger.** A request or instruction to conceal or deny AI involvement in professional
work; discovery that reliance was obtained through a false account of how work was produced.

**Prohibited practice.** Denying AI assistance that occurred; presenting AI-generated material as
wholly human-authored where authorship is relevant to reliance (including in PCI examinations and
CPD submissions); contractually promising unassisted work while using AI.

**AI application.** AI may help maintain production records and draft disclosure statements.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine whether disclosure is required; that judgement is the
professional's.

**Verification requirement.** Before issue, confirm the work product's account of its own production
— explicit or implied — is true.

**External references.** OECD AI Principles (OECD) — voluntary framework whose transparency
principle PCI characterises as the reference for honest disclosure of AI involvement; never
legislation. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST) — public voluntary framework supporting
documentation and transparency practices. EU Artificial Intelligence Act (European Union) —
legislation in its jurisdiction including transparency obligations for certain AI interactions; an
external requirement where applicable; verify current requirements. The official publications
govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Consumer-protection, advertising, procurement and professional-conduct
rules in some jurisdictions impose specific disclosure duties; obtain local advice where AI
disclosure could affect contractual or regulatory position.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-02, PCI-LAW-F-07, PCI-LAW-F-14.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI (especially KA 14.3); PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI
in finance (especially KA 16.2 and KA 16.4); also PML-AI KA 11.4 (misinformation and AI-generated
communication risks).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios on when AI assistance must be disclosed and
through the candidate's own declaration obligations in PCI assessments. No live examination content
is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: examination invalidation for false declarations,
conduct findings for concealment, and certification suspension or revocation for obtaining reliance
by deception about work production.

## Part II — Evidence, information and honest reporting

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-05 — Evidence and the Audit Trail

**Rule.** Every material professional judgement, decision, approval and reported figure within a PCI
certification scope shall be supported by a contemporaneous, attributable and retrievable record
sufficient for a competent independent reviewer to reconstruct what was decided, by whom, when, on
what evidence and by what method.

**Purpose.** To make professional work auditable: conclusions that cannot be traced to evidence and
method cannot be verified, defended or learned from.

**Scope.** All material judgements, decisions, approvals, baselines, changes, forecasts and reported
results in PCI-certified work, including those produced with AI assistance.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Record decisions and their basis at the time they are made;
keep source data, key assumptions, methods and versions with the work product; ensure records are
attributable to named individuals and protected from silent alteration.

**Required evidence.** Decision logs, approval records, basis-of-estimate and basis-of-forecast
documentation, change records, model version history, and the AI verification records required by
PCI-LAW-F-02.

**Decision owner.** The accountable professional for each work product; for organisational systems
of record, the role designated by the governance arrangement.

**Independent review.** Required at defined assurance points (gates, audits, financial close,
certification events) per the applicable governance arrangement; the audit trail must be reviewable
without the author present.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of missing, backdated or altered records supporting a material
decision; a system of record that silently overwrites decision history.

**Prohibited practice.** Reconstructing records after the fact and presenting them as
contemporaneous; deleting or overwriting the evidential basis of a decision; maintaining a "clean"
official record that differs from the real basis of decision.

**AI application.** AI may assist in capturing, structuring, indexing and retrieving records, and in
flagging gaps in an audit trail.

**AI restriction.** AI must not create, amend or dispose of evidential records without an
accountable human instruction, and must not fabricate contemporaneity.

**Verification requirement.** Periodically test that a sampled decision can actually be
reconstructed from the record alone; treat failure as a defect in the control environment, not a
formality.

**External references.** AACE International Total Cost Management framework (AACE International) —
industry professional guidance whose documentation discipline PCI characterises as the reference
for auditable cost-engineering records. PMBOK Guide and PMI standards (Project Management Institute)
— professional guidance on decision, change and issue records. FIDIC conditions of contract (FIDIC)
— contract framework in which contemporaneous records underpin claims and determinations;
characterised generically, no clause numbers cited. ISO 21500-family project-management standards
(ISO) — international standards contextualising governance records. The official publications
govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Evidential and disclosure rules for records in disputes and audits are
jurisdiction-specific; obtain local legal advice before destroying, redacting or restructuring
records connected to a live or foreseeable dispute.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-03, PCI-LAW-F-06, PCI-LAW-F-12.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PML-AI KA 3.3 (auditability and the decision record), PML-AI KA 4.4 (the change board and the
decision log) and PFL-AI KA 13.2 (model audit).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios on what must be recorded, when, and what an
adequate audit trail contains. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: adverse conduct finding, quality-review
referral, certification conditions requiring records-management remediation, and suspension or
revocation for falsified records.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-06 — Data Lineage and Integrity

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall know and be able to state the provenance of the
data on which professional analysis, reporting or AI processing relies — its source, date, owner,
transformations and known limitations — and shall not base professional conclusions on data whose
lineage cannot be established or whose integrity is compromised, without stating that limitation
wherever the conclusion is used.

**Purpose.** To prevent conclusions built on untraceable, stale, manipulated or silently transformed
data, and to keep data quality visible wherever data-driven and AI-assisted outputs travel.

**Scope.** All data used in PCI-certified work: project actuals, schedules, estimates, financial
statements and models, risk registers, AI training/reference/prompt data, and third-party data
feeds.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Record source, extraction date and material transformations
for datasets underpinning material conclusions; protect data against unauthorised alteration;
reconcile derived figures to their sources; carry known data limitations forward into every use of
the conclusion.

**Required evidence.** Data-lineage records (source, date, owner, transformation steps),
reconciliations, data-quality checks, and access/change controls on systems of record.

**Decision owner.** The accountable professional for the analysis; for shared datasets, the
designated data owner under the organisation's data-governance arrangement.

**Independent review.** Required for datasets feeding externally reported figures, financial models
under audit, or AI systems in continued organisational use.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of unexplained changes in a system of record; material conclusions
found to rest on data of unknown origin; an AI pipeline consuming data outside its authorised
lineage.

**Prohibited practice.** Massaging, trimming or selectively excluding data to reach a preferred
result; presenting transformed data as raw; feeding confidential or unauthorised data into AI tools
contrary to PCI-LAW-F-09; ignoring known corruption because the output "looks right".

**AI application.** AI may assist with lineage capture, anomaly detection, reconciliation and
data-quality profiling.

**AI restriction.** AI must not certify data as fit for purpose, and AI-generated (synthetic or
imputed) data must never be presented as observed data.

**Verification requirement.** Before material reliance, trace a sample of figures end-to-end from
source to output; confirm transformations are documented and reproducible; label estimated, imputed
or synthetic values as such.

**External references.** ISO 8000 data-quality standards (ISO) — international standards addressing
data-quality management; cited by name; PCI characterises them as the reference discipline for data
quality. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST) — public voluntary framework addressing data
provenance and integrity in AI systems. AACE International Total Cost Management framework (AACE
International) — industry professional guidance on basis documentation for estimates and forecasts.
The official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Data-localisation, privacy and financial record-keeping laws constrain
where data may be stored and processed; obtain local legal advice before moving project or personal
data across borders or into third-party AI services.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-02, PCI-LAW-F-05, PCI-LAW-F-07, PCI-LAW-F-09.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI (especially KA 14.1, data governance and common data environments); PFL-AI
Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance (especially KA 16.1, financial-data
architecture and anomaly detection); also PML-AI KA 9.4 (data quality and AI-output quality).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios involving flawed, stale or manipulated data
feeding analyses and AI tools. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, quality-review referral,
certification conditions on data-governance practice, and suspension or revocation for deliberate
data manipulation.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-07 — Honesty in Reporting and Forecasting

**Rule.** All professional reporting and forecasting within a PCI certification scope — including
progress, performance, cost, schedule, risk and financial reporting — shall present an honest,
complete and unbiased account of the best available evidence; a credential holder or candidate shall
not misstate, omit, smooth, defer or slant reported information to serve a preferred narrative, and
shall state material assumptions, uncertainty and limitations alongside every forecast.

**Purpose.** To protect the decision-makers, lenders, sponsors and publics who act on professional
reports, and to preserve the meaning of the numbers on which the entire discipline of project
controls, delivery and finance rests.

**Scope.** All internal and external reporting and forecasting in PCI-certified work, however
produced, including dashboards and AI-generated report content.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Report unfavourable information as promptly and prominently as
favourable information; base forecasts on documented method and evidence, not on targets; disclose
material assumptions, ranges and confidence honestly; correct known errors in issued reports without
delay.

**Required evidence.** Basis-of-forecast documentation, reconciliation of reported figures to
systems of record, assumption logs, and issue-and-correction records for reports.

**Decision owner.** The accountable professional issuing the report or forecast; a reviewer or
superior who directs a misstatement becomes accountable alongside, not instead of, the issuer.

**Independent review.** Required for externally reported performance and financial figures and for
forecasts supporting investment, funding or continuation decisions.

**Escalation trigger.** Pressure, instruction or incentive to misreport; discovery that an issued
report or forecast was materially wrong or biased; a persistent gap between reported and actual
performance.

**Prohibited practice.** Hiding bad news in detail or delay; presenting a target as a forecast;
cherry-picking scenarios; smoothing variances across periods; using AI-generated fluency to obscure
rather than convey the substance; certifying progress that has not been achieved.

**AI application.** AI may assist in drafting reports, generating scenarios, detecting anomalies and
stress-testing forecasts.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide what is disclosed or withheld, must not approve a report or
forecast for issue, and AI-generated narrative must not be issued without human verification of
every material statement.

**Verification requirement.** Before issue, verify reported figures to source, test forecasts for
method and bias, and confirm the narrative fairly represents the underlying data — including what it
leaves out.

**External references.** IFRS Accounting Standards, including IAS 1 *Presentation of Financial
Statements* (IFRS Foundation) — authoritative accounting standards whose fair-presentation
discipline PCI characterises as the benchmark for honest financial reporting. IFRS 15 *Revenue from
Contracts with Customers* (IFRS Foundation) — authoritative accounting standard governing revenue
recognition on contracts. AACE International Total Cost Management framework (AACE International) —
industry professional guidance on forecasting and performance-measurement practice. PMBOK Guide and
PMI standards (Project Management Institute) — professional guidance on performance reporting. The
official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Securities, public-procurement and financial-reporting laws attach
specific liability to misstatements in some jurisdictions; obtain local legal and accounting advice
for externally published or regulated reporting.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-02, PCI-LAW-F-05, PCI-LAW-F-06, PCI-LAW-F-11, PCI-LAW-F-13.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance
(especially KA 16.1, forecast automation); also PCL-AI BoK Domains 4 (Performance Management,
Variance Analysis & Reporting) and 6 (Earned Value Management & Forecasting), PML-AI KA 2.4 (kill
criteria and honest gates) and KA 11.2 (executive communication and reporting).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenario items on reporting under pressure, forecast
bias and the honest presentation of uncertainty. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, certification suspension or
revocation for deliberate misreporting, examination invalidation where breached in assessment, and
mandatory ethics remediation on any reinstatement.

## Part III — Professional conduct

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-08 — Competence Boundaries and Referral

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall undertake only work they are competent to perform,
shall state the limits of their competence honestly, and shall refer matters requiring legal, tax,
accounting, actuarial, engineering, regulatory or other specialist judgement beyond that competence
to appropriately qualified advisers; access to AI tools does not extend professional competence.

**Purpose.** To protect those who rely on professional work from confident incompetence — including
the modern form in which AI fluency masks the absence of underlying expertise.

**Scope.** All acceptance, performance and delegation of work within a PCI certification scope, and
all advice given in a professional capacity.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Assess honestly, before accepting work, whether competence,
capacity and information are sufficient; disclose material limitations to the client or employer;
obtain supervision, training or specialist referral where needed; maintain competence through
continuing professional development.

**Required evidence.** Engagement or role definitions reflecting actual competence; referral records
to qualified advisers; CPD records maintained under PCI's certification requirements.

**Decision owner.** The professional accepting the work; for team assignments, the professional
allocating work remains accountable for matching work to competence.

**Independent review.** Required where work proceeds in an area at the edge of the professional's
demonstrated competence with no qualified specialist involved; the review examines whether the
boundary is being respected.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovering mid-engagement that the work requires competence not present in
the team; pressure to opine on legal, tax or regulatory questions without qualified advice.

**Prohibited practice.** Holding oneself out as qualified in a specialism one does not hold; issuing
legal, tax or accounting conclusions on the strength of AI output; accepting work knowing it cannot
be competently performed; suppressing a competence limitation to win or keep an engagement.

**AI application.** AI may support research, orientation in an unfamiliar area, and preparation of
questions for qualified advisers.

**AI restriction.** AI must not substitute for a qualified adviser where specialist professional
judgement is required, and AI output must not be presented as such advice.

**Verification requirement.** Where AI assists work near a competence boundary, verification must be
performed or reviewed by someone with demonstrated competence in that subject matter, not merely by
the AI user.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 17024 (ISO/IEC) — international standard whose competence-based
certification discipline PCI characterises as the basis for defining and evidencing individual
competence. PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (Project Management Institute) —
professional guidance addressing honest representation of qualifications and competence. The
official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Reserved-activity rules (legal advice, audit, tax practice, engineering
sign-off) differ by jurisdiction; obtain local advice on what only licensed professionals may do
before offering adjacent services.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-11, PCI-LAW-F-13, PCI-LAW-F-14.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &

data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PFL-AI KA 1.3 (fiduciary and professional obligations), PFL-AI Domain 12's standing referral of
jurisdiction-specific contract matters to qualified counsel, and PML-AI KA 1.2 (professional
standards of care).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios testing recognition of competence boundaries
and the duty to refer. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, certification conditions
(supervised practice, additional training), suspension or revocation for practising beyond
competence, and CPD-compliance enforcement.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-09 — Confidentiality and Information Protection

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall protect confidential information obtained in
professional work — including client, employer, counterparty, personal and commercially sensitive
project information — against unauthorised access, use or disclosure, shall use it only for the
purpose for which it was entrusted, and shall not place such information into any AI tool, service
or dataset without authorisation and appropriate safeguards.

**Purpose.** To preserve the trust on which access to professional information depends, and to close
the modern leak path in which confidential material is pasted into external AI services without
thought for where it goes.

**Scope.** All confidential information handled in PCI-certified work, in any medium, during and
after the engagement or employment in which it was obtained.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Follow the owner's classification and handling rules; apply
access controls and secure storage; confirm an AI tool's data-handling terms and the information
owner's authorisation before submitting confidential material to it; report suspected breaches to
the information owner without delay.

**Required evidence.** Confidentiality undertakings, access-control and handling records, records of
authorisation for AI-tool use with confidential data, and breach reports where applicable.

**Decision owner.** The professional handling the information, within the information owner's rules;
authorisation for new uses rests with the information owner.

**Independent review.** Required before adopting any AI tool or external service for processing
confidential information at organisational scale.

**Escalation trigger.** Suspected or actual unauthorised disclosure; discovery of confidential data
in an unapproved tool or location; a request to use entrusted information for an unauthorised
purpose.

**Prohibited practice.** Disclosing or trading on confidential information; entering client,
employer or personal data into unapproved AI services; retaining confidential material after
authorisation ends; using information entrusted for one engagement to advantage another.

**AI application.** AI may be used with confidential information only within approved tools, under
the information owner's authorisation and the applicable safeguards.

**AI restriction.** AI tools must not be treated as confidential-by-default; no confidential
information may be used to train, fine-tune or ground external models without the owner's explicit
authorisation.

**Verification requirement.** Before using an AI tool with confidential information, verify its
data-handling, retention and training terms against the owner's requirements; periodically confirm
approved-tool lists are current.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 27001 (ISO/IEC) — international standard for information-security
management systems; PCI characterises it as the reference discipline for protecting information
assets. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (NIST) — public voluntary framework addressing privacy and
security risks in AI use. The official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Data-protection legislation (for example the EU General Data Protection
Regulation in its jurisdiction), banking-secrecy and state-secrecy regimes impose specific and
sometimes criminal obligations; obtain local legal advice on personal-data processing and
cross-border transfers, and verify current requirements.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05, PCI-LAW-F-06, PCI-LAW-F-10, PCI-LAW-F-12.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI (especially KA 14.4, cybersecurity and privacy); PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data,
automation and responsible AI in finance (especially KA 16.4, privacy and cybersecurity).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios on handling confidential information,
including AI-tool use with entrusted data. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, certification suspension or
revocation for breach of confidence, and mandatory information-handling remediation on any
reinstatement.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-10 — Conflict-of-Interest Disclosure

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall identify actual, potential and perceived conflicts
between their personal, financial or organisational interests and their professional duties, shall
disclose them promptly and in writing to those entitled to know, and shall not act in a conflicted
matter unless the affected parties have been informed and appropriate safeguards — or withdrawal —
are in place.

**Purpose.** To protect the independence of professional judgement and the fairness owed to clients,
employers, counterparties and the public, on which the credibility of certified professionals rests.

**Scope.** All engagements, appointments, approvals, procurements, evaluations and certifications
within a PCI certification scope, including interests held through family, associates or investments
and interests in AI tools or vendors being selected or assessed.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain awareness of one's interests against current duties;
disclose conflicts on discovery, before acting where possible; record the disclosure and the agreed
safeguard (recusal, independent review, information barriers, or withdrawal); refresh disclosures
when circumstances change.

**Required evidence.** Written conflict disclosures, conflict registers where the organisation keeps
them, and records of safeguards or recusals applied.

**Decision owner.** The conflicted professional owns the duty to disclose; the decision on
safeguards rests with the person or body to whom the duty is owed, not with the conflicted party.

**Independent review.** Required whenever a conflicted professional remains involved in the matter
after disclosure; the review confirms the safeguard operates.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of an undisclosed conflict affecting a decision already taken; a
conflicted party resisting recusal; a conflict involving the most senior available decision-maker.

**Prohibited practice.** Concealing an interest; self-approving one's own work or procurement;
evaluating a tool, supplier or claim in which one holds an interest without disclosure; structuring
interests to evade disclosure.

**AI application.** AI may assist in screening engagements against declared-interest registers and
flagging potential conflicts for human assessment.

**AI restriction.** AI must not adjudicate whether a conflict exists or is acceptable; that
judgement belongs to the humans to whom the duty is owed.

**Verification requirement.** Before high-stakes decisions (awards, certifications, releases),
confirm current conflict declarations exist for the decision participants.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 17024 (ISO/IEC) — international standard whose impartiality
requirements for certification activity PCI characterises as the reference for managing conflicts in
assessment and certification roles. PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (Project Management
Institute) — professional guidance addressing fairness and conflict-of-interest disclosure. The
official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Public-procurement, anti-corruption and fiduciary laws impose specific
conflict and gift rules in many jurisdictions, particularly for public officials; obtain local legal
advice in public-sector and regulated-finance contexts.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-09, PCI-LAW-F-11, PCI-LAW-F-13.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PFL-AI KA 1.3 (conflicts and independence) and PML-AI KA 12.4 (ethical leadership).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios requiring recognition, disclosure and
management of conflicts. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, invalidation of PCI assessment
outcomes tainted by an assessor's undisclosed conflict, and certification suspension or revocation
for concealment.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-11 — Duty to Escalate

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate who becomes aware of a material threat to safety,
integrity, funds, data, delivery or honest reporting within their professional work — including
unverified AI output in reliance, misreporting, records tampering or practice beyond competence —
shall escalate it promptly through the applicable governance route, shall persist to the next level
if the response is inadequate, and shall not participate in suppressing it.

**Purpose.** To ensure that problems travel upward faster than they compound, and that no
professional treats silence as a neutral option.

**Scope.** All PCI-certified work; the duty applies whether the professional caused, discovered or
was merely informed of the matter, and survives instructions to stay silent.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Know the escalation route for one's role before it is needed;
report the matter to the accountable level with evidence and without editorialising delay; record
the escalation and response; where the route itself is compromised or the response inadequate,
escalate to the next independent level available.

**Required evidence.** Escalation records (what was raised, to whom, when, response), and the
underlying evidence preserved per PCI-LAW-F-05.

**Decision owner.** The professional aware of the matter owns the duty to escalate; the decision on
remedy belongs to the accountable governance level receiving it.

**Independent review.** Required where an escalation is closed without remedy and the escalating
professional maintains the concern; an independent function (assurance, audit, or PCI's conduct
process where PCI rules are engaged) reviews the closure.

**Escalation trigger.** This law is itself the trigger discipline: any material threat listed in the
Rule, and additionally any attempt to penalise or silence a good-faith escalation.

**Prohibited practice.** Sitting on known material problems; retaliating against, or assisting
retaliation against, a good-faith escalator; "escalating" into a channel known to be captive;
downgrading a threat to avoid governance attention.

**AI application.** AI may help detect escalation-worthy conditions (anomalies, threshold breaches,
integrity flags) and draft escalation summaries.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide that a matter need not be escalated, and automated
suppression or filtering of alerts must never be configured to serve concealment.

**Verification requirement.** Periodically verify that escalation routes function — that a raised
item reaches the accountable level and produces a recorded response.

**External references.** ISO 31000 (ISO) — international standard whose risk communication and
monitoring discipline PCI characterises as the frame for timely escalation. PMBOK Guide and PMI
standards (Project Management Institute) — professional guidance on governance and issue escalation.
FIDIC conditions of contract (FIDIC) — contract framework whose formal notice mechanisms PCI
characterises as the contractual analogue of escalation discipline; characterised generically, no
clause numbers cited. The official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Whistleblower-protection and mandatory-reporting laws differ sharply by
jurisdiction (and may require external reporting for some matters); obtain local legal advice before
external disclosure of organisational information.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-05, PCI-LAW-F-07, PCI-LAW-F-13.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PML-AI KA 3.3 (assurance, gates and escalation design).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios testing when and how to escalate, including
against pressure to stay silent. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding for failure to escalate or for
retaliation, certification suspension or revocation in serious cases, and protective handling of
good-faith escalations within PCI's own processes.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-12 — Record Retention

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall retain the records that evidence their professional
work — including decision records, verification records for AI-assisted work, data-lineage records
and issued reports — for the period required by the applicable law, contract, employer policy or PCI
certification rule, whichever is longest, shall dispose of them only through an authorised process,
and shall never destroy or alter records to frustrate review, audit or dispute.

**Purpose.** To keep the evidential basis of professional work available for as long as reliance,
accountability or review may reasonably reach back to it.

**Scope.** All records generated or received in PCI-certified work, in any medium, including model
versions, prompt/verification records for material AI-assisted outputs, and records held in personal
tools that form part of the professional record.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Know the retention rules that apply to one's records; keep
records in the organisation's system of record rather than in private silos; apply authorised
disposal only after the retention period ends and no hold applies; suspend disposal immediately when
a dispute, audit or investigation is reasonably foreseeable.

**Required evidence.** Retention schedules applied, disposal authorisations and logs, legal-hold
notices honoured, and the continued retrievability of in-period records.

**Decision owner.** The professional for records they control; the organisation's designated records
owner for shared systems; disposal decisions follow the authorised schedule, not individual
convenience.

**Independent review.** Required for the design of retention schedules and for any early or bulk
disposal outside the standing schedule.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of unauthorised destruction or alteration; instruction to delete
records connected to a foreseeable dispute or review; retention systems failing such that in-period
records become irretrievable.

**Prohibited practice.** Destroying, altering or "losing" records to defeat scrutiny; running
professional work through ephemeral channels to avoid creating records; disposing of AI verification
records while the outputs they support remain in reliance.

**AI application.** AI may assist with classification, retention-schedule application, retrieval and
disposal-log maintenance.

**AI restriction.** AI must not autonomously dispose of records; every disposal requires an
authorised human decision under the applicable schedule.

**Verification requirement.** Periodically test that sampled in-period records can be retrieved
intact, and that disposal logs reconcile with the authorised schedule.

**External references.** ISO 15489 records-management standards (ISO) — international standards for
records management; PCI characterises them as the reference discipline for creating, controlling and
disposing of records. ISO/IEC 27001 (ISO/IEC) — international standard for information-security
management, relevant to protecting retained records. The official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory retention periods (tax, employment, financial, health and
safety) and litigation-hold obligations are jurisdiction-specific and sometimes conflicting across
borders; obtain local legal advice when setting or shortening retention periods.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05, PCI-LAW-F-06, PCI-LAW-F-09.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PML-AI KA 16.4 (responsible archive and model/data retention).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios on what must be retained, for how long, and
what may never be destroyed. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, adverse inference in PCI
quality and conduct processes where records were improperly destroyed, and certification suspension
or revocation for destruction intended to defeat review.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-13 — Ethical Conduct Toward Candidates, Employers and the Public

**Rule.** A credential holder or candidate shall act with honesty, fairness and respect toward
candidates, colleagues, employers, clients, counterparties and the public; shall not deceive,
exploit, intimidate, discriminate unlawfully or knowingly cause unjustified harm in their
professional conduct; and shall not assist, direct or knowingly benefit from another person's breach
of these laws.

**Purpose.** To state the general standard of professional conduct that underlies every specific law
in this set, and to extend the professional's duty beyond the paying client to everyone their work
affects.

**Scope.** All professional conduct within a PCI certification scope, including conduct in PCI
examinations and CPD, supervision and mentoring of others, procurement and counterparty dealings,
public statements made in a professional capacity, and the deployment of AI systems whose outputs
affect people.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Deal honestly in professional representations; treat
candidates and subordinates fairly, including in assessment, credit for work and references; give
the public's safety and legitimate interests due weight in professional decisions; refuse to
participate in another's breach and escalate it per PCI-LAW-F-11.

**Required evidence.** Conduct is evidenced through the ordinary records of the work: communications
and representations made, assessment and supervision records, procurement dealings, and complaint
and escalation records where conduct is challenged.

**Decision owner.** Each professional owns their own conduct; a supervisor who directs unethical
conduct is accountable alongside those who execute it.

**Independent review.** Required where a professional exercises power over others' outcomes
(assessment, references, panel evaluation) and a fairness challenge is raised; PCI's conduct process
provides the independent forum where PCI rules are engaged.

**Escalation trigger.** Witnessing or being directed toward deception, exploitation, discrimination
or harm in professional work; becoming aware that one's own AI deployment is treating people
unfairly.

**Prohibited practice.** Deceiving any party in professional dealings; taking credit for others'
work; abusing supervisory or assessment power; deploying AI in ways known to treat individuals
unfairly or opaquely where fairness is owed; assisting another's breach of these laws.

**AI application.** AI may support fair-treatment monitoring, accessibility and clear communication
with affected parties.

**AI restriction.** AI must not be used to manipulate, deceive or unfairly profile candidates,
counterparties or the public; adverse decisions about individuals must not rest on unexplained AI
output.

**Verification requirement.** Where AI outputs affect decisions about people, verify them for
accuracy and fairness before use, and provide a human review route for those affected.

**External references.** PMI Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (Project Management Institute)
— professional guidance articulating honesty, responsibility, respect and fairness for delivery
professionals. OECD AI Principles (OECD) — voluntary framework on human-centred AI; never
legislation. Equator Principles (Equator Principles Association) and IFC Performance Standards (IFC)
— voluntary environmental and social risk-management frameworks relevant to finance professionals'
duties to affected communities; never described as legislation. The official publications govern.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Anti-discrimination, employment, consumer-protection and human-rights
law vary by jurisdiction and may impose stricter duties; obtain local legal advice for employment
and assessment practices and for AI systems making or supporting decisions about individuals.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-07, PCI-LAW-F-10, PCI-LAW-F-11, PCI-LAW-F-14.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PML-AI KA 1.2 (obligations to sponsor, team, users and public) and KA 11.4 (public and community
stakeholders), and PFL-AI KA 1.1 (stakeholder objectives including government, operator and
community).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through conduct scenarios spanning candidates, employers,
counterparties and the public. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: conduct finding, examination invalidation for
misconduct in assessment, certification suspension or revocation, and permanent exclusion from PCI
credentials in the gravest cases.

### PCI LAW PCI-LAW-F-14 — No Misrepresentation of PCI Credentials or Accreditation Status

**Rule.** A person shall claim a PCI credential, candidate status, examination result or PCI's own
accreditation or recognition status only as it actually stands; a credential holder or candidate
shall not overstate the scope, level or currency of any PCI credential, shall not imply PCI
endorsement of themselves, their employer, their products or their AI tools, and shall represent
lapsed, suspended or revoked status accurately without delay.

**Purpose.** To protect the meaning of PCI credentials for everyone who relies on them — employers,
clients, lenders and the public — and to keep claims about PCI's own status honest.

**Scope.** All representations of PCI credentials or status in any medium: titles, post-nominals,
CVs, proposals, professional profiles, marketing, product claims, AI-generated biographies and
statements made on the holder's behalf which the holder permits or fails to correct.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Use credential titles and post-nominals exactly as PCI awards
them; state candidate status as candidacy, never as certification; update all professional
representations promptly when status changes; correct third-party misstatements of one's PCI status
once known; describe PCI's accreditation and recognition status only in terms PCI has published.

**Required evidence.** The PCI credential register entry is the authoritative record of status;
holders retain their award and status correspondence and evidence of corrections made.

**Decision owner.** The individual making or permitting the representation; PCI Global is the sole
authority on what a credential or PCI's accreditation status actually is.

**Independent review.** None in the ordinary case — representations are checked against the PCI
credential register, which is the review mechanism; organisational marketing that references PCI at
scale should be reviewed against PCI's published usage terms before release.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of a false or inflated claim of PCI status by oneself, a
colleague, an employer or a product; discovery that AI-generated material (including auto-drafted
profiles) misstates one's credentials.

**Prohibited practice.** Claiming a credential not held, lapsed or revoked; presenting candidacy or
partial completion as certification; implying PCI accreditation, endorsement or partnership that
does not exist; permitting an employer or tool vendor to trade on a fabricated PCI association;
using another person's credential identity.

**AI application.** AI may draft biographies, proposals and profiles that mention PCI credentials,
subject to verification before publication.

**AI restriction.** AI must not be relied upon to state credential or accreditation status; every
AI-drafted claim about PCI status must be verified against the actual status before it is published.

**Verification requirement.** Before publication, verify every statement of PCI credential or
accreditation status against the PCI credential register or PCI's published statements; re-verify
when status could have changed.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 17024 (ISO/IEC) — international standard for bodies certifying
persons, which PCI characterises as addressing, among other matters, the conditions under which
certified persons may use certificates and related claims. The official publication governs. No
further external instrument is cited because the authoritative source for PCI status is PCI's own
credential register.

**Jurisdictional caution.** False claims of qualification may engage advertising, consumer-
protection, fraud or professional-title law in some jurisdictions; obtain local legal advice where
credential claims form part of regulated marketing or tendering.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01, PCI-LAW-F-04, PCI-LAW-F-08, PCI-LAW-F-13.

**Related book content.** PCL-AI BoK Domain 13 — AI for Project Controls & PM: Concepts, Tools &
Practice; PML-AI KA 1.4 — Ethics and the responsible use of AI and Domain 14 — Digital delivery,
data and responsible AI; PFL-AI Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance; also
PFL-AI KA 1.3 (ethics and professional obligations).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenarios on accurate representation of credentials and
status, including AI-drafted professional profiles. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Within PCI's authority: refusal or invalidation of examination and
certification, suspension or revocation of credentials, correction notices on the PCI credential
register, and permanent exclusion for fraudulent credential claims.
