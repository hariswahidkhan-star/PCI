# PCI Standards Examination Blueprint

**Instrument status: none.** This is a derived register. It is not a PCI Standard, it creates no obligation, and nothing in it may be cited as a requirement (Charter §3).

It is element 24 — examination application — of all 113 PCI Standards: which assessment mode each standard states, at what cognitive level, and for which credential’s examination. It was derived by extracting element 24 from each standard and matching it against the six assessment modes Drafting Manual §5 names. **It contains no examination items.** A blueprint states what may be assessed and how; live items are kept separate, and putting them in a derived register that is regenerated on every edit would compromise them. Generated by `make_standards_registers.py` from the four standard files. **It is derived, not authoritative: it goes stale the moment a standard file changes.** Re-run the generator after any edit, and read the standard itself before relying on any row here.

## 1. Summary

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Standards with an assessment mode stated | **113** of 113 |
| Standards stating exactly one mode | **1** |
| Mode assignments in total | **277** (mean 2.5 per standard) |
| Standards stating a cognitive level | **0** |

**Every one of the 113 standards states at least one assessment mode.** That is the blueprint’s strongest finding and it is unusual: the commonest failure in a competence framework is a requirement nobody can assess, and this corpus does not have one.

**No standard states a cognitive level.** Element 24 names the *mode* and the *case*, never the level of cognition demanded. The Cognitive level column below is therefore **derived, not extracted** — by the rule in §3 — and it is the one column in this blueprint that an examination designer must review rather than adopt.

## 2. Coverage — which modes are over- and under-used

| Assessment mode | Standards | Share | FND | PCL-AI | PFL-AI | PML-AI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| scenario judgement | **77** | 68% | 12 | 19 | 19 | 27 |
| evidence selection | **63** | 56% | 8 | 12 | 17 | 26 |
| escalation decision | **22** | 19% | 6 | 5 | 4 | 7 |
| calculation review | **53** | 47% | 5 | 14 | 14 | 20 |
| ethical dilemma | **46** | 41% | 10 | 12 | 10 | 14 |
| AI-verification case | **16** | 14% | 4 | 4 | 6 | 2 |

**Over-used: scenario judgement (77 standards, 68%), with evidence selection close behind (63, 56%).** More than two-thirds of the corpus is assessed the same way. A blueprint in which one mode carries that share produces an examination rewarding one skill — reading a situation and choosing a course — and under-testing the rest.

**Most under-used: the AI-verification case (16 standards, 14%).** This is the striking one. The corpus’s governing principle is *AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable*; every one of the 113 standards carries elements 14, 15 and 16 on AI; and **element 16 (AI verification) is normative**, so a breach of it is a breach of the standard. The mode that tests it is stated in 14 per cent of them. The gap between what the corpus regulates and what it examines is widest here.

**Second under-used: the escalation decision (22 standards, 19%).** Escalation is the act several of the most important standards exist to compel — `PCI-FND-STD-11` is an entire standard about it, `D-10` and `D-20` define when it starts and how fast it must travel, and three of the red team’s closed findings (**C-13**, **C-14**, **U-8**) were escalation thresholds with no clock. A corpus that makes escalation central and assesses it in 22 of 113 standards is not examining what it says matters most.

## 3. How the cognitive level was derived

No standard states one, so the column is generated by this rule, applied in order to the element 24 text. It is a first cut for an examination designer, not a finding:

| Level | Assigned when element 24 | 
|---|---|
| **Evaluation** — weigh competing duties and defend a choice | poses an ethical dilemma, a conflict of duty, an instruction under pressure |
| **Analysis** — locate the defect in a body of material | asks the candidate to locate, identify, distinguish or diagnose within a scenario |
| **Application** — apply a stated rule or method to a case | asks for a recomputation, a reproduction, a selection or an escalation decision |
| **Comprehension** — recognise the correct record or route | does none of the above |

**Recall is deliberately absent.** Manual §5 element 24 ends "not by memorising standard numbers", and no element 24 in the corpus invites recall. That is a strength worth stating.

**PCL-AI carries the thinnest coverage** on modes per standard. A credential whose standards are assessed through fewer routes is examined more narrowly, and a candidate can pass by being good at one thing. Note that the foundational standards are examined **inside all three** credential examinations, so their modes are counted once here and delivered three times in practice.

## 6. What this blueprint deliberately does not contain

**No examination items, item stems, distractors, marking keys or worked scenarios.** Element 24 of each standard describes the *shape* of a case — "an instruction to leave that one until next month from a person senior to the candidate" — and that description is reported here as the standard states it. Turning any of them into a live item is a separate exercise, kept in a separate place, on a different access control. A blueprint is regenerated on every edit to a standard file; live items must not be.
