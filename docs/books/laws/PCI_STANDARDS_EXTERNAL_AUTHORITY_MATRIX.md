# PCI Law External-Authority Matrix

**Instrument status: none.** This is a derived register. It is not a PCI Law, it creates no obligation, and nothing in it may be cited as a requirement (Charter §3).

It is element 17 — external reference — of all 113 PCI Standards: every instrument the corpus names, who issues it, the Drafting Manual §6 category the laws give it, which laws cite it, whether its currency was verified, and whether the citing law states its applicability limitation. It was derived by parsing every element 17 bullet in the corpus and joining it, by its `EXT-` identifier, to `../registries/EXTERNAL_AUTHORITIES.md`, which claims to be the single disclosure point for every external authority in the programme. **No edition, clause number or effective date is asserted here that a law or the register does not already assert.** Generated by `make_standards_registers.py` from the four law files. **It is derived, not authoritative: it goes stale the moment a law file changes.** Re-run the generator after any edit, and read the law itself before relying on any row here.

## 2. The four findings

### 2.1 Cited by a law, absent from the register — **4 instruments**

`../registries/EXTERNAL_AUTHORITIES.md` §1 states that it is "the single place where the programme records" each authority, and §13 says the law files’ instruments were added "so that this file remains the single disclosure point it claims to be". These are the gaps in that claim.

| Instrument, as the law names it | Cited by | Manual §6 category the law gives it |
|---|---|---|
| Suite External-Reference Register (`../registries/EXTERNAL_AUTHORITIES.md`) | `PCI-FND-STD-06` · `PCI-PFL-STD-16.02` | 9 — PCI internal professional law |
| Standards issued by the Accounting and Auditing Organisation for Islamic Financial Institutions | `PCI-PFL-STD-09.02` | 5 — Professional framework |
| Voluntary market principles for green, social and sustainability-linked instruments | `PCI-PFL-STD-09.03` | 8 — Voluntary environmental or social framework |
| Jurisdictional taxonomy, labelling and disclosure regimes | `PCI-PFL-STD-09.03` | 1 — Applicable legislation or regulation |

**AAOIFI is the one that matters.** `PCI-PFL-STD-09.02` names the standards of the Accounting and Auditing Organisation for Islamic Financial Institutions and gives them Manual §6 category 5 (professional framework), yet no `EXT-` row exists. The law’s own file records the gap at its end. It is also the law the red team declined to attack (**R-16**, out of its competence), and the one whose element 12 correctly records that **PCI has no authority to grant an exception at all**. An unregistered instrument in that law is the worst place in the corpus for one.

The remaining entries are of two kinds and neither is a defect of the same order: `../registries/EXTERNAL_AUTHORITIES.md` itself, cited as a PCI internal instrument (category 9), which cannot register itself; and two **deliberately generic** citations in `PCI-PFL-STD-09.03` — voluntary market principles for green, social and sustainability-linked instruments, and jurisdictional taxonomy, labelling and disclosure regimes — which name no publisher, title, edition or date **and say so**. Generic citation is the correct treatment where nothing was verified; registering them would require naming instruments nobody checked.

**The EU AI Act is the serious one.** `EXT-100`, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, is cited as **category 1 — applicable legislation or regulation** in `PCI-FND-STD-04`, `PCI-FND-STD-14`, `PCI-PFL-STD-16.01` and `PCI-PFL-STD-16.03`, and as **category 10 — illustrative practice** in `PCI-PML-STD-01.02` and `PCI-PML-STD-14.02`. Both are defensible readings of the corpus’s own conventions — the foundational file states that "**only** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is described as legislation, and only within the European Union", while `../registries/EXTERNAL_AUTHORITIES.md` §2 deliberately registers real legislation as an *illustrative reference* because that is how the corpus uses it. **They cannot both be right on the face of the citations**, and Manual §6 says the categories are never combined. This is the one item in this matrix that bears on whether a reader thinks an instrument binds them, and it should go to the Interpretation Panel rather than be corrected by a register.

The remaining five are lower-stakes but the same defect: an instrument that is *industry guidance* in one law and *illustrative practice* in another (NIST AI RMF, the OECD AI Principles), or *professional framework* in one and *industry guidance* in another (the FAST Standard, COSO, the G20/OECD Principles). In each case the volumes are consistent with themselves and inconsistent with each other, which points at the same cause as everything else in this pass: **the volumes were written independently.**

### 2.3 Categories 11 and 12 exist and no law uses them

Drafting Manual §6 was extended to twelve categories, and records the extension as a deliberate deviation from the programme brief, precisely because **ANSI/EIA-748 is a national standard and the Basel framework and a banking supervisor’s model-risk expectations are supervisory**. Every law in the corpus predates that extension in its citation practice:

| Instrument | What the laws say | What Manual §6 now provides |
|---|---|---|
| **ANSI/EIA-748** (`EXT-130`) | "a **national standard**; Manual §6 has no such category, so it is classified here at closest fit as **category 3**, with the mismatch stated" — in all four citing laws | **Category 11 — national standard**, added for this instrument by name |
| **The Basel Framework** (`EXT-110`) | **category 10 — illustrative practice**, with "internationally agreed supervisory standards with no legal force of their own" written out beside it | **Category 12 — supervisory guidance** |
| **SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12** (`EXT-102`) | **category 10 — illustrative practice**, with "supervisory guidance, jurisdiction-specific" written out beside it | **Category 12 — supervisory guidance** |

The laws are not *wrong* — each states the mismatch on the face of the citation, which is the honest treatment of a category that did not exist. But the Manual now provides the categories, and until the citations move, the corpus contains four laws asserting that Manual §6 offers no national-standard category when it offers one. **This is a citation-maintenance task, not a red-team finding**, and it does not change any obligation.

**Read this carefully before treating it as 47 defects.** Many of these bullets carry the limitation in substance — "binding only where imported by contract", "voluntary and non-regulatory", "it is not the source of this law’s obligation" — without the word *limitation* or *applicability* introducing it. The count is of citations that do not **label** the limitation, which is what makes it mechanically checkable and what a reader scanning for it will look for. The house styles differ by file, and that is the underlying cause: `PCI_FOUNDATIONAL_STANDARDS.md` labels every field, `PML_AI_STANDARDS.md` uses a `·`-separated run with `limitation:`, and the two certification files vary between them.
