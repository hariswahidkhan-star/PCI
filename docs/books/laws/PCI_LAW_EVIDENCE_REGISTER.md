# PCI Law Evidence Register

**Instrument status: none.** This is a derived register. It is not a PCI Law, it creates no obligation, and nothing in it may be cited as a requirement (Charter §3).

It is element 7 — required evidence — of all 113 PCI Professional Laws, resolved into artefact families, with the laws that require each, who produces it, who retains it and the retention basis where one is stated. It was derived by splitting each law’s element 7 into its listed artefacts, normalising each to an artefact family, and joining the families to element 8 (responsible role) of the citing laws. Element 7 is **normative** under Manual §5.0: what appears here must exist for the law to be complied with. Generated by `make_law_registers.py` from the four law files. **It is derived, not authoritative: it goes stale the moment a law file changes.** Re-run the generator after any edit, and read the law itself before relying on any row here.

**Retention.** No element 7 in the corpus states a retention period. Retention is set once, by `PCI-FND-LAW-12` (Record Integrity), and the records owner (`D-23`) is accountable for the system of record; where the adopting organisation designates none, the issuing professional is the records owner for the records they control. The *Retention basis* column below therefore reads **`PCI-FND-LAW-12` + `D-23`** unless the law itself says otherwise, and the two places it says otherwise are named in §4.

|---|---|---|
| **The independent verification record** | `PCI-FND-LAW-03` element 7, and every certification law whose element 10 requires independence | needs a second person who did not prepare the item; a sole practitioner has none, and `PCI-FND-LAW-03` element 12 makes no waiver available where the item supports a payment, an irreversible commitment, an external report or a safety decision (red-team **R-2**) |
| **The three-signature payment record** | `PCI-PFL-LAW-14.04` element 7 and `-PR-02` | requires three named individuals for prepare, authorise and reconcile, with no *de minimis* (red-team **R-8**) |
| **The outbound-traffic and prompt-history search** | `PCI-FND-LAW-09` element 21(d), evidenced from element 7’s system register | requires a search of outbound traffic, prompt history, shared-drive and mailbox records; a two-person firm has no such logging, and several jurisdictions restrict the search (red-team **R-7**) |
| **The independent assurance opinion and the assurance-capture record** | `PCI-PML-LAW-01.03`, `PCI-PML-LAW-11.01` | presumes a standing assurance or internal-audit function separate from the delivery team |

The first is the serious one. `PCI-FND-LAW-03` element 12 permits a waiver only where **no** person meeting the independence test exists *and* no external reviewer can be engaged — and it withdraws the waiver altogether where the item supports an irreversible commitment, a payment to a third party, an external report or a safety decision. For a two-person firm essentially every material estimate supports a payment. The practical effect is that the foundational law requires a **paid external reviewer on almost every material output of a very small practice**. That may be the right answer; it is not obviously the intended one, and it is red-team **R-2**, still open.

Three artefacts are **producible but disproportionate** at the bottom of the size range rather than impossible: the tool configuration record per deliverable (`PCI-PCL-LAW-13.01`), the period-by-period detriment indicator (`PCI-PML-LAW-12.02-PR-06`, whose denominator on a small project may be two), and the capacity assessment per constrained pool (`PCI-PML-LAW-15.02`).

**What the register cannot tell you** is whether the burden as a whole is proportionate. That is Charter §5 Stage 7, and Stage 6 practitioner consultation has not been performed for any of the four files.
