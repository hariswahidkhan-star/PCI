# PFL-AI Professional Laws — PCI AI Project Finance Leader

**Status:** Certification Laws for the **PFL-AI** credential (AI Project Finance Leader), drafted
under and subordinate to the binding specification in [`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md). Twenty-four
laws, anchored to the sixteen-domain PFL-AI Body of Knowledge (`../pfl-ai/`).

> PCI Professional Laws are professional certification rules and standards of conduct established by
> PCI Global. They are not legislation, regulatory requirements or substitutes for applicable law,
> contractual obligations or authoritative professional standards. Where any applicable law,
> regulation, contract or authoritative professional standard imposes a stricter requirement, that
> requirement governs.

---

## How to read these laws

- **The drafting specification governs.** Definition, hierarchy, normative language, the mandatory
  field template, external-reference rules, the visual system and stable identifiers are all defined
  in [`LAW_SYSTEM.md`](LAW_SYSTEM.md). These laws conform to it; where any doubt arises, that
  specification prevails.
- **Identifiers are stable.** Each law is cited as `PFL-LAW-DD-NN`, where `DD` is the two-digit
  Body-of-Knowledge domain of primary anchorage and `NN` a sequence within that domain. Cite laws by
  ID, never by page number. A law that spans domains anchors to the domain that teaches it and lists
  the others under *Related book content*.
- **Normative language is deliberate.** `must` / `must not` / `shall` / `shall not` state mandatory
  professional rules; `should` states recommended practice from which departure needs a recorded
  reason; `may` is discretionary. Text is labelled, every time, as **Mandatory PCI Law**,
  **Mandatory PCI Rule**, **Recommended PCI Practice**, **External requirement**,
  **Illustrative example** or **Jurisdictional requirement** — never mixed.
- **External references are characterised, never reproduced.** Real instruments are named and
  described in PCI's own words; the official publication always governs. No clause numbers or
  editions are asserted unless verified.
- **Nothing here is legal, tax, accounting or investment advice.** These laws set professional
  conduct within PCI's certification scope. Every treatment, contractual position and structure they
  touch is jurisdiction-specific and requires advice from a qualified adviser for the specific
  entity, structure and date — a duty that is itself a law of this set (PFL-LAW-12-01).
- **The suite principle** applies to every law in this set, in its one approved formulation:

> **AI proposes; the professional verifies, decides and remains accountable.**

## Domain 1 — Foundations of project finance leadership

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-01-01 — Cash Before Appearance

**Rule.** A credential holder shall test every financing, structuring and investment judgement
against the project's cash position and the dates on which its obligations fall due, and shall not
present an accounting result, an earnings measure or an averaged ratio as evidence that a project
can meet an obligation when it becomes payable.

**Purpose.** A project financing fails on the day cash is not in the account, whatever the reported
profit. Accrual results, non-cash charges, revaluations and annualised averages can present a
comfortable picture over an account that will be empty on the payment date.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare, review or approve appraisals, forecasts, funding
plans, coverage assessments, lender reports, credit submissions or board papers, on any asset class
and any financing structure.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify the cash available for debt service and the dated
obligations it must meet; reconcile the accounting result to that cash line; state non-cash items
separately; test liquidity at each payment date rather than on an annual average; and disclose
wherever a favourable presentation depends on non-cash items, timing conventions or a horizon
choice.

**Required evidence.** The dated cash-flow forecast with each obligation on its due date; the
reconciliation from accounting profit to cash available for debt service; the schedule of non-cash
adjustments; the liquidity test at each payment date; the approver's sign-off.

**Decision owner.** The project finance leader accountable for the funding plan; the sponsor's,
lender's or board's approving officer for the decision that relies upon it.

**Independent review.** Required wherever a coverage or liquidity conclusion supports sanction,
financial close, a drawing, a distribution or a waiver request.

**Escalation trigger.** A forecast showing an obligation that cannot be met from available cash at
its due date, however presented; or an instruction to answer a solvency, coverage or funding
question on an accounting basis in place of a cash basis.

**Prohibited practice.** Offering an earnings measure, accounting profit or an annualised average as
evidence of ability to pay on a date; netting a dated cash shortfall against an unrealised or
non-cash gain; smoothing or re-phasing timing to conceal a shortfall; describing an accrued receipt
as available cash.

**AI application.** AI may build and re-phase cash-flow projections, reconcile accrual results to
cash, detect timing mismatches between obligations and receipts, and generate liquidity stress runs.

**AI restriction.** AI must not conclude that a project is solvent, adequately funded or able to
meet an obligation, nor approve a funding plan or a liquidity position.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute the cash available for debt
service for the binding periods from source lines, confirm each obligation date against the
underlying document, and confirm that AI-produced reconciliations tie to the released model and to
the financial statements.

**External references.** IFRS Foundation *Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting*
(authoritative accounting standard) — establishes the accrual basis and faithful representation,
which is precisely what makes an accounting result a different statement from a cash fact. IAS 7
*Statement of Cash Flows*, IFRS Foundation (authoritative accounting standard) — the presentation
and classification of cash movements against which a modelled cash line is reconciled. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Solvency, distributable-reserve and insolvency tests are matters of
local company and insolvency law and are not established by a cash forecast; obtain local legal and
accounting advice before any statement about the ability of an entity to continue or to pay.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PFL-LAW-10-01; PFL-LAW-14-01; PFL-LAW-15-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 1, KA 1.2 — Value, cash and risk (1.2.2 cash, not profit, is the
binding constraint); Domain 2, KA 2.1 — The accrual model; KA 2.2 — The three statements and their
articulation; Domain 10, KA 10.1 — Debt capacity and sizing (cash available for debt service).

**Examination relevance.** Assessed through scenario items in which a profitable-looking forecast
conceals a dated liquidity shortfall, and the candidate must identify the binding period and the
compliant presentation. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-01-02 — Conflict Disclosure and the Two-Hat Rule

**Rule.** A credential holder shall disclose in writing, before advising, modelling, negotiating or
approving, every interest that could reasonably be seen to influence their judgement on a financing,
and shall not act for more than one side of a transaction, or on a matter in which an undisclosed
interest exists, without the informed written consent of every affected party.

**Purpose.** Project finance concentrates advice: the same individual or firm can sit close to
sponsor, lender, grantor and contractor. An undisclosed interest converts advice into advocacy while
preserving the appearance of objectivity, and the parties relying on it cannot see the change.

**Scope.** All credential holders advising, modelling, structuring, reviewing, lending, approving or
negotiating on a project financing, whether in-house or as an external adviser, including their
personal financial interests and those of connected persons where these relate to the transaction.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a standing conflicts register; screen every new
instruction against it; disclose in writing before work begins and again whenever a new interest
arises; obtain written consent or decline the engagement; where an information barrier is used,
document it and test that it operates in practice.

**Required evidence.** The conflicts register with dates and outcomes; written disclosures and
consents; engagement terms recording role and side; information-barrier records and test results;
records of engagements declined for conflict.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for making their own disclosure; the engaging
organisation's responsible partner or officer for whether the engagement may proceed and on what
terms.

**Independent review.** Required whenever a credential holder or their firm acts for more than one
party to the same transaction, and whenever a disclosed conflict is managed by information barrier
rather than by declining.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of an undisclosed interest; an instruction to withhold or delay a
disclosure; a consent obtained on incomplete information; a mandate that would require advising both
sides on the same question.

**Prohibited practice.** Acting on both sides without informed written consent; accepting
undisclosed fees, commissions or success-linked benefits from a party other than the client; using
transaction information for personal or connected benefit; describing advice as independent while
holding an undisclosed interest.

**AI application.** AI may screen new instructions against the conflicts register and entity
relationship data, surface related-party links, monitor for newly arising interests, and draft
disclosure wording for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine whether a conflict exists, decide that a conflict is
manageable, waive a disclosure, or certify a person or firm as independent.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must check AI-generated conflict screens
against the register and against their own knowledge of relationships, and must retain the screening
evidence; a negative machine screen is not a finding of no conflict.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 17024 *Conformity assessment — General requirements for bodies
operating certification of persons* (international standard) — sets requirements including
impartiality and the management of conflicts, within which PCI's own certification processes
operate. OECD *Principles of Corporate Governance* (voluntary framework) — intergovernmental,
non-binding principles addressing disclosure, related-party transactions and the management of
conflicts in governance; not legislation. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Fiduciary duty, agency law, anti-bribery and financial-promotion rules
determine the legal consequences of an undisclosed interest and differ by jurisdiction; obtain local
legal advice before relying on consent as a cure.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-10; PCI-LAW-F-13; PFL-LAW-13-01; PFL-LAW-16-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 1, KA 1.3 — Ethics, fiduciary awareness and responsible AI (1.3.1
obligations and duties; 1.3.2 conflicts and independence); Domain 1, Toolkit 1.T.3 — Conflicts and
AI-use register; Domain 1, Case study B — the adviser with two hats; Domain 13, KA 13.1 — The
diligence streams.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which an adviser holds a second role, interest or fee
arrangement, testing whether the candidate discloses, declines or proceeds and on what record. No
live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 4 — Investment appraisal and capital budgeting

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-04-01 — Appraisal Discipline

**Rule.** A credential holder shall appraise an investment on a stated and internally consistent
basis — cash flows and discount rate on the same inflation and currency basis, one stated horizon,
one stated perspective — shall report the measure appropriate to the decision, and shall not select,
re-base or re-cut a measure in order to produce a preferred conclusion.

**Purpose.** Appraisal outputs are decision instruments. Nominal cash flows discounted at a real
rate, an internal rate of return quoted where the sign of the cash flows changes more than once, or
a horizon chosen after the answer is known, all produce numbers that are precise and wrong.

**Scope.** All credential holders producing or approving investment appraisals, screening analyses,
bid economics, refinancing evaluations, acquisition or disposal analyses and exit valuations.

**Minimum professional requirement.** State the basis on the face of the appraisal — real or
nominal, currency, pre- or post-tax, project or equity perspective, horizon and terminal-value
treatment; apply one consistent rate basis throughout; disclose the pathologies of any rate-of-return
measure quoted and report net present value alongside it; state the hurdle rate and who owns it; and
retain every case run, including those that did not support the decision.

**Required evidence.** The appraisal file with its stated basis; the identified model version used;
the hurdle-rate derivation and its approval; the full case set including unfavourable cases; the
sensitivity or breakeven output; the approver's sign-off.

**Decision owner.** The investment decision owner — investment committee or board; the project
finance leader owns the integrity of the appraisal placed before them.

**Independent review.** Required for appraisals supporting sanction, bid submission, acquisition or
disposal, and for any change to the hurdle rate or to the appraisal basis.

**Escalation trigger.** An instruction to change the horizon, rate or basis after the result is
known; discovery that a decision was taken on an inconsistent basis; the removal of an unfavourable
case from a decision pack.

**Prohibited practice.** Mixing real and nominal quantities; quoting a rate of return alone where
sign changes or scale differences make it unsafe; extending the horizon or inflating a terminal
value to reach a hurdle; discarding cases; presenting a single point estimate with no statement of
uncertainty.

**AI application.** AI may compute the measures, generate and run the case set, detect real–nominal
and currency inconsistency, produce net-present-value profiles, and assemble sensitivity tables.

**AI restriction.** AI must not set the hurdle rate, choose the appraisal basis or horizon, select
which case is reported, or take the investment decision.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute the headline measure
independently from the cash-flow line, confirm that the rate and the cash flows share one basis, and
confirm that the reported case set is complete before the appraisal is issued.

**External references.** IAS 36 *Impairment of Assets*, IFRS Foundation (authoritative accounting
standard) — for financial-reporting purposes it requires a recoverable amount determined on a
defined basis, with consistency between the projected cash flows and the rate applied to them; the
same consistency discipline underlies appraisal. ISO 31000 *Risk management — Guidelines*
(international standard) — the treatment of uncertainty in decision-making. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Tax rates, allowances and their timing determine any post-tax appraisal
and are jurisdiction- and entity-specific; obtain local tax advice before relying on a post-tax
result or presenting it to a decision-maker.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PFL-LAW-06-03; PFL-LAW-09-01; PFL-LAW-12-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 4, KA 4.1 — The discounted measures (4.1.2 IRR and its
pathologies); KA 4.3 — Decision contexts (4.3.3 the limits of the numbers); 4.A.2 —
Inflation-consistent appraisal; 4.A.3 — Reconciling the hurdle to the structure; Domain 3, KA 3.3 —
Real-world adjustments (3.3.1 the Fisher relation).

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting an appraisal whose rate and cash flows are on
different bases, or whose measure has been chosen to suit the answer, for the candidate to detect
and correct. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 5 — Project development and bankability

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-05-01 — The Bankability Test

**Rule.** A credential holder shall state a project's bankability as a conjunction of conditions all
of which must hold — revenue and payment mechanism, offtake or grantor covenant, permits, land and
consents, technology and track record, construction and operational readiness, and an allocated risk
position — and shall not represent a project as bankable while any of those conditions is
unresolved, unless the gap, its owner and its resolution path are stated with it.

**Purpose.** Bankability is not a score and does not average. A project described as bankable on the
strength of its strongest feature consumes development capital, misleads investors and lenders, and
fails at the condition nobody was tracking.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare or endorse bankability assessments, development-gate
papers, information memoranda, investment or credit submissions, and lenders' staff who rely on
them.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Assess each condition separately against evidence; record
status, owner, supporting evidence and residual gap for each; maintain a live gap register from
development to close; restate bankability whenever a condition changes; and state clearly wherever
the assessment rests on an assumption rather than an executed instrument.

**Required evidence.** The condition-by-condition assessment with evidence references; the gap
register with owners and dates; the permit, land and consent schedule; the contract status list;
technology and track-record evidence; the version history of the assessment.

**Decision owner.** The development or project finance leader who signs the bankability assessment;
the sanctioning body for the decision to continue spending against it.

**Independent review.** Required at each development gate, before an information memorandum or
credit submission is issued, and whenever a previously satisfied condition reopens.

**Escalation trigger.** A condition moving from satisfied to unresolved; pressure to publish a
bankability statement while a load-bearing condition is open; development spend continuing past a
gate whose kill criteria are met.

**Prohibited practice.** Presenting bankability as a weighted score that conceals an unsatisfied
condition; presenting an indicative offer, unsigned term sheet or draft permit as an executed
instrument; omitting a known consent, land or technology gap from a lender-facing document.

**AI application.** AI may assemble the condition register from documents, track permit and consent
status, compare a project's condition set against comparable financings, and flag items with no
supporting evidence.

**AI restriction.** AI must not declare a project bankable, reduce the conditions to a single score
presented as a bankability conclusion, or approve a development gate.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify each condition's status against
the underlying instrument or authority record rather than against a summary, and must trace every
AI-extracted status to its source document before it enters a lender-facing paper.

**External references.** Equator Principles (voluntary framework) — a risk-management framework
adopted by participating financial institutions for environmental and social risk in project
finance; adoption is voluntary and it is not legislation. IFC Performance Standards (voluntary
framework) — the International Finance Corporation's environmental and social performance
standards, widely used as a reference benchmark by lenders. ISO 31000 *Risk management —
Guidelines* (international standard) — the risk process within which conditions and gaps are
assessed. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Permits, land tenure, concession award and licensing are
jurisdiction-specific and may be open to administrative challenge; obtain qualified local legal
advice on the validity, transferability and conditionality of every consent relied upon.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PCI-LAW-F-11; PFL-LAW-07-01; PFL-LAW-11-01; PFL-LAW-13-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 5, KA 5.3 — The bankability test (5.3.1 bankability as a
conjunction; 5.3.2 the revenue model and the offtake; 5.3.3 permits, land and consents; 5.3.4
technology and the bankable track record); KA 5.4 — Construction and operational readiness; 5.A.2 —
Conditions precedent: the bankability list made contractual.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a project is presented as bankable while a
permit, offtake or technology condition remains open, testing whether the candidate identifies the
binding gap and what they do with it. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 6 — Financial modelling

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-06-01 — Model Integrity

**Rule.** Every financial model a credential holder builds, relies upon or presents for decision
must separate inputs, calculations and outputs, must carry a check block that computes its
arithmetic invariants as differences that must resolve to nil, and must not be quoted, circulated
or relied upon while any check is failing.

**Purpose.** The model is the transaction's shared instrument of belief: debt is sized on it,
covenants are set from it and a board sanctions against it. Structure is what makes it reviewable by
someone who did not build it, and the check block is what makes the model's silence mean something.

**Scope.** All credential holders who build, review, audit, operate or rely upon project financial
models — base case, bank case, bid model, financial-close model, operating and budget models,
restructuring models — in any tool.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Build in separated blocks with no backward flow; build the
check block before the outputs; compute at minimum the balance-sheet balance, sources equal to uses
in every construction period, debt fully repaid at maturity for every facility, scheduled principal
equal to total drawings, no negative cash balance in any period or account, and a defined
reconciliation of the cash-available-for-debt-service line to the statements; display one aggregate
flag; and stop reporting when it fails.

**Required evidence.** The model file with a visible check block and aggregate flag; the check
results for the version quoted; the structural review record; model documentation describing the
block layout, flags and conventions.

**Decision owner.** The named model owner for the model itself; the project finance leader for what
is quoted from it.

**Independent review.** Required before a model is used for debt sizing, credit submission,
financial close, distribution testing or restructuring, and after any structural change.

**Escalation trigger.** A check failing in a model on which a decision has already been taken; a
request to circulate outputs while the aggregate flag is failing; discovery of calculation logic
inside the output block or of assumptions inside the calculation block.

**Prohibited practice.** Quoting from a model with failing checks; deleting, disabling or narrowing
a check to clear a flag; building outputs that recompute rather than present; offering "the balance
sheet balances" as evidence that a model is correct.

**AI application.** AI may scan whole workbooks for structural defects, propose the check block,
generate adversarial test cases that break a waterfall, and maintain a regression suite of verified
figures rerun after every edit.

**AI restriction.** AI must not certify a model as correct, clear or waive a failing check, decide a
modelling convention, or be recorded as the model's owner.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute at least one construction
period and one operating period by independent means for every released version, and must confirm
each machine-listed finding individually before it is reported.

**External references.** ICAEW *Financial Modelling Code* (professional guidance) — principles for
structuring, documenting and reviewing spreadsheet-based financial models. The FAST Standard
(industry practice) — a published set of spreadsheet-modelling conventions whose name stands for
flexible, appropriate, structured and transparent. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Model structure is a professional discipline rather than a legal
requirement; the treatments the model implements — tax, accounting, covenant definitions — are
jurisdiction- and document-specific and are governed by PFL-LAW-10-02 and PFL-LAW-12-01.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-05; PFL-LAW-06-02; PFL-LAW-06-05; PFL-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 6, KA 6.1 — Model architecture (6.1.1 the three-block rule; 6.1.2
the timeline as the model's spine); KA 6.4 — Checks, sensitivity, model audit and AI controls (6.4.1
the check block and the six invariants); 6.A.3 — The reviewer's model eye; Domain 13, KA 13.2 —
Model audit.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting a model that balances while carrying a
consistent error, testing whether the candidate knows which check localises which failure and what
may be quoted. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-06-02 — Formula Transparency

**Rule.** A credential holder shall express every calculation as a visible, traceable formula
referencing a declared input, shall not embed a constant, an override or a pasted value inside a
calculation, and shall not present as calculated any figure that was typed.

**Purpose.** A hard-coded constant is invisible to a reviewer, survives every scenario switch and
produces a model that is stable in the wrong answer. A pasted value looks like a result and is only
a memory of one.

**Scope.** All model builders and reviewers, and all calculation content in decision-grade work,
including side calculations, extracts and analyses circulated outside the master model.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Keep the calculation block free of numerals other than
disclosed structural constants; one row, one formula, copied across; state the sign convention once
and enforce it everywhere; where an override is unavoidable, record it as an input with an owner, a
reason and an expiry; and convert or remove side calculations before release.

**Required evidence.** Formula-scan results for the released version; the disclosed list of
structural constants; the override register with owner, reason and expiry; the reviewer's structural
sign-off.

**Decision owner.** The named model owner; the author of any circulated extract for that extract.

**Independent review.** Required before first decision-grade use and after any change touching the
calculation block; a machine formula scan does not satisfy this requirement.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery of a hard-coded value behind a reported figure; a formula that
changes part-way along a row; an override still live beyond its stated expiry.

**Prohibited practice.** Typing a number into a formula; pasting values over links; hiding rows or
sheets that contain calculations; protecting or obscuring the calculation block to prevent review;
presenting a typed figure as a model output.

**AI application.** AI may scan at whole-workbook scale for embedded constants, mid-row formula
changes, inconsistent references and orphaned cells, and may draft plain-language explanations of a
formula for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide that a constant is acceptable, remove a flagged item from a
findings list, or declare a workbook clean.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm every machine-raised finding
individually, record the sample size used for any machine-produced finding list, and trace the
model's headline figures cell by cell back to declared inputs.

**External references.** The FAST Standard (industry practice) — spreadsheet-modelling conventions
directed at transparency and consistent structure. ICAEW *Financial Modelling Code* (professional
guidance) — build, documentation and review principles for financial models. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** None as to transparency itself, which is a modelling discipline; the
treatments the formulae implement remain jurisdiction-specific and are covered by PFL-LAW-12-01.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PFL-LAW-06-01; PFL-LAW-06-04; PFL-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 6, KA 6.1 — Model architecture (6.1.1 the cardinal defect is the
hard-coded constant); KA 6.4 — Checks, sensitivity, model audit and AI controls (6.4.4 AI-assisted
modelling controls); Domain 6, Case study B — the forecast that was pasted, not linked; Domain 13,
KA 13.2 — Model audit (findings by class).

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items containing an embedded rate or a pasted forecast row,
testing detection, correction and the consequences for figures already reported. No live examination
content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-06-03 — Assumption Traceability

**Rule.** Every assumption in a model must be entered once, in the input block, and recorded in an
assumption register that travels with the model, stating value, unit, basis, source, date and named
owner; an assumption without a recorded owner and basis shall not be used in a decision-grade case.

**Purpose.** Assumptions are where a model's judgement lives. A number nobody owns cannot be
challenged, updated or defended, and a case that differs from another in ways nobody recorded is not
a case at all.

**Scope.** All inputs — macroeconomic, technical, commercial, tax, financing and timing — in every
case, including sponsor case, bank case, bid case, downside cases and any case shown to a third
party.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Enter each assumption once; maintain the register with value,
unit, basis, source, date, owner and the cases to which it applies; record the difference between
sponsor case and bank case assumption by assumption; date-stamp time-sensitive assumptions and
re-confirm them before reliance; and identify the assumptions to which the conclusion is sensitive.

**Required evidence.** The assumption register issued with the model version; the case comparison
showing every difference; re-confirmation records for rates, prices and indices; the sensitivity or
breakeven output identifying the binding assumptions.

**Decision owner.** The named owner of each assumption; the model owner for the register's
completeness and its agreement with the input block.

**Independent review.** Required before debt sizing, credit submission and financial close, and
whenever a binding assumption changes.

**Escalation trigger.** A decision resting on an assumption with no owner or source; a case
difference discovered that was never recorded; an assumption changed between versions without a
record; a stale assumption carried into a current case.

**Prohibited practice.** Entering the same assumption in more than one place; changing an assumption
to reach a target ratio without recording the change, its basis and its owner; describing a
sponsor-case assumption as agreed by lenders; presenting an assumption as evidenced when it is
judgement.

**AI application.** AI may draft the assumption register from the input block so that the two cannot
drift apart, detect duplicate entries and stale values, and compare cases assumption by assumption.

**AI restriction.** AI must not set or change an assumption value, own an assumption, or decide
which case is the base case or the bank case.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must trace each material assumption to its
source document and confirm its currency, and must reconcile the register to the input block for
every released version.

**External references.** ICAEW *Financial Modelling Code* (professional guidance) — documentation of
assumptions and inputs in financial models. ISO 8000 data quality series (international standard) —
concepts and requirements for the quality of data used in decision-making. The official publications
govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Tax, escalation-index and regulatory assumptions are jurisdiction- and
entity-specific; they must be confirmed by qualified local advisers before a case is described as
reflecting them.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-06; PFL-LAW-06-04; PFL-LAW-07-01; PFL-LAW-12-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 6, KA 6.1 — Model architecture (6.1.3 basis, horizon and case); KA
6.4 — Checks, sensitivity, model audit and AI controls (6.4.4 AI-assisted modelling controls);
Domain 3, Toolkit 3.T.1 — TVM assumption register; Domain 8, KA 8.1 — Estimate classes and the
contingency a range implies.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which two cases differ in undocumented ways, or a
target ratio is achieved by an unrecorded assumption change, testing register discipline and
escalation. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-06-04 — The Source Line

**Rule.** Every input to a decision-grade model, and every figure quoted from it in a lender-facing,
board-facing or investor-facing document, must carry a source line identifying the document, its
version or date and the party that issued it; a figure whose source line cannot be produced on
request must be withdrawn from the document.

**Purpose.** The source line is the join between the model and the evidence. It is what makes a
workbook diligence-ready, what makes a paste detectable, and what allows a figure to be defended
years later in a claim, a refinancing or a dispute.

**Scope.** All inputs to decision-grade models and all quoted outputs — information memoranda,
credit papers, board packs, compliance certificates, draw requests, valuations and public
statements.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Record for each input the source document, the issuing party,
the version or date and the location within it; keep the source document, or a controlled reference
to it, in the transaction record; quote only from a released model version; and mark derived figures
as derived, identifying the derivation.

**Required evidence.** The input-to-source mapping covering every input; the referenced document
set; the model version register; the log tying each published figure to a model version and a source
line.

**Decision owner.** The model owner for the inputs; the author and signer of a document for the
figures quoted in it.

**Independent review.** Required at model audit, before financial close, and whenever a quoted
figure is challenged.

**Escalation trigger.** A quoted figure whose source cannot be produced; a source document
superseded without the model being updated; discovery of a value entered from memory, from a
conversation or from an unattributed spreadsheet.

**Prohibited practice.** Entering a value with no source; citing "the model" as the source of a
model input; quoting figures from an unreleased or superseded version; presenting an estimate as an
evidenced value.

**AI application.** AI may extract values from source documents and propose source lines, detect
inputs lacking provenance, and match quoted figures back to model cells and versions.

**AI restriction.** AI must not attest the accuracy of a source line, select which version of a
document is authoritative, or supply a value for which no source exists.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify sampled AI-extracted values
against the original document rather than an extract, and must confirm that every figure in a
lender-facing document ties to the released model version and its source lines.

**External references.** ISO 8000 data quality series (international standard) — data quality
requirements including provenance. ISO 15489 *Information and documentation — Records management*
(international standard) — capture, integrity and retention of the records that evidence a
transaction. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Data-protection, confidentiality and cross-border transfer rules
constrain which source documents may be copied, exported or stored, and where; obtain local legal
advice before moving transaction documents into external systems.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-06; PFL-LAW-06-03; PFL-LAW-13-03.

**Related book content.** Domain 6, KA 6.4 — Checks, sensitivity, model audit and AI controls (6.4.4
provenance for every input); Domain 6, Case study B — the forecast that was pasted, not linked;
Domain 13, KA 13.1 — The diligence streams (13.1.5 the data room and the diligence trail); Domain

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a headline figure in a credit paper cannot be

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-06-05 — Model Change Governance

**Rule.** A model that has been used for a decision must not be changed except under version
control: each change shall be recorded in a change log with author, date, reason and effect; the
check block and the regression suite shall be rerun; every movement shall be explained before
acceptance; and one authoritative version shall be identified. No change shall reach the
authoritative file without appearing in the change log under a named human author.

**Purpose.** A transaction model is edited continuously, by several hands, under time pressure.
Without version control the parties stop looking at the same project, and nobody can say when a
number changed, why, or on whose authority.

**Scope.** All models used for decisions, reporting or certification — base case, bank case,
financial-close model, operating and budget models, restructuring models — and every AI-assisted
edit to them.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain one authoritative file with a named owner and a
version identifier; log every change; rerun the check block and a regression suite of independently
verified figures after every edit, machine or human, and explain each movement before accepting it;
lock the agreed close model and treat later changes as new versions; and distribute controlled
versions rather than allowing local copies to circulate.

**Required evidence.** The version register; change-log entries with author, date, reason and
effect; regression results per version with explanations; the locked close model with its version
identifier and integrity control; the distribution record.

**Decision owner.** The model owner for the model; the lender's or sponsor's approver for any change
to an agreed or locked base case.

**Independent review.** Required for changes to a locked or agreed model, for changes affecting
covenant definitions, debt sizing or the waterfall, and at the revalidation interval set for
high-consequence models in the model inventory.

**Escalation trigger.** A change to a locked model without approval; an unexplained movement in a
regression figure; two versions in circulation bearing the same name; an AI edit discovered in the
authoritative file with no change-log entry.

**Prohibited practice.** Editing an agreed model without logging the change; distributing an
unlabelled copy; accepting an unexplained regression movement; recording a tool as the author of a
change; representing an updated model as the version agreed at close.

**AI application.** AI may produce version diffs as human-readable change lists, run the regression
suite, draft change-log entries for confirmation, and identify every cell that moved between
versions.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve a change, be recorded as its author, decide that a
regression difference is acceptable, or release a version.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm that every diff item corresponds
to an intended change, explain every regression movement, and sign the version as released before it
is used or distributed.

**External references.** *Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management* (SR 11-7), issued by United
States banking supervisors (public supervisory guidance) — expectations for model development,
implementation, use, validation and governance in supervised institutions in that jurisdiction.
ISO/IEC 42001 *Artificial intelligence — Management system* (international standard) — organisational
management of AI systems, including lifecycle and change controls. ICAEW *Financial Modelling Code*
(professional guidance) — version control and documentation of financial models. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Where a model is a contractual deliverable or a condition of financing,
the finance documents determine which version governs and which changes require consent; obtain
qualified legal advice on the contractual status of the model before amending an agreed version.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-12; PFL-LAW-06-01; PFL-LAW-13-03; PFL-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 6, KA 6.4 — Checks, sensitivity, model audit and AI controls (6.4.3
model audit, governance and its economics; 6.4.4 AI-assisted modelling controls); 6.A.2 — Model
reuse, templates and the drift problem; Domain 16, KA 16.3 — Explainability, validation, bias and

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a locked close model has moved, or a regression

## Domain 7 — Revenue, demand and commercial models

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-07-01 — Revenue Assumption Discipline

**Rule.** A credential holder shall characterise every revenue stream by its actual contractual and
market basis — contracted or merchant, volume-based or availability-based, indexed or fixed, and by
the credit standing of the party that pays — shall carry demand and price assumptions at values
supported by stated evidence, and shall not present a forecast revenue as though it were contracted.

**Purpose.** Revenue is where the coverage ratios come from, and the whole capital structure is
sized on it. A demand forecast dressed as a contract is the most expensive error in project finance
because every other number inherits it.

**Scope.** All credential holders preparing or reviewing revenue assumptions, demand forecasts,
tariff and payment-mechanism models, counterparty credit assessments and revenue stress cases for
appraisal, debt sizing, credit submission, refinancing or restructuring.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify the contract that produces each revenue line and the
mechanism by which it pays; state which portion is contracted and for how long; state the
counterparty and its credit standing; carry ramp-up assumptions explicitly with their evidential
basis; map indexation on revenue against indexation on cost and state any mismatch; run and retain a
stated downside case; and disclose the merchant tail beyond the contracted horizon.

**Required evidence.** The revenue basis note tying each stream to its contract; the market or
demand study with author, date and scope; counterparty credit evidence; the indexation mapping;
stress-case definitions and results; the statement of contracted horizon against merchant tail.

**Decision owner.** The commercial or project finance leader who signs the revenue basis; the credit
approver for the case adopted in sizing.

**Independent review.** Required where revenue assumptions drive debt sizing or a bid price, where a
market study is relied upon by lenders, and whenever a demand or price forecast is revised upward.

**Escalation trigger.** An upward revision of demand or price with no new evidence; discovery that a
contracted volume or availability payment is lower than modelled; deterioration in the credit
standing of a paying counterparty; removal of a downside case from a decision pack.

**Prohibited practice.** Presenting a forecast as contracted; relying on an unattributed market study
or one whose scope does not cover the question; modelling a ramp faster than any evidence supports;
assuming a cost pass-through the contract does not provide; deleting the downside case.

**AI application.** AI may generate scenario sets and coverage tests, detect anomalies in historical
demand data, extract payment-mechanism terms from contracts for human confirmation, and test
indexation arithmetic across cases.

**AI restriction.** AI must not set a demand or price assumption, judge a market study adequate,
characterise a revenue stream as contracted, or decide which case is the bank case.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm each characterisation against
the executed contract, confirm the scope and date of any market study relied upon, and recompute the
revenue build for at least one full period from its drivers.

**External references.** IFRS 15 *Revenue from Contracts with Customers*, IFRS Foundation
(authoritative accounting standard) — the contract-based analysis governing when revenue is
recognised for reporting purposes, which is a different question from when cash is received. ISO
31000 *Risk management — Guidelines* (international standard) — the treatment of uncertainty in
forecasting and stress testing. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Tariff regulation, price controls, subsidy regimes and
consumer-protection rules can alter a revenue mechanism after financial close and differ by
jurisdiction; obtain local regulatory and legal advice before relying on a mechanism's durability.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PFL-LAW-05-01; PFL-LAW-06-03; PFL-LAW-10-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 7, KA 7.1 — Contracted and merchant revenue (7.1.1 the revenue-risk
spectrum; 7.1.3 availability payments and the deduction mechanism); KA 7.2 — Demand models (7.2.1
demand forecasting and the ramp); KA 7.3 — Price escalation and volume risk (7.3.1 indexation
architecture and margin drift); KA 7.4 — Counterparty credit quality and revenue stress testing
(7.4.3 the bank case); 7.A.2 — The merchant tail and the contracted horizon.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which an availability payment, a minimum volume and a
merchant forecast are presented interchangeably, testing correct characterisation and its effect on
sizing. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or

## Domain 9 — Funding structure and sources of capital

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-09-01 — Capital-Structure Justification

**Rule.** A credential holder shall justify a proposed capital structure — gearing, tranching,
tenor, amortisation profile, hedging and the cost of each instrument — against the project's
cash-flow profile, its risk allocation and its required coverage, and shall not propose or accept a
structure whose feasibility depends on a refinancing, a further contribution or market conditions
that have not been stated as assumptions.

**Purpose.** Leverage is a claim on the same cash that must absorb risk. A structure that works only
if a refinancing is available at an assumed price has moved a market risk into the future without
pricing it, and the party that discovers this is usually equity at its worst moment.

**Scope.** All credential holders who structure, arrange, advise on, approve or review funding
plans, term sheets, tranche design, hedging strategy, government support, concessional funding and
refinancings.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Build the structure from the cash-flow profile and the
required coverage rather than from a target gearing; state each source's cost, tenor, ranking,
security and conditions; derive and state the blended cost including fees and credit-enhancement
cost; identify refinancing, tail and mini-perm exposure explicitly as an assumption; state hedging
policy and the residual exposure it leaves; and test the structure against the downside case.

**Required evidence.** The funding plan and sources-and-uses statement; the tranche schedule with
terms; the blended-cost derivation; the refinancing assumption note; the hedging policy and hedge
ratio calculation; downside-case coverage results; the approvals obtained.

**Decision owner.** The sponsor's finance director or investment committee for the structure
adopted; the arranger's credit approver for what is offered.

**Independent review.** Required before commitment to a term sheet, before financial close, and
before a refinancing or a material amendment to the structure.

**Escalation trigger.** A structure that meets coverage only in the sponsor case; reliance on an
unstated refinancing or on support not yet documented; hedging that leaves an undisclosed residual
exposure; a tranche whose conditions the project cannot meet.

**Prohibited practice.** Sizing debt to a target gearing and then adjusting assumptions to fit;
presenting a blended cost that omits fees, margins or credit-enhancement cost; describing an
indicative offer as committed capital; describing grant or concessional support as secured before it
is documented.

**AI application.** AI may generate structure options against stated constraints, compute blended
costs and hedge ratios, test profiles against coverage requirements, and draft comparative
sources-and-uses analyses.

**AI restriction.** AI must not select the capital structure, commit to or accept terms, set hedging
policy, or determine that a refinancing assumption is reasonable.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute coverage under the proposed
structure from the model's cash line, confirm each source's terms against the offer or executed
document, and confirm that all fees and costs are captured in the stated blended cost.

**External references.** Basel framework, Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (international
framework implemented through national regulation) — internationally agreed standards for bank
capital, leverage and liquidity that shape lenders' appetite, pricing and tenor; it takes effect
only as national authorities implement it. OECD *Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits*
(voluntary framework among participating authorities) — agreed terms and disciplines for officially
supported export credit. Equator Principles (voluntary framework) — environmental and social risk
management adopted by participating financial institutions, which conditions the availability of
some funding. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Thin-capitalisation rules, interest deductibility, withholding tax,
exchange control and the perfection of security differ by jurisdiction and can change both the
economics and the enforceability of a structure; obtain local tax and legal advice.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PFL-LAW-04-01; PFL-LAW-10-01; PFL-LAW-12-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 9, KA 9.1 — Equity and shareholder instruments (9.1.4 the leverage
trade-off); KA 9.2 — Senior, subordinated and mezzanine debt (9.2.3 the blended cost of a
multi-tranche stack); KA 9.3 — Islamic finance, export credit and development finance (9.3.4
assembling a multi-source stack); KA 9.4 — Government support, sustainable finance and refinancing;
9.A.1 — Why capital-structure irrelevance fails in a project SPV; Domain 11, KA 11.3 — Interest-rate

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a structure passes coverage only on the sponsor

## Domain 10 — Debt sizing, covenants and credit metrics

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-10-01 — Debt-Service Coverage Truth

**Rule.** A credential holder shall compute and report coverage ratios on the definitions set out in
the finance documents, from a cash-available-for-debt-service line reconciled to the financial
statements, and shall report the minimum ratio over the tested horizon alongside any average; a
coverage figure quoted without its definition, its period basis and its minimum is not a coverage
statement.

**Purpose.** Coverage is the number on which debt is sized, distributions are permitted and default
is declared. An average conceals the period that binds, and a definition borrowed from a textbook
rather than from the facility can flatter or breach a covenant that was never tested.

**Scope.** All credential holders who size debt, prepare or review compliance certificates, credit
submissions, distribution tests, refinancing analyses or restructuring proposals.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Apply the documented definitions of cash available for debt
service and of debt service, including every deduction the documents require; reconcile the cash
line to the statements; report the minimum and the average with the binding period identified; state
whether the test is historic, forward-looking or both and whose projection it uses; and disclose
every adjustment made.

**Required evidence.** The ratio calculation traceable to a released model version and to source
lines; the definition used with a reference to the document in which it appears; the reconciliation
to the statements; the period-by-period ratio series; the compliance certificate and its workings.

**Decision owner.** The finance director or project finance leader who signs the certificate or
credit submission; the credit approver for the sizing adopted.

**Independent review.** Required before financial close, before any certificate on which lock-up or
default depends, and at model audit.

**Escalation trigger.** A computed ratio at or below a covenant level in any period; a definitional
difference between the model and the finance documents; an adjustment that materially improves a
ratio and has not previously been applied.

**Prohibited practice.** Quoting an average in place of a period test; including in the cash line
items the definition excludes; moving cost out of a tested period; annualising to obscure a seasonal
shortfall; presenting a textbook ratio as the covenant ratio.

**AI application.** AI may compute ratio series across cases, identify the binding period, test
sculpting and sizing arithmetic, and generate stress runs and reconciliations for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not interpret a covenant definition, decide whether an item belongs in
the cash line, certify compliance, or determine debt capacity.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute the ratio for the binding
period and at least one other period from source lines, confirm the definition against the executed
document, and reconcile the cash line to the statements before certifying or submitting.

**External references.** IAS 7 *Statement of Cash Flows*, IFRS Foundation (authoritative accounting
standard) — the presentation and classification of cash flows against which a modelled cash line is
reconciled. Basel framework, Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (international framework
implemented through national regulation) — the supervisory context for lenders' credit assessment
and monitoring, within which coverage measurement sits. The official publications govern; verify
current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Coverage definitions are contractual rather than statutory; their
construction and the consequences of a breach are matters for the governing law of the finance
documents and for qualified counsel — see PFL-LAW-10-02.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-07; PFL-LAW-01-01; PFL-LAW-06-04; PFL-LAW-10-02; PFL-LAW-15-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 10, KA 10.1 — Debt capacity and sizing (10.1.1 cash available for
debt service; 10.1.2 sizing from coverage); KA 10.2 — The coverage ratios (10.2.1 the period test;
10.2.2 the horizon tests; 10.2.4 reading a ratio set together); 10.A.3 — The reviewer's coverage

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting an average coverage figure that conceals a

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-10-02 — Covenant Interpretation

**Rule.** A credential holder shall apply the covenants, definitions, test dates and cure mechanics
set out in the executed finance documents, shall not substitute a market convention, a textbook
definition or another transaction's terms, and shall not state a legal conclusion on the meaning or
consequence of a covenant — that opinion belongs to qualified counsel.

**Purpose.** Covenants are how lenders keep a project fixable while options remain. The credential
holder's duty is to compute and report faithfully against the documented terms, and to recognise the
point at which computation becomes legal construction.

**Scope.** All credential holders who monitor, model, certify, negotiate or advise on financial,
information, positive and negative covenants, distribution lock-up, events of default and cure
rights.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a covenant register recording, for each covenant, the
document and clause in which it appears, its definition set, test dates, thresholds, cure rights and
consequences; test on the defined dates using the defined inputs; document any interpretive question
and refer it to counsel rather than resolving it in the model; and record every waiver and amendment
as a change to both the register and the model.

**Required evidence.** The covenant register with document references; compliance certificates and
their workings; the record of interpretive questions referred and the advice received; the waiver
and amendment log with its effect on the register; the covenant dashboard version history.

**Decision owner.** The finance director or project finance leader for compliance reporting; the
lenders' agent for the lenders' position; qualified counsel for interpretation.

**Independent review.** Required when a covenant position approaches a threshold, before any waiver
or amendment request, and whenever a definition changes.

**Escalation trigger.** A position at or approaching a lock-up or default level; a definitional
ambiguity that changes the result; a proposed amendment whose effect on the register has not been
assessed; an event that may constitute a default.

**Prohibited practice.** Computing a covenant on a definition other than the documented one;
presenting a market convention as the transaction's requirement; giving a legal opinion on breach,
notice or cure; delaying notification of a likely breach to preserve a reporting position; treating
a waived condition or covenant as satisfied.

**AI application.** AI may extract covenant terms and definitions from documents into a register for
human confirmation, monitor positions against thresholds, generate early-warning alerts, and draft
compliance certificates for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not interpret a covenant, determine that a breach has or has not
occurred, certify compliance, or advise on cure, waiver or amendment.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm every extracted covenant term
against the executed document, re-confirm the register after every amendment, and refer rather than
resolve any question of legal meaning.

**External references.** IAS 1 *Presentation of Financial Statements*, IFRS Foundation
(authoritative accounting standard) — the presentation of financial statements, including the basis
on which liabilities are classified as current or non-current, which is where a covenant position
meets the reported position. Basel framework, Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (international
framework implemented through national regulation) — the supervisory context for lenders' covenant
monitoring. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** The construction of covenant language, the effect of a breach, notice
requirements and the enforceability of cure rights are determined by the governing law of the
finance documents; obtain qualified legal advice and never act on a modelled interpretation.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-08; PCI-LAW-F-11; PFL-LAW-10-01; PFL-LAW-12-01; PFL-LAW-15-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 10, KA 10.4 — Covenants, default and cure (10.4.1 covenant types;
10.4.2 distribution lock-up; 10.4.3 events of default and cure rights; 10.4.4 living with
covenants); 10.A.1 — Forward-looking tests and whose forecast counts; Domain 15, KA 15.3 — Waiver,
cure and amendment.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a modelled definition differs from the documented
one, or a candidate is asked to opine on whether a breach has occurred, testing the boundary between

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or

within PCI's authority only.

## Domain 11 — Risk identification and allocation

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-11-01 — Risk Allocation Honesty

**Rule.** A credential holder shall allocate each material risk to the party best able to control,
absorb or price it, shall record where the risk actually lands once contracts, caps, exclusions,
insurance and counterparty credit are read together, and shall not represent a risk as transferred
where that reading leaves it with the project.

**Purpose.** The allocation matrix is the transaction's real structure. A matrix that reads
"transferred" where a cap, an exclusion or a weak counterparty leaves the exposure with the project
company is worse than no matrix, because it stops anyone looking.

**Scope.** All credential holders who build or review risk registers and allocation matrices, price
risk transfer, negotiate contracts, or rely on an allocation for appraisal, sizing, credit approval
or contingency setting.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a register at allocation granularity; for each
transfer identify the instrument, the cap, the exclusions, the counterparty and its credit standing;
compute the residual exposure that survives cap and credit quality; identify gaps between packages
and interface risks; price retained risk into contingency and reserves; and keep the register live
through construction and operations.

**Required evidence.** The risk register and allocation matrix with document references; the cap and
exclusion analysis; the guarantee sufficiency assessment; the insurance schedule with its
exclusions; the residual-risk quantification feeding contingency; the register's version history.

**Decision owner.** The project finance leader or designated risk owner who signs the allocation;
the sanctioning body that accepts the residual position.

**Independent review.** Required before term sheet, before financial close, when a counterparty's
credit standing changes, and when a contract carrying an allocation is amended.

**Escalation trigger.** Discovery that a risk recorded as transferred is in fact retained; a
downgrade affecting a load-bearing guarantee; an interface gap identified after commitment; a
realised risk exceeding the provision made for it.

**Prohibited practice.** Recording a risk as transferred without naming the instrument and its cap;
valuing a guarantee without regard to the guarantor's credit standing; presenting insurance as cover
without reading its exclusions; suppressing a known interface gap; counting one mitigation against
several risks.

**AI application.** AI may assemble registers from the document set, extract caps and exclusions for
confirmation, detect risks addressed in no document, and simulate the distribution of retained risk.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide an allocation, conclude that a risk is transferred, judge a
guarantee sufficient, or approve a residual risk position.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm each transfer against the
executed contract, test the sufficiency arithmetic independently, and confirm the register's
completeness against the full document set rather than against a summary.

**External references.** ISO 31000 *Risk management — Guidelines* (international standard) — the
risk-management process and the treatment of uncertainty. FIDIC suite of contract forms,
International Federation of Consulting Engineers (contract framework) — standard forms widely used
as the basis for negotiated construction risk allocation; the executed contract governs. IFC
Performance Standards and the Equator Principles (voluntary frameworks) — environmental and social
risk expectations adopted by many lenders and often carried into project documents. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** The enforceability of caps, liquidated damages, indemnities and
exclusion clauses varies with governing law, as does insurance regulation; obtain qualified legal
advice before relying on a transfer as effective.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-11; PFL-LAW-05-01; PFL-LAW-09-01; PFL-LAW-12-01; PFL-LAW-14-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 11, KA 11.1 — Construction and completion risk (11.1.2 the two

Guarantee sufficiency; KA 12.4 — The allocation matrix as a document map.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a capped, excluded or weakly guaranteed transfer

## Domain 12 — Contracts and transaction structure

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-12-01 — The Advice Boundary

**Rule.** A credential holder shall not give legal, tax, accounting, regulatory or insurance advice,
shall confine their work to the commercial and financial analysis within their competence, and shall
obtain written advice from an adviser qualified in the relevant jurisdiction before any treatment,
contractual position or structure is adopted or represented as correct.

**Purpose.** Project finance rests on jurisdictional facts. A modelled tax rate is a commercial
assumption, not a tax opinion; a summarised clause is a reading, not a construction. Presenting
either as advice transfers risk to a client who believes it has been advised.

**Scope.** All credential holders in every role, including those qualified in another discipline —
the PFL-AI certification scope does not extend any licence held elsewhere.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Identify at the outset which questions require qualified
advice; state on every deliverable that legal, tax and accounting treatments are assumptions pending
advice; obtain written advice identifying adviser, entity, jurisdiction, scope and date before a
treatment is adopted; carry that advice into the model and the assumption register; and re-confirm
when the structure, entity, jurisdiction or law changes.

**Required evidence.** The register of advice obtained with adviser, scope, jurisdiction, entity and
date; the list of assumptions flagged as pending advice; the deliverable's stated limitation;
correspondence recording each referral; the assumption-register lines citing the advice.

**Decision owner.** The credential holder for recognising the boundary and referring; the client or
employer for instructing the adviser; the qualified adviser for the advice itself.

**Independent review.** Required before financial close and before any transaction document,
information memorandum, certificate or filing represents a tax, accounting or legal position.

**Escalation trigger.** An instruction to model or state a treatment without advice; discovery that
a relied-upon treatment was never advised; a change of law, entity, structure or jurisdiction
affecting advice already given; advice inconsistent with what the model assumes.

**Prohibited practice.** Stating a tax, legal or accounting conclusion as fact; carrying a treatment
across from another transaction or jurisdiction; relying on general commentary or an AI summary in
place of advice for the specific entity and structure; describing an unadvised assumption as a
treatment.

**AI application.** AI may locate material for a qualified adviser, draft the questions on which
advice is sought, compare structures for discussion, and summarise documents for the professional's
own understanding.

**AI restriction.** AI must not give legal, tax, accounting or regulatory advice, determine a
treatment, construe a contract, or stand in place of a qualified adviser.

**Verification requirement.** Any AI-produced summary of a document or treatment must be checked
against the source instrument by a competent professional and must never be used as advice; every
treatment relied upon must be traceable to written advice from a qualified adviser.

**External references.** IAS 12 *Income Taxes*, IFRS Foundation (authoritative accounting standard)
— accounting for current and deferred tax; it governs financial reporting, not the tax position in

**Jurisdictional caution.** Every matter named in this law is jurisdiction-specific: tax residence,
withholding, deductibility, indirect taxes, stamp duties, licensing, security perfection, insurance
regulation and accounting treatment all differ and all change. Advice must be obtained for the
specific entity, structure and date, and re-confirmed when any of those change.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-08; PCI-LAW-F-14; PFL-LAW-06-03; PFL-LAW-10-02;

## Domain 13 — Due diligence and financial close

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-13-01 — Independence of Review

**Rule.** A model audit, diligence review or lender's adviser report relied upon by any party other
than its author's own team must be performed by a person independent of the work reviewed and of its
outcome; a credential holder shall not review their own work, and shall not accept a review
engagement whose fee, continuing mandate or benefit depends on the conclusion reached.

**Purpose.** A review is worth exactly its independence. Reviews performed by the builder, or by a
party rewarded for a comfortable answer, deliver the appearance of assurance to third parties who
cannot see the arrangement and who price the transaction as though assurance existed.

**Scope.** Model audit, every diligence stream, lenders' technical, financial, insurance and market
advisers, internal second-line review, and any credential holder asked for an opinion on which
another party will rely.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Confirm independence in writing before accepting; decline
where the reviewer built or materially contributed to the work, reports to its author, or holds an
interest in the outcome; agree scope in writing including what is not covered; report findings by
class rather than as a single total; report every finding, including those the engaging party would
prefer omitted; and retain the working papers.

**Required evidence.** The independence confirmation; the scope and reliance letter; the findings
register with classification and status; the working papers and test record; the final report and
its distribution list; the record of any disputed finding and its resolution.

**Decision owner.** The reviewer for the review's conclusions; the engaging party for scope and
reliance arrangements.

**Independent review.** The review is itself the independent step; where a conclusion is challenged
or a finding is closed without correction, a second reviewer independent of the first must consider
the point.

**Escalation trigger.** Pressure to reclassify, downgrade or omit a finding; a scope limitation that
prevents an opinion; discovery that the reviewer is not independent; a finding closed without either
correction or documented acceptance.

**Prohibited practice.** Auditing a model one built or materially contributed to; accepting a
success fee or contingent benefit tied to the outcome; issuing a report that omits known findings;
allowing the reviewed party to edit the findings; describing a limited review as a full audit.

**AI application.** AI may run structural scans, generate test cases, compare versions, and draft
the findings register for the reviewer's confirmation.

**AI restriction.** AI must not be the reviewer, sign a review report, close a finding, or supply
the independence this law requires.

**Verification requirement.** The reviewer must confirm every machine-produced finding individually
before reporting it, record the verification sample size for any machine-produced list, and
re-perform a defined portion of testing manually.

**External references.** IESBA *International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants (including
International Independence Standards)* (professional guidance) — an international ethics code
addressing objectivity, independence and the threats to them, binding on those subject to it.
ISO/IEC 17024 (international standard) — impartiality requirements in the certification of persons.
Equator Principles (voluntary framework) — review processes for environmental and social risk
adopted by participating financial institutions. The official publications govern; verify current
requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Statutory audit, licensing and professional-body independence rules
differ by jurisdiction and may impose stricter requirements than this law; where a review is a
regulated engagement, those requirements govern — obtain local advice.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-10; PCI-LAW-F-13; PFL-LAW-01-02; PFL-LAW-06-05; PFL-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 13, KA 13.2 — Model audit (13.2.1 scope and what the audit is not;

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-13-02 — Conditions Precedent

**Rule.** A credential holder shall treat a condition precedent as satisfied only when the evidence
the finance documents require has been delivered and accepted by the party entitled to it, shall
record a waived or deferred condition as waived or deferred and never as satisfied, and shall not
certify or report that conditions are met while any remains outstanding.

**Purpose.** The condition schedule is the bankability list made contractual: it is the last point
at which the parties can decline to proceed. A condition recorded as met on the strength of an
expectation removes the protection every party negotiated for.

**Scope.** All credential holders who compile, track, certify or rely upon condition schedules for
first drawing, subsequent drawings, financial close, completion or the release of security.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain the schedule as a dependency network with owner,
required evidence, status and date for each condition; identify the critical path through it; hold
status against delivered evidence rather than intention; record waivers, deferrals and post-close
undertakings separately with their long-stop dates; and report the outstanding set honestly to all
parties before close.

**Required evidence.** The condition schedule with evidence references and dates; the evidence pack;
the waiver and deferral log with conditions and expiry dates; the satisfaction certificate; the
correspondence recording acceptance.

**Decision owner.** The party entitled to the benefit of the condition — usually the lenders acting
through their agent — decides satisfaction or waiver; the borrower's project finance leader owns the
accuracy of the status reported.

**Independent review.** Required before the satisfaction certificate is issued and before any
drawing that depends on a condition recorded as outstanding, waived or deferred.

**Escalation trigger.** A condition reported satisfied without its evidence; a waiver granted with
no recorded condition or expiry; a long-stop date at risk; a post-close undertaking approaching its
deadline unmet.

**Prohibited practice.** Reporting a condition satisfied on an undertaking to deliver; back-dating
evidence; describing a waived condition as satisfied; omitting a deferred condition from the close
report; drawing against a condition the entitled party has not accepted.

**AI application.** AI may track the schedule, extract required conditions from documents into a
checklist for confirmation, monitor the dependency network and its critical path, and generate
status reports.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine that a condition is satisfied, grant or record a waiver,
or issue a satisfaction certificate.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must match every condition to its specific
evidence document and to the acceptance record before its status changes, and must reconcile any
AI-extracted condition list to the executed documents.

**External references.** ISO 15489 *Information and documentation — Records management*
(international standard) — the capture and integrity of records evidencing satisfaction. IFC
Performance Standards and the Equator Principles (voluntary frameworks) — environmental and social
requirements that participating lenders frequently carry into conditions and undertakings. The
official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** What constitutes valid evidence of corporate authority, a permit, a land
right, an insurance endorsement or a perfected security interest is determined by local law, as is
the effect of a legal opinion delivered at close; obtain qualified local legal advice.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PFL-LAW-05-01; PFL-LAW-13-03; PFL-LAW-14-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 13, KA 13.3 — Conditions precedent and documentation (13.3.1 the
condition set and its categories; 13.3.2 the conjunction with a critical path; 13.3.5 waivers,
long-stop dates and post-close undertakings); Domain 5, 5.A.2 — Conditions precedent: the
bankability list made contractual; Domain 14, KA 14.1 — The draw request and the conditions to each

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which close is pressed with conditions outstanding or

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-13-03 — Financial-Close Evidence

**Rule.** At financial close a credential holder shall ensure that the executed document set, the
agreed and locked base-case model version, the final sources-and-uses statement, the
condition-satisfaction record and the funds-flow instruction are captured as one complete, retained
and identifiable record, and shall not treat a transaction as closed on an incomplete or unretained
record.

**Purpose.** Close is the moment the transaction's terms become fixed. Every later question — a
covenant test, a claim, a refinancing, a dispute, a restructuring, a handback — is answered from the
close record or is not answered at all.

**Scope.** All credential holders who manage, advise on or participate in financial close, and all
who later rely upon the close record.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Assemble the closing index and document set; identify the
locked model version and apply an integrity control to it; record the final sources and uses; retain
the condition evidence and every waiver; retain the funds-flow instruction and the payment
confirmations; record the time of effectiveness; and assign retention responsibility and period.

**Required evidence.** The closing index; the executed documents; the locked model with its version
identifier and integrity control; the final sources-and-uses statement; the satisfaction certificate
and waiver log; the funds-flow statement and confirmations; the retention record.

**Decision owner.** The transaction lead for the completeness of the close record; the borrower or
its agent for its retention and controlled access.

**Independent review.** Required as a completeness check after close and before archiving, and
whenever the record is relied upon in a dispute, refinancing or restructuring.

**Escalation trigger.** A document executed but not captured; a model version quoted at close that
cannot be identified; a payment made that is not on the funds-flow statement; any indication that
the record has been altered after close.

**Prohibited practice.** Declaring close with an incomplete index; substituting a later model for
the agreed version without recording the substitution; destroying records subject to a retention
obligation; storing the record where the retention or confidentiality obligation cannot be met.

**AI application.** AI may index the document set, cross-check the index against the executed
documents, extract key terms into a summary for confirmation, and check the funds-flow arithmetic.

**AI restriction.** AI must not declare the record complete, certify that close has occurred, or
determine what must be retained.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm the index against the executed
documents item by item, confirm that the identified model version is the one quoted in the credit
decision, and confirm the retention arrangements before the record is archived.

**External references.** ISO 15489 *Information and documentation — Records management*
(international standard) — capture, integrity, retention and disposition of records evidencing a
transaction. ISO/IEC 27001 *Information security management systems* (international standard) —
controls for the integrity of and access to the closing record. The official publications govern;
verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Retention periods, acceptable evidential form (originals, counterparts,
electronic execution) and data-protection constraints differ by jurisdiction; obtain local legal
advice before setting or applying a retention and storage arrangement.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-12; PFL-LAW-06-05; PFL-LAW-13-02; PFL-LAW-14-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 13, KA 13.4 — Syndication and financial close (13.4.5 signing,
close and funds flow); KA 13.3 — The close-cost budget and sources and uses; KA 13.1 — The data room
and the diligence trail; Domain 14, KA 14.1 — The sources-and-uses statement as a control document.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a later question cannot be answered because the

## Domain 14 — Construction monitoring and drawdown

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-14-01 — Drawdown Integrity

**Rule.** A credential holder shall support every draw request with certified evidence of the value
being funded, a current cost-to-complete demonstrating that the project remains in balance, and
confirmation that the conditions to that drawing are satisfied; and shall not request, certify or
approve a drawing for work not performed, for costs not incurred or evidenced, or while the project
is out of balance without disclosing it.

**Purpose.** Drawdown is where the financing meets the construction. A draw request is an arithmetic
claim, an evidential claim and a representation, and it is on the representation that a monitoring
relationship is actually conducted.

**Scope.** Borrowers' and sponsors' finance teams, lenders' staff and their technical advisers, and
every credential holder who prepares, certifies, checks or approves draw requests, contingency draws
or equity contribution certificates.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Build the request from certified value with retention and
advance recovery applied; attach the certification the documents require; produce a cost-to-complete
from the current forecast and reconcile it to the project controls estimate at completion;
demonstrate that remaining commitments cover it; confirm that no default is continuing and that the
representations remain true; and disclose every change in forecast since the last request.

**Required evidence.** The draw request and its build-up; the independent engineer's or technical
adviser's certificate; the cost-to-complete and in-balance test; the contingency position and
approval for any contingency draw; the underlying valuations and invoices; the record of who made
each representation.

**Decision owner.** The borrower's authorised signatory for the request and its representations; the
lenders' agent, on the technical adviser's certificate, for the advance.

**Independent review.** Required to the extent the finance documents require certification for each
drawing, and specifically before any contingency draw and wherever the in-balance test is close.

**Escalation trigger.** A cost-to-complete exceeding remaining commitments; a certificate refused or
qualified; a variation not yet reflected in the forecast; funding sought for work whose value or
entitlement is disputed.

**Prohibited practice.** Certifying value for work not in place; funding an invoice already funded;
drawing to build cash outside the documented purposes; applying contingency without the required
approval; presenting an out-of-balance position as in balance by deferring a known cost.

**AI application.** AI may assemble draw requests from certification and cost data, reconcile them
to the sources-and-uses statement, detect duplicate or out-of-sequence claims, and recompute
cost-to-complete from project controls data.

**AI restriction.** AI must not certify value or progress, make or generate the representations in a
draw request, approve a drawing, or determine that the project is in balance.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm certified quantities against the
certificate, check the arithmetic chain independently, and reconcile the lender's cost-to-complete
to the project controls estimate at completion, explaining every difference.

**External references.** FIDIC suite of contract forms, International Federation of Consulting
Engineers (contract framework) — standard forms under which interim certification and payment
mechanisms are commonly structured; the executed contract governs. ISO 15489 (international
standard) — records management for the certification and payment record. The official publications
govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Construction payment legislation, retention rules, security-of-payment
regimes and lien or priority rules affect certification, payment and recovery, and differ by
jurisdiction; obtain local legal advice.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-07; PFL-LAW-11-01; PFL-LAW-13-02; PFL-LAW-14-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 14, KA 14.1 — Sources and uses, draw requests and conditions

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items in which a draw is prepared while the cost-to-complete has

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-14-02 — Funds-Flow Control

**Rule.** Project funds shall move only through the accounts, in the order and for the purposes the
finance documents specify, on instructions authorised by named signatories under segregation of
duties; a credential holder shall not initiate, approve or conceal a payment outside the documented
funds flow.

**Purpose.** The funds flow is the transaction's plumbing and its principal fraud surface. Money
that leaves the accounts in the wrong order, to the wrong purpose or to the wrong payee is rarely
recovered, and payment-instruction fraud is aimed precisely at close day and at drawdown day.

**Scope.** All credential holders involved in account establishment, close-day funds flow,
construction payments, operating waterfall payments, reserve movements and distributions.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain the account map and mandates; require dual
authorisation with preparation separated from approval; verify payee details through a channel
independent of the instruction that provided them; execute close-day movements against a single
agreed funds-flow statement; reconcile actual movements to sources and uses; and report every
exception.

**Required evidence.** The account map and bank mandates; the funds-flow statement and payment
confirmations; payment authorisations identifying each signatory; bank reconciliations; payee
verification records; the exception log and its closure record.

**Decision owner.** The authorised signatories for each payment; the finance director for the
control design; the account bank or agent for operating the accounts as instructed.

**Independent review.** Required at account establishment, before close-day funds flow, and
periodically over payment controls during construction and operations.

**Escalation trigger.** A payment instruction that varies from the funds-flow statement; a request
to change bank details; an account operating outside its mandate; an unexplained reconciliation
difference.

**Prohibited practice.** Single-signature authorisation of project payments; changing payee details
on an emailed request without independent verification; paying out of waterfall order; using project
accounts for unrelated purposes; splitting payments to stay below an authorisation threshold.

**AI application.** AI may reconcile movements to the sources-and-uses statement, detect anomalous
or duplicate payments, monitor waterfall order, and draft exception reports for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not authorise or release a payment, amend payee details, act as a
signatory, or close an exception.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must confirm every payment against the
funds-flow statement or waterfall and against the authorising record; payee verification must be
performed through an independent channel and evidenced; AI-flagged exceptions must be confirmed by a
person before closure.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 27001 *Information security management systems* (international
standard) — controls relevant to the integrity of payment instructions and resistance to
interception and impersonation. COSO *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (voluntary framework)
— authorisation, segregation of duties and reconciliation as control activities. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Account operation, payment-services regulation, sanctions screening and
anti-money-laundering obligations are jurisdiction-specific and carry legal consequences for
individuals as well as institutions; obtain local legal and compliance advice.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-05; PCI-LAW-F-09; PFL-LAW-13-03; PFL-LAW-14-01; PFL-LAW-15-01.

**Related book content.** Domain 14, KA 14.1 — Sources and uses, draw requests and conditions

13.4 — Signing, close and funds flow; Domain 15, KA 15.2 — The operating waterfall in priority

## Domain 15 — Operations, performance and restructuring

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-15-01 — Distribution Restriction

**Rule.** A credential holder shall permit, certify or make a distribution only when every
distribution condition in the finance documents is satisfied at the test date on the documented
basis — coverage tests, reserve balances, no default continuing and any forward-looking test — and
shall not release cash to shareholders ahead of a prior claim in the waterfall or during a lock-up.

**Purpose.** Distribution restrictions are how lenders keep cash in the project while it may still
be needed. Once distributed, cash is gone; the restriction is the only control that operates before
the loss, rather than after it.

**Scope.** All credential holders who prepare, certify or approve distributions, operating waterfall
payments, reserve movements and payments on shareholder instruments, including subordinated interest
and principal and fees payable to shareholders.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Run the waterfall in documented priority order with each tier
floored at nil; confirm every reserve at its required level; compute each distribution test on the
documented definitions, including any forward-looking test and the projection it uses; confirm that
no default or potential default is continuing; obtain the certification the documents require; and
record the distributable amount and its derivation.

**Required evidence.** The waterfall computation for the period; reserve balance confirmations; the
distribution test calculations with the definitions applied; the distribution or compliance
certificate; the board resolution and the accounting basis relied upon; the payment record.

**Decision owner.** The project company's board for the decision to distribute; the finance director
or project finance leader for the certificate; the lenders' agent where consent is required.

**Independent review.** Required where a test is close to its threshold, where a forward-looking
test relies on a revised projection, and after any waiver or amendment affecting the tests.

**Escalation trigger.** A distribution proposed while a test fails or a default may be continuing; a
reserve below its required level; a forward-looking test dependent on assumptions changed since the
last certificate; pressure to distribute ahead of a prior waterfall claim.

**Prohibited practice.** Distributing during a lock-up; paying shareholder instruments ahead of
their waterfall position; funding a distribution by deferring a required reserve top-up or
maintenance spend; re-cutting a projection so that a forward-looking test passes; treating a
shareholder loan repayment as outside the restrictions when the documents include it.

**AI application.** AI may compute the waterfall and the tests, monitor reserve balances, forecast
lock-up risk under stress, and draft distribution certificates for review.

**AI restriction.** AI must not determine that distribution conditions are met, approve a
distribution, certify compliance, or set the assumptions of a forward-looking test.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must recompute the waterfall and each
distribution test from the source cash line for the test period, confirm the definitions against the
executed documents, and confirm the no-default position before certifying.

**External references.** IAS 1 *Presentation of Financial Statements*, IFRS Foundation
(authoritative accounting standard) — presentation of financial statements including amounts
recognised as distributions to owners; it does not determine what an entity may lawfully distribute.
OECD *Principles of Corporate Governance* (voluntary framework) — board responsibilities and
shareholder rights within which a distribution decision is taken; not legislation. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Lawful distribution depends on company-law tests of distributable
profits or reserves and on solvency, and is affected by withholding tax and exchange control;
satisfying the finance documents is not the same as being lawfully able to distribute — obtain local
legal, accounting and tax advice before any distribution.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-11; PFL-LAW-01-01; PFL-LAW-10-01; PFL-LAW-10-02; PFL-LAW-14-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 15, KA 15.2 — The cash waterfall in operation (15.2.1 the operating

## Domain 16 — Data, automation and responsible AI in finance

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-16-01 — Transaction Confidentiality

**Rule.** A credential holder shall protect confidential transaction, counterparty and personal
information, shall use it only for the purpose for which it was provided, and shall not place it
into an AI tool, external service or storage location that has not been authorised for that
classification of information.

**Purpose.** Project finance runs on information that is commercially sensitive, sometimes
price-sensitive and often personal. A deployment choice made casually can disclose an entire
transaction in a single prompt, and the disclosure cannot be withdrawn.

**Scope.** All credential holders handling deal information, diligence materials, models,
counterparty data and personal data, and every tool or service used with it — AI assistants, cloud
storage, translation, extraction and analysis services.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Classify information before use; know and observe the
deployment authorised for each classification, including whether inputs are retained, reused or
processed outside a permitted region; observe non-disclosure obligations and information barriers;
minimise personal data; apply insider-information and wall-crossing procedures where they apply; and
report any suspected disclosure immediately.

**Required evidence.** The information classification scheme and the list of tools authorised for
each classification; the non-disclosure register and wall-crossing records; access logs; incident
reports and their closure.

**Decision owner.** The information owner — usually the client or employer — for permitted use; the
credential holder for their own handling; the organisation's security or compliance function for
authorising tools.

**Independent review.** Required before a new tool or service is used with confidential deal
information, and after any suspected or actual disclosure.

**Escalation trigger.** Confidential information entering an unauthorised tool or service; a
suspected or actual disclosure; a request to use information outside its permitted purpose; a change
in a tool's terms such that inputs may be retained or reused.

**Prohibited practice.** Pasting confidential or personal information into a consumer AI service or
any unapproved tool; retaining deal information after an engagement where return or destruction was
agreed; using deal information for personal advantage or for another client; disclosing a
counterparty's terms as a comparable without permission.

**AI application.** AI may be used on confidential information only within an authorised deployment,
where it may assist with redaction, classification, extraction and summarisation.

**AI restriction.** AI must not decide the classification of information, authorise a tool or
deployment, or waive a confidentiality obligation; no AI service may be used with confidential
information absent an authorisation covering that classification.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must verify that an authorisation covering
the tool and the classification exists before use, verify any redaction applied, and confirm that
outputs do not reproduce confidential material outside the permitted circle.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 27001 *Information security management systems* (international
standard) — the management system and controls for protecting information. ISO/IEC 42001
*Artificial intelligence — Management system* (international standard) — organisational management

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-16-02 — AI-Assisted Model Verification

**Rule.** Where AI builds, edits, checks, extracts for or interprets any part of a financial model
or its outputs, the professional relying on that work shall verify it — by recomputation, by
regression against independently verified figures and by tracing to source — before it is used;
machine checking is additive to professional review and never substitutive.

**Purpose.** AI scans a workbook faster and more consistently than a person, and misses precisely
the errors that cost most: the definitional ones — whether the cash line matches the facility's
definition, whether the tax treatment is the jurisdiction's, whether the waterfall follows the
drafted priority. A model machine-checked and not reviewed carries the appearance of assurance
without its substance.

**Scope.** All AI-assisted modelling work: structure and formula scanning, version diffing, test
generation, documentation drafting, forecast automation, anomaly detection and document extraction
feeding a model or a report.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Maintain a regression suite of independently verified figures
and rerun it after every edit, machine or human, explaining each movement before acceptance; confirm
each machine-raised finding individually and record the sample size; recompute at least one
construction period and one operating period per released version by independent means; and never
rely on a machine scan to establish that a definitional treatment is correct.

**Required evidence.** The regression suite and its results per version; the record of findings
raised, confirmed or rejected, with sample size; the manual recomputation record; the AI-use record
for the model, stating tool, task and version.

**Decision owner.** The professional who relies on the output owns its verification; the model owner
owns the model.

**Independent review.** Required before a machine-assisted model enters decision-grade use, and at
the revalidation interval set for any model tiered as high-consequence in the model inventory.

**Escalation trigger.** A regression figure moving without explanation; a machine-raised finding
reported without confirmation; an AI edit found in the authoritative file with no change-log entry;
a tool showing systematic error in a decision-grade role.

**Prohibited practice.** Reporting a machine-produced finding list as verified without confirming
its items; treating a clean machine scan as evidence of correctness; substituting an AI pre-check
for a required model audit; quoting from a model whose checks are failing, whatever produced it.

**AI application.** AI may scan, diff, generate adversarial tests, draft documentation and
cross-check one method against another — as an aid to verification, never as its discharge.

**AI restriction.** AI must not verify its own output as the sole check, declare a model correct,
determine a definitional treatment, close a finding, or discharge the professional's verification
duty.

**Verification requirement.** Verification must be performed by a professional competent in project
financial modelling, at a depth proportionate to consequence, and recorded; a stated verification
sample size applies to every machine-produced finding list.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 42001 *Artificial intelligence — Management system* (international
standard) — organisational controls for the responsible use of AI. ISO/IEC 23894 *Artificial
intelligence — Guidance on risk management* (international standard) — managing risks arising from
AI systems. NIST *AI Risk Management Framework* (voluntary framework) — trustworthiness
characteristics and risk treatment; a voluntary framework, not a regulation. *Supervisory Guidance
on Model Risk Management* (SR 11-7), issued by United States banking supervisors (public supervisory
guidance) — expectations for model development, validation, use and governance in supervised
institutions in that jurisdiction. The official publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** AI-specific regulation is emerging and uneven, and financial-sector
model-risk expectations differ by jurisdiction and by institution type; obtain local legal and
compliance advice on obligations attaching to AI use in regulated work.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-02; PFL-LAW-06-01; PFL-LAW-06-05; PFL-LAW-13-01; PFL-LAW-16-03.

**Related book content.** Domain 16, KA 16.2 — Model assistance and the verification that must
replace the build (16.2.3); KA 16.3 — Explainability, validation, bias and model risk (16.3.2
validation and test cases; 16.3.4 inventory, tiering, revalidation); Domain 6, KA 6.4 — AI-assisted
modelling controls (6.4.4); Domain 13, KA 13.2 — Model audit.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting a machine-scanned model with a definitional
error intact, testing what the scan can and cannot establish and what verification remains
outstanding. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.

### PCI LAW PFL-LAW-16-03 — Human Approval

**Rule.** Every financial decision, certification, representation and external report within a
credential holder's scope must be approved by a named, competent and authorised human being before
it takes effect; a credential holder shall not design or operate a pipeline in which an AI output
becomes a payment, a certificate, a representation to a lender or a published figure without human
approval.

**Purpose.** Approval is where accountability attaches. Automation without an approval boundary
launders accountability, and certification carries consequences to third parties: nothing in an
automated pipeline may generate a representation.

**Scope.** All workflows — manual, automated or agentic — producing draw requests, compliance and
distribution certificates, lender reports, board and investment papers, payments, published
financial information and advice.

**Minimum professional requirement.** Define the approver for every decision point and tier approval
by consequence, with a written mandate for any delegation naming what may move and within what
bounds; ensure the approver has the competence, information and time to engage substantively;
distinguish review, approval and certification and record which was given; hold decision-grade
automated outputs for approval rather than releasing them; and record identity, date and basis.

**Required evidence.** The approval matrix with tiers and written delegated mandates; sign-off
records stating identity, date, level and basis; pipeline design documentation showing every
approval gate; the record of what the approver was shown.

**Decision owner.** The named human approver for each decision; the process owner for the approval
architecture.

**Independent review.** Required when automation is introduced or extended into a decision path, and
periodically to test that approvals are substantive rather than pass-through.

**Escalation trigger.** An output that took effect without approval; an approver acting outside
their mandate or competence; systematic pass-through of AI output; a delegation operating without a
written mandate.

**Prohibited practice.** Configuring a system to release decision-grade outputs automatically;
recording an AI agent or a shared account as approver; certifying on the basis of an output one has
not reviewed; describing an undocumented delegation as a tier of approval.

**AI application.** AI may prepare complete approval packages, route items to the correct tier,
monitor items awaiting approval, and check that an approval carries the authority the matrix
requires.

**AI restriction.** AI must not approve, certify, sign or make a representation, and must not be
recorded as the approver of record under any circumstances.

**Verification requirement.** A competent professional must periodically test sampled approvals for
substantive engagement and audit automated pipelines for undeclared automatic-release paths; every
certification must be traceable to the human who made it.

**External references.** ISO/IEC 42001 *Artificial intelligence — Management system* (international
standard) — human oversight within the management of AI systems. OECD AI Principles (voluntary
framework) — human-centred values, oversight and accountability for AI outcomes; not legislation.
European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (legislation of the European Union) — a risk-tiered legal
framework for AI systems whose application depends on role, classification and timing; it binds only
where it applies, and legal advice should be obtained. COSO *Internal Control — Integrated
Framework* (voluntary framework) — authorisation and approval as control activities. The official
publications govern; verify current requirements.

**Jurisdictional caution.** Some jurisdictions regulate automated decision-making affecting
individuals and require human involvement by law; obtain local legal advice for automated workflows
touching personal data or producing regulated representations.

**Related PCI laws.** PCI-LAW-F-01; PCI-LAW-F-03; PCI-LAW-F-04; PFL-LAW-14-01; PFL-LAW-16-02.

**Related book content.** Domain 16, KA 16.4 — Privacy, cybersecurity, human approval and AI
governance (16.4.3 human approval — what it is and what it costs; 16.4.4 the AI governance frame);
16.A.1 — Agentic automation and the authorisation boundary; Domain 14, KA 14.1 — The draw request
and its representations.

**Examination relevance.** Scenario items presenting an automated pipeline that issues a certificate
or payment without a human gate, testing approval architecture, tiering and the certification
boundary. No live examination content is exposed.

**Consequences of breach.** Failure of the associated examination competency; refusal, suspension or
revocation of the PFL-AI credential; mandatory remediation and referral to PCI's conduct process,
within PCI's authority only.
