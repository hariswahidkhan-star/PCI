## Why this comparison is usually done badly

Both credentials involve money and both are taken by people with "finance" in their job title, which
is enough for most comparison articles to treat them as substitutes. They are not obviously
substitutes, and the interesting question is not which is better but **which problem you have**.

**Settled (PCI side, verified):** PFL-AI — officially the PCI AI Project Finance Leader — *"covers
project finance, financial modelling, capital structure, bankability, coverage ratios, PPP
structures and financial close."* Entry: three years of professional experience in any field, no
degree requirement, no accounting prerequisite. Scenario-based, online, remotely proctored. Valid
three years, then recertification with CPD including a mandatory AI-currency component. Fees: USD 500
standard, currently USD 350; membership USD 99/yr currently USD 49.50; bundle USD 399.50; renewal USD
99 per cycle; retake TBC. `[PRICE UNVERIFIED]` pending live check.

**Critically, and verifiable now:** PFL-AI is **not a licence**. It does not confer audit rights, it
is not an accountancy qualification, and it changes nothing about who may sign what in a regulated
jurisdiction. PCI states its credentials do not replace a regulated licence, a degree or a
statutory accounting qualification.

**Not settled (CMA side):** everything, starting with the issuer.

## Who should choose which

- **PFL-AI if** your problem is at the asset level: you need to understand how *this* project is
  funded, what the lenders test, why a schedule slip moves a coverage ratio, and what has to be true
  at financial close. That problem is not addressed by general management accounting, however deep.
- **CMA if** ⟨FETCH-INFORMED, derived from the issuer's stated purpose⟩.
- **Both if** ⟨FETCH-INFORMED only⟩. Plausible on the face of it — one is enterprise-level, one is
  asset-level — but plausibility is not evidence and this cell stays empty until read.
- **Neither if** you need a *licensed* qualification for a regulated role. Neither credential is a
  licence, and this article must not imply that either substitutes for one.

## Scenario decision guide

**1. Management accountant whose company is entering infrastructure.**
PFL-AI addresses the specific gap — asset-level funding structures and lender tests — that
enterprise-level accounting training does not cover. ⟨FETCH-INFORMED: what the CMA already gives
this person, so the article does not oversell the gap.⟩

**2. Project cost engineer who keeps being handed the model.**
PFL-AI maps directly. ⟨FETCH-INFORMED: whether the CMA is a realistic alternative for this
profile or an ill-fitting one.⟩

**3. Someone who wants a recognised accounting qualification for its own sake.**
Neither PFL-AI nor a project-finance credential is that. Say so plainly and point at the accountancy
bodies — that is an honest answer, not a lost conversion.

**4. Candidate in a jurisdiction where finance roles are regulated.**
Regulatory requirements decide this, not credential marketing. ⟨FETCH: jurisdiction constraints on
both sides.⟩
