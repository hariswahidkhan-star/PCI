Two things sit under the PCI name and they do different jobs. Confusing them is the single most common misunderstanding about the ecosystem, and it matters most at the exact moment someone is deciding whether to believe a claim on your profile.

The Institute certifies. PCI World practises.

What PCI World is

PCI World is a global learning and challenge platform operated by the Project Controls Institute. It is free — "Free, and it always will be" — and it exists as an open practice ground rather than an assessment surface.

PCI's own statement of the boundary is worth quoting because it is unusually direct:

"The Institute is the certification authority; PCI World is its open practice ground. Completing challenges builds skill and shareable evidence — formal credentials are earned only through the Institute's own examinations."

If you take nothing else from this page, take that sentence.

The Passport: what it is, precisely

A free account turns completed challenges into a PCI World Passport — described by PCI as "a page of verified practice evidence that its owner controls entirely."

Three properties define it, and each is a deliberate design decision:

1. Publication is consent at every level. You choose which results appear, and separately whether scores, decision profiles and completion dates are shown at all. There is no "publish everything or nothing" switch.

2. Answers are never published. In PCI's words: "Answers are never published, to anyone, under any setting." This is not a privacy default you can override — it is a property of the system. Publishing answers would destroy the challenges for everyone who came after you.

3. The owner controls the link, permanently. A published Passport is one link, and:

"The owner can rotate the link, set it to expire, or withdraw it at any moment, and a withdrawn link simply stops resolving."

There is also a one-page PDF version, which points back at the live page — so a stale printout cannot masquerade as current. That detail matters more than it sounds: most credential fraud is not forgery, it is an out-of-date document presented as today's truth.

What a Passport is not

Every verification result carries this, verbatim:

"PCI World Passport records selected professional practice and challenge evidence. It is not, by itself, a PCI certification, examination result, accreditation, licence, or guarantee of professional competence."

That sentence is doing real work. A Passport is evidence that you practised something and how you performed — genuinely useful, and genuinely not the same as passing an examination. Anyone presenting a Passport as certification is misrepresenting it, and the verification page says so to whoever checks.

For recruiters and hiring managers: how to read one

If a candidate sends you a Passport link, here is what you are looking at and how to test it.

QuestionAnswer
Is this a certification?No. It is practice evidence. Certifications are verified separately, by credential ID.
Is it live or a snapshot?Live. You are looking at the current record, not a claim about it.
Can the owner have edited the results?They control what is shown, not what the results were. Hidden ≠ altered.
What if the link stops working?The owner rotated, expired or withdrew it. That is a normal action, not evidence of anything.
Can I verify without the link?Yes — verification is by PCI Student Number, which has the form PCI-YYYY-NNNNNN.
What does "no record available" mean?Deliberately ambiguous: unknown, withdrawn and expired all return the same neutral answer, so a lookup cannot be used to fish for information about someone.

That last row is a privacy design worth naming. A verification system that distinguished "never existed" from "withdrawn" would let anyone probe a person's history by guessing numbers. PCI's returns one uniform answer instead.

Rooms, forum and careers

Alongside the Passport, PCI World is designed to carry community rooms, a professional forum, and a careers surface connecting candidates and employers.

⚠️ These are feature-flagged off by default. On a fresh deployment, community rooms, the forum, careers and community images each sit behind a setting that starts disabled, and several will not enable until a prerequisite is recorded — a moderation provider, a candidate privacy notice, contributor terms. That is the design working, not a fault. This article must not describe them as publicly live until the flags are on and PCI confirms the launch decisions (MISSING_INFO_APPROVALS.md, product-decision list).

Described as designed: rooms are topic spaces for practitioners; the forum is a longer-form, moderated discussion surface whose content is server-rendered and therefore indexable; careers connects verified employers with candidates. The moderation posture across all three is fail-closed — with no moderation provider configured, nothing publishes.

Anonymous practice, and what happens to it

You can complete challenges before you have an account. When you create one, work completed anonymously in that browser joins your record automatically. Nothing is lost by trying before you commit, which removes the usual reason people never start.

Your PCI Student Number

Your Student Number is the stable identifier across the ecosystem — issued once, in the form PCI-YYYY-NNNNNN, and never your email address. It is what a Passport is verified by, and it is what lets your identity persist across PCI World and MyPCI without exposing a contact address to anyone doing a lookup.

Student FAQ

Is PCI World free? Yes — free, and PCI states it always will be.

Is a Passport a certification? No. It records selected practice and challenge evidence and explicitly is not a certification, examination result, accreditation, licence or guarantee of competence.

Who can see my results? Only what you publish. Publication is consent at every level, and you can also choose whether scores, decision profiles and completion dates appear at all.

Are my answers ever published? No — never, to anyone, under any setting.

Can I un-share a Passport? Yes. Rotate the link, set it to expire, or withdraw it; a withdrawn link simply stops resolving.

How does an employer verify one? By PCI Student Number. Unknown, withdrawn and expired records all return the same neutral answer by design.

Do challenges I completed before signing up count? Yes — anonymous work in that browser joins your record when you create an account.

Are the community rooms and forum live? They ship switched off and are enabled by PCI, with prerequisites recorded first. Check the platform rather than assuming.

— If you are going to practise anyway, practise where it leaves a record you own: PCI World. If you are checking someone else's claim, verify by Student Number rather than trusting a screenshot.
