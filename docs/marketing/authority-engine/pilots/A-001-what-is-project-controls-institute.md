## 9–14. Article (Markdown, ~1,450 words)

Most professional bodies introduce themselves with adjectives. This page uses facts, because the
only sensible reason to read it is to decide whether a PCI credential means anything — to you, to a
candidate you are considering, or to a team you are about to invest in.

## What PCI is

The Project Controls Institute is an independent certifying body for the integrated discipline of
project controls, cost engineering and project finance, with governed use of AI running through all
of it. Its legal entity is **Project Controls Institute Global, Inc.**, a Delaware Non-Stock
Corporation, founded in 2025. Its working motto — *AI proposes. The professional disposes.* — is
not decoration; it is the assessment stance, and you will meet it in every examination scenario.

PCI does three things and deliberately does not do a fourth:

1. It **defines the standard** — the bodies of knowledge that describe what a competent practitioner
   should know and be able to do.
2. It **examines against that standard**, under remote proctoring, using scenario-based papers.
3. It **issues and maintains credentials**, including the renewal cycle and public verification.
4. It **does not sell the training**. Preparation is a separate business, provided by PCI's official
   partner, Certuvo.

That fourth point is the one worth pausing on.

## Why the training is separate from the examination

A body that writes the exam, sells the course that teaches the exam, and then marks the exam has an
obvious commercial incentive to keep candidates coming back. PCI's public position on this is
explicit: *"PCI develops and owns the standard and the examination. Preparation and training are
provided separately by Certuvo, our official partner. This separation supports the impartiality
expected of a body developed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles."*

In practice that means you can prepare however you like. Certuvo is the official route and mirrors
the exam closely, but PCI's own guidance is blunt about it: *"Do I have to use Certuvo to sit the
exam? No."* And no preparation provider — Certuvo included — can promise you a result:
*"Preparation through Certuvo or any other provider does not guarantee exam success or
certification."*

## What PCI certifies

Three credentials form the **PCI AI Project Leadership Certification Suite**:

| Credential | Official full title | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **PCI PCL-AI** | PCI AI Project Controls Leader | Project-controls governance, cost, planning, earned value, forecasting and risk, with AI-enabled project controls |
| **PCI PFL-AI** | PCI AI Project Finance Leader | Project finance, financial modelling, capital structure, bankability, coverage ratios, PPP structures and financial close |
| **PCI PML-AI** | PCI Project Management Leader – AI | Project management, leadership and delivery: governance, planning, execution, agile/hybrid delivery and AI-enabled project management |

Note the third title. PCL-AI and PFL-AI put "AI" at the front; PML-AI puts it at the end. That is
the official form, not a typo — worth getting right if you are writing it on a CV.

The entry requirement is the same across all three and is unusually simple: **three years of
professional experience in any field**. Not three years in project controls, and no degree
requirement. The examination is scenario-based and sat fully online under remote proctoring.

## How a credential is earned, and how long it lasts

There are **three routes** into recognition by PCI, and they are not equivalent:

- **Standard Route** — for candidates meeting the education and professional-experience
  requirements. Paid, examined, and the ordinary path.
- **Founding Route** — a limited founding cohort, joined *by invitation* during the founding stage.
  Free, but still examined: the credential is earned, not conferred.
- **Honorary Route** — recognition of distinguished contribution, assessed rather than examined.
  Anyone may **apply for the board's consideration**; conferral is at the board's discretion. It
  confers **Honorary Fellow (PCI)**, which is labelled honorary and is *never* the examined
  credential.

That last distinction is enforced in the platform itself, not merely promised in copy: approving an
honorary application never creates an examined credential record. If someone tells you they hold an
honorary PCI certification, the correct reading is "Honorary Fellow (PCI)" — a recognition of
contribution, not an assessment of examined competence.

A PCI credential is valid for **three years**, after which recertification applies. Maintenance runs
on a three-year continuing professional development cycle that includes a mandatory AI-currency
component — the discipline moves, and a credential that ignored that would decay into a wall
ornament. Credentials that are not maintained lapse at the end of the cycle; reinstatement routes
exist.

## What PCI does not claim

This is the part most certification pages leave out, and the part a careful reader actually wants.

PCI's own footer states it plainly: **PCI is not currently accredited by ANAB, IAS, or any ISO/IEC
17024 accreditation body.** Its framework is *developed with reference to* ISO/IEC 17024
personnel-certification principles — a design reference, not an accreditation.

On legal status: Project Controls Institute Global, Inc. is a Delaware Non-Stock Corporation which
**intends to seek** recognition as a tax-exempt nonprofit under Section 501(c)(3) of the U.S.
Internal Revenue Code. That status **has not yet been granted**, and contributions are not currently
represented as tax-deductible.

And on outcomes: *"PCI does not guarantee employment, promotion, salary improvement, immigration
benefits, licensing eligibility or acceptance by any third party."* Recognition of any credential
varies by employer, industry, institution and jurisdiction. A credential is evidence you can point
at; it is not a contract with the labour market, and anybody who tells you otherwise about any
certification is selling something.

## How to verify a PCI credential

Every issued credential is verifiable publicly. If a candidate gives you a credential ID, you can
confirm it is genuine and current without contacting anyone.

Separately, PCI World — PCI's open practice ground — issues a **PCI World Passport**, which is a
different object and must not be confused with certification. Its own mandated disclaimer says so:
*"PCI World Passport records selected professional practice and challenge evidence. It is not, by
itself, a PCI certification, examination result, accreditation, licence, or guarantee of
professional competence."* Passports are verified by PCI Student Number, and the owner can rotate,
expire or withdraw the link at any time.

The line between the two is deliberate: **the Institute certifies; PCI World practises.**

## A three-minute due-diligence checklist

Use this on PCI, and on any other body asking for your money:

1. **Who owns the standard, and who sells the training?** If they are the same organisation with no
   separation statement, ask why.
2. **Is the accreditation claim specific?** "Accredited" with no named accreditor is not a claim, it
   is a mood. Look for the body's own statement of what it is and is not.
3. **Can you verify a credential without asking the issuer?** If there is no public check, the
   credential cannot be trusted by a stranger.
4. **What are the total costs?** Exam, membership, renewal, resit — a single headline fee usually
   hides at least one of these.
5. **What happens after you pass?** A credential with no maintenance cycle tells you the body stopped
   caring on the day your payment cleared.
6. **What does the body refuse to promise?** The absence of guarantees is a good sign, not a bad one.

## Student FAQ

**Is PCI accredited?** No — not currently by ANAB, IAS or any ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body. Its
certification framework is developed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Anyone telling you
otherwise is overstating it.

**Is PCI a nonprofit?** It is a Delaware Non-Stock Corporation that intends to seek 501(c)(3)
tax-exempt recognition. That recognition has not yet been granted.

**Do I need a degree?** No. The entry requirement is three years of professional experience in any
field.

**Do I have to buy training from PCI?** You cannot — PCI does not sell preparation. Certuvo is the
official partner, and using it is optional.

**Is an honorary award the same as certification?** No. Honorary Fellow (PCI) recognises
contribution and involves no examination. It is always labelled honorary.

**How long does a credential last?** Three years, then recertification, with CPD across the cycle
including a mandatory AI-currency component.

**Can an employer check a credential?** Yes — every issued credential is publicly verifiable.
