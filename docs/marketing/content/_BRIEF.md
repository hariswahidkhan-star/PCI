# Production brief — read this before writing a single word

Every writer on this run works from this file. It is the difference between 300 publishable assets
and 300 pieces of generic AI content nobody links to.

Grounding data is in `_brief_data.json` beside this file: 87 researched keywords with intent and
difficulty, 260 article briefs from the Article Bank spread across all seven pillars, and the
publishing rules for ten platforms including their canonical behaviour.

---

## 1. Who PCI is, and the one idea

Project Controls Institute Global. An independent certifying body with three AI-era credentials,
each with its own Body of Knowledge and examination:

| Code | Full name — use exactly | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **PCL-AI** | PCI AI Project Controls Leader | 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas |
| **PFL-AI** | PCI AI Project Finance Leader | 16 domains, 61 knowledge areas |
| **PML-AI** | PCI Project Management Leader – AI | 16 domains, 63 knowledge areas |

**The thesis every piece ladders to.** A chartered accountant is examined on when revenue may be
recognised and what a provision must satisfy — almost never on a critical path or an earning rule.
An engineer is examined on float and progress measurement — almost never on cut-off or a contract
asset. A project lives in the overlap, and the overlap is where the money is lost. PCL-AI examines
both sides.

You do not have to state the thesis in every piece. You do have to write pieces that are *consistent
with* it, and at least one in three should make the finance/delivery overlap explicit.

## 2. The five domains

`projectcontrolsinstitute.org` · `pciai.org` · `pciglobal.ai` · `pciworld.org` · `credentialfinder.org`

Every link you write lands on one of these. A link anywhere else is off-estate and does not ship.
Your brief tells you which domain a piece points at; respect it, because the point of five domains
is a link graph, not five copies of the same call to action.

## 3. Numbers — the register

**You may publish these. Do not round them, improve them, or add to this list.**

- 13 / 16 / 16 domains and 61 / 61 / 63 knowledge areas across PCL / PFL / PML
- 92 sector case studies across the three volumes (26 + 33 + 33)
- 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements
- 40 / 40 / 20 — the *Body of Knowledge's* proportions: finance and reporting, project management,
  governed AI
- 15,613 machine calculation checks, all passing — **and the same sentence must say this covers
  PFL-AI and PML-AI only.** PCL-AI has no equivalent suite. The figure without that scope is false.

**You may not publish:** any examination weighting (the syllabus is settled, the exam blueprint is
an open decision); worked-example or question-bank counts; student numbers; pass rates; salary
uplift; anything about how many people hold a PCI credential.

**Never, under any framing:** claim accreditation, recognition, endorsement, affiliation or
partnership that has not happened; promise an award, an outcome or a guarantee; describe the PCI
Standards as law (they are certification requirements established by the Institute, and nothing PCI
publishes is legal, tax or accounting advice); reproduce protected text, tables, figures or
questions from ISO, IFRS, IAS, PMI or AACE — name them and describe them in PCI's own words;
invent a statistic, testimonial, case study, project detail or source.

**The rule that matters most: if you cannot point at where a number came from, cut the sentence.**
An article with four real numbers beats one with twenty invented ones, and the invented ones are the
only thing that can destroy a certifying body.

Technical facts you may use freely because they are the discipline's own, not claims about PCI:
the EVM formulas, the four EAC methods, the IFRS 15 five-step model described in your own words,
the cash conversion cycle, precision/recall/F1, critical path method. Work them properly.

## 4. SEO, AEO, GEO and AIO — what each actually requires

Four names, one job: be the thing that gets cited. They differ in who is doing the citing.

**SEO — a search engine ranks your page.**
- One primary keyword per piece, from the Keyword Plan. It appears in the H1, the first 100 words,
  one H2, and the meta description. Nowhere else on purpose.
- Title 50–60 characters. Meta description 140–158 characters, written to be clicked, not stuffed.
- One H1. H2s that describe sections a person would look for. H3s only where a real sub-branch exists.
- 1,200–1,800 words for a guide, 1,800–2,600 for a pillar. Length follows the question, not a target.
- Internal links: two to three, to other PCI pieces or the pillar page. Descriptive anchors, never
  "click here".
- Image alt text that describes the image, for the reader who cannot see it.

**AEO — an answer engine lifts your paragraph into a snippet.**
- **Answer the title question in the first 40–60 words.** Before any preamble, before any history.
  If a reader could stop after your first paragraph and have the answer, you have done this right.
- One question per H2, phrased the way a person types it.
- A definition sentence that could stand alone out of context: "Earned value is X." Not "Let's
  explore what earned value might mean."
- A FAQ block of 4–6 real questions at the end, each answered in 40–80 words.

**GEO — a language model cites you in its answer.** This is where 2026 differs from 2020, and where
most content fails.
- **Short paragraphs — two to three sentences.** Models extract self-contained blocks, not flowing
  narrative. A 200-word paragraph is invisible to them however good it is.
- **Every section must survive being read alone.** No "as we saw above", no "building on the
  previous point". Each block carries its own context.
- **Put a comparison table in every piece where one is honest.** Tables are among the most cited
  formats there is. Four EAC methods against their assumptions. Three credentials against their
  domains. Cash versus accrual. If the subject has axes, table it.
- **Statistics with their source attached.** A number with a source gets cited; a number without one
  gets skipped, and rightly.
- Structured lists, tables and quotable statistics measurably raise the odds of being cited.

**AIO — the same, extended to how the whole site reads to a machine.**
- Suggest the schema type in your front matter: `Article`, `FAQPage`, `HowTo`, `Course`.
- Consistent entity naming. "PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI)" on first mention, "PCL-AI"
  after. Never invent a variant.
- Pillar-and-cluster: every supporting article links to its pillar, and says in one clause how it
  relates. That is what builds topical authority.

## 5. The platforms — where each piece goes and what changes

**The rule that governs all republishing:** publish on the PCI site first, let it index, then
republish elsewhere with the canonical pointing home. Republishing first, or without a canonical,
means a platform with far more authority than yours ranks for your own article and you rank beneath
it.

| Platform | Canonical home? | What that means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Own site** (the five domains) | it *is* home | The original. Everything else derives from it. |
| **Medium** | Yes | May carry a near-copy with the canonical set. |
| **DEV Community** | Yes (`canonical_url`) | May carry a near-copy. Technical framing. |
| **Hashnode** | Yes (republishing field) | May carry a near-copy. |
| **Blogger** | Manual canonical possible | May carry a near-copy if the canonical is added. |
| **WordPress.com** | You control it | Can host originals. |
| **LinkedIn Articles** | **No** | **Must be original or substantially rewritten.** Never a copy. |
| **Substack** | **No** | **Must be newsletter-first original.** Never a copy. |
| **Vocal Media** | **No** | **Must be rewritten.** Never a copy. |
| **Quora** | n/a | Original answers. Answer fully *before* any link. Never lead with a link. |

Nofollow reality: Medium and Quora links pass no direct equity. They are worth doing for qualified
traffic and for being seen by models, not for link juice. Say so honestly in the plan; do not
pretend a nofollow link is a backlink win.

**Backlinks — the honest version.** You cannot manufacture authority by linking to yourself from
platforms you control. What you *can* do, and what this run does: build a clean internal link graph
across the five domains, earn citations by being the most extractable answer on a subject, and give
a genuine reason for a third party to link — an original worked example, a comparison table that
does not exist elsewhere, a definition written more clearly than anyone else's. Write for that.

## 6. Social formats

| Format | Spec |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn text post | 1,300–1,900 characters. First two lines earn the "see more". No link in the body — link goes in the first comment. 3–5 hashtags at the end. |
| LinkedIn document/carousel | 8–14 slides, 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 is the one people screenshot — put the arithmetic there. |
| X/Threads | Under 280 per post; a thread is 5–9 posts, each able to stand alone. |
| Instagram/Facebook | Caption 125 characters before the fold. Carousel same 1080 × 1350. |
| Quora answer | 300–600 words. Answer completely, then one contextual link at the end if it genuinely helps. |

Hashtags: 3–5 on LinkedIn, up to 10 on Instagram, 1–2 on X. Use real ones practitioners follow —
`#ProjectControls` `#EarnedValue` `#CostEngineering` `#ProjectFinance` `#PMO` `#Primavera`
`#ProjectManagement` `#AIGovernance` `#Scheduling` `#RiskManagement`. Never invent a hashtag and
never use more than the platform's convention.

## 7. Voice — how to not sound like AI wrote it

British English. Short sentences. Specific over clever.

Banned outright because they are the signature of generated text: "in today's fast-paced world",
"delve", "landscape" as a metaphor, "unlock", "leverage" as a verb, "it's important to note",
"navigate the complexities", "game-changer", "robust solution", "seamless", "tapestry", "testament
to". Also banned: three-item lists used purely for rhythm, em-dash pile-ups, a rhetorical question
opening every section, and emoji as bullet points.

Write like a practitioner who has run a month-end and been asked to defend a forecast. Use the
concrete case. Admit where a method fails. That is what a senior reader trusts and what a model
finds worth citing.

## 8. What you output

One markdown file per piece, in the directory your task names, with this front matter:

```yaml
---
platform:      # e.g. Own site — projectcontrolsinstitute.org, LinkedIn Article, Medium, Quora
type:          # pillar | guide | how-to | data-study | glossary | qa-list | linkedin-post | carousel | thread
title:         # 50–60 characters
meta:          # 140–158 characters
primary_kw:    # from the Keyword Plan
secondary_kw:  # 2–4
pillar:        # one of the seven
credential:    # PCL-AI | PFL-AI | PML-AI | suite
target_domain: # which of the five this points at
canonical:     # "original" | "canonical -> <the original's URL slug>"
schema:        # Article | FAQPage | HowTo | Course
word_count:
hashtags:      # where the platform uses them
ab_id:         # the Article Bank ID if the brief came from one
---
```

Then the piece itself. Start with an `# H1`. For articles: the direct answer inside the first 60
words, then the body, then a FAQ block, then a one-line internal-linking note naming which PCI
pieces this should link to and with what anchor text.

Filenames: `NNN-platform-slug.md`, zero-padded, lowercase, hyphenated.
