# The month-end close is where project controls goes to die

*LinkedIn text post — 1514 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Your ledger says the project ran at CPI 1.103 last month.
Corrected for accruals it ran at 0.982. Twelve points, same month, same project, and nobody lied.

Here is the arithmetic, because it is the whole argument.

The ledger shows £6.480m posted in the period. Three adjustments apply before that figure means anything.

Goods receipted and not invoiced: +£0.510m. The steel is on site and consumed.
Subcontract work performed and not yet certified: +£0.365m. The labour went in this period.
Site insurance prepaid into next period: −£0.075m.

Corrected actual cost: 6.480 + 0.510 + 0.365 − 0.075 = £7.280m.
Earned value at the same cut-off: £7.150m.
CPI = 7.150 / 7.280 = 0.982.
Against the raw ledger: 7.150 / 6.480 = 1.103.

The error runs in the flattering direction by default, because unbilled work is almost always the largest of the three adjustments.

Then the quieter failure. Progress surveyed on the 28th, ledger closed on the 31st. Three days of cost sitting against no measured work, and an index that moves for a reason that has nothing to do with performance.

A cost engineer who cannot defend an accrual and an accountant who has never earned a quantity will produce two sincere and different answers for one month. The close is the handshake between them, and it is the first thing compressed when the calendar tightens.

Five working days. Five reconciliations that tie to nil. The forecast locked before the ledger closes, never after.
