# Contract administration, defined in one honest paragraph

*LinkedIn text post — 1515 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Contract administration is not filing.
It is the operation of a legal instrument to a clock, and the clock does not pause because you were busy.

The paragraph a lawyer would accept:

Contract administration is the day-to-day operation of an agreement after signature. Issuing and receiving notices inside the periods the contract sets. Keeping records capable of substantiating entitlement. Valuing and certifying payment. Administering instructions and changes. Maintaining the programme against which time is measured. It creates no rights. It preserves the ones already bought.

That last line is the job.

Take a 28-day notice period. You become aware of the event on 3 March, so notice falls due on 31 March. On 1 April the same event, the same records and the same £1.4m of cost are worth nothing. Merit does not revive a barred claim, and a good story told late is still late.

Which is why the work sits with project controls rather than with the lawyers. The person who knows the event happened is the person holding the progress data, and they are usually three weeks ahead of anybody in a legal department.

A test you can run this afternoon. Pick an instruction from three months ago and ask for four numbers: date of awareness, date of notice, value quoted, days it moved the completion date. Under five minutes, or the regime is not running.

Read your own contract. Nothing here is legal advice, and an amended standard form changes the periods.
