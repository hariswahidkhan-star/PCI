# Your dashboard has 22 KPIs. Four of them are decisions.

**Post body (1,579 characters):**

Twenty-two KPIs on the front page of a steering pack is a normal sight. Ask which of them changed a decision last quarter and the answer is usually four.

A number earns its place on that page when it has an owner, a formula, a threshold, and an agreed action that fires when the threshold is crossed. Miss the last one and you are reporting, not controlling.

Here is one that qualifies. Take a package with a budget at completion of £12.0m. At cut-off, earned value is £5.2m and actual cost is £6.1m.

CPI = EV / AC = 5.2 / 6.1 = 0.85
TCPI = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) = 6.8 / 5.9 = 1.15

You have been converting 85p of budgeted work for every £1 spent. To land the package on £12.0m you now need 1.15 on the remaining £6.8m of work. That is a 35% improvement in cost efficiency, and nobody has ever produced one by asking the team to try harder.

Two numbers, one page, and the committee has to choose: rebaseline, take scope out, or accept the overrun and fund it. That is what a KPI is for.

The other eighteen asked nobody for anything. Percent complete with no rule of credit behind it. A red-amber-green square with no threshold written down. Headcount, because it was on last month's pack.

Two tests before a metric goes on the front page. Name the person who owns it. Name what happens the week it breaches. If either answer is missing, move it to the appendix and give the space to something that bites.

Nine indicators that trigger action beat twenty-two that decorate. The appendix is not a demotion. It is where the evidence lives when somebody challenges the four.
