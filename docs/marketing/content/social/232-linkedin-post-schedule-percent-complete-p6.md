# Schedule % complete in P6: three settings, three answers

**Post body (1,882 characters):**

One activity. One data date. Three percent complete types in P6, and three different answers, none of them wrong.

Cable pulling. Original duration 40 days, 2,000 budgeted labour hours, 30 drums to pull, budget at completion £600,000. At the cut-off: 20 days gone, remaining duration re-estimated at 30 days, 1,100 hours booked, 12 drums pulled.

Duration % complete = (40 − 30) ÷ 40 = 25%
Units % complete = 1,100 ÷ 2,000 = 55%
Physical % complete = 12 ÷ 30 = 40%

Now push each one through earned value against a £600,000 budget and £330,000 of actual cost.

EV at 25% = £150,000, so CPI = 0.45
EV at 55% = £330,000, so CPI = 1.00
EV at 40% = £240,000, so CPI = 0.73

The activity did not change between those three lines. The setting did.

Look at what each type is really measuring. Duration % complete is a statement about time remaining, not work done — reduce the remaining duration and the percentage jumps while nothing gets pulled.

Units % complete is hours booked over hours planned, so where cost is labour-driven it drags CPI towards 1.00 by construction, which is the most flattering and least informative number on the page. Physical % complete is the only one that can be tied to something a second person can walk out and count.

There is a fourth field people confuse with these. Schedule % complete is not a progress type at all: it compares the data date against the baseline dates and tells you how much of the baseline duration should have elapsed. It is a plan number, not an actual.

The working rule. Physical, with a written rule of credit behind it, for discrete work. Duration for level of effort, where time really is the measure. Units almost never, and never as the driver of earned value.

Then check it at the activity. P6 sets a project default and lets each activity override it, and one override inside a control account moves that account's CPI.
