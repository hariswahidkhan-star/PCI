# CPI 0.92 on a £40m job: what it costs by completion

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

CPI 0.92 sounds survivable. The cost variance is £1.4m and the programme is half gone.
Run the same performance to the end and it is £3.48m, which is 87% of the margin on the contract.

Ten slides on what a cost performance index below 1 actually costs, and what has to change to stop it.

---

**Slide 1 — What 0.92 means literally**
For every £1.00 of budgeted work delivered, you have spent £1.09. Not "slightly behind". Not "a bit hot". Nine pence on every pound, on everything built so far, and on everything left if nothing changes.

**Slide 2 — The inputs**
Water treatment works. BAC **£40.0m**, contract price **£44.0m**, month 12 of 24.
EV **£16.1m**. AC **£17.5m**.
CPI = 16.1 ÷ 17.5 = **0.92**. CV = 16.1 − 17.5 = **−£1.4m**.
Complete by value: 16.1 ÷ 40.0 = **40.25%**.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
EAC = BAC ÷ CPI = 40.0 ÷ 0.92 = **£43.48m**.
VAC = 40.0 − 43.48 = **−£3.48m**.
The variance you can see today is £1.4m. The variance you have already bought is £3.48m.
**3.48 ÷ 1.4 = 2.48. It multiplies by two and a half, and the multiplier is just the work you have not done yet.**

**Slide 4 — Why it multiplies**
A rate error is not an event, it is a property of the remaining scope. You have earned £16.1m of the £40.0m budget, so £23.9m of budgeted work is still to be bought at £1.09 in the pound. The variance grows because the scope does, not because anything gets worse.

**Slide 5 — What recovery has to look like**
TCPI = (BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC) = 23.9 ÷ 22.5 = **1.062**.
To still land on £40.0m, the remaining work must run 6.2% under budget having run 8.7% over. Name the change that delivers that. If nobody can, the £40.0m number is not a forecast any more.

**Slide 6 — Check the measurement before you panic** Four questions, in order. Is actual cost carrying accruals that earned value has not been credited for? Are the rules of credit generous at the start of a work package?

Is a cut-off mismatch putting cost against unmeasured work? Has scope entered the actuals that never entered the budget? Three of those four are reporting faults, not performance faults.

**Slide 7 — Then find the cause, one sentence**
Rate, quantity, productivity or scope. "Cable pulling is running at £92.54/m against £84.00/m tendered, on 1,350m still to install." That sentence tells you which EAC method applies and whether recovery is even available. A CPI trend on its own tells you nothing you can act on.

**Slide 8 — Ask when it last moved**
Ask when your CPI last moved by more than two points in a month without the measurement method changing. On most jobs the honest answer is never. That is why "we will recover it in the second half" needs evidence from this project, not optimism about it.

**Slide 9 — What each point of CPI is worth here**

| CPI | EAC | VAC | Margin on £44.0m |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.95 | £42.11m | −£2.11m | £1.89m |
| 0.92 | £43.48m | −£3.48m | £0.52m |
| 0.88 | £45.45m | −£5.45m | **−£1.45m** |

**Slide 10 — The row that changes the accounting** At CPI 0.88 the expected cost passes the contract price. That is not just a bad month. Where total expected costs exceed the consideration expected, the loss is recognised in full in the period it becomes expected, not spread over the twelve months remaining.
