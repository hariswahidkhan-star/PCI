# Earned value measures work done, not the money spent

**Post body (1,592 characters):**

Earned value is measured in baseline money, not in what you spent. Get that one sentence wrong and every index below it is decoration.

Earned value (EV) is the budgeted cost of the work actually complete at the cut-off date. Budgeted. Not invoiced, not committed, not what the work turned out to cost.

Worked through. A fit-out package with a budget at completion of £8.0m. At the cut-off, progress measured against agreed rules of credit is 40% complete.

EV = 40% × £8.0m = £3.2m
The baseline said 45% should be done, so PV = £3.6m
Actual cost booked to the package, accruals included, AC = £3.9m

CV = EV − AC = 3.2 − 3.9 = −£0.7m
SV = EV − PV = 3.2 − 3.6 = −£0.4m
CPI = EV / AC = 3.2 / 3.9 = 0.82
SPI = EV / PV = 3.2 / 3.6 = 0.89

You are getting 82p of budgeted work per £1 spent, and you are £0.4m of value short of the plan.

Now the shortcut that kills the system. Somebody claims progress from spend, because spend is the only number available on the day of the cut-off. EV is set equal to AC.

CPI comes out at 1.00. It will come out at 1.00 every month until handover, and then the package will overrun by exactly the amount the system never showed.

If your CPI has sat between 0.98 and 1.02 for six months, that is not stability. Go and look at how percent complete is derived.

EV has to come from something physical: spools erected, drawings issued and accepted, metres tested. Something a second person could count on site and get the same answer.

The three inputs are cheap to define and expensive to fix later. Define them before the baseline is frozen, not in month four.
