# Six NEC4 main options. One decides your cost report

*LinkedIn text post — 1551 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

The cost report says £43.0m. The payable outturn is £41.5m.
Both are correct, and which one you should be quoting depends entirely on which main option was signed.

The six, in plain terms.
A: priced contract with activity schedule. B: priced with bill of quantities. C: target contract with activity schedule. D: target with bill of quantities. E: cost reimbursable. F: management contract.

A and B fix a price, and Defined Cost is the Contractor's problem. C and D pay Defined Cost plus Fee against a target, then share the difference. E pays Defined Cost plus Fee with no target to share against. F does the same through a management contractor.

The arithmetic a finance director actually needs, on a target contract:

Total of the Prices, adjusted for compensation events: £40.0m.
Forecast Defined Cost plus Fee: £43.0m.
Overrun: £3.0m.
Contractor's share in the applicable band, 50% in this example: £1.5m.
Paid at completion: 43.0 − 1.5 = £41.5m.

Run it the other way. Outturn £37.0m, saving £3.0m, same share, and the Contractor is paid £38.5m.

So the delivery number and the payable number differ by £1.5m in either direction, and only one of them belongs in a revenue forecast. Under Option A neither line exists at all.

Share percentages and bands live in Contract Data, not in the option letter. Read them before you forecast anything.

A target-cost forecast is a finance answer assembled from delivery data. That is the overlap, and it is where the argument starts every month.
