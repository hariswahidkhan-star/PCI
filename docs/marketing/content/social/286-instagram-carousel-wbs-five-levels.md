# The five levels of a work breakdown structure, drawn

*Instagram and Facebook carousel — 9 slides, 1080 × 1350. Instagram captions carry no clickable link, so the link goes in the bio; on Facebook it goes in the post.*

**Caption (the first 125 characters have to earn the swipe):**

The level 3 breakdown summed to £13.9m. Its parent said £14.4m. That £500,000 of scope had no owner.

A work breakdown structure fails quietly. Nine slides: the five levels drawn on one project, the roll-up that does not tie, what each extra level actually costs in reporting hours, and the one rule that catches both errors.

Save it before you build the next one.

---

**Slide 1 — A WBS is a decomposition of scope**

A work breakdown structure divides the total scope of a project into progressively smaller deliverables, each of which rolls up completely into its parent.

It is a structure of things produced, not of things done. If your level 2 reads "planning, execution, closeout", you have written a schedule and called it a WBS.

**Slide 2 — The five levels**

Level 1 the project. Level 2 major deliverables or facilities. Level 3 systems or subsystems within them. Level 4 control accounts. Level 5 work packages.

No standard fixes the number at five. Five is where most capital projects settle, because level 4 is where a manager can own a budget and level 5 is where work can be scheduled and progressed.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**

Project **£48.0m** at level 1. Level 2:

| Element | Budget |
|---|---:|
| Civil and structural | £18.0m |
| Mechanical and electrical | £14.4m |
| Buildings | £9.6m |
| Project management and commissioning | £6.0m |
| **Total** | **£48.0m** |

Now open mechanical and electrical at level 3: process equipment £6.8m, pipework £3.4m, electrical £2.5m, instrumentation £1.2m. That sums to **£13.9m** against a parent of **£14.4m**.

**£500,000 of scope with no home** — in this case cable containment, which everyone assumed was inside somebody else's package.

And the cost of depth: 40 control accounts, each with 8 work packages, is **320** work packages. At 6 minutes a month to status each one, that is **1,920 minutes — 32 hours every month**, before anyone analyses anything.

**Slide 4 — The 100% rule, in both directions**

Children must sum to their parent, and nothing may appear that is not in the parent. PMI describes this rule in its practice standard for work breakdown structures; in plain words it means the structure contains all of the scope and none of anything else.

Test it downwards to find missing scope, as above. Test it upwards to find scope that was never authorised. Both failures cost money, and the second one is harder to argue about later.

**Slide 5 — Level 4: the control account**

A control account is the point where budget, schedule and a named owner meet. It is the lowest level at which performance is formally measured and reported.

Size it by management span, not by symmetry. A control account small enough to be invisible in the report is overhead, and one large enough to hide a failing package defeats the purpose.

**Slide 6 — Level 5: work packages and planning packages**

A work package has defined scope, a budget, a duration, an earning rule and a start and finish. It is the unit that gets scheduled and progressed.

A planning package is authorised scope inside a control account that is not yet detailed enough to plan. It holds real budget and converts to work packages as definition matures. Using one is honest. Pretending future scope does not exist is not.

**Slide 7 — The dictionary is what makes it enforceable**

For each element: a scope statement, what is included, what is explicitly excluded, the deliverables, the owner, the budget and the earning rule.

Without it, two people read the same code differently and the cost report reconciles to nothing. The exclusions line does most of the work, because arguments are almost always about what somebody assumed was inside the box.

**Slide 8 — WBS, OBS and CBS are three different structures**

| Structure | Answers | Level of interest |
|---|---|---|
| WBS | What is being produced | Work package |
| OBS | Who is accountable | Team or supplier |
| CBS | How cost is classified | Cost type or account code |

The control account is where the WBS and the OBS intersect: one piece of scope, one owner. The CBS runs across both, which is why cost type must never become a WBS level.

**Slide 9 — What the level decides for finance**

The control account is usually the denominator in a cost-to-cost measure of progress, and the work package is where the earning rule lives. Get the structure wrong and the progress percentage that drives recognised revenue is being computed over a boundary that does not match the performance obligation in the contract.
