# The four EAC formulas, one slide each, with the maths

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Four forecasts from one set of numbers: £89.5m, £95.1m, £97.0m, £102.8m. Budget is £86m.
Nothing changed between them except what you are prepared to assume about the work still to come.

Twelve slides: every EAC method, the arithmetic on slide 3, and the assumption you sign when you pick one.

---

**Slide 1 — Why there are four**
An estimate at completion is cost already incurred plus the cost of the work remaining. The four methods differ only in how they price the remaining work. Choosing between them is a judgement about cause, not a preference about arithmetic.

**Slide 2 — The inputs, all to one cut-off**
Rail depot package. BAC **£86.0m**, month 11 of 26.
PV **£29.8m**. EV **£27.4m**. AC **£30.9m**.
CPI = 27.4 ÷ 30.9 = **0.887**. SPI = 27.4 ÷ 29.8 = **0.919**.
Work still to earn: 86.0 − 27.4 = **£58.6m**.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic, all four**

| Method | Formula | EAC | VAC |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 Remaining at budget | AC + (BAC − EV) | **£89.5m** | −£3.5m |
| 2 Remaining at CPI | BAC ÷ CPI | **£97.0m** | −£11.0m |
| 3 Remaining at CPI × SPI | AC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI) | **£102.8m** | −£16.8m |
| 4 Bottom-up | AC + fresh ETC of £64.2m | **£95.1m** | −£9.1m |

Checks: 30.9 + 58.6 = 89.5. 86.0 ÷ 0.887 = 96.99. 0.887 × 0.919 = 0.815, then 58.6 ÷ 0.815 = 71.9, plus 30.9 = 102.8.

**Slide 4 — Method 1: the rest runs at plan**
EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) = **£89.5m**.
You are signing that the £3.5m already lost is behind you and nothing about it repeats. Defensible after a flood, a one-off remediation, a settled claim. Indefensible when the cause is a rate.

**Slide 5 — Method 2: performance to date continues**
EAC = BAC ÷ CPI = **£97.0m**.
The fewest assumptions and the most use of measured data. It simply says the project keeps behaving as it has behaved. This is the default you should have to argue your way off, not onto.

**Slide 6 — Method 3: recovery keeps costing money** EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI) = **£102.8m**. For a job buying back a slipping programme with overtime and extra shifts.

Multiplying the two indices is severe, which is why some organisations weight them instead. Whichever you use, write the rule in the cost control procedure rather than inventing it in the spreadsheet.

**Slide 7 — Method 4: re-estimate the remaining scope**
EAC = AC + a fresh estimate to complete = 30.9 + 64.2 = **£95.1m**.
The most accurate method and the most expensive, and the only one that can price scope the indices have never seen. It is also the easiest to bias, because the people producing it are the people being measured by it.

**Slide 8 — Diagnose before you calculate**
Write the cause in one sentence before touching a formula. "Cable pulling rate is 10% below tender and the remaining 1,350m carries the same rate." If you cannot write that sentence, you are not forecasting, you are extrapolating.

**Slide 9 — The reality check nobody runs**
TCPI = (BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC) = 58.6 ÷ 55.1 = **1.063**.
The work to date has cost £1.13 for every £1 of budget. To still finish at £86.0m the remaining work must run 6.3% better than budget. Ask what changes on Monday to make that true.

**Slide 10 — The spread is the message**
£89.5m to £102.8m is £13.3m, or 15.4% of the budget. That range is not a defect in the method. It is the method showing you how much of the forecast is assumption rather than measurement.

**Slide 11 — Where the number goes next**
Under an input method of measuring progress, cost incurred over total expected cost drives the revenue recognised. Your EAC is the denominator. And if expected cost passes expected consideration, the whole expected loss is recognised as soon as it becomes expected, not spread across the remaining fifteen months.

**Slide 12 — Report all four, publish one**
Show the range, name the method, name the cause, and record the movement each month. A forecast that never moves is stale rather than stable.
