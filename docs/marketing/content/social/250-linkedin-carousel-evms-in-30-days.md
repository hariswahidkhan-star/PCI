# Standing up an EVMS in 30 days: the full checklist

*LinkedIn document post — 13 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Same 40 foundations, same £720,000, same day on site. Earned value comes out at £396,000 or £485,100.
The difference is £89,100 and it is not performance. It is which rule of credit somebody chose.

Thirteen slides on standing up an earned value management system in a month, in the order that works.

---

**Slide 1 — What an EVMS is**
A method for measuring what has been achieved in the same units as what has been spent, against a baseline that does not move without a record. It is a measurement system. It does not manage anything.

**Slide 2 — Day one: the work breakdown structure**
Deliverable-based, not department-based. The 100% rule applies at every level: the children fully describe the parent, with nothing extra and nothing missing. Write the dictionary entry as you go, because the entry is what stops scope drifting between the estimate and the site.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic that decides everything downstream**
Work package: 40 foundations at £18,000 = **£720,000**.
At cut-off: 22 complete, 9 part-built (rebar 30% and formwork 25% done, concrete 45% not poured).
0/100 credit: 22 × 18,000 = **£396,000**.
50/50 credit: 396,000 + (9 × 9,000) = **£477,000**.
Physical measurement at 55%: 396,000 + (9 × 18,000 × 0.55) = **£485,100**.
Spread **£89,100**, which is **12.4%** of the package budget, with nothing different happening on site.

**Slide 4 — So write the rules of credit first**
Per work package type, agreed before the first measurement, and never changed mid-package. Short packages take 0/100. Repetitive units take physical measurement. Level of effort takes time, and should be as small a proportion of the baseline as you can manage.

**Slide 5 — Control accounts: one owner, one budget**
Size them so a manager can genuinely see the work, typically a few months and a few hundred thousand pounds rather than a year and several million. One named person per account. No shared ownership, because a variance owned by two people is owned by nobody.

**Slide 6 — Time-phase the budget**
Spread each control account's budget across the periods the work is planned in. That curve is your planned value, and without it there is no schedule performance measurement at all. A budget that exists only as a total is a ledger, not a baseline.

**Slide 7 — Keep management reserve outside**
Base estimate, contingency for identified risk, management reserve for what nobody has thought of. Only the first two belong in the performance measurement baseline. Blend them and you cannot tell a drawdown from an overrun, which is the exact question the board will ask in month four.

**Slide 8 — Fix the cut-off calendar**
One date for progress, cost, commitments and accruals. Publish it for the whole year. Where the survey genuinely cannot happen on the cut-off, accrue the gap rather than moving the date, or the indices will report performance that is really a calendar.

**Slide 9 — Set the thresholds before the first report**
Decide what gets a written variance explanation, at what level, at what value. Report by exception so the pack stays readable. Once the threshold exists, silence on an account means something, which is most of the value.

**Slide 10 — One change log, one baseline**
Every movement of scope, budget or date goes through a single register with a before value, an after value, an authority and a date. A baseline that changes without a log is not a baseline, and it makes every variance from that point unarguable.

**Slide 11 — Dry-run on last month's real data**
Week four, before you go live: rebuild last month using the new structure and see whether the numbers are explainable. Every failure you find here is free. Every one you find after go-live costs you the credibility of the whole system.

**Slide 12 — The month, in four weeks**

| Week | Output |
|---|---|
| 1 | WBS to control account level, dictionary, scope signed off |
| 2 | Control accounts, named managers, time-phased baseline |
| 3 | Rules of credit, cut-off calendar, thresholds, change log |
| 4 | Dry run on last period, first report, integrated baseline review booked |

**Slide 13 — What it will not do**
It will not fix a bad estimate, and it will not survive actual costs that arrive without accruals. Earned value measures; people manage.
