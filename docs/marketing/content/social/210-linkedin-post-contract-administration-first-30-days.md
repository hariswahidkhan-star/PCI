# Standing up contract administration in the first 30 days

*LinkedIn text post — 1621 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

You have about 30 days before a notice period expires on something you have not read yet.
This is the order that survives contact with a live job.

Days 1 to 5. The notice calendar. Read the contract once with a highlighter on periods only, and put every dated obligation into a shared diary: notice periods, reply windows, the date a detailed claim falls due, the payment cycle. None of them care that you started late.

Days 5 to 10. One register, serially numbered, covering instructions, early warnings, notices and changes. One. Two registers become three, and then nobody knows which is current.

Days 10 to 15. Records. Daily allocation of labour and plant by location, photographs indexed to a location and a date, delivery records. The test is whether somebody who was not there can reconstruct a Tuesday eighteen months later.

Days 15 to 20. Get the programme accepted, and know which version time will be measured against. An unaccepted baseline is not a scheduling inconvenience. It is an evidential hole.

Days 20 to 30. Delegated authority. Who can instruct, who can certify, up to what value, and what happens while they are on leave.

The arithmetic that pays for the month: twelve instructions a month across eighteen months is 216 events, each carrying a reply window. Miss five per cent and eleven of them get valued by the other party, on their assumptions, without you in the room.

Day 30 test. Pick any instruction from week two. Date of awareness, date of notice, value quoted, days moved. Five minutes, or the regime is not running yet.
