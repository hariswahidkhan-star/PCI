# The risk breakdown structure explained in plain English

*X / Threads thread — 5 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link sits in the final post. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters, so the live figures run lower.*

**Post 1/5 — the hook** (220 characters)
Two hundred and fourteen lines in the risk register. Six categories.

Forty-one per cent of the quantified exposure sat in one of them and nobody had seen it, because the register was sorted by owner instead of by cause.

**Post 2/5 — what an RBS is** (227 characters)
A risk breakdown structure is a hierarchy of the sources of risk on a project, the risk-side counterpart to the work breakdown structure. The WBS organises the work. The RBS organises what can go wrong with it, sorted by cause.

**Post 3/5 — the arithmetic** (230 characters)
Same register, mapped to the RBS. Expected value £15.0m:

Ground and subsurface £6.2m
Interfaces £3.3m
Market and supply £2.6m
Consents £1.7m
Design £0.8m
Other £0.4m

6.2 ÷ 15.0 = 41%. That is where the mitigation budget belongs.

**Post 4/5 — source, not consequence** (236 characters)
Three levels, categories that do not overlap, and every line classified by cause. "Switchgear delivered late" is a consequence. "Single-source supply of long-lead switchgear" is a source. Only the second one can be mitigated by anybody.

**Post 5/5 — the payoff people miss** (256 characters)
Risks in the same RBS branch usually share a driver, so the structure tells you your correlation groups in the cost model instead of leaving them to a guess.
