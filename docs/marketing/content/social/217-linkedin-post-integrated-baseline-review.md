# The integrated baseline review nobody prepares for

**Post body (1,843 characters):**

An integrated baseline review is not an audit of your numbers. It is a test of whether the person who owns a control account can explain how it earns value, without opening a spreadsheet.

Four questions do most of the work. What scope sits in this account and where is it in the WBS. What logic drives its dates. What rule of credit earns the value. What is the estimate to complete built from, and who resourced it.

If the answers come back as "the tool calculates it", the baseline is a document rather than a plan, and the first variance report will be argued rather than actioned.

Sample by value, not by count. Take 60 control accounts totalling £12.0m, an average of £200,000 each. Review 12 at random and you have covered 20% of the accounts and roughly £2.4m. Review the eight largest, carrying £7.2m between them, and eight conversations cover 60% of the budget.

Then check one reconciliation that is failed more often than any other. Contract target cost £13.0m. Performance measurement baseline £12.0m. Management reserve £1.0m, held by the project sponsor, outside the PMB and not distributed to any account.

If the control account budgets sum to £13.0m, reserve has been buried in the work, and every CPI on the job is flattered by the contingency hiding inside it.

Preparation is a week, not a day. Print the responsibility assignment matrix and make sure every intersection has a named person. Walk the schedule logic on the four biggest accounts. Re-read the rules of credit and confirm they match what the site is actually being asked to report.

The review does not exist to catch you out. It exists so that in month six, when the forecast moves, the argument is about the work and not about whether the baseline ever meant anything.

Book it before the baseline is frozen. After that, everyone is defending a position.
