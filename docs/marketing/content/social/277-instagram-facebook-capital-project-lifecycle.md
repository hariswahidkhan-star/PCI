# The capital project lifecycle, concept to operation

*Instagram / Facebook carousel — 9 slides, 1080 × 1350. Caption below; the first 125 characters carry it before the fold.*

**Caption (first 125 characters before the fold):**

The build costs £240m. Running it for thirty years costs £450m. Guess which number the design meeting talks about.

Nine slides on the capital project lifecycle: what each stage decides, why the cheapest stage sets the dearest number, and the arithmetic on slide 3.

Full guide in the link in bio.

**Slide 1 — What a capital project is**
A capital project creates or materially improves a long-lived asset: a plant, a line, a terminal, a network. The spend is committed up front and recovered over decades of use, which is why the decision to build is a financing decision before it is an engineering one.

**Slide 2 — The lifecycle, one line each**
Identify the need. Select an option. Define it well enough to commit. Sanction. Execute. Commission and hand over. Operate, maintain, and eventually decommission. Each stage buys better information than the one before it.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Worked case. Capital cost **£240m**. Operating cost **£15m a year for 30 years = £450m**.

A design change adds **£6m** of capital cost and removes **£0.9m a year** of operating cost.

Simple payback = 6.0 ÷ 0.9 = **6.7 years**
Undiscounted return over the life = (0.9 × 30) − 6 = **£21m**
Discounted at 6% = (0.9 × 13.76) − 6 = **£6.4m** of net present value

Still worth doing. It has to be decided in the stage that costs the least to run.

**Slide 4 — Why the early stages decide the number**
Studies and design are a small fraction of the spend and they set most of what the asset will cost to build and to run. Once steel is on order the options have closed. Controls effort at concept is cheap and it is the only effort that changes the outcome rather than reporting it.

**Slide 5 — Sanction: what is actually being approved**
At final investment decision a board approves a number, a date and a scope, and accepts the risk range around all three. The estimate class, the basis of estimate and the contingency behind that number matter more than the number itself, because they say how wrong it is allowed to be.

**Slide 6 — Execute: the baseline earns its keep**
Now the job is measurement. Progress against a written rule of credit, cost booked to the same breakdown as the baseline, one cut-off, one estimate at completion with its method named. A forecast that never moves is stale rather than stable.

**Slide 7 — Commissioning is where programmes slip**
Construction completion is not readiness. Testing, snagging, regulatory sign-off, operator training and spares all sit between the last weld and first production. They are routinely planned as a single bar on the programme, and they are routinely where the last three months go.

**Slide 8 — Where it lands in the accounts** Capital spend does not hit the profit and loss when it is paid. It sits as an asset under construction and starts depreciating when the asset is ready for its intended use.

Which cost qualifies to be capitalised, and the date the clock starts, are decisions taken from delivery evidence. That is the overlap where finance and delivery either agree or discover they disagree at year end.

A planner is rarely examined on capitalisation. An accountant is rarely examined on stage gates. The PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) sits across 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas for exactly that reason.
