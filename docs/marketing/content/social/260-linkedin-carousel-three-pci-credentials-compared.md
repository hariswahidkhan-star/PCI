# PCL-AI, PFL-AI, PML-AI: three credentials compared

*LinkedIn document post — 13 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

A planner moved the estimate at completion by £3.7m.
Recognised revenue for the period fell by £1.93m, and nobody in the chain had been examined on the crossing.

Thirteen slides on the three PCI credentials, what each one covers, and how to pick.

---

**Slide 1 — Three credentials, three shapes**

PCI Global issues three AI-era credentials, each with its own Body of Knowledge and examination: the PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI), the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) and the PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI). They differ in where they sit relative to the boundary between delivery and the reported number.

**Slide 2 — The idea all three answer**

A chartered accountant is examined on when revenue may be recognised and what a provision must satisfy. They are almost never examined on a critical path or a rule of credit. An engineer is examined on float and progress measurement, almost never on cut-off or a contract asset.

The project lives in the overlap, and the overlap is where the money is lost.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic of the overlap**
Fixed-price contract worth **£60.0m**. Budget at completion **£48.0m**. Cost incurred to date **£21.6m**. Control transfers over time, and progress is measured by a cost-to-cost input method.

**With EAC held at £48.0m:**
Progress = 21.6 ÷ 48.0 = **45.00%**
Revenue recognised = 0.4500 × 60.0 = **£27.00m**

**With EAC forecast at £51.7m:**
Progress = 21.6 ÷ 51.7 = **41.78%**
Revenue recognised = 0.4178 × 60.0 = **£25.07m**

**A £3.7m move in a delivery forecast moves recognised revenue by £1.93m**, and expected margin from £12.0m to £8.3m.

The person who moved the EAC was a planner. The person who explains the revenue movement is a financial controller. Neither has been examined on the other's half.

**Slide 4 — What the accounting model asks, in PCI's own words**

Revenue recognition under IFRS 15 follows five steps, described here rather than reproduced:

1. Identify the contract — an agreement creating enforceable rights and obligations, with commercial substance and collection judged probable.
2. Identify the performance obligations — the distinct promises in it. On construction work these are frequently combined into one obligation, because the promises are highly interrelated and the entity provides a significant integration service.
3. Determine the transaction price — the consideration expected, including variable amounts such as claims and incentives, constrained so a significant revenue reversal is not highly probable.
4. Allocate the price across the obligations, normally on relative stand-alone selling prices.
5. Recognise revenue as each obligation is satisfied — at a point in time, or over time where the criteria are met, measured by progress towards complete satisfaction.

Step 5 is where your EAC enters the accounts. Apply the standard itself with your auditor; this is a description, not advice.

**Slide 5 — And the test nobody warns a planner about**

If the forecast cost to complete a contract rises above the consideration expected from it, the loss becomes an expected loss and is recognised in full in the period it becomes expected — not spread across the months remaining.

A forecast that quietly crosses the contract price is a same-period accounting event. The project director is usually the last person in the building to hear about it.

**Slide 6 — PCL-AI: the integrated controls credential**

The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) covers **13 domains and 61 knowledge areas**, spanning planning, cost, risk, performance measurement, the handover into reporting, and governed AI.

It suits project controls managers, senior planners and cost leads who own the numbers that leave the project.

**Slide 7 — PFL-AI: the finance side of a project**

The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) covers **16 domains and 61 knowledge areas**, weighted towards project accounting, revenue and margin recognition, cash and working capital, funding and the controls that sit around them.

It suits commercial and finance leads on capital projects, and controls professionals who have to defend a forecast in front of finance.

**Slide 8 — PML-AI: delivery leadership with the reporting attached**

The PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) covers **16 domains and 63 knowledge areas**, across delivery method, contract and change, stakeholders, benefits, and the reporting obligations a project manager carries.

It suits project and programme managers who are accountable for outcomes and for what those outcomes look like in the accounts.

**Slide 9 — Side by side**

| | PCL-AI | PFL-AI | PML-AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full name | PCI AI Project Controls Leader | PCI AI Project Finance Leader | PCI Project Management Leader – AI |
| Domains | 13 | 16 | 16 |
| Knowledge areas | 61 | 61 | 63 |
| Centre of gravity | Measurement and the crossing | The reported number | Delivery and its reporting |
| Typical holder's day job | Controls manager, senior planner, cost lead | Commercial or project finance lead | Project or programme manager |

**Slide 10 — What sits behind all three**

Each Body of Knowledge is proportioned **40 / 40 / 20** across finance and reporting, project management, and governed AI. Behind the syllabus sit **113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements**, and **92 sector case studies across the three volumes (26 + 33 + 33)**.

The PCI Standards are certification requirements established by the Institute. They are not law.

**Slide 11 — How the arithmetic itself is checked**

The calculation content of the PFL-AI and PML-AI volumes is verified by **15,613 machine calculation checks, all passing**; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

That figure covers PFL-AI and PML-AI only. Quoting it as a whole-suite number would be false, so it is never written here without its scope.

**Slide 12 — What PCI does not claim**

No accreditation, recognition, endorsement, affiliation or partnership that has not happened. No promised salary, role or outcome. No published pass rates or holder counts. And nothing PCI publishes is legal, tax or accounting advice.

Read any certifying body's published requirements — PCI's included — before you pay anything.

**Slide 13 — Choosing between them**

Start from the number you are accountable for. If you own the measurement and the forecast, PCL-AI. If you own what that forecast becomes in the accounts, PFL-AI. If you own the delivery outcome and the reporting that follows it, PML-AI.

If the honest answer is that you sit on the boundary and have never been examined on the other side of it, that is the point of the suite.
