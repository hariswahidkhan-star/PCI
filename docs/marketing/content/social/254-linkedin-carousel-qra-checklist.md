# QRA checklist: what good looks like, in ten slides

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Your base estimate already held £1.8m of allowances.
Then the QRA added £3.2m of contingency on top of it, for the same risks.

You have just asked the board to fund one uncertainty twice. Ten checks that stop it happening.

---

**Slide 1 — What a QRA is supposed to produce**

Quantitative risk analysis turns a ranked risk register and an estimate into a distribution of possible out-turn cost or dates, and from that distribution a funded contingency at a stated confidence level. One number, one confidence level, one documented basis.

**Slide 2 — Check one: the base is clean**

Every allowance, contingency-by-another-name and deliberate rounding-up has to come out of the base before modelling. Ask the estimator directly which lines carry comfort. They always know, and they are rarely asked.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic of double counting**
Base estimate as submitted: **£20.0m**, including **£1.8m** of allowances.

**Done properly:**
Clean base = 20.0 − 1.8 = **£18.2m**
QRA on the clean base returns P80 = **£21.4m**
Contingency = 21.4 − 18.2 = **£3.2m**
Total funding required = **£21.4m**

**Done badly, with the allowances left in:**
Base = £20.0m, QRA returns P80 = **£23.2m**
Contingency = **£3.2m**
Total funding required = **£23.2m**

**£1.8m of the same uncertainty funded twice**, and a bid that is 8.4% less competitive than it needed to be.

**Slide 4 — Check two: uncertainty and risk events modelled separately**

Uncertainty is a range on work that will definitely happen — a rate, a quantity, a productivity factor. A risk event is a probability with an impact, and it either occurs in an iteration or it does not.

Fold a 25% × £2.4m risk into the base as a £600k allowance and the mean stays right while the tail disappears. The model can no longer see the outcome you built it to price.

**Slide 5 — Check three: ranges have a documented basis**

Each three-point range carries a one-line reason in the model: firm quotation, benchmarked rate, engineering judgement, or single-source estimate with no comparator. Ranges without a basis are the first thing an auditor pulls and the hardest thing to defend six months later.

**Slide 6 — Check four: correlation is set deliberately**

Items driven by the same market or the same crew move together. If steel, fabrication and erection are sampled independently, their variation cancels and the model returns a spuriously narrow answer.

Group items by driver, apply a stated correlation within each group, and print the assumption on the output sheet. An undocumented correlation setting is the single largest unexplained swing in most QRA results.

**Slide 7 — Check five: risks map to something in the model**

Every quantified row on the register points at a specific cost item, activity or group. A risk with no mapping either does not affect the estimate or has been left out of it, and both need saying out loud.

Run the mapping as a two-way reconciliation: register to model, model to register. The rows that fall out of either side are the finding.

**Slide 8 — Checks six to eight: iterations, convergence, sensitivity**

Run enough iterations that the percentile you are quoting stops moving, not just the mean — tail percentiles settle far later than the average does. Then produce a tornado chart and check that the top five drivers are risks the team recognises.

If the largest driver is a line nobody in the room can explain, the model has an input error, not an insight.

**Slide 9 — Checks nine and ten: the stated confidence, and the drawdown plan**

| What must appear on the output | Why |
|---|---|
| The confidence level quoted | P50 and P80 differ by real money; the reader must know which |
| Clean base, contingency, total | Three numbers, so the split can be audited |
| Correlation and distribution assumptions | The two settings that move the answer most |
| Date and version of the register modelled | Ties the number to a point in time |
| Drawdown rule | Contingency released against closed risks, not by elapsed time |

A contingency with no drawdown rule is spent by month six and defended by nobody.

**Slide 10 — Why finance cares about your P80**

Contingency drawn down becomes cost incurred, and under a cost-to-cost input method cost incurred over total expected cost is the measure of progress that drives revenue. Release contingency early and you flatter progress; hold it to the end and you understate it.
