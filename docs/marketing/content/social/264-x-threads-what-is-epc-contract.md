# What an EPC contract actually transfers, and to whom

*X / Threads thread — 5 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link sits in the final post. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters, so the live figures run lower.*

**Post 1/5 — the hook** (230 characters)
£180m as a reimbursable estimate. £207m for the same scope as an EPC lump sum.

The £27m is not margin. It is the quoted price of the risks you are handing over, and whether it is worth paying is arithmetic rather than preference.

**Post 2/5 — what EPC is** (251 characters)
An EPC contract puts engineering, procurement and construction with one contractor for a fixed price and a fixed date. The contractor owns the interfaces between the three. That interface risk is the part clients under-price when they split the scope.

**Post 3/5 — the arithmetic** (214 characters)
Worked case, straight off the cost risk model.

Base estimate £180m.
P50 £191m. P80 £214m. Expected out-turn £196m.
The lump sum of £207m sits near P70.
Premium over expectation = 207 − 196 = £11m, or 6.1% of base.

**Post 4/5 — what never transfers** (242 characters)
Change you instruct. Information and free-issue items you supply late. Consents you hold. Every risk the contract carves out by name. Read the exclusions before you decide you have bought certainty, because that is where the money comes back.

**Post 5/5 — lump sum is not a reason to stop measuring** (237 characters)
The transfer leaks back through the change register, so the client still needs earned value, a change log and a forecast of its own.
