# The FIDIC 2017 claims procedure, drawn as a clock face

*LinkedIn text post — 1522 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Day 0 you knew. Day 28 you notify, or the claim is barred.
Day 84 the fully detailed claim. Day 168 a determination, and that is the version where nobody is late.

The 2017 FIDIC forms run claims on a clock. Draw it once and put it on the wall.

Day 0. Awareness of the event or circumstance. Every other date counts from here, which is exactly why awareness is what gets argued about. Record it when it happens, not when it hurts.

Day 28. Notice of claim. A late notice is not automatically dead in the 2017 forms: the claiming party gives its justification and the other side has 14 days to disagree, failing which the notice stands. That is a relief valve, not a licence.

Day 84. The fully detailed claim. Contractual and legal basis, supporting particulars, and substantiation of the time and money claimed. This is the document that decides the outcome. The notice only buys the right to write it.

Day 126. The Engineer has 42 days to consult both parties and try to reach agreement.

Day 168. Failing agreement, a further 42 days to determine. No determination inside the time limit counts as a rejection, which is what opens the route to the DAAB.

168 days from awareness to something you can act on. Your monthly cost report will not wait that long, so decide now how a claim in progress appears in a forecast, because "pending" is not a number.

Your contract governs. Particular conditions amend these periods routinely, and none of this is legal advice.
