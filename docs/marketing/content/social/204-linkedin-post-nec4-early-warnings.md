# NEC4 early warnings: the meeting nobody ever books

*LinkedIn text post — 1525 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

The same event cost £180,000 to answer in week 6 and £640,000 in week 14.
Nothing about the event changed. The options did, and options are the only thing early warning buys.

Early warning under NEC4 is a mutual duty. The Project Manager and the Contractor each notify anything that could raise the Prices, delay Completion or a Key Date, or impair the performance of the works in use. It goes on the Early Warning Register with an owner and a date, which is what separates it from a risk register with a heat map on it.

The register is not the mechanism. The meeting is. NEC4 expects the first early warning meeting within two weeks of the starting date, then at the interval set in the Contract Data. Teams that skip the first one rarely book the second.

The arithmetic:

Week 6. Resequence two work fronts, extend one hire agreement, no effect on Completion. £180,000.
Week 14. Crane already on hire, follow-on trade mobilised, 11 days on the critical path. £640,000.
Difference: £460,000.

There is a sanction attached, and it is the part people miss. Where the Contractor failed to give an early warning that an experienced contractor could have given, and the Project Manager notifies that decision, the compensation event is assessed as if the warning had been given.

The assessment lands on the week-6 answer. The £460,000 stays with the party that stayed quiet.

Early warning is not a courtesy. It is a valuation rule with a price on silence.
