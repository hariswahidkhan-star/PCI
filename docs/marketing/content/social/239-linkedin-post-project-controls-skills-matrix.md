# Rate yourself against the project controls skills matrix

**Post body (1,794 characters):**

Score yourself 1 to 5 on eight things. Whatever you score below 3 on is what your next interview will be about, and probably your next appraisal too.

The project controls skills matrix has eight rows: schedule construction and CPM; progress measurement and earning rules; cost control from commitment to actual; estimating and its classification; change and contract administration; risk and quantitative analysis; reporting that triggers a decision; and the finance interface, meaning revenue recognition, cash and the ledger.

The five levels: 1 aware of it. 2 can do it with the file open in front of you. 3 can do it unsupervised on a live project. 4 can defend it to a client or an auditor. 5 can set the standard and teach it.

Two honesty rules, because self-assessment without them is decoration. Score 3 only if you have done it on a live project in the last 18 months. Score 4 only if somebody outside your own organisation has challenged you on it and you held your ground.

Now weight it, because the rows are not equal and an unweighted total hides what matters. For a planning-led role: rows 1 and 2 at weight 3, rows 3 and 7 at weight 2, the rest at weight 1. Maximum = 5 × (3+3+2+2+1+1+1+1) = 5 × 14 = 70.

Score 44 out of 70 and you are at 63%. Useful, but the number is not the output. This is:

A 2 on row 2 at weight 3 carries 3 × 3 = 9 points of upside.
A 1 on row 6 at weight 1 carries 4 × 1 = 4 points.

Your instinct says fix the 1. The arithmetic says fix the 2. Development effort goes to the lowest-scoring heavy row, not the lowest score on the sheet.

One prediction. Most people in this discipline, at every level, score lowest on row 8. That row is where the schedule meets the accounts, and it is exactly where projects lose money that neither side sees coming.
