# Gulf giga-project hiring: what is actually being bought

*Instagram and Facebook carousel — 8 slides, 1080 × 1350. Instagram captions carry no clickable link, so the link goes in the bio; on Facebook it goes in the post.*

**Caption (the first 125 characters have to earn the swipe):**

Five contractors. Five different cut-off dates. One report headed "as at month end", missing 12.4%.

The Gulf giga-programmes are not hiring planners to plan. They are hiring people who can turn dozens of contractors reporting in different formats on different cycles into one cost position and one schedule position. Eight slides on what that job actually is, what it pays attention to, and what this page will not pretend to know.

Save it if the Gulf is on your list.

---

**Slide 1 — What is actually being bought**

Programme-scale delivery fails at the seams between contracts, not inside them. What the giga-programmes are buying is consolidation: one cost position and one schedule position across many contractors.

That is a different job from project controls on a single contract. It is why experienced planners arrive and still find the first six months hard.

**Slide 2 — What this carousel will not tell you**

There is no credible count of open project controls roles in the Kingdom, so no number appears here. Any figure quoted to you is a job board's stale index or a recruiter's marketing.

No salary figures either, and no claim about what a credential does to pay. And the capital values attached to publicly announced programmes are statements of intent, not audited spend. Giga-programmes everywhere get rescoped and rephased as they mature.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**

Five contractors on one programme, all reporting into a monthly pack headed "as at month end".

| Contractor | Cut-off | Days stale | Monthly spend | Not in the report |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 25th | 5 | SAR 60m | SAR 9.9m |
| B | 28th | 2 | SAR 45m | SAR 3.0m |
| C | Month end | 0 | SAR 70m | SAR 0.0m |
| D | 20th | 10 | SAR 42m | SAR 13.8m |
| E | 26th | 4 | SAR 33m | SAR 4.3m |
| **Total** | | | **SAR 250m** | **SAR 31.0m** |

Each figure is spend ÷ 30.4 days × days stale. Total missing = **SAR 31.0m**, which is **12.4%** of a month's spend on a page that says "as at month end".

Nothing was falsified. Five cut-off dates were accepted because changing them was a contractual conversation nobody wanted.

**Slide 4 — Interface float belongs to nobody**

The classic programme failure. Contractor A finishes early into a slot Contractor B cannot use for three weeks. Neither owns the gap, both consume it, and it disappears while every package still reports green.

Programme roles exist to see that float, put a name on it, and price it before it is gone.

**Slide 5 — What changes from project to programme**

| | Project scale | Programme scale |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | One schedule, one cost breakdown | Many baselines that must reconcile to one |
| Reporting | One cut-off | Many cut-offs forced into one calendar |
| Critical path | One network | Interface paths where float has no owner |
| Forecast | One method, one estimator | A method that survives packages at different maturities |
| The question | Is this contract on track? | Which of forty things do we fix first, and what does it do to the funding profile? |

**Slide 6 — Hired, and stuck**

Hired: evidence of consolidating multiple contractors into one position, a forecast you chose the method for and defended in front of someone who disagreed, and the ability to explain an interface delay to a person who does not read schedules.

Stuck: deep skill in one tool and one package type, reporting that has never been challenged by an external party, and a career spent entirely on one side of the client-contractor line.

**Slide 7 — What the Kingdom adds that is not planning technique**

Engineers working in Saudi Arabia are generally required to register with the Saudi Council of Engineers, with qualifications verified as part of the process; confirm the current position with the employer and the Council before you resign anything.

The Nitaqat framework sets nationalisation targets that shape which roles are open to expatriate hire. Attestation, medicals, visas and client CV approval run in series more often than in parallel — three to four months from offer to start is normal. Much of the work is camp-based on rotation rather than in Riyadh or Jeddah.

**Slide 8 — Where the money is actually lost**

A chartered accountant is examined on when revenue may be recognised and what a provision must satisfy, almost never on an interface float. An engineer is examined on progress and float, almost never on cut-off or a contract asset. Programme controls sits exactly on that boundary.

The person who can say that package D's slip moves a certification milestone past the period end, which moves recognised revenue and delays a payment two quarters, is doing the job these programmes are hiring for.
