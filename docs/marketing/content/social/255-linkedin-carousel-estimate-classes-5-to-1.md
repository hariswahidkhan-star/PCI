# Cost estimate classes 5 to 1 and the accuracy bands

*LinkedIn document post — 11 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

You sanctioned £40m. The estimate's own band ran from £32m to £56m.
The midpoint of that band was £44m, so the project started £4m adrift and nobody did anything wrong.

Eleven slides on what an estimate class actually promises, and what its band commits you to.

---

**Slide 1 — A class is a statement about definition**

An estimate class describes how much of the project is defined when the estimate is produced. It is a property of the engineering, not of the estimator's effort, and no amount of extra work moves the class while the deliverables stay immature.

**Slide 2 — Five classes, numbered downwards**

Classes run from 5 at concept to 1 at near-complete definition. AACE International publishes the classification framework and its accuracy ranges in its recommended practices; read the ranges there rather than from anybody's slide, including this one. What follows is the mechanism, in PCI's own words, with illustrative arithmetic.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic of a band**
Estimate: **£40m**. Basis supports an illustrative band of **−20% / +40%**.

Low end = 40 × 0.80 = **£32m**
High end = 40 × 1.40 = **£56m**
Band width = **£24m**, which is **60% of the point estimate**
Midpoint of the band = (32 + 56) ÷ 2 = **£44m**

Approving £40m approves a number **£4m below the midpoint of its own range**, before a single risk materialises.

Now take definition far enough for an illustrative **−10% / +15%** band:
£36m to £46m. Width **£10m**. Midpoint **£41m**. The structural gap falls from £4m to £1m.

**£3m bought by front-end definition, not by cost control.**

**Slide 4 — What actually moves a class**

Deliverables move it: drawings issued, equipment specified, quantities taken off, packages quoted. Time does not move it, and neither does pressure from the sanction calendar.

If an estimate is described as Class 3 but the equipment list is still indicative, it is a Class 4 estimate wearing a Class 3 label, and every downstream number inherits the lie.

**Slide 5 — Class 5: is this worth studying**

Almost nothing is drawn. You have a capacity, a location and perhaps a block diagram. The estimate is built from capacity factors, historical curves and judgement, and its purpose is to decide whether to spend money finding out more.

A confident Class 5 number is the most dangerous document on a project, because it is the one that gets remembered.

**Slide 6 — Class 4: choosing between options**

A preliminary equipment list, indicative layouts, single-line diagrams. Parametric models and factored equipment costs do the work here. The purpose is comparing options against each other, not committing to any of them.

**Slide 7 — Class 3: the sanction estimate**

Process and layout substantially settled, major equipment specified, preliminary quantities available. Semi-detailed unit rates against assembly-level line items. This is normally the estimate that becomes the authorised budget and the baseline that earned value will be measured against.

It is also the estimate where the band is widest relative to the commitment being made, which is why the basis of estimate matters more here than anywhere else.

**Slide 8 — Class 2: checking the bid**

Detailed engineering well advanced, quantities taken off from drawings, most packages returned from the market. Detailed unit-cost build-up with real take-offs. The purpose is checking a tender or confirming the control estimate before a contract is signed.

**Slide 9 — Class 1: the last check**

Engineering essentially complete for the scope being estimated, quantities measured, prices quoted and mostly firm. The band is narrow because there is little left to be uncertain about, not because the estimator has become more careful.

**Slide 10 — A band is not contingency, and never has been**

| | Accuracy band | Contingency |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A property of the estimate | A funded amount inside the estimate |
| What it describes | How much the number could move | Specific quantified exposure |
| Where it appears | Basis of estimate | The cost plan, as a held line |
| Can you spend it? | No | Yes, against closed risks |

Quoting a range instead of holding contingency is how a project reaches financial close having listed its risks and funded none of them.

**Slide 11 — Three things on the front page, every time**

The class. The deliverables the class rests on. The exclusions. If the class is not written down, the reader assumes the number is firm and commits to it.

The estimate class also decides what finance can do with your number: a Class 5 concept figure cannot support a capitalisation judgement or a cost-to-cost progress measure, and a Class 3 sanction estimate becomes the denominator in both.
