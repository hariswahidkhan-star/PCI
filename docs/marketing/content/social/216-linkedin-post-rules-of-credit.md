# Rules of credit beat percentage guesses every month

**Post body (1,729 characters):**

Ask a foreman for a percentage and you will get 90% for three months running. Rules of credit end that, because the credit is agreed before anybody is behind.

A rule of credit fixes, at baseline, what fraction of a work package is earned at each verifiable step. Pipe spool erection, for example:

Received and inspected 10%
Rigged and set 30%
Aligned and bolted 25%
Welded out 25%
Punched and handed over 10%

Now the arithmetic. 400 spools, £4.0m budget, so £10,000 a spool. At the cut-off, 120 spools are rigged and set and no further.

By rule of credit: 120 × 40% × £10,000 = £480,000 earned
By spool count: 120 × £10,000 = £1,200,000 claimed

£720,000 of earned value that does not exist. Feed the second figure into CPI = EV / AC and the package reports healthy for two more months, which is exactly long enough for the recovery options to close.

Four methods cover almost every work package. Milestone weightings for discrete steps like the spools. 0/100 for short-duration work, where nothing is earned until it is finished, so nothing can be fudged.

Units complete for repetitive work, cubic metres poured against the total, provided the total is fixed. Level of effort for supervision and project management, which earns with time whether or not anything happens.

Level of effort is the one to watch. It earns automatically, so a package with too much of it reports a CPI near 1.00 by construction. Keep it under a written cap and review the cap at every rebaseline.

Two disciplines make the whole thing hold. Write the rules before the baseline is frozen, and require a second person, not the one claiming the credit, to verify the step.

That is the difference between measuring progress and being told about it.
