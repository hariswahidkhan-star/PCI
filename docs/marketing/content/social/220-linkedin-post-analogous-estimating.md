# Analogous estimating: fast, cheap, predictably wrong

**Post body (1,793 characters):**

Analogous estimating prices the new job from one you have already built. It is the fastest method there is, and it fails in one predictable direction: low.

It fails low for two reasons. The comparable people reach for is the job that went well, because that is the one they remember fondly. And memory drops the scope that was added after contract, so the analogue in your head is cheaper than the analogue in the ledger.

Four adjustments fix most of it. Size, time, location, scope difference. Worked through:

Analogue: 6,000 m² office fit-out, final cost £9.0m, completed 30 months ago. That is £1,500 per m².

New job, 8,500 m².

Straight scaling: 8,500 × £1,500 = £12.75m
Escalation at 5% a year for 2.5 years, 1.05^2.5 = 1.13 → £14.41m
Location factor 1.08 for the new city → £15.56m
Scope: the analogue excluded AV and specialist lighting the client supplied directly, about £100 per m² this time → +£0.85m → £16.41m

Straight scaling was 22% light. None of that gap is a surprise, a risk event or bad luck. It is four adjustments that took ten minutes.

Two more things worth doing before the number leaves your desk.

Pull the analogue's final account, not its contract sum. The two are rarely the same, and the difference between them is usually the most honest data you hold.

Check that the scaling is even linear. Fit-out roughly is, per square metre. Plant capacity usually is not, which is why capacity factors carry an exponent rather than a straight multiplier.

Analogous estimating belongs at concept, quoted as a range with the four adjustments listed underneath. It is the right tool when there is nothing to measure yet.

It stops being the right tool the moment there is a drawing set, and hanging on to it past that point is how a concept number becomes a commitment.
