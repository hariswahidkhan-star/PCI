# Earned value in one worked month: PV, EV, AC and EAC

*Instagram and Facebook carousel — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. Instagram captions carry no clickable link, so the link goes in the bio; on Facebook it goes in the post.*

**Caption (the first 125 characters have to earn the swipe):**

You have spent 35.8% of the budget and earned 31.5% of the work. Month 7 of 20. Nobody has told the board yet.

Earned value management is the arithmetic that turns that sentence into a forecast you can defend. Ten slides, one water treatment contract, one cut-off, every number shown. Budget £24.0m. By slide 8 you will know what it finishes at, and which of the four forecasts you are actually signing.

Save it for the next cost report.

---

**Slide 1 — Three numbers, one moment**

Planned value (PV) is the budgeted cost of the work that was scheduled to be done by the cut-off. Earned value (EV) is the budgeted cost of the work actually done. Actual cost (AC) is what was spent doing it.

All three are stated in budget currency at the same data date. That is the whole trick: two of them price work, one prices spend.

**Slide 2 — The contract, month 7 of 20**

Water treatment works. Budget at completion (BAC) **£24.0m**.

PV **£8.40m** · EV **£7.56m** · AC **£8.60m**

Planned to be 35.0% complete. Actually 31.5% complete. Spent 35.8% of the budget.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**

| Measure | Formula | This month |
|---|---|---:|
| Cost variance | EV − AC | 7.56 − 8.60 = **−£1.04m** |
| Schedule variance | EV − PV | 7.56 − 8.40 = **−£0.84m** |
| Cost performance index | EV ÷ AC | 7.56 ÷ 8.60 = **0.879** |
| Schedule performance index | EV ÷ PV | 7.56 ÷ 8.40 = **0.900** |
| Per cent complete | EV ÷ BAC | 7.56 ÷ 24.0 = **31.5%** |
| Per cent spent | AC ÷ BAC | 8.60 ÷ 24.0 = **35.8%** |

Read the last two together. You are 4.3 percentage points of budget adrift, which on £24.0m is **£1.03m** of value bought and not received.

**Slide 4 — What CPI 0.879 actually says**

Every £1 of budgeted work has cost **£1.138** to buy. That is AC ÷ EV, the same index the other way up.

It is a rate, not a total. Applied to the £16.44m of budget still to earn, a rate is what turns a £1.04m variance today into something much larger at handover.

**Slide 5 — What SPI 0.900 cannot tell you**

SPI says 90% of the planned work has been earned in value terms. It says nothing about whether the missing 10% sits on the critical path.

A project can run SPI 0.90 entirely in float and finish on time, or SPI 0.97 on the driving path and finish three months late. Read SPI beside the critical path, never instead of it.

**Slide 6 — Four forecasts from one set of numbers**

| Method | Formula | EAC |
|---|---|---:|
| Rest runs at plan | AC + (BAC − EV) | **£25.04m** |
| Performance continues | BAC ÷ CPI | **£27.30m** |
| Recovery costs money | AC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI) | **£29.38m** |
| Re-estimate the rest | AC + fresh ETC of £18.2m | **£26.80m** |

Checks: 8.60 + 16.44 = 25.04. 24.0 ÷ 0.879 = 27.30. 0.879 × 0.900 = 0.791, and 16.44 ÷ 0.791 = 20.78, plus 8.60 = 29.38.

**Slide 7 — What each one assumes**

Method 1 says the £1.04m already lost was a one-off and nothing about it repeats. Defensible after a flood or a settled claim, indefensible when the cause is a labour rate.

Method 2 says the project keeps behaving as it has behaved. It uses the most measured data and the fewest assumptions, which is why it should be the number you argue your way off, not onto.

Method 3 is for a job buying back time with overtime. Method 4 is the only one that can price scope the indices have never seen, and the easiest to bias, because the people producing it are the people being measured by it.

**Slide 8 — The reality check nobody runs**

To-complete performance index = (BAC − EV) ÷ (BAC − AC) = 16.44 ÷ 15.40 = **1.068**.

The work so far has run at 0.879. To still land on £24.0m, everything remaining must run at 1.068 — about **21% better than demonstrated performance**. Ask what changes on Monday to make that true.

**Slide 9 — Where the earned value came from**

EV is only as honest as the rule that produced it. If the site said "about a third done" and somebody multiplied, you have an opinion with a currency symbol in front of it.

Rules of credit fix the earning steps before the work starts, so the same progress claim gives the same number whoever fills the form in.

**Slide 10 — Where the number goes next**

A £3.30m variance at completion is not a cost report line. It changes the funding drawdown, it changes what may be recognised on an input measure of progress, and if expected cost passes expected consideration the whole expected loss is recognised as soon as it becomes expected — not spread over the remaining thirteen months.
