# Qualitative risk analysis: the 5x5 and its real limits

**Post body (1,857 characters):**

Two risks on the same register both score 12 on the 5x5 matrix. Same colour, same cell, same place in the ranking. One is worth £990k and the other £240k.

Qualitative risk analysis rates each risk on ordinal bands for probability and impact, multiplies the two scores, and ranks the register by the product. It is a screening tool. It does not price anything.

The arithmetic behind those two rows:

Risk A: probability 55% (band 3), impact £1.8m (band 4). Score 3 × 4 = 12.
Expected value 0.55 × 1.8 = £990k

Risk B: probability 75% (band 4), impact £320k (band 3). Score 4 × 3 = 12.
Expected value 0.75 × 0.32 = £240k

Identical score. Four times the money. The matrix cannot see it, and it never will, because that is not what a matrix does.

Three limits worth stating out loud before anyone puts a heat map in front of a board.

Multiplying ordinal scores is not arithmetic. Bands 1 to 5 are labels, not quantities. A score of 12 is not twice as bad as a score of 6 unless the bands happen to be geometric, and almost nobody defines them that way.

The top band swallows everything above it. If band 5 impact reads "over £2m", then a £2.1m risk and a £21m risk land in the same cell and rank identically. The one exposure capable of ending the project is filed alongside a bad week.

Probability bands compress at the ends. The difference between 2% and 15% is the difference between ignoring something and resourcing it, and most scales put both in band 1.

None of that makes the method useless. It makes it a first pass.

Its real job is triage: take 140 rows, put the team in a room for an hour, and come out with the 15 worth the cost of quantifying. That is a genuine piece of work and nothing else does it as cheaply.

The failure is not using the matrix. The failure is stopping there, then reporting a colour to people who asked for a number.
