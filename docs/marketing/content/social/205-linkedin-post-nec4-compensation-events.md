# NEC4 compensation events, without the clause numbers

*LinkedIn text post — 1480 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

A compensation event is a forecast, agreed once, and not reopened because it turned out wrong.
That single sentence decides who carries the next £170,100.

A compensation event is a listed event that changes the Prices, the Completion Date or a Key Date. It is assessed as the change to forecast Defined Cost plus Fee, together with any delay to planned Completion measured on the Accepted Programme.

Forecast is the word doing the work.

Take a quotation built on 14 days of additional work at £22,500 a day.
14 × 22,500 = £315,000.
Fee at 8% = £25,200.
Quotation: £340,200, plus 9 days to planned Completion.

The work then takes 21 days.
The extra 7 days = £157,500 + £12,600 fee = £170,100, carried by the Contractor.
Finish in 10 days instead and the Contractor keeps £97,200.

That symmetry is the bargain. It is also why a quotation assembled in an afternoon is a pricing decision rather than administration.

Then the clock. Notify within 8 weeks of becoming aware. The Project Manager replies within one week. The quotation follows within three weeks. The reply to it comes within two weeks. A late notification can take 14 weeks to reach the cost report, which is a full reporting quarter spent forecasting a number everybody already knows is wrong.

Your cost report shows what was agreed. Your Defined Cost shows what happened. When those two separate, somebody is carrying risk they never priced.
