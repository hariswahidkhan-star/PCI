# Master data management is why your AI pilot failed

**Post body (1,821 characters):**

The pilot did not fail on the model. It failed because the same subcontractor appears four times in your commitment register and none of the four match the ledger.

ACME MECH LTD. Acme Mechanical. Acme Mech. Ltd. ACME-MECH. Four suppliers to a machine, one to everybody in the room, and no shared identifier anywhere.

Then the arithmetic that ends the pilot. 18,400 commitment lines to be matched against 2,310 cost accounts. An exact key match hits 71%, so 13,064 lines land cleanly and 5,336 do not.

Send those to a fuzzy match and a human still has to adjudicate each one. At 90 seconds a line that is 133 hours a month.

Nobody funds 133 hours a month to keep a pilot alive, so it quietly stops in month three and the note in the retrospective says the model was not accurate enough. The model was fine. The keys were missing.

Master data management on projects comes down to four keys.

One supplier register with a single identifier, and the ledger, the commitment system and the site record all using it. One WBS to CBS mapping held as a table in the database, not as a spreadsheet on the analyst's laptop, because P6 runs four levels and the cost system runs six and the translation between them is where the money goes missing.

One period calendar, so "September" means the same cut-off in both systems. One currency table with the rate and the rate date recorded on the transaction, not applied at reporting time.

Here is the test to run before you fund anything. Pick 20 rows at random from last month's report and trace each one back to a source document. If your team cannot finish 20 in an hour, a model has nothing to stand on.

This join is the finance and delivery overlap in its most literal form. It fails at the key, not at the algorithm, and no amount of model quality repairs a broken identifier.
