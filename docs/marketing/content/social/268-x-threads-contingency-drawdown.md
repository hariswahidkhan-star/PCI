# Contingency drawdown, month by month, with the maths

*X / Threads thread — 6 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link sits in the final post. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters, so the live figures run lower.*

**Post 1/6 — the hook** (198 characters)
Month 9 of 26. Contingency remaining £6.1m. Open risk exposure £11.4m.

Cover has fallen from 1.13 at sanction to 0.54, and the cost report still shows no overrun. The project is already in trouble.

**Post 2/6 — what drawdown is** (239 characters)
Contingency drawdown is the planned release of contingency over time, profiled against when the risks it covers expire. The curve is the plan. What you report each month is the distance between that curve and the balance you actually hold.

**Post 3/6 — the arithmetic** (228 characters)
£16.9m at sanction against £15.0m of quantified exposure: cover 16.9 ÷ 15.0 = 1.13.
Profile: 25% released by month 6, 60% by month 14.
Month 9 plan says £9.6m left. Actual is £6.1m, exposure still open £11.4m.
6.1 ÷ 11.4 = 0.54.

**Post 4/6 — what may draw it, and what may not** (218 characters)
A registered risk occurring, and its allowance released. That is the list. Estimating error is not a risk occurring. Nor is scope somebody quietly added, nor a change that should have gone to the client as a variation.

**Post 5/6 — the control that works** (196 characters)
Every drawdown carries a register line ID, a date, an amount and an approver. No ID, no draw. A contingency ledger you cannot reconcile back to the register is a budget move wearing a better name.

**Post 6/6 — the three lines to report** (246 characters)
Drawn, remaining, and exposure still open. The third is the one teams leave off, and it is the only one that answers whether you are still covered.
