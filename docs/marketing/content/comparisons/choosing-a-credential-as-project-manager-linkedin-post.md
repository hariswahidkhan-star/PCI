# The £800k swing that nobody on site caused

*LinkedIn text post — 1,730 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

A forecast moved £800k in a week. Nothing happened on site.

An £8.0m package. At cut-off, earned value was £4.0m — half the work done. The ledger showed £3.6m of cost.

CPI = 4.0 ÷ 3.6 = 1.111
EAC = 8.0 × 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = £7.2m, which is £800k better than budget.

Then finance posted £400k of goods received and not yet invoiced. The steel was on site. The invoice was not.

CPI = 4.0 ÷ 4.0 = 1.000
EAC = £8.0m.

Budget, exactly. The £800k of good news was a timing difference in the ledger, and the person who knew it was coming was not in the room when the report was signed.

Whoever explains that swing needs two vocabularies at once: what earned value is measuring, and what the ledger is recognising.

Which is why the credential question, for a project manager who owns the numbers, is a question about scope rather than about ranking.

Project management certifications examine managing a project across people, process and the business environment. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and control. Each examines its own subject properly, because each was built to.

So: if your hard month is scope, change and stakeholders, take the first. If it is method — how an estimate is built, how a network behaves — take the second. If your employer mandates a certification, the mandate decides and scope is a conversation for the next one.

If the argument you keep losing is at the join between delivery and reporting, that is a third scope again, and worth reading a syllabus for.
