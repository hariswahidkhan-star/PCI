# £2.4m of working capital, none of it in your ledger

*LinkedIn post. No link in the body — it goes in the first comment.*

---

£2.4m of working capital tied up, and not one line of it was a finance decision.

Monthly certified value on a contract: £1.5m. The client pays 60 days after certification. Subcontractors are paid at 30.

That gap is one month of spend funded from your own balance sheet — about £1.5m outstanding at any time.

Retention is 5% of certified value. Over a year of certifications totalling £18.0m, that is 18.0 × 0.05 = £0.9m held back, released long after the work.

1.5 + 0.9 = £2.4m, before a single dispute.

Now the part finance does not control. If certification slips two weeks because a measurement disagreement is unresolved, the receipt slips two weeks, and the exposure grows by half a month of spend — about £0.75m.

Nobody spent more money. A valuation meeting ran late.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control, at a depth no project credential attempts. On capital projects the exposure sits upstream of all four: an estimate you did not build, progress you cannot see, and a cash position driven by certification dates rather than invoices.

Where your own qualification is the answer: if your path runs through the finance function, finish that one. If your projects are short and low-variance, there is little upstream risk to fix. If a lender or a regulated role names a qualification, that names itself.
