# Assessed competence or examined scope: what differs

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Three people were asked how much work had been done last month and gave three different answers.
£13.92m, £12.60m, £11.97m — all correct, on the same job, in the same month.

Ten slides on assessed competence, examined scope, and which one your next role is actually asking for. The arithmetic is on slide 3.

---

**Slide 1 — Two ways of being judged**
One route asks whether you have practised competently, evidenced over years and judged by peers. The other asks whether you can do the work on a published syllabus, on the day. Different instruments, different questions, and the question is usually set by the role.

**Slide 2 — What a chartered route assesses**
Structured, responsible experience mapped to a published competence framework. An evidence portfolio that shows the work was yours. A professional review by assessors who have done it. A code of conduct, a duty to practise within your competence, and continuing development. The judgement looks backwards, deliberately.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Fixed price £24.0m. Total expected cost £20.0m, so expected margin £4.0m. Costs incurred to date £11.6m.

Cost-to-cost progress: 11.6 ÷ 20.0 = **58%**.
Revenue: 0.58 × 24.0 = **£13.92m**. Margin to date: 0.58 × 4.0 = £2.32m.
Certified valuation this month: **£12.60m**.
Retention at 5%: £0.63m withheld → **£11.97m** payable.

**£1.32m contract asset. £1.95m between the highest number and the cash.**

**Slide 4 — Three rulebooks, three right answers**
Measurement produces the certified valuation. Progress towards satisfying the obligation produces the revenue. The payment terms produce the cash. Nobody in that chain is wrong, and somebody still has to explain the gaps to a director on Thursday.

**Slide 5 — What an examination assesses**
A declared scope, marked against published objectives, on a date. Its strength is precision about what was tested. Its limit is equally plain: a paper cannot tell you whether somebody ran a difficult job well for six years, and it is not built to.

**Slide 6 — Where the two do not overlap**
A portfolio review is not a knowledge test with a longer form, and an examination is not a shortened professional review. Treat one as a substitute for the other and you will be surprised in an interview — usually the one where somebody asks for the thing you did not bring.

**Slide 7 — When the chartered route is the answer**
A client, a contract or a grade structure asks for chartered standing. You want peer-assessed status in your own discipline. You are close to submission and your hours belong to the portfolio. You need a credential from an accredited body today. In those cases, that is the work.

**Slide 8 — Why the join needs a syllabus**
A measurement discipline examines measurement to a professional standard. A finance qualification examines recognition, measurement and disclosure. The three-number month sits between them, and the person who can explain it in one sentence is rarer than either qualification suggests.

**Slide 9 — What the PCI suite examines**
PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI examine 13 / 16 / 16 domains and 61 / 61 / 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge weighted 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI. 113 mandatory PCI Standards carry 532 process requirements; 92 sector case studies run across the three volumes.

**Slide 10 — What PCI does not claim**
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. It does not confer chartered standing. No pass rates, no salary claims, no holder numbers. Read the Body of Knowledge first.
