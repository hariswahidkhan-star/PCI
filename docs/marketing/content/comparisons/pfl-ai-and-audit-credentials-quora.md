# An audit credential or a project finance credential?

Take an audit credential if your job is to give assurance: testing whether controls existed, operated and left evidence, independently of the people who produced the numbers. Take a project finance credential if your job is to produce those numbers on capital projects. They examine different questions, and the answer for you is usually obvious once the questions are separated.

**Disclosure first:** credentialfinder.org, where the longer version of this comparison sits, is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials mentioned below. That is an interested party writing, so check what follows against a syllabus.

## The two questions

Audit credentials examine governance, risk and control, and the assurance process itself — planning, evidence, independence, conclusion and reporting. That is a deep subject and these credentials go a long way into it.

A project finance credential examines the chain that produced the figure in the first place: how work was measured, on which rule of credit, against which cut-off date, with what evidence behind the accrual, and what the forecast does to revenue and margin.

Neither category is a lesser version of the other. A syllabus that tried to cover both properly would end up covering neither, which is why the boundary sits where it does.

## Why the difference bites

Testing is done on what was recorded. The item that was never recorded is reached from the other direction — from the goods received log, the site measurement and the subcontract applications, back into the ledger.

Those records are project records. If they are thin, everything that rests on them is thin too, and that is a project controls problem before it is anybody else's.

A quick sum. A contract with expected cost of **£10.0m**, price **£12.0m**, progress measured cost-to-cost. Work worth **£0.55m** has been done and delivered, and nothing has reached the ledger for it.

That is 0.55 ÷ 10.0 = **5.5 percentage points** of measured progress, and 5.5% of £12.0m = **£0.66m of revenue** sitting in the wrong period. No control failed. The evidence to accrue it existed on site and did not travel.

## How to choose

Choose the audit route if independence is the point of your role, if you audit systems or processes, if you sit in an assurance function, or if your progression runs through one. That is a clean answer and worth acting on.

Choose a project finance credential if you own the cost report, defend the forecast, and get asked why an accrual moved. The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) covers 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas on exactly that chain. It confers no assurance standing, no independence and no right to give an opinion, and does not pretend to.

Hold both if you audit capital projects and keep finding that the difficult part is the measurement rather than the control framework.
