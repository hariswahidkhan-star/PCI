# The exposure sits upstream of your ledger

Your qualification already examines the part you sign: recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. On a capital project almost none of the risk to those numbers is created inside finance. It arrives from an estimate you did not build and a progress figure you cannot see. So the useful credential is one that examines the inputs, not one that examines the reporting a second time.

Here is the arithmetic, and you can check it as you read.

A contract priced at **£20.0m**. Expected total cost at sanction **£16.0m**. Cost incurred to date **£8.0m**.

Measure progress by cost and you are 8.0 ÷ 16.0 = **50% complete**. Revenue to date is 50% × 20.0 = **£10.0m**, and margin to date is **£2.0m**.

Now the project team rebuilds the forecast and expects **£20.0m** of cost rather than £16.0m.

Progress becomes 8.0 ÷ 20.0 = **40%**. Revenue to date becomes **£8.0m**. Margin to date is nil, and £2.0m already reported has to come back.

Nothing moved in the ledger. A forecast owned by delivery moved, and it restated revenue. Whether you saw that coming depended on whether anybody showed you the trend log before the quarter closed.

That is why this is a scope question rather than a ranking question.

**Accountancy and finance qualifications** examine recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial analysis and control. That is your subject, examined at a depth no project credential attempts.

**Cost and scheduling credentials** examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. That is where the £16.0m and the 50% were built in the first place.

**Audit credentials** examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. That is the discipline for testing whether the controls over those inputs actually operated.

None of the three is incomplete. They were built to examine different things, and a finance lead on capital projects sits where the first depends on the second.

Four questions that will move a number you report, all of which have factual answers:

When was the estimate at completion last rebuilt from the bottom rather than escalated? Which earning rule produced this month's progress? What is instructed but not yet priced? What does the certification calendar look like next quarter, as opposed to the invoice calendar?

Where your own qualification is the answer: if your career runs through the finance function, finish the qualification that profession recognises and let it do its work. If a lender, a regulator or a public framework names a specific qualification, that settles it — requirements do not lose to scope arguments.
