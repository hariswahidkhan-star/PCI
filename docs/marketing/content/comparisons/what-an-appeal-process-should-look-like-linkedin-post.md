# An appeal is not a complaint, and the difference matters

*LinkedIn text post — 1,876 characters. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post body:**

An appeal asks a certifying body to look again at a decision it made about you. A complaint is about how you were treated. Different routes, different people, and sending one down the other's channel is why so many go nowhere.

What a working appeals process contains:

It is published before you sit, not sent on request.

It states what can be appealed — the result, an eligibility refusal, an integrity finding, an invalidation, a refused accommodation — and what cannot.

It puts a deadline on the body as well as on you. A window that only binds the candidate is not a process.

It is heard by somebody who did not make the original decision.

It ends in a written outcome with reasons.

And it says what your record shows while the appeal is open. A result under review should show no score and no pass or fail, rather than a provisional one that has to be taken back.

What makes a ground rather than a grievance:

EV £26.6m ÷ AC £30.0m = 0.886666…
To two decimal places, 0.89. To three, 0.887.

If the paper asked for two and the marking scheme used three, that is a process error you can point at, and it is checkable by anyone. "The question felt unfair" is not a ground unless you can show it sits outside the published syllabus.

Where PCI is honest about its own position: its appeals route is internal. An accredited scheme gives you an external layer as well — a body that can be told the scheme is not following its own policy. PCI does not have that today, and if you need it, take the accredited route.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it.

Ask any body for its appeals policy before you pay. How fast it arrives tells you most of what it says.
