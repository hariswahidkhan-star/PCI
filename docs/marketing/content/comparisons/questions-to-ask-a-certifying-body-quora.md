# What should I ask a certification body before enrolling?

Ask a certification body before enrolling, and ask these ten things. How each one is answered matters as much as the answer itself.

## Before you pay

**1. Can I read the whole syllabus before I pay anything?** A good answer is a link to the document the examination is written from. A weak answer is that the syllabus arrives on enrolment, which asks you to buy the description of what you are buying.

**2. Who writes the examination, and are they separate from whoever sells the preparation?** If the people taking your training fee also decide your result, the decision has an interest in it. Ask how the separation is enforced, not whether it exists.

**3. How was the pass mark set, and by whom?** Seventy per cent of what? A cut score is only meaningful next to the difficulty of the paper and the method used to choose it.

## What you actually receive

**4. What will the certificate say I was examined on?** This is the one candidates skip and the one that decides most.

| Category | What it sets out to examine |
|---|---|
| Cost and scheduling credentials | Estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis |
| Accountancy and finance qualifications | Recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial control |
| Audit credentials | Governance, risk, control and the assurance process |
| Project management certifications | Managing projects across people, process and the business environment |

Each examines its own subject thoroughly and is built to. The risk is not a bad credential; it is a good one answering a question your job does not ask.

**5. Can a third party verify it without going through me?** A register a hiring manager can query, returning a state rather than a yes, beats a downloadable certificate every time.

## When it goes wrong, and what the body claims

**6. Who hears an appeal, and are they independent of whoever marked me?** Appeals "handled case by case" means there is no rule, and a process invented after your result is shaped by your result.

**7. What does recertification actually check?** A renewal fee with a form attached is a subscription. Ask what evidence counts and what happens if you cannot produce it.

**8. Are you accredited, by whom, to what standard, and if not do you say so plainly?** What should worry you is not the absence of accreditation but a sentence built to blur whether anybody else assessed the body.

**9. What will you not claim?** Anyone promising a salary, a role or an employer's preference is claiming something they do not control.

**10. If you closed tomorrow, where would the record of my examination live?** Unglamorous, and the question that most exposes whether a body has thought past its launch.

One honest caveat. If your employer mandates a specific certification, or a client's procurement rule requires an accredited one, question 8 settles it before the other nine. Take the mandated route.
