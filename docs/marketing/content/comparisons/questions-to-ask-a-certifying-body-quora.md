# What should I ask a certification body before enrolling?

Ask ten things, and pay as much attention to how they are answered as to what the answer is.

**1. Can I read the whole syllabus before I pay anything?** A good answer is a link to the document the examination is written from. A weak answer is that the syllabus arrives on enrolment, which asks you to buy the description of what you are buying.

**2. Who writes the examination, and are they separate from whoever sells the preparation?** If the people who take your training fee also decide your result, the decision has an interest in it. Ask how the separation is enforced, not whether it exists.

**3. How was the pass mark set, and by whom?** A number on its own means nothing. Seventy per cent of what? A cut score is only meaningful next to the difficulty of the paper it sits on and the method used to choose it.

**4. What will the certificate say I was examined on?** This is the one candidates skip and the one that decides most. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and assurance. Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment. Each examines its own subject thoroughly and is built to. The risk is not a bad credential; it is a good one answering a question your job does not ask.

**5. Can a third party verify it without going through me?** A register a hiring manager can query, returning a state rather than a yes, beats a downloadable certificate every time.

**6. Who hears an appeal, and are they independent of whoever marked me?** Appeals "handled case by case" means there is no rule, and a process invented after your result is shaped by your result.

**7. What does recertification actually check?** A renewal fee with a form attached is a subscription. Ask what evidence counts and what happens if you cannot produce it.

**8. Are you accredited, by whom, to what standard, and if not do you say so plainly?** What should worry you is not the absence of accreditation but a sentence built to blur whether anybody else assessed the body.

**9. What will you not claim?** Anyone promising a salary, a role or an employer's preference is claiming something they do not control.

**10. If you closed tomorrow, where would the record of my examination live?** Unglamorous, and the question that most exposes whether a body has thought past its own launch.

One honest caveat before you use the list. If your employer mandates a specific certification, or a client's procurement rule requires an accredited one, question 8 settles the matter before the other nine and you should take the mandated route.
