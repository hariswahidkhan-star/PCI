# Accreditation: what it certifies and what it does not

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Two cost performance indices for the same month, 0.897 and 1.000, both defensible.
The £1.2m between them is an instruction nobody has priced — and no accreditation assessment on earth would look at it, because that is not what accreditation is for.

Twelve slides on the two axes of a certification decision.

---

**Slide 1 — Two indices, one month**
A project reports a cost performance index of 0.897. The same month, on the same data, 1.000 is equally defensible. Nobody has miscounted and nobody is being dishonest. The gap is a presentation rule that lives half in the cost procedure and half in the accounting policy.

**Slide 2 — The figures**
A £30.0m fixed-price contract. Original budget at completion £26.0m. At cut-off, earned value on the base scope is £10.4m, which is 40% complete, and actual cost is £11.6m. Of that actual cost, £1.2m sits against an instructed change with no agreed price.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Change carried inside the reported figures: 10.4 ÷ 11.6 = **0.897**.

Change held outside, base scope only: 10.4 ÷ 10.4 = **1.000**.

**The entire apparent overrun is one instruction nobody has priced.** And until recognition of that variation is highly probable, the same £1.2m is cost with no matching revenue — one number, two disciplines, one month.

**Slide 4 — Why this is a slide about accreditation**
Because the obvious question, "is the credential accredited?", would not have helped. The candidate who can reconcile that month learned it from a syllabus, and syllabuses are not what an accreditation assessment examines.

**Slide 5 — What an assessment does examine, part one**
Impartiality: whether the body has a structural interest in candidates passing, and what it does about it. Examination development: how a question is written, reviewed, trialled and retired. The pass standard: whether it was reached by a defensible method rather than a round number.

**Slide 6 — What an assessment does examine, part two**
The certification decision: who makes it and on what evidence. Appeals: whether an unhappy candidate reaches people not involved the first time. Records: what is kept, for how long, and who can see it. Review: whether the scheme is revisited on a stated cycle.

**Slide 7 — That list is demanding**
A body assessed against it, on a repeating cycle, by somebody with no stake in the result, has been through something real, and its candidates benefit. Nothing on these slides argues otherwise. The point is only that the list has an edge.

**Slide 8 — What it does not examine**
Whether the syllabus suits your job. How hard the examination is. Whether an employer or a framework will accept it. Whether the content is the best available account of a fast-moving subject. And scope, which is the property with the largest effect on your working week.

**Slide 9 — Two axes, four quadrants**
Accredited and fits your work: the straightforward case. Accredited and does not fit: a sound process examining somebody else's job. Not accredited and fits: you do the process assessment yourself, from the published documents. Not accredited and does not fit: no.

**Slide 10 — Where PCI stands**
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Building with reference to a standard is not being assessed against it, and PCI will not blur the two.

**Slide 11 — What PCI publishes instead**
The certification scheme, 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and the full Body of Knowledge before anybody pays: 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas for PCL-AI, 16 and 61 for PFL-AI, 16 and 63 for PML-AI. Evidence you can weigh. Not third-party assurance, and not offered as a substitute.

**Slide 12 — When to take the accredited credential**
If a regulator, a client framework or a public tender requires accreditation, take the accredited credential. That is a requirement, not a preference, and PCI cannot meet it today. Where the choice is genuinely yours, ask both questions. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the three credentials named here.
