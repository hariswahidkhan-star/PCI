# Prestige or scope: which should decide the credential I pick?

Prestige or scope is only a live question when the two point at different credentials. Recognition decides how quickly a stranger reads your CV. Scope decides whether the examination covers the decisions you are paid to sign. Where they conflict, recognition wins the shortlist and scope wins the year that follows, so the honest answer depends on which of those two is actually blocking you.

**Measure recognition rather than arguing about it.** Open ten current advertisements for the exact role you want, in the exact market you want it in, and write down every credential each one names, marking whether it sits under essential or desirable. Then do the same with ten profiles of people already doing that job. That takes an hour, the answer is specific to your market, and it beats any general claim about employer preference, including a certifying body's about itself.

**Measure scope by starting from your own signature.** List the decisions you personally sign: a forecast at completion, a valuation, a percentage complete, an accrual, a change position, a schedule submission. Then open the syllabus and mark each one examined, assumed or excluded. Count rows rather than pages: a syllabus with four hundred topics you will never touch is length, not scope.

**Why the scope axis is not soft.** Take a monthly report where progress is measured to the 25th and costs are booked to the 30th. Earned value is £4.20m. Actual cost is £4.65m, of which £0.35m is spend on work that will be earned next month. As reported, the cost performance index is 4.20 ÷ 4.65 = 0.903. Aligned to a single date it is 4.20 ÷ 4.30 = 0.977. On a £12.0m budget at completion, that is a forecast outturn of £13.29m against £12.28m: just over a million pounds of difference, produced by a calendar rather than by performance. Nobody in that example did anything wrong, and closing the gap needs somebody examined on both sides of the cut-off.

**When recognition should win, and it often should.** If a client's prequalification names a certification, take the named one. If an internal grading matrix lists one against the band you want, take the listed one. If you are moving country and your CV will get thirty seconds from somebody with no context for anything unfamiliar, recognition is what gets you into the room, and a scope argument you never get to make is worth nothing.

**When scope should win.** When you already get the interviews and the difficulty is the work: a forecast you cannot defend, a month-end where the numbers move for reasons nobody can explain, a client asking why the cost report and the accounts disagree. Also when your role has moved and your credential has not, which is more common than people admit.

One more thing worth saying: if what you want is assessed chartered standing, that is years of supervised practice assessed by a professional route, and no examination substitutes for it.
