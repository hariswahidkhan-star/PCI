# Project controls certifications: where PCL-AI sits

*LinkedIn document carousel. 12 slides at 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the
arithmetic, because slide 3 is the one people screenshot.*

---

### Slide 1 — cover
**Two forecasts. Same data. £490k apart.**
Both of them defensible.
*Alt text: title slide reading "Two forecasts, same data, £490k apart — both defensible".*

### Slide 2 — the package
Budget at completion **£8.0m**
Cost to date **£4.0m**
Earned value **£3.6m**

CPI = 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = **0.90**
*Alt text: slide setting out a package with £8.0m budget, £4.0m spent, £3.6m earned and a cost
performance index of 0.90.*

### Slide 3 — the arithmetic
**Remaining work at budget**
EAC = 4.0 + (8.0 − 3.6) = **£8.4m**

**Remaining work at the achieved rate**
EAC = 8.0 ÷ 0.90 = **£8.89m**

Gap: **£490k**
*Alt text: calculation slide comparing two estimate-at-completion methods giving £8.4m and
£8.89m.*

### Slide 4 — why it matters
If the package is heading for a loss, that loss reaches the accounts as soon as it is known.

The forecast method somebody picked sets its size.
*Alt text: slide explaining that the forecast method determines the size of a loss recognised in
the accounts.*

### Slide 5 — the same problem, smaller
40 valves at £10,000. Twenty delivered, none installed.

0/100 rule → EV **£0**
50/50 rule → EV **£100k**

Same steel. Same day.
*Alt text: slide comparing two earning rules producing £0 and £100k of earned value for the same
delivered valves.*

### Slide 6 — what the category examines
The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
the control of delivery.

Thoroughly, and by design.
*Alt text: slide listing what the established project controls certifications set out to
examine.*

### Slide 7 — where the boundary sits
What the number does after it leaves the control cycle sits outside their scope — as it does for
every credential in that category.

A scope boundary is a design decision.
*Alt text: slide stating that the reporting handover sits outside the scope of the established
project controls certifications by design.*

### Slide 8 — what PCL-AI examines
**13 domains. 61 knowledge areas.**

The same control cycle, plus the handover into reporting, plus the governed use of AI inside the
cycle.
*Alt text: slide summarising PCL-AI as 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas covering the control
cycle and the reporting handover.*

### Slide 9 — the honest weakness
The established category has years of scheme operation behind it.

PCI does not. We are new. No syllabus substitutes for that, and we will not pretend it does.
*Alt text: slide stating plainly that PCI is new and has no long operating record.*

### Slide 10 — when to take the other one
You need accreditation now → go where it exists.

Your employer mandates a certification → take that one.

Your gap is control method itself → the established route goes deeper.
*Alt text: slide listing three situations in which a different credential is the right answer.*

### Slide 11 — the accreditation position
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. Not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and not claiming to be.

Built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.
*Alt text: slide stating that PCI is not accredited and does not claim to be.*

### Slide 12 — the test, and the disclosure
Take your last pack. Find the EAC. Work out which method produced it. Then produce the other one.

Then read the syllabus — ours is free, in full, before anyone pays.

*credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PCL-AI.*
*Alt text: closing slide with a self-test on forecasting method, the ownership disclosure and an
instruction to read the published syllabus.*
