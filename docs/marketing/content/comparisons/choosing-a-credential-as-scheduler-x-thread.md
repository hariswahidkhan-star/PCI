# Scheduler to controls: the three things you now sign

*X / Threads. Eight posts, each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply, not in
post 1.*

---

**1/**
Moving from scheduling into controls adds three things to your signature.

An earning rule. A forecast. A cut-off date.

Here is what the first one does to a month, in arithmetic you can check.

**2/**
A package worth £1.2m starts this period and finishes next.

50/50 rule: half its value earns on start.
0/100 rule: nothing earns until it is complete.

Period EV: £3.0m under 50/50, £2.4m under 0/100.

**3/**
Actual cost in the period is £2.8m either way.

CPI = 3.0 ÷ 2.8 = 1.07 → ahead.
CPI = 2.4 ÷ 2.8 = 0.86 → behind.

Nothing physical differs. A template decision picked the story.

**4/**
And it does not stop in the pack.

The index feeds the forecast. The forecast is the denominator in a percentage complete. The percentage complete drives what gets recognised.

A rule nobody discussed has reached a set of accounts.

**5/**
Scheduling credentials examine schedule development, maintenance and analysis — thoroughly, and by design.

The earning rule, the forecast and the cut-off sit past that boundary. That is a design decision, not a shortcoming.

**6/**
Product certifications for scheduling software examine operating a tool.

That is a real skill, sold in language that sounds like certification of the discipline.

Both are worth holding. Know which one your CV line is claiming.

**7/**
When not to move:

Programme work is what you enjoy → stay, and go deep.
A prequalification names a certification → take that one.
You need accreditation today → go where it exists.

None of those answers is ours.
