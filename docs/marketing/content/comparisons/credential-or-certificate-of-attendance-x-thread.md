# Can you fail it? One question, two very different documents

*X / Threads thread — 7 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. No link in the thread; the link sits in the first reply. X counts a URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/7 — the hook** (204 characters)
Before you pay for anything with the word certification on it, ask one question:

Can you fail it?

If everyone who attends receives the document, you are buying training. Worth buying. Just not evidence.

**Post 2/7 — why that question settles it** (199 characters)
A document that cannot record a failure cannot record a pass either.

There is nothing on the other side of the line for the pass to mean. That is the whole difference, and it costs nothing to check.

**Post 3/7 — the arithmetic** (231 characters)
Budget at completion £8.0m. At cut-off: earned value £3.10m, actual cost £3.55m.

CPI = 3.10 ÷ 3.55 = 0.873
If that holds: 8.0 ÷ 0.873 = £9.16m
If the overrun is behind you: 3.55 + (8.0 − 3.10) = £8.45m

£0.71m apart. Both correct.

**Post 4/7 — what an assessment has to do** (229 characters)
A course can cover forecasting by defining it.

An assessment has to hand you the site conditions — wet season ending, subcontractor replaced, rate agreed for the rest — and make you say which of those two numbers you would sign.

**Post 5/7 — the other three questions** (248 characters)
Three more questions, in order.

Who decides the outcome: the people you paid to teach you, or somebody with no stake in the answer?

What can you read before paying?

What happens afterwards — nothing, or a renewal cycle with a stated requirement?

**Post 6/7 — when the course is the right buy** (227 characters)
The case nobody makes often enough:

If what you need is fifteen development hours by December, buy the course. An attendance record is the correct evidence for that, and an examination is a slower and dearer way to produce it.
