# Two margins for one month on the same valuation

*LinkedIn text post — 1,742 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Two margins for the same month, on the same job, from the same valuation. Plus £0.4m and minus £0.2m.

The difference is one instructed change nobody has agreed yet.

The figures. Cumulative valuation of work executed £8.0m, of which £0.6m is an instruction still being negotiated. Cost incurred to date £7.6m.

As valued: 8.0 − 7.6 = **+£0.4m**.

Amounts that depend on a negotiation are recognised only when it is highly probable they will not reverse later. Constrain the £0.6m and the same month reads 7.4 − 7.6 = **−£0.2m**.

The work was done. The cost is in the ledger. Your entitlement position is defensible. And the reported result has swung by £0.6m on one line you priced and somebody else has not agreed.

This is the part where a surveyor is usually arguing from instinct rather than from something they were assessed on.

Chartered routes in surveying and engineering assess professional competence and standing over years of supervised practice. Measurement, valuation and entitlement are their own subject, examined to a standard built over a long time, and no paper substitutes for that assessment.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control, at a depth far beyond the part that touches your valuation.

Neither sets out to examine the join, and the join is one line in your monthly application.

If what you need is professional standing in surveying, take the chartered route. That is not a close call. The case for anything else is narrow: you already have standing, and the part of your week that costs you is what happens after the valuation leaves your desk.
