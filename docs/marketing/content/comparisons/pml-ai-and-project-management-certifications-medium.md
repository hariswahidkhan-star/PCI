# Project management certifications: where PML-AI sits

If your work is managing projects — scope, stakeholders, schedule, quality, risk, procurement and the delivery approach — a project management certification is the right thing to sit, and nothing here argues otherwise. If you are accountable for what those projects report, and for what a model is allowed to decide inside them, the PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) is aimed at that.

## The two categories on scope axes

| Axis | Project management certifications | PML-AI |
|---|---|---|
| Primary subject | Managing projects across people, process and the business environment | Leading a project where delivery, the reported numbers and governed AI meet |
| Unit of analysis | The project and its delivery | The project, the contract it sits under, and the report it produces |
| The forecast | A delivery output, owned and managed | Traced onward into revenue, margin and any provision it forces |
| Change | Control, approval and impact on scope, cost and time | The same, plus the evidence a change needs before it can be counted |
| AI | Part of a changing business environment | An examined subject: model evidence, decision rights, and the record left behind |
| Assumed knowledge | Project delivery practice | The same, plus the reporting chain the numbers land in |
| Accreditation status | Established routes, many of them accredited | New, independent, not accredited |

The last row belongs in the table. A comparison that buries it is not worth the reader's time.

## What project management certifications examine

They examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment: initiating and planning, stakeholders, scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, procurement, the delivery approach, and leading the people doing the work.

A holder can set a project up, run it, and hold it against a plan. That is the core of the discipline, and these certifications go a long way into it.

The step where a delivery decision becomes an accounting event sits outside that scope, by design. So does the governance of outputs produced by a model. Neither omission is a fault; a syllabus is a set of decisions about what to examine properly.

## What PML-AI examines

PML-AI covers 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge weighted 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI. Those are the proportions of the syllabus and not an examination weighting.

In practice: the delivery discipline, the chain that carries the project's numbers into the reported result, and the rules about what a model may propose and who must decide.

The Body of Knowledge is published in full before anyone pays anything, which is the fair way to check that description rather than take it on trust.

## Worked: a six-week slip, and what it did to a reported result

A fixed-price contract of **£26.0m**. Expected cost at completion **£23.4m**, so an expected margin of **£2.6m**. Costs incurred **£12.87m**, which is **55.0%** on a cost-to-cost measure. Time-related cost runs at **£180k a week**.

**Before.** Cumulative revenue = 0.550 × 26.0 = **£14.30m**. Cost £12.87m. Cumulative margin **£1.43m**, which is 55% of £2.6m — the cross-check ties.

**The slip.** A late permit pushes six weeks into the programme. Time-related cost of 6 × £180k = **£1.08m** goes into the forecast. Expected cost at completion becomes **£24.48m**, and expected margin falls to 26.0 − 24.48 = **£1.52m**.

**After.** Measured progress = 12.87 ÷ 24.48 = **52.57%**. Cumulative revenue = 0.5257 × 26.0 = **£13.67m**. Cost is unchanged at £12.87m. Cumulative margin **£0.80m**.

**£0.63m comes out of the reported result this month**, on a project where nothing has yet been overspent. The slip has not happened; the forecast of it has.

Two things follow, and both are ordinary. The first is that measured progress fell without any work being un-built, because the denominator moved. The second is that the person who could have explained this to the board was the project manager, and it is not their subject.

## Where the AI part comes in

A model that predicts slip, drafts a narrative or ranks risks is now normal on large programmes. The governance question is not its accuracy. It is what the model is allowed to decide, who signs, and what record survives.

A model that flags 120 activities of which 78 slip has a precision of 0.65. If 130 slipped in total, its recall is 0.60. Those are useful numbers for ordering attention and poor numbers for closing a risk, and a manager should be able to say so out loud in a meeting.

The rule PML-AI examines is short enough to write on a page: a model may propose, a named person decides, and the record shows who decided, on what basis and when. If a decision could not be reconstructed a year later, the tool is running the project.

## The accreditation position, stated plainly

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

If accreditation is a condition — from a client, a regulator or your own risk judgement — that answers the question, and it answers it against PCI today.

## How to decide

**Take a project management certification** if your employer names one, if you want the established route into project delivery, if accreditation is a condition rather than a preference, or if somebody else owns the numbers on your projects and you have no wish to. A mandate is not a comparison and there is nothing to weigh.

**Sit PML-AI** if you are the person asked why the forecast moved and what it does to the reported result, if you sign off model-assisted outputs, or if you are moving from running projects to being accountable for what they report.

**Both, delivery first,** is a reasonable order mid-career. The [Body of Knowledge for the PML-AI credential](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/certifications/pml-ai) sets out the assumed starting point, and an afternoon with it will tell you whether the scope matches your week.

## FAQ

**Does PML-AI replace a project management certification?**
No. It examines a different combination: delivery leadership, the reporting chain the numbers land in, and governed AI. If your employer names a specific certification, sit that one. Nothing here ranks credentials by employer preference, because that is not a claim anybody can make honestly.

**Is PML-AI accredited?**
No. PCI is a new, independent certifying body, not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. The syllabus is published in full so the scope can be judged directly rather than through a claim.

**I already run projects well. What would this add?**
The parts managers most often say they were never examined on are what a forecast movement does to the reported result, what evidence a change needs before it can be counted, and who is allowed to decide when a model is involved. If your work already covers those, it adds little.

**What does 40 / 40 / 20 refer to?**
The proportion of the Body of Knowledge given to finance and reporting, project management and governed AI. It describes the syllabus. It is not an examination weighting, and none is published here, because the examination blueprint is a separate decision.

**Is this saying project management certifications are out of date?**
No. They examine their subject thoroughly and are built to. The argument is narrower: the join between a delivery decision and a reported number, and the governance of model-assisted outputs, sit outside that subject, and somebody on a large programme should be examined on them.
