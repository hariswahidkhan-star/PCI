# Two credentials, six rows, and one evening of work

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Two people can weigh the same pair of credentials honestly and reach opposite answers.
That is not indecision, and it is why no league table can help you. Twelve slides on the grid that can.

Disclosure before you read: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.

---

**Slide 1 — There is no league table, and there cannot be**

A ranking has to assume one buyer. Certification has millions, each signing a different set of decisions.

Change the set of decisions and the order changes with it. That is not a flaw in the rankings. It is what makes them impossible.

**Slide 2 — The six rows, which you fill in yourself**

1. What is examined, and at what depth?
2. What does it assume you already know?
3. Who decides the result?
4. What does it cost in hours, not fees?
5. What changes on the day you hold it?
6. What happens if you fail?

Two columns, six rows, one evening. The weights are yours because the decisions are.

**Slide 3 — Row four, in numbers**

Credential A extends work you already do daily. Your honest estimate: **60 hours**.
Credential B covers a subject half of which is new. Your estimate: **140 hours**.

At **four hours a week**:
A = 60 ÷ 4 = **15 weeks**
B = 140 ÷ 4 = **35 weeks**

At **two hours a week**: **30 weeks** and **70 weeks**.

Twenty to forty weeks of difference. No comparison table anywhere carries it, and it is the largest input to the decision.

**Slide 4 — Row one, done properly**

Write out every decision you put your name to in a normal month.

A forecast. A valuation. A progress percentage. A programme reissue. An accrual.

Eight to twelve lines is typical. Mark each line against both syllabuses: **examined**, **assumed** or **excluded**. Count the examined lines.

A preference has become a number.

**Slide 5 — Compare like with like**

| Category | What it examines |
|---|---|
| Cost and scheduling credentials | Estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis |
| The established project controls certifications | Measurement, progress, forecasting, control of delivery |
| Accountancy and finance qualifications | Recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial control |
| Project management certifications | Managing projects across people, process and the business environment |
| Chartered routes in surveying and engineering | Assessed professional competence over years of practice |

Different subjects, each examined thoroughly by design.

**Slide 6 — What that table means for your choice**

Where your two candidates sit in different rows, you are not choosing between grades of the same thing. You are choosing which subject goes on the record.

Where they sit in the same row, the syllabus comparison gets finer and the tie-breaks below start to matter.

**Slide 7 — Tie-break one: who decides**

Where the organisation that sells the preparation also awards the result, the evidence is weaker, whatever the syllabus says.

Ask how the two are held apart, and who sits on the certification decision. A scheme that has thought about this will answer in a sentence.

**Slide 8 — Tie-break two: verify a stranger**

Pick a name you do not know who claims that credential. Try to confirm it today, without an account, in five minutes.

If you cannot, neither can a hiring manager in four years. A credential nobody can check from the outside is a line on a CV.

**Slide 9 — Tie-break three: read before you pay**

A syllabus published in full, free, before any money changes hands, is a body inviting you to check its work.

A syllabus you can only see after enrolling has told you the order it prefers: money first, information second.

**Slide 10 — The two-year test**

Credentials are slow to earn and slower to be recognised.

Score both against the decisions you expect to be signing in two years, not the ones you signed last year. If your role is moving, the longer route can be the right one — as a choice, not a surprise.

**Slide 11 — What this body publishes, and what it does not**

Published: three Bodies of Knowledge in full and free before anybody pays — 13, 16 and 16 domains and 61, 61 and 63 knowledge areas — with 92 sector case studies and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements. A suite of 15,613 machine calculation checks covers the PFL-AI and PML-AI material; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

Not published: pass rates, examination weightings, salary outcomes, holder counts.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**Slide 12 — When to stop filling in the grid**

If a client's prequalification or an employer's grading matrix names one of your two, take the named one. A requirement outranks fit.

If a procurement rule in your market requires an accredited credential, that settles it, and it settles it against this Institute today.

If what you want is chartered standing, no examination substitutes for an assessment of competence built over years.
