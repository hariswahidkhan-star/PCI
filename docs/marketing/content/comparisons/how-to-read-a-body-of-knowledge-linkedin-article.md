# Ninety minutes with a Body of Knowledge before you pay

Ninety minutes with a published syllabus is the cheapest piece of due diligence in this field, and most candidates skip it. Seven passes, in a fixed order, will tell you whether a credential examines the work you actually do. Read the last domain first, count the verbs, find one number, find the exclusions, then trace a topic you already know all the way through.

Everything below works on any published Body of Knowledge, in any category. It needs no subject expertise beyond the areas you already work in.

A disclosure, because this is published on a site with an interest: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials mentioned here. That is precisely why the method has to work when you turn it on PCI, and it does.

This is a LinkedIn original. It is not a copy of anything on the PCI estate.

## Pass one, ten minutes: read the last domain first

Introductions are written to be persuasive. The final domain is where a scheme puts what would not fit anywhere else, and it shows you the real edge of the syllabus faster than the contents page does.

Two things to look for. Does the last domain hold something the body clearly cares about, or is it a bin? And does the scope you were promised on the cover survive to the back of the book?

## Pass two, fifteen minutes: count the verbs, not the pages

Page count tells you nothing. Verbs tell you everything.

Highlight the verb attached to every knowledge area. If most of them are *understand*, *appreciate*, *be familiar with*, you are holding a reading list and the examination standard is being set somewhere you cannot see. If they are *calculate*, *reconcile*, *classify*, *select and justify*, *reject and state why*, you are holding a syllabus and you can predict the questions.

This pass needs no expertise at all. You are reading grammar. What a markable outcome looks like once the verb is right is set out in [what makes a credential examinable](https://credentialfinder.org/what-makes-a-credential-examinable).

## Pass three, ten minutes: find one number

Any credential that touches money should show you a worked calculation in its own materials. Not a formula in a box — a calculation, with figures, reaching an answer that could be wrong.

If nothing in the document produces a number, nothing in the examination will either, and you are about to sit a vocabulary test. That may still be the right purchase. You should know it going in.

## Pass four, ten minutes: find the exclusions

A serious syllabus says what it does not cover. It is the hardest paragraph to write and the most useful one to read.

A document with no exclusions has not finished thinking about scope, and the gap becomes your problem in the month a question lands on the boundary. Every category draws a boundary somewhere: cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis; accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control; product certifications for scheduling software examine operating the tool rather than the discipline behind it. Those are design decisions, made deliberately, and each category examines its own subject thoroughly. What you need to know is where the line sits, because your job may not respect it.

## Pass five, twenty minutes: trace one topic you already know

This is the pass that decides the purchase. Pick a subject you have run in anger, find every place the document touches it, and read whether the treatment deepens or repeats.

Take progress measurement. A weak document lists it. A strong one makes you choose a rule and defend the choice, because the choice moves the answer.

Here is why, on one package.

A package carries a budget of £6.00m across 400 units. At cut-off, 150 units are complete and 60 more are started but unfinished. Actual cost is £2.55m.

Under a units-complete rule, only finished units earn: 150 ÷ 400 = 37.5%, so earned value is 0.375 × 6.00 = **£2.25m**. Cost performance index = 2.25 ÷ 2.55 = **0.88**.

Under a rule that credits half the value at start, the same field data gives (150 + 0.5 × 60) ÷ 400 = 180 ÷ 400 = 45%, so earned value is 0.45 × 6.00 = **£2.70m**. Cost performance index = 2.70 ÷ 2.55 = **1.06**.

**One survey, one month, £0.45m apart, and the index lands on opposite sides of 1.00.** Nobody has miscounted anything. The rule was chosen before the job started, probably by someone who has left.

A Body of Knowledge worth paying for makes you make that choice and defend it. One that lists "progress measurement" as a topic leaves you to discover it in the month it matters, in front of people who will not enjoy the explanation.

## Pass six, fifteen minutes: read what the body holds itself to

The syllabus tells you what the body expects of you. The scheme documents tell you what it expects of itself: how questions are developed, how a certification decision is made, who hears an appeal, how a credential is suspended or withdrawn, how long records are kept.

A body that publishes both has given you two sides of a contract. A body that publishes only the first has given you a prospectus.

## Pass seven, ten minutes: read one case study to its decision

Find a case study and read it to the end. Does it name a decision and a number, or does it narrate a project and conclude that communication is important?

A case study that does not commit to an answer cannot be examined, and it is usually the clearest signal of how the rest of the document was written.

## What ninety minutes cannot tell you

It cannot tell you whether a credential is respected, whether a particular employer will accept it, or how hard the examination is. Those are different questions with different evidence behind them.

And if your employer mandates a specific certification, or a framework you bid into scores an accredited one, this method is interesting and the mandate is binding. Buy the credential you are required to hold. Reading syllabuses is for the choices that are genuinely yours.

## FAQ

**Do I have to read the whole document?**
No, and trying to is why people give up. The seven passes are deliberately partial: the last domain, the verbs, one calculation, the exclusions, one topic traced through, the scheme documents and one case study. That is a representative sample of how the document was made, which is what you are really assessing.

**What if the Body of Knowledge is not published before payment?**
Ask for it, and treat the answer as data. A body that will not show a candidate the syllabus before taking money is telling you something about how it sees the relationship. Some publish an outline rather than the full text, which is a reasonable middle position — the question is whether the outline carries verbs.

**Is a longer syllabus a better one?**
No relationship at all. Length usually tracks how many topics a scheme wanted to appear to cover. A short document with observable verbs and stated exclusions will prepare you better than a long one that lists subject headings, and it will take you a third of the time to check.

**Can I do this on a credential in a field I do not know?**
Passes one to four and six to seven, yes — they read structure, not content. Pass five needs a topic you can already argue about, so use the areas where you have run the work. If none of the syllabus is familiar, that is worth noticing before you commit a year to it.

**What should I do with what I find?**
Write down three questions and send them to the body; there is a standing list in [questions to ask a certifying body](https://credentialfinder.org/questions-to-ask-a-certifying-body). How the questions are answered, and how fast, tells you more about a certifying body than the document did, and it is the same test whether the body is fifty years old or two.
