# Every invoice was right. The report was £1.1m light.

*LinkedIn text post — 1636 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Every invoice tested was approved, coded and correct. The cost report was still £1.1m light.

A £24.0m fixed-price contract. Expected cost £20.0m. Costs recorded in the ledger to date: £8.4m.

Outside the ledger sat £0.6m of goods received and not invoiced, and £0.5m of subcontract work measured on site and not yet applied for. True cost incurred: £9.5m.

Cost-to-cost progress on recorded cost: 8.4 ÷ 20.0 = 42.0%. Revenue = £10.08m.
On complete cost: 9.5 ÷ 20.0 = 47.5%. Revenue = £11.40m.

£1.32m of revenue and £0.22m of margin, on work that had already been built.

Nothing in the testing was wrong. A sample drawn from recorded items cannot reach an item that was never recorded, which is exactly why completeness is approached from the other direction — from the goods received log and the site measurement, back into the ledger.

That log is a project record. Where it is thin, the assurance resting on it is thin, and that is a project controls problem before it is anybody else's.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. They examine it thoroughly and are built to. How a quantity got measured on site, on which rule of credit, sits outside that scope by design.

The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) is examined on that side of the line: evidence at source, the accrual it supports, and the forecast it feeds.

Disclosure: credentialfinder.org, where the full comparison sits, is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PFL-AI. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it.
