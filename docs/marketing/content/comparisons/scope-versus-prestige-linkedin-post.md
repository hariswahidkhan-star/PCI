# Recognition gets the interview. Scope gets the year

**Post body (1,827 characters):**

Recognition gets you the interview. Scope decides the year that follows.

Two axes, and most people only ever run one of them.

Recognition is measurable in an hour. Open ten current advertisements for the exact role, in the exact market you want it in. Write down every credential each one names, and whether it appears under essential or desirable. Do the same for ten profiles of people already in that job. That count is your recognition data: local, current, and better than any general claim about what employers prefer, including a claim a certifying body would make about itself.

Scope is harder to assess, so it gets waved through. Then it decides the year.

Take a monthly report where progress is measured to the 25th and costs are booked to the 30th. Earned value £4.20m. Actual cost £4.65m, of which £0.35m is spend on work that will be earned next month.

As reported: CPI = 4.20 ÷ 4.65 = 0.903
Aligned to one date: CPI = 4.20 ÷ 4.30 = 0.977

On a £12.0m budget at completion, that is a forecast outturn of £13.29m against £12.28m. Slightly over a million pounds of difference, produced by a calendar rather than by performance. Nobody did anything wrong. Closing that gap needs somebody who has been examined on both sides of the cut-off, and no amount of recognition answers it.

So run both axes and take the one that is blocking you.

If a prequalification list or an internal grading matrix names a certification, recognition is what stands between you and the next step. Take the named one.

If you already get the interviews and the difficulty is defending a forecast, scope is the only axis that changes anything.
