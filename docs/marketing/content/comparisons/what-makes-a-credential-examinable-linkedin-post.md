# 95% pass rate, or 28.5%? Both true. One gets quoted.

*LinkedIn text post — 1,838 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

A 95% pass rate and a 28.5% pass rate can describe the same cohort.

400 people enrol. 120 reach the examination. 114 pass.
114 ÷ 120 = **95%**.
114 ÷ 400 = **28.5%**.

Both are arithmetic. Only one reaches the brochure, and it rarely arrives with its denominator attached.

So judge a credential on things that cannot be framed. Seven questions, all answerable before you pay:

1. Can you read the whole syllabus first — structure, domains, knowledge areas — not a topic list?
2. Is there an assessment at all? Attendance is not assessment. A certificate that proves you were in the room proves you were in the room.
3. Is the paper marked against published objectives, the same for every candidate?
4. How is the pass standard set: by what procedure, and by whom?
5. How is the candidate identified and the sitting supervised, in writing?
6. Are appeals and retakes published, with a time limit and a named route?
7. Can a third party verify a certificate without asking the holder for a scan?

A body that answers those in writing has told you what it is. A body that answers with adjectives has told you something as well.

Ask them of this Institute too. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

The Body of Knowledge is published in full before anyone pays — 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes. No pass rate is published, and none will be until there is one that can be evidenced.
