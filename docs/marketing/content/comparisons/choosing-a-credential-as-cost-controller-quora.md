# Which certification is worth it for a cost controller?

Ask a narrower question first: how far past the cost report does your signature travel?

If your work ends when a commercial manager takes the report and does something with it, a credential examining estimating and cost control examines your job properly. If your forecast is the denominator in somebody's revenue calculation, you want an examination that carries on past the report, because that is where your errors land.

A disclosure before the argument, since I work for an interested party: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials I name at the end. Discount for that and check the arithmetic yourself.

**Here is the arithmetic that decides it**

Earned value for the period is £4.40m. Actual cost as booked in the ledger is £4.10m. Two subcontractors have done £0.30m of work and have not invoiced.

As booked, the cost performance index is 4.40 ÷ 4.10 = **1.073**.
With the accrual in the period it belongs to, 4.40 ÷ 4.40 = **1.000**.

Now forecast against a budget at completion of £11.0m. At 1.073 the outturn is £10.25m. At 1.000 it is £11.0m.

**£0.75m of difference, produced by a missing invoice rather than by anything that happened on site.** Report the first number and you have told a board the job will come in under budget when it will not, and the correction arrives when the invoices land.

**Which kind of credential examines that**

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. They cover how that earned value and that forecast are built, and they cover it thoroughly.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. For a controller inside an integrated controls function that is the widest single frame around the day job.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. They assume competent delivery data arrives from somewhere they do not examine, which is a sound design decision for a syllabus about reporting.

Each examines its own subject well. The accrual sitting between them belongs to neither as a matter of course, and that outturn figure does not stop at your cost report — it is the denominator when progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, so it moves recognised revenue too.

**When to take something else, plainly**

If an employer's grading matrix or a client's prequalification names a certification, take the named one. A requirement is not a comparison, and no scope argument beats it.

If you are moving into a finance function, take an accountancy qualification and take the whole thing rather than a fragment of it.

If you have fewer than two or three month-end cycles behind you, do the reps first. These papers ask what you would do when the numbers disagree, and that judgement comes from having been in the room when they did.

**What I will not claim**

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything — PCL-AI is 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, PFL-AI is 16 and 61, and both are readable in full before you pay.
