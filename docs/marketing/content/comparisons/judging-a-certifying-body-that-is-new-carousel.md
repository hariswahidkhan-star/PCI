# Seven checks to run on a certifying body that is new

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

A new certifying body cannot show you a track record, because it does not have one.
Here are seven things it can show you today, and the one check that goes against every new body including ours.

Disclosure before you read: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.

---

**Slide 1 — The problem with a new body**

Age is a proxy. It stands in for a published standard, a decision that has been separated from selling, an appeal route that has been used, and a record a stranger can check.

You can inspect all four directly. That is what the next seven slides do.

**Slide 2 — The seven checks on one page**

| Check | What good looks like | How you verify it today |
|---|---|---|
| The syllabus | Published in full, free, before payment | Download it and read the boundary statements |
| The pass standard | A written policy on how the line is set | Ask; a considered answer arrives quickly |
| Separation | Preparation does not decide the result | Ask who marks and who sets the standard |
| The appeal route | Published grounds, timescale, decision-maker | Read it before you need it |
| The record | A result a third party can confirm | Try verifying somebody else's credential |
| Renewal | A stated cycle and a stated requirement | Read what it asks of you in three years |
| The refusals | Nothing claimed that has not happened | Look for the claims that are absent |

**Slide 3 — Check one: read the exclusions, not the topic list**

Any syllabus can list topics. What tells you something is the boundary: what the examination assumes you already know, and what it deliberately leaves to another discipline.

A body that publishes the whole document before payment has given you the means to disagree with it. A body that publishes a brochure and holds the syllabus back has told you where its confidence sits.

**Slide 4 — Check two: how the line gets set**

Ask a single question. Is the pass standard fixed against a written description of a competent candidate, or against the distribution of everyone who sat?

The first means your result is about you. The second means it is partly about the room. Both are legitimate instruments, and only one of them is built for certifying competence.

**Slide 5 — Check three: who sells the preparation**

Ask who writes the items, who sets the standard, and who marks. Then ask whether any of them earns money from the course.

Where the same organisation prepares candidates and decides results, the incentive runs the wrong way. It is a structural point, not an accusation about anybody's character, and the fix is structural too: separation, written down.

**Slide 6 — Check four: the worst day, designed in advance**

Read the appeal policy before you enrol, not after a result you did not expect.

Four things should be in it: the grounds you may appeal on, the timescale, the person who decides, and confirmation that they were not part of the original decision. It is a short document. A body that has thought hard about it has thought about the thing that will eventually test it.

**Slide 7 — Check five: the thirty-second test**

A credential is worth what a stranger can confirm without your help, years after you sat.

Try it before you enrol. Find a holder, take the number, and see what the record returns: name, credential, status, date. If verification needs an email to somebody's inbox, that is what your future employer will get too.

**Slide 8 — Check six: read for absence**

In its first years a body has no pass rates worth quoting, no holder population, no employer survey and no salary data. There is nothing to compute them from.

So a new body arriving with a full set of impressive figures has told you one of two things: it is older than it looks, or the numbers are decoration. Absence is evidence here.

**Slide 9 — Check seven: the one that goes against every new body**

Elapsed time buys something publishing cannot substitute for. An established body has heard appeals, revised a syllabus under pressure, and survived a cohort that went badly.

A new one has done none of that. That is a real difference, it belongs in your decision, and the only cure for it is elapsed time.

**Slide 10 — Language to distrust, from anybody**

"Globally recognised" is a claim about other people's behaviour, and in year one nobody can evidence it.

"Endorsed by industry", with no named and checkable endorsement, is the same claim wearing a coat. So is a partnership announced without a partner. The test is simple: could you check it by tonight, without asking the body?

**Slide 11 — This body, put through its own seven checks**

Published in full and free: the Bodies of Knowledge behind PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI — 13, 16 and 16 domains and 61, 61 and 63 knowledge areas, with 92 sector case studies across the three volumes and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements underneath them. The PCI Standards are certification requirements set by the Institute, not law.

Published: the appeal route, the renewal cycle, how the certification decision is made. Testable today: the verification record.

Not published, and not going to be: pass rates, holder numbers, salary outcomes, examination weightings.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**Slide 12 — When a new body is the wrong answer**

If a client's prequalification, a framework agreement or your employer's policy requires an accredited credential, take the accredited route. A procurement condition beats every scope argument ever written.

If your next grade depends on a certification named in an internal matrix, take the named one.

If you want assessed chartered standing, that is years of supervised practice assessed by a professional route, and no examination substitutes for it.

Come back to the seven checks when the choice is genuinely open.
