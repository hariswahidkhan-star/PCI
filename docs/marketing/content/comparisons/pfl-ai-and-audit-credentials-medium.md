# Audit credentials: where PFL-AI sits alongside them

If your job is assurance — testing whether a control existed, operated and left evidence, independently of the people who produced the numbers — an audit credential is what you should hold, and the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) is no substitute for it. If your job is producing the project numbers that assurance is given over, PFL-AI is aimed at you.

*Republished on Medium with the canonical pointing at the original on credentialfinder.org.*

**Disclosure.** credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. This is a careful piece written by an interested party. Check it against the syllabus rather than against the tone.

## The two categories on scope axes

| Axis | Audit credentials | PFL-AI |
|---|---|---|
| The question asked | Did the control exist, operate as designed and leave evidence? | Is this the right measure, and what does it do to revenue and margin? |
| Unit of analysis | The process and its control environment | The contract and the control account |
| Independence | Central to the credential and to the role | Not applicable; the holder sits inside the reporting chain |
| Evidence | How to obtain, test, document and conclude on it | How to produce it at source: measurement rules, delivery records, applications |
| Risk | Governance, risk and control across the organisation | Contract-level exposure: forecast, provision, contract asset, retention |
| AI in the chain | The control environment around its use | What a model may propose, who decides, and what record survives the decision |
| Accreditation status | Established routes, many of them accredited | New, independent, not accredited |

The last row is in the table because a comparison that hides it would not be worth reading.

## What audit credentials examine

Audit credentials examine governance, risk and control, and the assurance process: planning an engagement, obtaining and evaluating evidence, preserving independence, forming a conclusion and reporting it.

A holder can look at a process, decide whether it does what it claims, and document that in a form that survives challenge. It is a demanding body of knowledge and these credentials go a long way into it.

What sits outside that scope, by design, is the construction of the project measure itself: which rule of credit turns work into earned value, whether the survey date and the ledger date are the same date, and what a forecast movement does to recognised revenue.

## What PFL-AI examines

PFL-AI examines the chain that turns delivery data into financial data on one contract: measurement rules, accrual evidence, cut-off, the estimate at completion, and the accounting consequences that follow from it. It covers 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas.

The Body of Knowledge is published in full before anyone pays anything, so a candidate can check the scope instead of taking a claim about it on trust.

PFL-AI confers no assurance standing, no independence, and no right to give an opinion on anything. That is not a modest framing of the credential; it is the boundary of what it is for.

## Worked: every invoice correct, the report £1.1m light

A fixed-price contract of **£24.0m**. Total expected cost **£20.0m**, so an expected margin of £4.0m. Progress is measured cost-to-cost. Costs recorded in the ledger to date: **£8.4m**.

Outside the ledger sit two things. **£0.6m** of goods received and not invoiced. **£0.5m** of subcontract work measured on site and not yet applied for. True cost incurred is **£9.5m**.

**On recorded cost:** 8.4 ÷ 20.0 = **42.0%**. Cumulative revenue = 0.420 × 24.0 = **£10.08m**. Margin = 0.420 × 4.0 = **£1.68m**.

**On complete cost:** 9.5 ÷ 20.0 = **47.5%**. Cumulative revenue = 0.475 × 24.0 = **£11.40m**. Margin = 0.475 × 4.0 = **£1.90m**.

**£1.32m of revenue and £0.22m of margin**, on work that had already been built, missing from the period because the paperwork had not travelled.

Every invoice that existed was approved, coded and correct. Testing performed on recorded items cannot reach an item that was never recorded, which is precisely why completeness is approached from the other direction — from the goods received log and the site measurement, back into the ledger.

## Why that is a shared problem, not a criticism of anyone

The goods received log and the site measurement are project records. When they are complete, timely and evidenced, the assurance built on them is worth something. When they are thin, everything above them is thinner, and no amount of testing repairs it after the fact.

That is a project controls responsibility. It sits with the people who run measurement, commitment tracking and the accrual process — and it is exactly the ground PFL-AI is examined on.

The reverse holds too. Somebody has to ask whether the control operated at all, independently, and record the answer. That is the audit question, and a project finance credential does not answer it.

## The accreditation position, stated plainly

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

If accreditation is a condition — of a client requirement, a regulatory expectation, or your own judgement about risk — that settles the question, and it settles it against PCI today.

## How to decide

**Take an audit credential** if independence is the point of your job, if you work in an assurance function, if you audit systems or processes rather than build the numbers inside them, or if your progression runs through an audit team. Nothing here should talk you out of that.

**Sit PFL-AI** if you produce the cost report, defend the forecast, and get asked why the accrual moved. The parts of a month-end that get challenged hardest are the parts it examines.

**Hold both, in either order,** if you audit or oversee capital projects and keep finding that the difficult part is not the control framework but the measurement underneath it. The [Body of Knowledge for the PFL-AI credential](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/certifications/pfl-ai) will tell you in an afternoon whether that describes your work.

## FAQ

**Does PFL-AI qualify me to audit anything?**
No. It confers no assurance standing, no independence and no right to give an opinion. It examines how project financial information is produced, which is the subject matter an auditor tests, rather than the testing itself.

**I hold an audit credential and cover capital projects. Would this add anything?**
Possibly. The areas holders most often say they were never examined on are rules of credit, cut-off between site survey and ledger close, accrual evidence, and how a forecast movement lands in revenue and margin. If your work already covers those in depth, it would add little.

**Is PFL-AI accredited?**
No. PCI is a new, independent certifying body, not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. The syllabus is published in full so you can judge the scope rather than a claim about it.

**Is this piece saying audit credentials miss things?**
No. A scope boundary is a design decision, not a failing. Audit credentials examine assurance thoroughly and are built to do so. The argument here is only that the design of a project measure is decided elsewhere, and somebody should be examined on it.

**My employer requires a named certification. Does this change that?**
No. Sit the one your employer names. A mandate is not a comparison, and nothing published here ranks credentials by employer preference, because that is not a claim anyone can make honestly.
