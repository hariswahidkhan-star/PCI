# How do I judge a syllabus before paying for an exam?

Read it in the wrong order deliberately. Front-to-back is how a document persuades you; seven partial passes in a fixed order is how you assess one. It takes about ninety minutes and it is the cheapest check available before a certification decision.

A disclosure first: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials mentioned at the end. Turn this method on PCI too. It was written so that it works either way.

**The seven passes**

1. **Read the last domain first.** Ten minutes. It is where a scheme puts whatever would not fit elsewhere, so it shows the true edge of the scope before the introduction can frame it.
2. **Count the verbs, not the pages.** Fifteen minutes, and no subject knowledge needed — you are reading grammar. "Understand" and "appreciate" describe an internal state nobody can mark consistently. "Calculate", "reconcile", "select and justify" describe something that leaves evidence on a page.
3. **Find one number.** Ten minutes. A credential that touches money should show a worked calculation in its own materials, reaching an answer that could be wrong.
4. **Find the exclusions.** Ten minutes. The hardest paragraph to write, and the most useful to read.
5. **Trace one topic you already know.** Twenty minutes, and the pass that decides it.
6. **Read what the body holds itself to.** Fifteen minutes: how questions are developed, who decides, who hears an appeal, how records are kept.
7. **Read one case study to its decision.** Ten minutes. A decision and a number, or a narrative with a moral?

**Why pass five decides it**

Take progress measurement. A package with a budget of £6.00m across 400 units. At cut-off, 150 units complete, 60 started and unfinished, actual cost £2.55m.

Units-complete rule: 150 ÷ 400 = 37.5%, so earned value is £2.25m and the cost performance index is 2.25 ÷ 2.55 = **0.88**.

Half credit at start: (150 + 30) ÷ 400 = 45%, so earned value is £2.70m and the index is 2.70 ÷ 2.55 = **1.06**.

£0.45m apart from one survey, and the index falls either side of 1.00. A Body of Knowledge worth the money makes you choose the rule and defend it. One that lists "progress measurement" as a topic leaves you to find out in the month it matters.

**What ninety minutes will not tell you**

Not difficulty, not standing, not whether a given employer will accept the credential. Cost and scheduling credentials, accountancy and finance qualifications, project management certifications and product certifications for scheduling software each publish a syllabus for their own subject and examine it thoroughly. This method tells you what a credential undertakes to make you able to do, and nothing about how anyone ranks it.

If your employer mandates a specific certification, or a framework you bid into scores an accredited one, the method is interesting and the mandate is binding. Take the credential you are required to hold.
