# A planner's credential choice, sorted by signature

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Twenty days of float at £8,500 a day is £170,000, and eight of those days may have no payer attached.
Twelve slides on choosing a credential as a planner, sorted by what you sign rather than what your title says.

Disclosure before you read: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.

---

**Slide 1 — The question is not which credential is best**

It is how far past the programme your signature travels.

Two planners with the same title on the same site sign very different things, and the credential that fits one of them fits the other badly.

**Slide 2 — Write your signature block first**

Every decision you put your name to in a normal month.

Programme development, logic and baseline. Monthly progress and status. Delay analysis and an extension-of-time position. Earned value from the programme. Forecast dates feeding a cost forecast.

Five to ten lines. This list, not the job title, is the input to the decision.

**Slide 3 — Float looks free until somebody prices it**

A package carries **20 days** of total float to a sectional completion date.
Time-related preliminaries: **£8,500 a day**.

Total float, priced: 20 × 8,500 = **£170,000**.

Split it: **12 days** instructed change, **8 days** subcontractor performance.

Entitlement worth pursuing: 12 × 8,500 = **£102,000**.
Outturn cost with no payer: 8 × 8,500 = **£68,000**.

**Slide 4 — Follow the £68,000 one step further**

Package value **£24.0m**. Cost forecast **£21.0m**. Forecast margin **£3.0m**.

Add the £68,000 and the forecast margin is **£2.932m**.

Three tenths of a point of margin, out of eight days of float that looked free on a programme. That is the step where scheduling stops and reporting begins.

**Slide 5 — And the £102,000 on the other side**

It sits in a claim position that a commercial team values and a finance team decides how much of to recognise, if any.

Unagreed amounts are constrained until it is highly probable they will not reverse. Whether the entitlement survives depends on records made in the month each event happened, not on the analysis anybody runs at the end.

**Slide 6 — What each category examines**

| Category | What it examines |
|---|---|
| Cost and scheduling credentials | Estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis |
| The established project controls certifications | Measurement, progress, forecasting, control of delivery |
| Project management certifications | Managing projects across people, process and the business environment |
| Product certifications for scheduling software | Operating a specific tool competently |

Each publishes a boundary and examines its own subject in depth. None of them is deficient.

**Slide 7 — Map your lines onto the table**

Programme, logic, status and delay analysis sit squarely in one category, examined thoroughly and by design.

Earned value from the programme, forecast dates feeding a cost forecast and resource loading straddle a boundary that professions draw and projects do not.

Count how many of your lines fall on each side. That count is your answer.

**Slide 8 — A tool certificate is a different purchase**

It proves you can drive a specific scheduling product. That is a genuine, marketable skill with its own market.

It is not evidence about the discipline the product serves, and the two are often sold in the same sentence. Decide which one you are buying before you pay.

**Slide 9 — When a scheduling-focused credential is the right answer**

Plainly, and often.

If you plan, status and analyse programmes, and the cost report is somebody else's document, a credential examining schedule development, maintenance and control examines your actual job.

It will not ask you to study a boundary you never cross, and the evenings you save are real ones.

**Slide 10 — Three things that end the exercise early**

A client's prequalification names a certification. Take the named one.

An employer's grading matrix names one. Take that one.

A procurement rule in your market requires an accredited credential. That settles it, and today it settles it against this Institute.

**Slide 11 — What this body publishes, and what it does not**

The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) runs to 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. All three Bodies of Knowledge are published in full and free before anybody pays, with 92 sector case studies and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements underneath them.

Not published: pass rates, examination weightings, salary outcomes, holder counts.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**Slide 12 — Two things to do this week**

Write the signature block. Mark each line examined, assumed or excluded against two syllabuses, and count.

Then read ten current advertisements for the job you want next, in the market you want it in, and note what they ask for. That is your own recognition data, gathered in twenty minutes, and it beats anybody's general claim about employers — mine included.
