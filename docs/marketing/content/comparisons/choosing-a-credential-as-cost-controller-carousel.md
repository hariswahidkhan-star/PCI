# The accrual that decides a cost controller's choice

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

One subcontractor invoice that has not arrived is worth about a million pounds of forecast on this job.
Twelve slides on the arithmetic, and on which kind of credential examines the step where it happens.

Disclosure before you read: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.

---

**Slide 1 — The cost report is only as honest as the accrual behind it**

Everything downstream — the index, the forecast, the reported margin — inherits whatever the accrual got wrong.

And the accrual is the one line on the report that nobody outside the cost team ever asks to see.

**Slide 2 — The month, in three numbers**

Earned value for the period: **£6.60m**
Actual cost as booked in the ledger: **£6.30m**
Work done by two subcontractors, not yet invoiced: **£0.45m**

Nothing unusual. This is an ordinary month on an ordinary job.

**Slide 3 — Two indices, one month**

As booked: 6.60 ÷ 6.30 = **1.048** → the job is outperforming.

With the accrual: 6.60 ÷ 6.75 = **0.978** → the job is behind.

Same site, same work, same period. One missing invoice, and the story reverses.

**Slide 4 — What that does to the forecast**

Budget at completion: **£15.0m**

At 1.048 → outturn = 15.0 ÷ 1.048 = **£14.32m**
At 0.978 → outturn = 15.0 ÷ 0.978 = **£15.34m**

Just over **£1.0m** apart, produced by a cut-off gap rather than by anything that happened on site.

**Slide 5 — Where the £1.0m goes after you sign**

It does not stop at the cost report.

That outturn figure is the denominator when progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs. Move the denominator and you move the measure of progress, which moves recognised revenue, which moves reported margin.

A one-week cut-off gap has become a movement in a set of accounts.

**Slide 6 — What a cost controller actually signs**

| What you sign | Which category examines it |
|---|---|
| The cost report and committed position | Cost and scheduling credentials |
| The accrual for work done, not invoiced | Split across two categories |
| The estimate at completion | Cost and scheduling credentials |
| Variance analysis against budget | The established project controls certifications |
| Cash flow phasing from the cost plan | Split across two categories |

Rows two and five are the ones worth choosing a credential over.

**Slide 7 — What each category examines**

Cost and scheduling credentials: estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis, and the economics of an asset's life.

Accountancy and finance qualifications: recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control.

Audit credentials: governance, risk, control and the assurance process.

Each publishes a boundary and examines its own subject in depth.

**Slide 8 — The accrual sits between them**

Accountancy syllabuses assume competent delivery data arrives from a source they do not examine. That is a reasonable design decision for a syllabus about reporting.

Cost syllabuses examine how the delivery number is built, and hand it over at the report.

The join is examined by neither as a matter of course. That is a fact about how professions were organised, not a failing in anybody's syllabus.

**Slide 9 — Test your own discipline before you buy anything**

Take the last three months. Recalculate cost performance twice: once as booked, once with accruals in the period they belonged to.

If the two lines move more than a couple of points against each other, the discipline is the problem before the credential is.

That test costs an afternoon and no money.

**Slide 10 — When the shorter route is the right one**

If you own the cost report and hand it to a commercial manager who takes it from there, a credential examining estimating and cost control examines your job. Buy that, not a wider syllabus you will not use.

If you are moving into a finance function, take an accountancy or finance qualification, and take the whole thing rather than a fragment.

If a grading matrix or a prequalification names a certification, take the named one. A requirement is not a comparison.

**Slide 11 — What this body publishes, and what it does not**

The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) runs to 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas; the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) to 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas. All three Bodies of Knowledge are published in full and free before anybody pays, with 92 sector case studies and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements underneath them.

Not published: pass rates, examination weightings, salary outcomes, holder counts.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**Slide 12 — The evening's work**

List what you sign in a normal month. Mark each line examined, assumed or excluded against the syllabuses you are weighing, and count.

Then add the column your list does not have: what happens downstream of each number. Where a line has consequences you cannot trace, that is the gap a credential should close — and if there is no such line, you have just saved yourself a fee and thirty evenings.
