# Choosing a credential as an auditor on capital projects

Choosing a credential as an auditor who works on capital projects is a question about one gap. Governance, risk, control and the assurance process transfer to a project engagement intact. The technical subject those controls are meant to govern does not come with them: how progress is measured, how a forecast is built, and which numbers can be tested against evidence rather than against a process.

Disclosure before the argument. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials named here. The piece argues for a narrow addition to what you already hold, and it has an interest in doing so.

## The accrual that moved a forecast by £2.7m

A contract with a budget at completion of **£40m**.

In the period under review, earned value is **£4.2m** and cost booked to the ledger is **£4.0m**.

- CPI = 4.2 ÷ 4.0 = **1.05**
- EAC = 40 ÷ 1.05 = **£38.1m**

The project reports a £1.9m saving against budget, and everyone is pleased.

Then you find the accrual list. A subcontractor has completed **£300k** of work that has not been invoiced and has not been accrued. The cost belongs in the period; it simply has not arrived.

- Corrected AC = 4.0 + 0.3 = **£4.3m**
- CPI = 4.2 ÷ 4.3 = **0.98** (to two places)
- EAC = 40 ÷ 0.98 = **£40.8m**

One omission of £300k moves the forecast out-turn by roughly **£2.7m**, and turns a reported saving into a reported overrun. Nothing else about the project changed.

## Why that is not a control finding

You can reach that number by testing controls, and the write-up will say the accrual process did not operate. True, and useful, and it will be closed with a revised procedure.

The version that changes behaviour says something different: the period's cost is understated by £300k, the index is overstated as a result, and the forecast the board is planning against is £2.7m optimistic. Same evidence, different competence.

That competence is technical rather than procedural, and it is the whole of the gap. It is also finite.

## The four artefacts worth learning to test

| Artefact | What you are testing | What a weakness does to the numbers |
|---|---|---|
| The earning rule | One rule per work type, applied consistently | Changes what counts as earned this period |
| The cut-off calendar | Whether progress and cost are measured to the same date | Creates variance where none exists |
| The accrual list | Whether work done and uninvoiced is captured and supportable | Understates cost, overstates the index |
| The estimate at completion | Whether it was rebuilt or escalated, and when it was last challenged | Moves the forecast and the percentage complete |

Four artefacts, each testable with evidence, each producing findings a board understands on the first reading. That is a smaller body of material than a second profession, and it is the part that makes project engagements land.

## What each category examines, in its own terms

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. That is a full profession with its own standards, and nothing here suggests it needs supplementing for the work it was built for.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. On a project engagement, that is the reporting end of the chain above.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. That is the technical subject in the four artefacts.

Three distinct subjects, each examined properly by its own category. A project assurance engagement crosses all three, which is why staffing one is harder than scoping it.

## Where the PCI credentials sit

The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) covers the reporting boundary at 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas. The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) covers the delivery side at 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas.

Each Body of Knowledge is proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20% governed AI. Those are the proportions of the syllabus and not an examination weighting — the syllabus is settled, the blueprint is an open decision, and we would rather say that than imply a precision we do not have.

For an auditor, the 40% covering project management is the unfamiliar material. The 20% covering governed AI is increasingly what engagements ask about, because reports are being drafted by models and somebody has to say what evidence must survive that — the problem set out in [keeping an audit trail when reporting is generated](https://pciai.org/generative-ai-project-reporting).

## When your existing credential is the whole answer

**If you audit information systems**, the credential built for that subject is exactly right and no project credential improves it.

**If your internal audit portfolio is compliance, financial controls and operational process**, the technical project subject is a small part of a wide brief, and a full second syllabus is a poor trade for it.

**If your firm's methodology and progression are built around a specific credential**, take the one your progression requires. That is not a scope decision.

**If you need an accredited credential today**, go where accreditation already exists. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

The case for adding project material is narrow: you are on capital project engagements regularly, the findings that matter are about numbers rather than process, and you would rather test the number than the control around it.

## Three questions to put to any syllabus, ours included

Does it say how an earning rule is chosen, documented and evidenced, and what a change of rule mid-project does to the reported position?

Does it say which date governs when progress and cost are cut on different days, and what evidence supports the reconciliation between them?

Does it say what a person must decide when a model produces the forecast or drafts the commentary, and what has to be recorded about that decision?

A syllabus that answers all three is examining the work as it happens on a real engagement. One that answers none may still be an excellent credential in its own subject.

## What to do with your last three engagements

Take them out and, for each, write down the highest-value finding and mark whether it came from control testing or from understanding how the number was built.

If they all came from control testing, your existing credential is doing the work and you can stop reading. If the ones that mattered came from the number, the addition is worth costing, and the category comparison sits in [the auditor's guide to choosing a credential](https://credentialfinder.org/choosing-a-credential-as-auditor).
