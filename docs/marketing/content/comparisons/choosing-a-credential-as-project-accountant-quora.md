# Is a project credential worth it for a qualified accountant?

Only for the delivery half, and you can test that in one evening with your own numbers.

If your qualification already covers recognition, measurement and disclosure, a second syllabus that repeats them is money spent being examined on what you already hold. The gap, if you have one, is upstream: whether the estimate you divide by is current, whether the progress behind it was earned, and whether the accrual is complete.

A disclosure before the argument: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials I mention at the end. Discount for the interest and check the figures.

**Why the exposure is upstream**

Contract price £24.0m. Cost incurred to date £9.0m. The estimate of total cost you were given at the last review is £22.5m.

Progress = 9.0 ÷ 22.5 = **40.0%**. Revenue = 0.40 × 24.0 = £9.60m. Margin = 9.60 − 9.00 = **£0.60m**.

Now the project team revises the estimate to £23.4m, for delivery reasons: a rate agreed above allowance, a subcontractor replaced, rework.

Progress = 9.0 ÷ 23.4 = **38.5%**. Revenue = £9.23m. Margin = **£0.23m**.

**£0.37m off the reported margin, and the accounting was correct both times.** The number that moved is one you did not build, from a process your qualification does not set out to examine.

**What each category is for**

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. That is a deep subject, examined properly, and it is what most project accountants hold.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis — how that £22.5m is built and maintained in the first place.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process, which is whether the controls around those inputs operated. A third question, and a different profession.

None of the three is deficient. They were built to examine different things.

**The test worth running**

Take your last four margin movements. For each, write down what caused it, when the causing event actually happened, and when you found out.

If the lag is routinely a month or more, the gap is upstream information rather than accounting judgement, and it is worth costing a credential against it. If the lag is short and the causes were inside your own control, your qualification covers your job and you should keep your evenings.

Two clear cases for not bothering: you are progressing inside a finance function, where finishing the accountancy qualification is the route and nothing project-side substitutes for a stage of it; and your projects are short, repeatable and low-variance, where estimates rarely move at all.

**What I will not claim**

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

The PFL-AI Body of Knowledge is 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20% governed AI. Those are the syllabus proportions and not an examination weighting. For a qualified accountant the middle 40% is the only part worth paying for, and you can read the whole thing free before deciding.
