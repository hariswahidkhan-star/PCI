# What a certificate of attendance can and cannot prove

A certificate of attendance proves that a course ran and that you were on it. A credential proves that somebody with no stake in the answer marked your work against a standard published before you started. Both documents are worth having. Only one of them tells a stranger what you can do, and four questions separate them before you pay.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. Apply everything below to PCI as readily as to anybody else.

## Start with the rudest question: can you fail it?

Ask it in those words, early, and to a person rather than a brochure.

If the honest answer is that everyone who attends receives the document, you are buying training. Training is worth buying, and a good course is usually the fastest route into an unfamiliar subject.

What you are not buying is evidence. A document that cannot record a failure cannot record a pass either, because there is nothing on the other side of the line for the pass to mean.

## The four questions, and the two columns they produce

| Question | Attendance or completion record | Examined certification |
|---|---|---|
| What is being recorded? | Hours in the room, or a course finished | Performance against a standard set out beforehand |
| Who decides the outcome? | The organisation that took your fee to teach you | An assessment your preparation provider does not mark |
| Can you fail? | Frequently not at all | Yes, with a published resit route and an appeal |
| What can you read before paying? | An outline and a duration | The syllabus, the assessment format, the appeal route |
| What happens afterwards? | Nothing; the document is final | A stated renewal cycle with a stated requirement |
| What does it prove to a stranger? | That the course ran and you were present | That you met a standard on a day you did not control |

Neither column is the better product. They answer different questions. A buyer who needs the first and pays for the second has wasted money in a way nobody refunds, and the reverse mistake is worse, because it is discovered by somebody else.

## What an examinable item actually looks like

A course does not have to test anything, so it can cover forecasting by defining it. An assessment has to produce evidence, so it has to make you choose between defensible answers and then stand behind the choice.

Take a package with a budget at completion of **£8.0m**. At the cut-off, earned value is **£3.10m** and actual cost is **£3.55m**.

- Cost performance index = 3.10 ÷ 3.55 = **0.873**
- Forecast if performance to date continues = 8.0 ÷ 0.873 = **£9.16m**
- Forecast if the overrun is behind you = 3.55 + (8.0 − 3.10) = **£8.45m**

Two forecasts from one dataset, **£0.71m** apart, both arithmetically correct.

A recall question asks which formula is which, and a course certificate is a fair record of having sat through the answer. An examinable item hands you the site conditions — a wet season ending, a subcontractor replaced, a rate agreed for the remaining scope — and asks which figure you would put your name to, and why. Each of [the four forecasting methods a practitioner is expected to hold](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/four-eac-formulas) assumes something different about the rest of the job, and choosing between them is the competence being tested.

That is the difference in one page. One document says you heard about forecasting. The other says somebody watched you do it and marked it against something written down.

## Who marks it is the question underneath all the others

Where the organisation taking the fee also awards the result, both roles sit in the same hands. That is not automatically wrong, and plenty of bodies manage it with real separation inside one organisation.

It does mean you should ask how. Two questions do the work: who sets the pass standard, and does anybody involved in preparing candidates take part in the decision on those candidates?

A body that has genuinely separated the two answers quickly, because the separation is something it designed on purpose and can describe. A body that has not will answer with adjectives.

The same test applies to the document itself rather than to the organisation, and [the conditions a claim has to meet before anything can be examined at all](https://credentialfinder.org/what-makes-a-credential-examinable) are the ones to run it against.

## Where an attendance record is exactly the right document

Often it is precisely what you need, and buying an examination instead is an expensive detour.

If your professional body asks for a number of development hours a year, an attendance record is the evidence it wants. If your employer has bought a new release of a scheduling tool and needs the team on it by March, tool training with a completion certificate is the correct purchase. If a site requires an induction record, only the induction record will do.

Product certifications for scheduling software sit across this line. Some are examined and some record attendance, and they are sold in similar language, so check which one you are buying rather than assuming. What they examine is the operation of a specific product, which is a real skill with its own market and a different thing from the discipline the product serves.

None of that is a criticism of a course. It is a purchase-order question: are you buying hours, or evidence?

## What a renewal requirement tells you before you sit

A document that never expires makes a claim about the future nobody can support: that what you showed once still holds a decade later, in a discipline whose tooling moved underneath it.

A renewal requirement is a body admitting that competence decays and asking you to show that yours has not. It is also a recurring obligation you are taking on, so read what it will ask of you in three years before you sit anything today.

An attendance certificate carries no renewal because there is nothing to keep current. That is consistent rather than careless. It records an event, and events do not expire.

## What PCI publishes, and what it will not claim

The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI), the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) and the PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) each have their own Body of Knowledge and their own examination.

The three run to 13, 16 and 16 domains and 61, 61 and 63 knowledge areas, with 92 sector case studies across the three volumes and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements underneath them. All of it is published and readable before anybody pays. The PCI Standards are certification requirements set by the Institute; they are not law, and nothing here is legal, tax or accounting advice.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

PCI also publishes no pass rates, no holder numbers and no salary figures. At this stage it would be inventing them.

## Where the honest answer is to buy the course instead

If what you need is fifteen development hours by December, buy the course. An examination is a slower and dearer way to produce that evidence, and the hours count either way.

If your employer's grading matrix names a specific certification, take the named one. If a client's pre-qualification requires an accredited credential, settle that before you weigh anything else, because a scope argument does not win against a procurement condition.

And if you want the software skill rather than the discipline, the product route is the direct one. Those cases are real, they are common, and a comparison that never reaches them is an advertisement.

## FAQ

**Is a certificate of attendance worthless?**
No. It is the correct evidence for development hours, tool training and induction records, and it is usually cheaper and faster than an examination. It only becomes a problem when it is sold, or listed on a CV, as proof of competence — a claim it was never built to carry.

**Does a digital badge make something a credential?**
No. A badge is a display format. It can carry a verified examination result or a course completion and it looks identical either way. Follow the badge through to the issuing record and read what that record says the holder actually did.

**My employer calls our course certificate a certification. Does the difference matter?**
Internally, often not. Externally it does, because a hiring manager who checks will find a course completion where the CV implied an examination. That conversation is worse than never having made the claim. Describe the document as what it is.

**How do I find out who marks the assessment?**
Ask the body in writing and keep the reply. Two questions: who sets the pass standard, and whether anyone who prepares candidates takes part in deciding those candidates' results. Speed and specificity in the answer tell you most of what the policy would.

**Does an unaccredited certification count for anything?**
It counts for what its published syllabus and its assessment can demonstrate, no more and no less. Accreditation is third-party assurance of the process rather than proof of the content, so its absence is a reason to read the syllabus and the pass-standard policy closely rather than a reason to stop reading.
