# How to read a Body of Knowledge before you pay for it

How to read a Body of Knowledge: the way you would read a scope of works. Check what is included, what is excluded, what is assumed and what depth is required. An hour with the syllabus tells you more than any comparison page, this one included. If the syllabus is not published before payment, that is already an answer.

> **Who publishes this page.** credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. The method below is meant to be used on PCI's syllabus as hard as on anyone else's.

## Step one: check it exists, in full, free, before payment

A Body of Knowledge is the document that says what will be examined. If what you can see before paying is a list of module titles, a benefits page and a testimonial, you have not seen the syllabus.

This is the cheapest filter in the whole decision, and it removes a surprising proportion of what is sold as certification.

## Step two: read the verbs, not the nouns

The nouns tell you the subject. The verbs tell you the depth, and depth is what you are buying.

| Verb in the syllabus | What it asks of you | What preparation it implies |
|---|---|---|
| Define, list, state | Recall | Reading, flashcards |
| Calculate, apply, prepare | Application under stated conditions | Working problems until the method is automatic |
| Select, justify, reconcile | Judgement between defensible options | Scenario practice, and an explanation of why the other options fail |
| Understand, appreciate, be aware of | Nothing that can be marked | None available, because there is nothing to prepare for |

A syllabus whose verbs are mostly in the bottom row is a course outline. That is not a criticism of courses; it is a statement about what the document can support.

## Step three in how to read a Body of Knowledge: map it against your month

This is the step almost nobody does, and it is the one that decides fit.

Write down every deliverable you personally produced last month. Not your job description, your outputs. Then tick which domain of the syllabus would have examined each one.

Take a worked case. A cost engineer lists **12 deliverables**: a monthly cost report, a forecast update, three variance commentaries, two change estimates, a cash flow update, an accrual schedule, a progress reconciliation, a risk drawdown review and a board pack extract.

Against syllabus A, **9 of the 12** map onto four domains. Coverage of your actual month = 9 ÷ 12 = **75%**.
Against syllabus B, **5 of the 12** map on. Coverage = 5 ÷ 12 = **42%**.

The three deliverables that syllabus A does not cover are the accrual schedule, the board pack extract and the reconciliation between the cost report and the ledger. Now you know exactly what the credential will not vouch for, which is more useful than knowing what it will.

Run the same tally for the role you want next rather than the one you have, and the answer often changes. That is the version to act on.

## Step four: read what it excludes

A serious syllabus states its boundaries. It says where its subject stops and another profession's begins.

A syllabus that appears to cover everything has not been scoped, and an examination cannot be built on it. Exclusions are a sign of drafting discipline rather than a gap in the offer.

## Step five: check the shape is published as a shape

Domains and knowledge areas describe the structure of the syllabus. They are not the same as examination weightings, which describe the composition of the paper.

PCI publishes the structure: PCL-AI has 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, PFL-AI has 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and PML-AI has 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas. Across the three volumes there are 92 sector case studies, and the scheme rests on 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements. The proportions of each Body of Knowledge are 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20% governed AI.

Those are syllabus proportions. PCI does not publish an examination weighting, because the blueprint is a settled-later decision and publishing a number it has not fixed would be misleading. When you read any body's published percentages, check which of the two you are being shown.

## Step six: look for the arithmetic

A syllabus that examines a technical discipline should require candidates to compute things and defend the result. Look for objectives that name a calculation and ask for the assumption behind it.

Where the syllabus is quantitative, ask whether the body checks its own worked material. For PFL-AI and PML-AI, PCI runs 15,613 machine calculation checks across the quantitative content; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite, so that figure covers two of the three credentials and not the third.

## What PCI does not claim

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

No pass rates, salary outcomes or holder numbers are published, here or anywhere else on this estate.

## The hour, summarised

Twenty minutes on the verbs, twenty on mapping your own month, ten on the exclusions, ten on the structure and the arithmetic. If the syllabus survives that, book the examination. If it does not, you have saved yourself a fee and several months.

Whether the objectives can be examined at all is the companion test, set out in [what makes a credential examinable](https://credentialfinder.org/what-makes-a-credential-examinable), and [what a syllabus should tell you before you enrol](https://credentialfinder.org/what-a-syllabus-should-tell-you) covers the administrative half: eligibility, conditions, and what happens if you fail. If your immediate question is about a training course rather than a credential, the hub's guide to [judging a course before you pay for it](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/project-controls-course) applies the same discipline to teaching rather than assessment.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a Body of Knowledge be?**
Long enough to state its objectives and its boundaries, which usually means tens of pages rather than a page or several hundred. Length is a weak signal on its own. A short document with precise, levelled objectives beats a long one built from topic headings every time.

**What if two credentials cover the same domains?**
Compare the verbs and the depth rather than the domain names. Two syllabuses can list "cost control" and mean recall in one case and judgement between defensible treatments in the other. The mapping exercise against your own deliverables usually separates them within half an hour.

**Should I read the whole thing?**
Read the contents, the exclusions and one domain in full, chosen as the one closest to your daily work. If that domain reads as though someone who has done the job wrote it, read further. If it reads as though it was assembled from a textbook index, you have your answer.

**Does a published syllabus mean the examination is good?**
No. It means you can check the claim, which is the precondition for everything else. Publication is necessary and not sufficient, and the next questions are how items are written and reviewed, and how the pass mark is set.
