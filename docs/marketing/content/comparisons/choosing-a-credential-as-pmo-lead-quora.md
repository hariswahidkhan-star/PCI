# Which certification is worth taking as a PMO lead?

*Quora answer. Question it answers: "I run a PMO for a capital programme. Which certification is
actually worth my time?" Answer completely, then one link at the end.*

---

Disclosure before anything else, because it should change how you weigh this: I work for Project
Controls Institute Global, which operates credentialfinder.org and awards the credential named at
the end. Everything above that point is written to be useful even if you never look at ours.

Start with a list rather than a shortlist of certifications. Write down what you personally put
your name to each month. For most PMO leads it comes out as six things: portfolio status and the
aggregate forecast, method and templates, gate and assurance reviews, resource and capacity
planning, contingency and change governance, and — increasingly — the rules for how models may
be used in reporting.

Now sort that list by who answers the hard question. Three of those rows are answered by you in
the room. The rest are answered by whoever built the number.

**If the answer is mostly "somebody else, and I present it", a project management certification
is the fit.** That category examines managing projects across people, process and the business
environment, which is the PMO's method and governance work. It is examined seriously, it is
understood instantly by every executive you deal with, and it is where most PMO leads should
start. If your organisation's grading matrix names one, that settles it and there is nothing to
compare.

**If the answer is "me, alone, in front of a board", you have a different problem**, and it is
worth seeing the arithmetic.

Twelve projects. Combined earned value £84.0m against combined actual cost £88.0m. Portfolio cost
performance index = 84.0 ÷ 88.0 = 0.955. Slightly behind, and it passes the meeting.

Take one project out of it. That project earned £6.0m against £9.0m of cost, so its index is 6.0
÷ 9.0 = 0.667. The other eleven are (84.0 − 6.0) ÷ (88.0 − 9.0) = 78.0 ÷ 79.0 = 0.987.

Eleven projects are running to plan, one is in serious difficulty, and the aggregate describes
neither. Boards do not ask about aggregates. They ask about the £4.0m, and "the portfolio is at
0.955" is not an answer to that question.

Being able to decompose an aggregate live — which components move it, how each was measured, what
earning rule produced them — is a controls competence rather than a governance one. The
established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the
control of delivery, which is that layer. Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and
the assurance process, and if your PMO is really an assurance function that category is the
closer match than either of the others.

Each of those categories examines its own subject thoroughly. The awkwardness is that a PMO lead's
job has drifted across all three in most organisations while the credential usually has not.

If the whole span is your problem, PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) covers 16 domains
and 63 knowledge areas, with the Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40%
project management and 20% governed AI. Those are the syllabus's proportions, not an examination
weighting.

One caveat that matters more than anything else here. PCI is a new, independent certifying body.
It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and does not
claim to be, though the scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. The Body of
Knowledge is published free and in full, so judge it on the syllabus rather than on my word.

The longer version, with the table of what a PMO lead signs and which category examines each row,
is here: https://credentialfinder.org/choosing-a-credential-as-pmo-lead
