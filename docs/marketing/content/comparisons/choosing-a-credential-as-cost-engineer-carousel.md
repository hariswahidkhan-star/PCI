# Choosing a credential as a cost engineer

*LinkedIn document carousel. 12 slides at 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the
arithmetic, because slide 3 is the one people screenshot.*

---

### Slide 1 — cover
**A £3.0m exposure.**
**£0.6m of contingency.**
Nobody in the room said it out loud.
*Alt text: title slide contrasting a £3.0m stated exposure with £0.6m of approved contingency.*

### Slide 2 — the set-up
A sanction estimate of **£20.0m**.

Stated accuracy: **±15%**.

Approved contingency: **£0.6m**.

Two numbers written by two different conversations.
*Alt text: slide listing a £20.0m sanction estimate with ±15% stated accuracy and £0.6m of
approved contingency.*

### Slide 3 — the arithmetic
Band = 20.0 × 0.15 = **±£3.0m**

Honest statement of the estimate: **£17.0m to £23.0m**

Contingency held: **£0.6m** = **3%**

Held against a stated upside exposure of **£3.0m**.
*Alt text: calculation slide showing a ±£3.0m accuracy band against £0.6m of contingency.*

### Slide 4 — what that actually decided
A risk appetite, set by arithmetic rather than by anybody choosing it.

Both numbers are defensible alone. Side by side, they say something nobody said.
*Alt text: slide stating that the gap between the accuracy band and the contingency sets risk
appetite by accident.*

### Slide 5 — then escalation
**£8.0m** of spend remains. Indexation at **4%**.

8.0 × 0.04 = **£0.32m**

More than half the contingency, before a single risk event has happened.
*Alt text: slide showing £0.32m of escalation exposure against £0.6m of contingency.*

### Slide 6 — what is left
Draw the escalation down and **£0.28m** stands against everything else on the risk register.

None of this is an estimating error.
*Alt text: slide showing £0.28m of contingency remaining after escalation is drawn down.*

### Slide 7 — what went wrong, precisely
An accuracy range, a contingency and an escalation allowance, set in three separate conversations
and never reconciled on one page.

Putting all three in front of a sponsor is the competence.
*Alt text: slide explaining that the three numbers were set in separate conversations and never
reconciled.*

### Slide 8 — what your own category examines
**Cost and scheduling credentials**: estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis and
the economics of an asset's life.

Your subject, examined in depth and by design.
*Alt text: slide listing what cost and scheduling credentials set out to examine.*

### Slide 9 — what the neighbours examine
**The established project controls certifications**: measurement, progress, forecasting and the
control of delivery.

**Accountancy and finance qualifications**: recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial
control.
*Alt text: slide summarising what project controls certifications and accountancy qualifications
each set out to examine.*

### Slide 10 — what PCL-AI examines, honestly
**13 domains. 61 knowledge areas.**

Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed
AI. Syllabus proportions, not examination weightings.

A large share of it is not estimating. Read the domain list before deciding.
*Alt text: slide summarising PCL-AI as 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, noting that much of the
syllabus is not estimating.*

### Slide 11 — when this is not for you
You estimate for a living → take the depth of a cost-focused credential.

Your grading matrix names one → take that one.

You are early in estimating → build reps and benchmark data first.
*Alt text: slide listing three situations in which a different route is the right answer for a
cost engineer.*

### Slide 12 — the position, and the disclosure
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. Not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and not claiming to be. Built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024
principles.

*credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PCL-AI.*
*Alt text: closing slide stating that PCI is not accredited and disclosing that it operates
credentialfinder.org.*
