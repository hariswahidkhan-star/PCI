# Read the appeals policy before you book the exam, not after

*X / Threads thread — 7 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. No link in the thread; the link sits in the first reply. X counts a URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/7 — the hook** (237 characters)
Read a certifying body's appeals policy before you book, not after you fail.

An appeal challenges a decision. A complaint is about how you were treated. Different routes, different people, and mixing them is why most appeals go nowhere.

**Post 2/7 — the deadline test** (207 characters)
The fastest test of an appeals policy: find the deadline that binds the body.

Most policies give the candidate a window and give themselves none. A window that only binds one side is a queue, not a process.

**Post 3/7 — who hears it** (215 characters)
Second test: who reviews the decision, and did they make it?

A useful policy names a role outside the original decision. "The relevant team will review" names nobody and commits to nothing you could hold anyone to.

**Post 4/7 — a ground, worked** (251 characters)
A ground is checkable. A grievance is felt.

EV £26.6m ÷ AC £30.0m = 0.886666…
Two decimal places: 0.89. Three: 0.887.

If the paper asked for two and the scheme accepted only 0.887, that is a defect anyone can verify — and it hit everyone who sat it.

**Post 5/7 — what a held result should show** (243 characters)
While a result is under review it should show no score and no pass or fail. Held, and labelled held.

A provisional number that is later withdrawn cannot be recalled from the people who already saw it. The candidate carries that, not the body.

**Post 6/7 — remedies** (248 characters)
"Upheld" is not a remedy. A policy worth reading lists them: re-mark, re-sit at no further charge, record corrected, credential reinstated, and the item fixed for everyone who sat it.

Fixing it for one candidate only is a settlement, not a remedy.

**Post 7/7 — the disclosure, and PCI's weak point** (268 characters)
PCI's appeals route ends inside PCI. An accredited scheme adds a body above the scheme. If you need that, take the accredited route.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it.
