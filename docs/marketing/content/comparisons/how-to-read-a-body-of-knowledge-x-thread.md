# Reading a Body of Knowledge before you pay: 7 posts

*X / Threads thread — 7 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. No link in the thread itself; the link goes in the first reply. X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/7 — the hook** (226 characters)
Same package. Same survey date. A cost performance index of 0.88 or 1.06, depending on a rule somebody chose before the job started.

Nobody miscounted. Here is the arithmetic, and why it is really a question about a syllabus.

**Post 2/7 — the data** (147 characters)
The package. Budget £6.00m across 400 units.

At cut-off: 150 units complete, 60 started and unfinished. Actual cost £2.55m. One survey, one month.

**Post 3/7 — rule one** (146 characters)
Units-complete rule. Only finished units earn.

150 ÷ 400 = 37.5%
Earned value = 0.375 × 6.00 = £2.25m
Cost performance index = 2.25 ÷ 2.55 = 0.88

**Post 4/7 — rule two** (180 characters)
Half credit at start, on the same survey.

(150 + 30) ÷ 400 = 45%
Earned value = 0.45 × 6.00 = £2.70m
Index = 2.70 ÷ 2.55 = 1.06

£0.45m apart. The index lands either side of 1.00.

**Post 5/7 — why it is a syllabus problem** (205 characters)
A Body of Knowledge worth paying for makes you choose that rule and defend the choice against the evidence.

One that lists "progress measurement" as a topic leaves you to find out in the month it matters.

**Post 6/7 — the ninety-minute method** (220 characters)
Seven passes before you pay:

Last domain first
Count verbs, not pages
Find one worked calculation
Find the exclusions
Trace a topic you already know
Read what the body holds itself to
Read one case study to its decision
