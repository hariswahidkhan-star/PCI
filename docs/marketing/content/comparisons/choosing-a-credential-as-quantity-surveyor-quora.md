# Should a quantity surveyor take a certification as well?

Usually no, and the exception is specific enough to test in an evening.

If what you need is assessed professional standing in surveying, the chartered route assesses exactly that, and an examination in an adjacent subject does not substitute for it. Take the chartered route, and do not let anyone talk you into a shortcut that measures something else.

A disclosure before I go further, since I am not neutral: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials I mention at the end.

**The exception, in figures**

Cumulative valuation of work executed £4.5m, of which £0.35m is an instructed variation still being negotiated. Cost incurred to date £4.3m.

As valued, the job is making 4.5 − 4.3 = **+£0.2m**.

Amounts that depend on a negotiation are recognised only to the extent it is highly probable they will not reverse later. Constrain the £0.35m and the same month reads 4.15 − 4.3 = **−£0.15m**.

Nothing is in dispute on site. The work was done, the cost is in the ledger, your entitlement position is sound. And the reported result has moved by £0.35m on one line.

**Whose subject is that?**

Chartered routes in surveying and engineering assess professional competence and standing over years of supervised practice, tested by submission and professional interview. Measurement, valuation and entitlement are the profession's own subject, assessed to a standard built over a long time.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control — the downstream subject, at a depth and breadth far beyond the part that touches your valuation.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis, which is the matching subject for the cost-to-complete rather than the valuation.

Each of the three is examining what it set out to examine, thoroughly and by design. The join between a valuation and a recognised number belongs to none of them as a matter of course.

**How to tell whether it is your problem**

Take the last three final accounts you were close to. For each, write down the month when the commercial position and the reported position diverged, and what you would have needed to know to see it coming.

If that exercise produces nothing, you are working inside one category and the chartered route is the whole answer. If it produces three uncomfortable examples, you have found the gap, and it is worth reading a domain list against it.

Two other cases where the answer is simply no: a framework or client panel that names professional standing as a condition, and a role where measurement and negotiation are the whole job. Requirements do not lose arguments to scope, and there is nothing for a second credential to fix in the second case.

**What I will not claim**

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

The PFL-AI Body of Knowledge is 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and it can be read in full, free, before anybody pays. For a surveyor, much of it will not be your job — which is the point of reading it before deciding rather than after.
