# Choosing a credential as a PMO lead

*X / Threads. Nine posts, each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply, not in
post 1.*

---

**1/**
A portfolio cost performance index of 0.955 that hides one project at 0.667.

Here is the arithmetic, and the credential question a PMO lead should be asking instead of the
usual one.

**2/**
12 projects. Combined earned value £84.0m. Combined actual cost £88.0m.

Portfolio CPI = 84.0 ÷ 88.0 = 0.955.

Slightly behind. Unremarkable. It passes the board meeting.

**3/**
Take one project out. It has earned £6.0m against £9.0m of cost.

That project: 6.0 ÷ 9.0 = 0.667.

The other eleven: 78.0 ÷ 79.0 = 0.987.

**4/**
Eleven projects running to plan. One in real difficulty. The portfolio number describes neither.

Ask about the £4.0m gap and "the portfolio is at 0.955" is not an answer to the question.

**5/**
Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the
business environment.

That is the PMO's method and governance work, examined properly and by design.

**6/**
Decomposing an aggregate in the room is a different competence: which components move it, how
each was measured, what earning rule produced them.

No template produces that.

**7/**
The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
the control of delivery — the layer under your portfolio numbers.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and assurance.

**8/**
PML-AI is aimed at this seat: 16 domains, 63 knowledge areas, Body of Knowledge proportioned
40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed AI.

Syllabus proportions, not exam weightings.

**9/**
If your PMO is method and governance, take a project management certification. If your grading
matrix names one, take that one.

Disclosure: credentialfinder.org is run by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards
PML-AI. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it.
