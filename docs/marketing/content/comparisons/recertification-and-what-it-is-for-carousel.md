# Ten slides on recertification and what it measures

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the arithmetic and is the one people screenshot. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

A certificate records a decision made on a date. Recertification is what keeps that date meaning something.
Same contract, same month, two measurement rules, £5.04m apart — the arithmetic is on slide 3.

Ten slides on what recertification measures, what CPD is worth when nobody checks it, and the five things to read in a policy before you sit.

---

**Slide 1 — What it is for**
Recertification answers one question: does this credential still describe the person holding it? Not a subscription, not a loyalty scheme, not a second examination for its own sake.

**Slide 2 — What moves under a career**
Reporting requirements, contract forms, measurement conventions and the governance expected around automated tooling. A credential awarded years ago is silent on whether the holder moved with them.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Fixed price **£42.0m**. Costs to date **£22.8m**. Expected total cost **£38.0m**.
Cost-to-cost progress = 22.8 ÷ 38.0 = **60%**
Revenue = 0.60 × 42.0 = **£25.2m**
By units completed, 48% done: 0.48 × 42.0 = **£20.16m**
Same data, **£5.04m** apart. Which rule applies is a question with a moving answer.

**Slide 4 — Two mechanisms, two things measured**
Recorded CPD measures continued contact with the subject across a cycle. Re-examination measures current command of the syllabus at a point. A scheme can use both, for different reasons, and should say which does what.

**Slide 5 — How each one fails**
CPD fails when hours are counted and relevance is never checked — that measures attendance. Re-examination fails when the paper never changes — that measures memory. Both designs work when the body does the work behind them.

**Slide 6 — What good CPD looks like**
Mapped, not counted: name the knowledge area it touched. Evidenced, not asserted: keep the procedure you rewrote. Sampled, not audited to death. And include the parts of the subject you avoid, because more of what you already do well changes nothing.

**Slide 7 — The five checks, part one**
Is the validity period published before you apply? What counts as CPD, and against which parts of the syllabus? Who verifies, and what happens when a sampled claim does not hold up?

**Slide 8 — The five checks, part two**
What happens if you lapse — renewal with conditions, a full re-sit, or permanent loss? And does public verification show status on the date it is checked: current, expired, suspended, withdrawn?

**Slide 9 — The check employers actually use**
A register that shows everyone who ever passed is a list. A verification that shows status as at today is a control. Check which one you are joining before you sit, not when a client asks.

**Slide 10 — Where PCI stands**
The CPD policy, the recertification route and the verification page are public before anyone applies.
