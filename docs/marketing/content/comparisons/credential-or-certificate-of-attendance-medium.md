# Credential or certificate of attendance: how to tell

*Publish only after the credentialfinder.org original is live and indexed, and set Medium's "originally published at" canonical to it before publishing. Medium links are nofollow, so this republish earns qualified readers and model citations rather than link equity.*

**Subtitle:** Four questions that separate a certificate of attendance from an examined credential, all of which you can ask before you pay.

A credential is awarded on an assessment marked by somebody other than the people who prepared you, against a standard published in advance. A certificate of attendance records that you were in the room. Both are worth having. Only one of them tells a stranger what you can do.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. This is not an independent comparison service, and you should read it knowing that.

## The four questions, side by side

| Question | Attendance or completion certificate | Examined certification |
|---|---|---|
| What does the document record? | Hours attended, or a course finished | Performance against a standard published beforehand |
| Who decides the outcome? | The organisation you paid to teach you | An assessment the preparation provider does not mark |
| Can you fail? | Often not at all | Yes, with a published resit and appeal route |
| What can you read before paying? | A course outline and a duration | The full syllabus, the assessment format, the appeal route |
| What happens after? | Nothing; the document is final | A renewal cycle with a stated requirement |
| What does it prove to a stranger? | That the course ran and you were on it | That you met a standard on a day you did not control |

Neither column is the better product. They answer different questions, and a buyer who wants the first and pays for the second has wasted money in a way nobody will refund.

## Can you fail it?

Ask that in those words, before anything else. If the honest answer is that everybody who attends receives the certificate, you are buying training. Training is worth buying, and a good course is often the fastest route into an unfamiliar subject.

What you are not buying is evidence. A document that cannot record a failure cannot record a pass either, because there is nothing on the other side of the line for the pass to mean.

Then ask the follow-up: who marks it. Where the organisation taking the fee also awards the result, the two roles sit in the same hands, which is the arrangement that [holding preparation apart from the certification decision](https://credentialfinder.org/separation-of-training-and-certification) is designed to avoid.

## What an examinable item looks like

A course does not have to test anything, so it can cover a forecast by defining one. An examination has to produce evidence, so it has to make you choose between defensible answers and stand behind the choice.

Take a package with a budget at completion of **£12.0m**. At the cut-off, earned value is **£5.20m** and actual cost is **£5.85m**.

- Cost performance index = 5.20 ÷ 5.85 = **0.889**
- Forecast if performance to date holds = 12.0 ÷ 0.889 = **£13.50m**
- Forecast if the overrun was a one-off = 5.85 + (12.0 − 5.20) = **£12.65m**

Two forecasts from one dataset, **£0.85m** apart, both arithmetically correct.

A recall question asks which formula is which. An examinable item gives you the site conditions — a wet season ending, a subcontractor replaced, a rate agreed for the remaining work — and asks which number you would sign and why. [The four accepted forecasting methods and what each one assumes](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/four-eac-formulas) are the working set here, and choosing between them is the competence, not reciting them.

That is the practical difference. One document says you heard about forecasting. The other says somebody watched you do it and marked it against a written standard.

## Where a certificate of attendance is exactly the right document

Plenty of the time, it is what you actually need.

If your professional body requires a number of development hours a year, an attendance record is the evidence it asks for, and an examination is a slower and dearer way to produce it. If your employer has bought a new release of a scheduling tool and needs the team on it by March, tool training with a completion certificate is the correct purchase. If a site requires an induction record, only the induction record will do.

Product certifications for scheduling software sit across this line. Some are examined and some record attendance, and the two are sold in similar language, so check which one you are buying before you assume. What they examine is the operation of a specific product, which is a real skill with its own market, and separate from the discipline the product serves.

None of that is a criticism of a course. It is a purchase-order question: are you buying hours, or evidence?

## What a renewal cycle tells you

A credential that never expires is making a claim about the future that nobody can support: that what you demonstrated once still holds a decade later, in a discipline whose tooling has changed underneath it.

A renewal requirement is the body admitting that competence decays and asking you to show it has not. It is also a recurring obligation you take on, so read what it demands before you sit anything, because it is a question in its own right and separate from what the examination covers.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a certificate of attendance worthless?**
No. It is the correct evidence for development hours, for tool training and for induction records, and it is usually cheaper and faster than an examination. It is only worthless when it is sold as proof of competence, which is a claim it was never built to carry.

**How do I find out who marks the assessment?**
Ask the body directly, in writing, and ask two things: who sets the pass standard, and whether anybody involved in preparing candidates takes part in the decision on those candidates. A body that has separated the two will answer quickly, because the separation is something it designed on purpose.

**Does a digital badge make something a credential?**
No. A badge is a display format. It can carry a verified examination result or a course completion, and it looks identical either way. Follow the badge to the issuing record and read what the record actually says the holder did.

**My employer calls the course certificate a certification. Does the difference matter?**
Internally, often not. Externally, yes, because a hiring manager who checks will find a course completion where the CV implied an examination, and that conversation is worse than never having claimed it. Describe it as what it is.

**Does an unaccredited certification count for anything?**
It counts for what its published syllabus and its assessment can demonstrate, no more and no less. Accreditation is third-party assurance of the process rather than proof of content, so its absence is a reason to read the syllabus and the pass-standard policy closely, not a reason to stop reading.
