# Recertification says current, not that you once were

*LinkedIn text post — 1,884 characters. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post body:**

A certificate records a decision made on a date. Recertification is what keeps that date meaning something.

It is not a subscription and not a second exam for its own sake. It answers one question: does this credential still describe the person holding it?

Why the question is real. Same contract, same month, two measurement rules.

Fixed price £42.0m. Costs to date £22.8m. Expected total cost £38.0m.
Cost-to-cost progress = 22.8 ÷ 38.0 = 60%
Revenue recognised = 0.60 × 42.0 = £25.2m

Measured instead by units completed, the job is 48% done.
0.48 × 42.0 = £20.16m

Same data, £5.04m apart. Which rule applies and what the tooling does with it are things that move under a career, and a certificate issued years ago says nothing about whether the holder moved too.

Two mechanisms, each good at something different. Recorded CPD keeps a holder in contact with the subject, and is easy to game if nothing checks relevance. Re-examination measures the subject rather than attendance, at a real cost in candidate time.

What to check in any scheme, before you sit rather than three years later:

Is the validity period published up front?
What counts as CPD, and against which parts of the syllabus?
Does anybody verify the claims, or sample them?
What happens if you lapse — renewal, or start again?
Does public verification show status on the date it is checked, including expired and withdrawn?

That last one is the one employers use. A register of everyone who ever passed is a list, not a verification.

If your employer's framework recognises a particular scheme's cycle, that cycle is the one that matters to you. Work to it.
