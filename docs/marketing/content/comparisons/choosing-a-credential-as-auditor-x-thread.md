# Auditing a project: test the number, not just the control

*X / Threads. Eight posts, each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply, not in
post 1.*

---

**1/**
Same evidence, two write-ups. One goes in the appendix. One goes in the executive summary.

Here is the arithmetic that separates them on a capital project engagement.

**2/**
You sample 30 progress claims. Four have no measurement record.

4 ÷ 30 = 13.3%

£6.0m of progress claimed in the period → roughly £0.80m claimed without evidence behind it.

**3/**
Write-up A: "records were missing."

Accurate. Closed with a revised template. Nobody's forecast changes.

**4/**
Write-up B follows the chain.

Unsupported progress inflates earned value.
Inflated earned value raises the index.
A higher index lowers the forecast out-turn.

**5/**
And where progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, a lower forecast raises the percentage complete — so it raises the revenue recognised in the period.

£0.80m of unevidenced progress has reached a reported number.

**6/**
Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. That transfers to projects intact and is a full profession in its own right.

The technical subject underneath — earning rules, cut-off, accruals, the forecast — does not come with it.

**7/**
When your existing credential is the answer:

You audit information systems → it is the right one.
Your firm's progression names a credential → take that one.
You need accreditation today → go where it exists.
