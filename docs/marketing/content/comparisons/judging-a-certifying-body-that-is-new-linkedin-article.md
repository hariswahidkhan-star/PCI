# How to judge a new certifying body before you pay

How to judge a new certifying body comes down to three things it can show you today, because a track record is not one of them. What it publishes before you pay. Which decisions it has separated from each other. And what it openly refuses to claim. The third is the most informative and the one nobody looks at.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here — and which is itself new. Read this as an argument you are entitled to turn back on its author.

## Seven checks you can run this week

| Check | What good looks like | How to test it today |
|---|---|---|
| The syllabus | Published in full, free, before payment | Download it and read the exclusions first |
| The pass standard | A written policy on how the standard is set | Ask; a considered answer comes back quickly |
| Separation | Preparation does not decide the result | Ask who marks, and who sets the standard |
| The appeal route | Published grounds, timescale and decision-maker | Read it before you need it |
| The record | A third party can confirm a result without you | Try to verify somebody's credential yourself |
| Renewal | A stated cycle with a stated requirement | Read what it will ask of you in three years |
| The refusals | Nothing claimed that has not happened | Look for the claims that are absent |

Six of those seven are things you can check in an afternoon. The seventh takes longer, because absence is harder to notice than presence.

## Read for what is not being claimed

An organisation at founding stage has no pass rates worth quoting, no holder population, no employer survey and no salary data. Any of those figures published in year one has been manufactured, because there is nothing to compute them from.

So read for absence. A new body that publishes no pass rate, claims no recognition it has not been granted, and states plainly that it is not accredited has made itself checkable. One arriving with a full set of impressive numbers has told you either that it is older than it looks, or that the numbers are decoration.

The same test applies to the adjectives. "Globally recognised" is a claim about other people's behaviour that nobody can evidence in year one. "Endorsed by industry", with no named and verifiable endorsement attached, is the same claim wearing a coat.

## Three artefacts, free to inspect, that settle most of it

The syllabus, read for scope, depth and exclusions rather than for topic count.

The pass-standard policy, which tells you whether the line is set against a description of competence or against the cohort that happened to sit with you. That difference decides whether your result depends on strangers.

And the appeal route, which tells you what happens on the worst day. A body that has thought carefully about the day a candidate disputes a result has thought about the thing that will eventually test it.

If those three are published and coherent, a new organisation has given you enough to form a judgement. If they are not, its age is not the problem you have.

## Test a syllabus line yourself, with a pen

Here is a way to check a syllabus that takes ten minutes and does not require you to trust anybody.

Pick a line from it and try to write an examinable question from that line. If the line cannot generate a question with a defensible answer, it is a heading rather than a standard.

Try it on this. A contract is priced at **£30.0m**. The client has instructed variations worth **£2.4m** which are not yet enforceable, and the job is **45%** complete on the agreed measure.

- The delivery report measures progress against £32.4m: 0.45 × 32.4 = **£14.58m** of value earned
- The accounts recognise against £30.0m: 0.45 × 30.0 = **£13.50m** of revenue
- The two systems are **£1.08m** apart, and neither of them is wrong

A syllabus line that says "variations" cannot examine that. A line that says "determine the consideration that may be recognised where a variation is instructed but not yet enforceable, and reconcile it to the reported progress position" can, and you can tell which kind of document you are holding in the time it takes to read one page.

That reconciliation is also the join where money goes missing on real jobs, which is why it is worth using as the test.

## What a new body cannot borrow

Longevity demonstrates one thing that publishing cannot substitute for: the organisation was still there when the hard questions arrived. An established body has heard appeals, revised a syllabus under pressure and survived a bad cohort. A new one has done none of that and cannot claim to have.

That is a real difference and it belongs in your decision. It is not a moral point about anybody. It is a fact about elapsed time, and the only cure for it is elapsed time.

What a new organisation can do is design for those questions in advance and publish the design, so that you can judge the design rather than the marketing. That is the whole of the case, and it is worth exactly what the published documents are worth.

PCI is new. That is the first thing to weigh, not the last.

The syllabus is published in full and free. The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI), the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) and the PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) run to 13, 16 and 16 domains and 61, 61 and 63 knowledge areas, with 92 sector case studies across the three volumes and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements underneath them. ## When a new body is the wrong choice

A comparison the publisher always wins is an advertisement, so here is the other side, stated plainly.

If you need an accredited credential — because a client's pre-qualification requires one, because a framework agreement lists it, because your employer's policy is written that way, or because the role is regulated — take the accredited route. No argument about scope beats a procurement condition, and you will lose a year finding that out.

If your next promotion depends on a certification named in an internal grading matrix, take the named one and do not overthink it.

And if you want assessed chartered standing, the chartered routes in surveying and engineering assess professional competence over years of supervised practice. No examination is a substitute for that assessment, and nobody should tell you otherwise.

A new body is worth considering when the scope of its examination matches decisions you are already accountable for, and when you can read enough of it in advance to make that judgement yourself. Those cases are real, and they are narrower than any founder would like.

## FAQ

**How old does a certifying body need to be before it is worth holding?**
There is no threshold, because age is a proxy for things you can inspect directly: a published standard, a separated decision, a working appeal route and a verifiable record. Inspect those, then treat elapsed time as one input among several rather than the whole judgement.

**Is an unaccredited credential a red flag?**
Not on its own. Accreditation is third-party assurance that a certification process meets a standard, which is valuable and takes years to obtain. Its absence tells you the assurance has not happened yet, so you do the reading yourself. A body that pretends otherwise is the red flag.

**What happens if the new body disappears?**
Ask what survives it: a published syllabus, an examination record held so a third party can confirm it, and any successor or escrow arrangement the body has committed to. Ask before you enrol. The answer, or the silence, is informative either way.

**Can I ask a certifying body these questions directly?**
Yes, and how it answers is part of the evidence. Written questions, written answers, kept on file. [The ten questions worth putting to any certifying body](https://credentialfinder.org/questions-to-ask-a-certifying-body) are the full set, and a body that treats them as hostile has answered the important one.

**Does a big syllabus mean a serious body?**
No. It means a lot has been written down. Seriousness shows in the boundary statements, the pass-standard policy and the appeal route — short documents, and much harder to write honestly than a long topic list.
