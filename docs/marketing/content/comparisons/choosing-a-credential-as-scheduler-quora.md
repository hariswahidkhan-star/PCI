# Choosing a credential as a scheduler: a scope answer

*Quora answer. Question it answers: "I plan and schedule on capital projects and I am being moved
into a project controls role. Which certification is actually worth taking?" Answer completely,
then one link at the end.*

---

Choosing a credential as a scheduler comes down to one question: are you staying on the
programme, or are you taking on the measurement rule as well? Those are different subjects and
different categories of credential examine them.

Disclosure first, because it should change how you weigh this: I work for Project Controls
Institute Global, which operates credentialfinder.org and awards the credential I mention at the
end. The answer is written to be useful either way.

**What actually changes when you cross over?** Three items start carrying your name.

| The new item | What it decides |
|---|---|
| The earning rule for each work type | What counts as earned this period |
| The estimate at completion | The forecast the business plans against |
| The cut-off calendar | Whether progress and cost describe the same days |

Here is the first one in numbers, from a concrete package. 12,000 m³ at £180 per m³, so budget is
£2.16m. At cut-off, 7,200 m³ are placed and nobody disputes it.

Under a units-complete rule, earned value is 7,200 × 180 = £1.296m. Under a milestone rule that
credits a pour only on sign-off, with five of ten pours signed, earned value is half of £2.16m =
£1.08m. Actual cost is £1.35m either way.

That gives CPI of 1.296 ÷ 1.35 = 0.96, or 1.08 ÷ 1.35 = 0.80. Forecast at completion of 2.16 ÷
0.96 = £2.25m, or 2.16 ÷ 0.80 = £2.70m.

£450,000 of forecast difference, from a measurement rule. The programme is correct in both
versions.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis
in real depth, and they are built for that. The measurement rule, the forecast handover and the
cut-off calendar sit past that boundary, which is a design decision rather than a shortcoming.
Product certifications for scheduling software examine operating a tool, which is a genuine skill
and a different claim from competence in the discipline.

So the honest routing. If the programme is the work you want, take a cost and scheduling
credential and go deep — schedule analysis at a senior level is scarce and it pays. If a client's
prequalification or an internal grading matrix names a certification, take the named one, because
a requirement does not lose to a scope argument. If you need an accredited credential now, go to a
body that already holds accreditation.

If you are the person who will sign the earning rule and defend the forecast, look for a syllabus
that examines both sides of the handover. PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) covers 13
domains and 61 knowledge areas across the control cycle and the reporting boundary.

One caveat that matters. PCI is a new, independent certifying body: it is not accredited by ANAB,
UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and does not claim to be, though the
scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. The Body of Knowledge is published
free and in full, so judge it on the syllabus rather than on my say-so.
