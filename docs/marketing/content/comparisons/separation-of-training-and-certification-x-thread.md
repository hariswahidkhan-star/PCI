# Five checks on any scheme that sells you the course

*X / Threads thread — 7 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. No link in the thread; the link sits in the first reply. X counts a URL as 23 characters, so the live count on the reply runs lower than the text suggests.*

**Post 1/7 — the hook** (240 characters)
The organisation that sold you the course should not be the organisation that decides whether you passed.

That is the whole case for separating preparation from certification.

It is not an argument against training. It is about who signs.

**Post 2/7 — checks one and two** (228 characters)
Two things you can check in five minutes on any scheme's site.

1. Can you read the entire syllabus before you pay? Domains, knowledge areas, depth expected.

2. Is a named course required before you are allowed to sit the exam?

**Post 3/7 — checks three to five** (267 characters)
3. Does the body imply its own preparation improves your odds? Usually phrased as "official", rarely as odds.

4. Who reviews a decision you dispute, and did they make it?

5. Is there a published impartiality policy naming the commercial conflict, not just fairness?

**Post 4/7 — why an examinable item matters** (248 characters)
Independence is a slogan until the questions have defensible answers.

BAC £24.0m. EV £4.8m. AC £5.6m.
CPI = 4.8 ÷ 5.6 = 0.857
EAC = 24.0 ÷ 0.857 = £28.0m

£28.0m or it is wrong. Nobody gets talked round on that because of who taught the candidate.

**Post 5/7 — what the separation costs** (256 characters)
Publishing the whole syllabus means competitors read it on day one and can build courses against it without asking.

Not selling preparation gives up the larger half of the market.

And it means nobody can promise you a route through, because none is sold.

**Post 6/7 — when this is not your deciding factor** (249 characters)
If your employer mandates a specific certification, the mandate decides and none of this applies to you this year.

If a tender or a regulator requires an accredited scheme today, take the accredited route. Syllabus transparency is not a substitute.
