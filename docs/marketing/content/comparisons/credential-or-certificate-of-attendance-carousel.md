# Ten slides on evidence versus hours in the room

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the arithmetic and is the one people screenshot. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

"Can you fail it?" is the whole difference between a credential and a certificate of attendance.
Two forecasts from one dataset, £0.71m apart, both correct — the arithmetic is on slide 3.

Ten slides on what each document can prove, who should be marking it, and the case for buying the course instead.

---

**Slide 1 — The question to ask first**
Can you fail it? Ask it in those words, of a person rather than a brochure. If everybody who attends receives the document, you are buying training rather than evidence.

**Slide 2 — Why that question settles it**
A document that cannot record a failure cannot record a pass either. There is nothing on the other side of the line for the pass to mean. Nothing else about the product changes that.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Budget at completion **£8.0m**. At cut-off, earned value **£3.10m**, actual cost **£3.55m**.
Cost performance index = 3.10 ÷ 3.55 = **0.873**
If that performance holds: 8.0 ÷ 0.873 = **£9.16m**
If the overrun is behind you: 3.55 + (8.0 − 3.10) = **£8.45m**
Two forecasts, one dataset, **£0.71m** apart, both arithmetically correct.

**Slide 4 — What an examinable item does with that**
A course can cover forecasting by defining it. An assessment has to hand you the site conditions — a wet season ending, a subcontractor replaced, a rate agreed for the rest — and make you say which number you would sign, and why.

**Slide 5 — Who decides the outcome**
The organisation you paid to teach you, or an assessment your preparation provider does not mark? Where both sit in one organisation, ask how they are kept apart, in writing, before you apply.

**Slide 6 — What you can read before paying**
An outline and a duration is a course. The full syllabus, the assessment format and the appeal route is a certification. Ask for all three and see what arrives.

**Slide 7 — What happens afterwards**
Nothing, or a renewal cycle with a stated requirement? An attendance record carries no renewal because an event does not expire. A competence claim does, which is why a good one has a date on it.

**Slide 8 — Where the course is the right buy**
Fifteen development hours by December. A new release of a scheduling tool your team needs by March. A site induction. In all three the attendance record is the correct evidence, and an examination is the slower, dearer route to it.

**Slide 9 — Product certifications sit across the line**
Some are examined, some record attendance, and they are sold in similar language. Check which one you are buying. What they examine is the operation of a specific product — a real skill, with its own market, and a different thing from the discipline the product serves.

**Slide 10 — Where PCI stands**
The Body of Knowledge, the assessment format and the appeal route are published before anyone applies. PCI publishes no pass rates and no holder numbers, because neither has been earned. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI.
