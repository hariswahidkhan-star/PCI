# Ten questions to ask any certifying body before you pay

**Post body (1,895 characters):**

Ten questions to ask a certifying body before you pay. They work on any category, and the answers tell you more than the brochure does.

1. Can I read the whole syllabus before I pay anything?
2. Who writes the examination, and are they separate from whoever sells the preparation?
3. How was the pass mark set, and by whom?
4. What will the certificate say I was examined on?
5. Can a third party verify it without going through me?
6. Who hears an appeal, and are they independent of the people who marked me?
7. What does recertification actually check?
8. Are you accredited, by whom, to what standard, and if not, do you say so plainly?
9. What will you not claim?
10. If you closed tomorrow, where would the record of my examination live?

Question 4 is the one candidates skip, and it decides the most. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. Each examines its own subject thoroughly and is built to. Neither sets out to examine the join.

The join has arithmetic in it. Fixed price £60.0m, budget £48.0m, cost to date £21.6m. Progress 21.6 ÷ 48.0 = 45.00%, revenue 0.45 × 60.0 = £27.00m. Move the forecast to £51.7m and progress is 41.78%, revenue £25.07m. A £3.7m move in a delivery forecast moved the reported number by £1.93m.

Question 9 separates bodies. Anyone promising a salary, a job or an employer's preference is selling what it does not control.

Mine, answered: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.

And if your employer mandates a certification, question 8 settles it before the other nine. Take the mandated one.
