# Where PCL-AI sits against cost and scheduling credentials

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis
and the economics of the asset life cycle. If that is the work in front of you, the category was
built for your problem and it goes deeper into method than any broad credential does. PCI AI
Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) examines a different span: the control cycle and the point
where its numbers become reported financial numbers.

Disclosure before anything else. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute
Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. This article is
written by the body that awards them, and you should discount it accordingly and go and read the
syllabus yourself.

## The month-end that starts the argument

I have watched this happen on four projects and heard about it on twenty more.

A pipeline package. 1,000 metres, £1,000 a metre, budget at completion £1.0m.

The planner cuts the schedule on the 25th, because that is when the site returns the progress
sheets and the report has to be drafted. 800 metres are in the ground. Earned value is £800k.

Finance cuts the ledger on the 30th, because that is the accounting period. £950k of cost has
been booked, which includes the five days of work that went in after the planner stopped
counting.

Put those two numbers side by side, as the monthly pack does:

- CPI = 800 ÷ 950 = **0.84**
- EAC = 1,000 ÷ 0.84 = **£1.19m**
- Forecast overrun: **£190k**

Recut both to the 30th. 950 metres in the ground, £950k earned against £950k spent.

- CPI = **1.00**
- EAC = **£1.0m**

Nothing on that site changed. The overrun was a calendar difference between two functions who
each did their own job correctly.

Both categories are examined properly inside their own boundary. The table is about where the

## Why no credential in the cost and scheduling category answers this

Because it is not their subject, and it was never meant to be.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine how an estimate is built and classed, how a network
behaves under change, how progress is measured against a rule of credit, how a forecast is
produced and defended. That is a deep technical body of material and the people who hold these
credentials can do things a general manager cannot.

The reconciliation above is not a scheduling question or a cost engineering question. It is a
question about the interface between a control cycle and a reporting cycle: which date governs,
what an accrual is doing, which number the auditor will test. Revenue recognition sits outside
the scope of a scheduling credential, as it does for every scheduling credential, and that is a
fact about design rather than a failing.

## The second twist, which is worse

Change one rule and the same package moves again.

Say 40 valves sit in the package at £10,000 each, £400k in total. Twenty have been delivered to
site. None are installed.

- Under a 0/100 earning rule, earned value from those valves is **£0**.
- Under a 50/50 rule, with half credited on delivery, earned value is 20 × £5,000 = **£100k**.

Same site, same steel, same day. £100k of earned value, purely from which rule the project
adopted. That moves CPI, it moves the forecast, and where progress drives what gets recognised,
it moves the reported position too.

Somebody owns that rule. Somebody has to document it, apply it consistently, and defend it when
it is tested. Which credential examines that person's job?

## What PCL-AI examines

PCL-AI runs to 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. It examines the control cycle — measurement,
progress, forecasting — and then keeps going, into how those outputs turn into reported numbers,
and into the governed use of AI inside that cycle.

The Body of Knowledge sets its proportions at 40 finance and reporting, 40 project management
and 20 governed AI. Those are the Body of Knowledge's proportions and not an examination
weighting; the syllabus is settled and the exam blueprint is an open decision, and we would
rather say that than imply a precision we do not have.

Behind the three volumes sit 92 sector case studies, and 113 PCI Standards carrying 532 process
requirements.

## Where this credential is the wrong answer

A comparison the awarding body always wins is an advertisement. So, plainly:

**If your work is estimating, take the cost and scheduling route.** If you build and defend
estimates for a living, a credential examining estimating methods and classes in depth will
serve you better than one that spends 13 domains on the whole cycle. Depth in your own subject
beats breadth across somebody else's.

**If you need accreditation today, go where it exists.** PCI is a new, independent certifying
body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and
does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. If a
framework, a client's approved list or a visa route requires an accredited credential, an
established accredited scheme is the right answer and we are not it.

**If your employer mandates a specific certification, take it.** That is a business requirement,
not a scope decision, and no argument about syllabus design changes it.

**If your problem is the handover, look at PCL-AI.** The £190k in the first example and the
£100k in the second both live at the join. If you are the person who has to explain them, you
want a syllabus that examines both sides.

## How to judge us, given we cannot lean on a name

We publish no pass rate, no salary uplift, no holder count and no accreditation claim, because
we have no honest way to produce any of them yet. A body that tells you what it cannot claim is
telling you the truth about what it can.

What we can do is show the work. All three Bodies of Knowledge are published in full, free,
before anyone pays anything. Read the PCL-AI domain list. Find the three knowledge areas closest
to work you have personally done. Check whether the treatment matches what you know to be true
on a real project. If it does not, do not buy it, and say so publicly.

That test is the one we would apply to any new certifying body, including this one.

## Three questions to put to any syllabus, ours included

Does it say who owns the earning rule, and what happens when the rule changes mid-project?

Does it say which date governs when the schedule and the ledger are cut on different days, and
what evidence supports the reconciliation between them?

Does it say what a human must decide when a model produces the number, and what has to be
recorded about that decision?

A syllabus that answers all three is examining the work as it actually happens. One that answers
none of them may still be an excellent credential in its own subject — it is simply examining a
different subject from the one those three questions come from.

## What to do next

Do the arithmetic on your own last month-end. Take your CPI and check which date each side of it
was cut on. If the two dates differ by even three days, work out what your forecast at
completion would have been had they matched. Most people are surprised by the size of the
number.

Then read the syllabus of whichever credential you are considering, ours included, and ask
whether it examines the thing you just found.
