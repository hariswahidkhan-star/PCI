# Choosing a credential as a controls manager

*LinkedIn post. No link in the body — it goes in the first comment.*

---

Three forecasts. One dataset. £3.7m between them, and all three defensible.

Budget at completion £40.0m. Actual cost £18.0m. Earned value £16.4m.

CPI = 16.4 ÷ 18.0 = 0.911. SPI 0.95.

Overrun was a one-off: 18.0 + (40.0 − 16.4) = £41.6m
Performance to date holds: 40.0 ÷ 0.911 = £43.9m
Both pressures persist: 18.0 + 23.6 ÷ (0.911 × 0.95) = £45.3m

Nothing separates them but an assumption a person chooses and defends. Selecting among them is the job.

Where progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, that forecast is the denominator. So the £3.7m spread is also a spread in recognised revenue.

Present £43.9m, and a finance director asks what happens at £45.3m. That question is not about arithmetic. It is about whether the difference has already been provided for.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Both examine their own subject thoroughly and are built to.

The cut-off calendar is examined systematically in no single category, and it is the row that produces the arguments nobody settles at month-end.

That handover is what PCL-AI examines: 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas.
