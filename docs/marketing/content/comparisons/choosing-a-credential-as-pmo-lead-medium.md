# The credential for a PMO lead, decided on what you sign

*Republished from credentialfinder.org. Set the Medium canonical to
https://credentialfinder.org/choosing-a-credential-as-pmo-lead before publishing.*

---

The right credential for a PMO lead turns on one question: do you defend the portfolio numbers,
or do you assemble and present them? If your PMO sets method, runs assurance and reports the
portfolio, a project management certification examines most of that and is the natural fit. If
you personally answer for the aggregate in front of a board, you need to have been examined on
how those numbers are built.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI,
PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. Read the rest knowing who wrote it.

## What a PMO lead signs, and which category examines it

| What you put your name to | Which category examines it | What it leaves to somebody else |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio status and the aggregate forecast | Project management certifications | How each component number was built |
| Method, templates and reporting standards | Project management certifications | Whether the method survives a hard month |
| Stage gates and assurance reviews | Audit credentials, in part | The technical adequacy of what is reviewed |
| Resource and capacity planning | Project management certifications | The schedule logic underneath the demand |
| Contingency and change governance | The established project controls certifications | The accounting treatment of the drawdown |
| Rules for how AI may be used in reporting | Rarely examined anywhere yet | Almost everything, in most schemes |

Rows one, five and six are the ones that expose a PMO lead. They are also the rows most often
assumed rather than examined.

## Why an aggregate hides the question you will be asked

A portfolio of **12** projects reports a combined earned value of **£84.0m** against a combined
actual cost of **£88.0m**.

Portfolio cost performance index = 84.0 ÷ 88.0 = **0.955**. Slightly behind, unremarkable, and it
will pass a board meeting.

Now take out one project, which alone has earned **£6.0m** against **£9.0m** of cost.

That project = 6.0 ÷ 9.0 = **0.667**.
The other eleven = (84.0 − 6.0) ÷ (88.0 − 9.0) = 78.0 ÷ 79.0 = **0.987**.

Eleven projects are running to plan, one is in serious difficulty, and the portfolio number
describes neither. If the board asks a single question about that £4.0m gap, "the portfolio is at
0.955" is not an answer to it.

Defending an aggregate means being able to decompose it in the room: which components move it,
how each was measured, and what earning rule produced them. That is a controls competence rather
than a governance one, and no template produces it.

## What each category examines, in its own terms

Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the
business environment. For method, governance and stakeholder work, that is the matching subject
and it is examined properly.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
the control of delivery. That is the layer under the portfolio numbers.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. If your PMO's
centre of gravity is assurance rather than delivery support, that is a closer match than either
of the other two.

Each is doing its own job, thoroughly, and each publishes what it sets out to examine. The
difficulty for a PMO lead is that the role has drifted across all three in most organisations
while the credential usually has not.

## The row nobody was examined on

Status reporting is being drafted by models in a great many PMOs now, and the rules for it are
being written by whoever happens to be in the room.

That is a governance question with a technical core: what a model may draft, what it may never
decide, what evidence has to survive so a reviewer can trace a number back to its source, and
what the reviewer is accountable for having checked.

If your PMO issues that policy, you are signing something almost no credential currently
examines. Writing it as a control rather than a ban is the part most drafts get wrong.

## What PML-AI examines

PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) is the one aimed at this seat: 16 domains and 63
knowledge areas, with the Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project
management and 20% governed AI. Those proportions describe the syllabus and not an examination
weighting.

The 20% covering governed AI maps to the last row of the table above. The 40% covering finance
and reporting is what lets you decompose an aggregate in front of a finance director rather than
promising to come back with an answer.

Across the three volumes there are 92 sector case studies, and 113 mandatory PCI Standards
carrying 532 process requirements sit underneath them. A suite of 15,613 machine calculation
checks, all passing, covers PFL-AI and PML-AI; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

## The accreditation statement

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other

## How to decide

**Take a project management certification** if your PMO is a method and governance function.
Standards, templates, gate discipline, reporting cadence and benefits tracking are what that
category examines, and it is read instantly by every executive you deal with.

**Take whatever your grading matrix names**, if it names one. That decision was made before you
arrived, and a fit argument does not beat it.

**Look at the audit category** if your PMO is genuinely an assurance function rather than a
delivery support one. It is a serious match, not a curiosity.

**Look at PML-AI** if you are the person who answers for the aggregate, and the questions that
catch you are about how a number was built rather than how it was presented.

And if you run a PMO in a domain with little cost exposure — internal change, product, service
transition — the portfolio arithmetic above is not your problem, and buying a syllabus to cover
it is buying reading rather than evidence.

## The test to run this week

Take the last three board packs your PMO issued. For each number on the front page, write down
who built it, what method produced it, and whether you could reconstruct it under questioning
without help.

Rows you can reconstruct are covered by whatever you already hold. Rows you cannot are the scope
gap, and they are usually the forecast, the contingency position and anything with a percentage
complete in it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a PMO lead need technical controls knowledge, or just governance?**
It depends who defends the numbers. If a controls manager sits in the room and answers the
technical questions, governance depth is enough. If you are alone in front of a board with a
portfolio forecast, you need to be able to decompose it, and that is technical.

**Should a PMO lead take an audit credential?**
If your PMO is an assurance function, that category examines governance, risk, control and the
assurance process, and it is a genuine fit. If your PMO supports delivery, it is a wide subject
to study for a narrow part of your week.

**Two of my projects use different earning rules. Does that matter at portfolio level?**
Yes, and it is the commonest reason an aggregate misleads. Different rules produce different
earned value from the same physical progress, so the sum describes a mixture rather than a
position. Standardise the rules before you standardise the template.

**How should a PMO handle AI in reporting when no credential examines it?**
Write the policy anyway, and write it as a control rather than a ban: what may be drafted, what
may never be decided, what evidence must survive, and who signs. Then review it quarterly,
because the tools move faster than the policy does.

**Is a second credential duplication?**
It is if it examines the same subject at the same depth, which is a common and expensive mistake.
It is not if it examines the rows your signature has moved onto. Count the decisions you sign
that your current credential never examined, and let that count decide.
