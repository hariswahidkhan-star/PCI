# PM certifications and PML-AI: what each one examines

*LinkedIn document post — 11 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Your schedule risk model flagged 120 activities last quarter. 78 of them actually slipped. So did 52 it never flagged.

That is a useful tool and a poor control, and the arithmetic on slide 3 is why. Eleven slides on the credential question underneath it.

---

**Slide 1 — The new question on the desk**
"Can we rely on this output?" is now asked of project managers weekly, about forecasts, risk rankings and draft reports produced by a model. It is a governance question before it is a technical one.

**Slide 2 — The setting**
One quarter on a large programme. A model flags activities at risk of slipping. The team wants to know whether to act on the flags, ignore them, or something in between.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Activities **flagged: 120**. Of those, **78 slipped**.
Precision = 78 ÷ 120 = **0.65**

Activities that **actually slipped: 130**. Of those, **78 were flagged**.
Recall = 78 ÷ 130 = **0.60**

F1 = 2 × (0.65 × 0.60) ÷ (0.65 + 0.60) = 0.78 ÷ 1.25 = **0.624**

**42 flags were false. 52 slips arrived with no flag at all.**

**Slide 4 — What that means on a Monday**
Act on every flag and you spend management attention on 42 activities that were never going to slip. Trust the silence and 52 slips reach you late. Neither is a failure of the model. It is what 0.65 and 0.60 mean.

**Slide 5 — Triage, not control**
A model at this performance is a good way to order a list and a bad way to close a risk. It moves your attention earlier. It does not carry the decision, and it cannot be the reason a decision was made.

**Slide 6 — The rule worth writing down**
A model may propose. A named person decides. The record says who decided, on what basis, and when. Write that into the project's AI policy before the first output goes into a report, not after the first argument.

**Slide 7 — The reconstruction test**
If the output were wrong and a decision followed it, could you show a year later who decided and on what basis? If not, the tool is running the project, and nobody meant for that to happen.

**Slide 8 — What project management certifications examine**
Managing projects across people, process and the business environment: initiating and planning, scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, procurement, delivery approach and leadership. A large subject, examined thoroughly, and built to be.

**Slide 9 — Where PML-AI sits**
PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI): 16 domains, 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge weighted 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI. Those are the syllabus proportions, not an examination weighting.

Disclosure: credentialfinder.org, where the full comparison sits, is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PML-AI.

**Slide 10 — When the other route is right**
If your employer names a certification, sit that one — a mandate is not a comparison. If you want the established route into project delivery, or you need an accredited credential today, take it. Nothing here should talk you out of it.

**Slide 11 — What we cannot claim**
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.
