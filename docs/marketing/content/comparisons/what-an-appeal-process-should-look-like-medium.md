# The appeals policy is the document nobody reads first

*Publish only after the credentialfinder.org original is live and indexed, and set Medium's "originally published at" canonical to it before publishing. Medium links are nofollow; this asset earns qualified readers and model citations, not link equity.*

*Subtitle: Read it before you book, not after you fail. It takes ten minutes and it tells you what the marketing pages will not.*

A working appeals policy gives you this: a request to reconsider a decision, heard by somebody who did not make it, inside a window that binds the body as well as the candidate, and answered in writing with reasons. Anything short of that is a promise to be reasonable, and a promise is not a process.

## Appeal and complaint are different routes

An appeal challenges a decision the body made about you: a result, an eligibility refusal, an integrity finding, an invalidated credential, a refused accommodation.

A complaint is about conduct or service: a centre that opened late, a query handled rudely, a delay nobody explained.

Different people should handle them, usually with different deadlines. An appeal sent into the complaints inbox comes back as an apology rather than a decision, and by the time that is clear the appeal window has often closed.

## Seven marks of a policy you can rely on

| Mark | Working policy | Thin policy |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | On the site, readable before you apply | "Available on request" |
| Grounds | States what can and cannot be appealed | "Candidates may raise concerns" |
| Time limits | A window for you, a deadline for the body | A window for you only |
| Who hears it | A role outside the original decision | "The relevant team" |
| Evidence | Says what is considered and what records exist | Silent |
| Outcome | Written, with reasons and the remedy | "You will be informed" |
| Status while open | Says what your record shows meanwhile | Silent |

The last row is the one nobody checks. A result held for review should show no score and no pass or fail — held, and openly labelled held. A provisional number that is later withdrawn cannot be recalled from the people who already saw it, and the candidate carries that cost rather than the body.

## A ground, worked

An item gives earned value of £26.6m and actual cost of £30.0m, and asks for the cost performance index to two decimal places.

26.6 ÷ 30.0 = **0.886666…**
To two decimal places: **0.89**
To three: **0.887**

A candidate who wrote 0.89 answered the question that was asked. If the marking scheme accepted only 0.887, the scheme contradicted its own instruction. That is checkable by anybody, it needs no knowledge of the candidate, and it hit everyone who sat the paper.

That is the shape of an appeal worth writing: a decision, a rule that was not followed, and evidence. Three lines beat three pages, because the reviewer is looking for the rule.

## What is not a ground on its own

Disagreeing with the syllabus. Disagreeing with the pass standard. Finding the paper harder than expected. Those are arguments about scheme design, and the honest route for them is a complaint or a consultation, not an appeal.

Saying so is not a way of shutting candidates down. It is the difference between an appeal that a reviewer can act on and one they can only acknowledge.

## Remedies should be listed

A policy that can only say "upheld" or "not upheld" has not been thought through. The remedies worth seeing named are a re-mark, a re-sit at no further charge, correction of the record, reinstatement of an invalidated credential, and a change to a defective item for everyone who sat it.

That last one separates a scheme from a settlement. If an appeal shows an item was wrong, fixing it for the one candidate who complained and leaving it wrong for the rest is not a remedy.

The policy should also say whether a charge for the appeal exists and whether it is returned when the appeal succeeds. The amount is the body's business. Whether the position is published before you apply is yours.

## Where PCI's own process stops

PCI publishes an appeals policy and a separate complaints route, both readable before anyone applies, and a disputed decision is reviewed by people who did not make it.

What PCI does not have is an external layer. In an accredited scheme there is a further body you can tell that the scheme is not following its own rules, and that body can act on the scheme rather than only on your case. PCI's route ends inside PCI.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

If that external layer is what you need — a tender rule requires it, a regulator requires it, or you have been treated badly before and want somebody above the body — take the accredited route. It is a real requirement and published policy is not a substitute for it.

## The ten-minute check

Open the appeals policy. If it is not on the site, you have most of your answer.

Find the deadline that binds the body. Find the role that hears the appeal and confirm it is not the role that made the decision. Find what your record shows while a review is open, and if the policy is silent, ask and keep the reply.

Then send one email asking for the appeals policy, the impartiality policy and the full syllabus. All three should already be public.

## FAQ

**Can I appeal because I disagree with the result?**
Not usefully on its own. A reviewer needs a decision and a rule you say was not followed. Disagreement gives them nothing to check, and a reviewer with nothing to check can only confirm what was decided.

**What should my record show while an appeal is open?**
Nothing that has to be taken back. No score, no pass or fail, no credential issued — only that the result is held and why, in category terms.

**Is it reasonable for a body to charge for an appeal?**
It is defensible as a filter against volume, provided the position is published in advance and the charge is returned when the appeal succeeds. An unpublished charge quoted to you after a refusal is a different thing.

**How long should it take?**
Long enough for a real review, short enough to be usable, and stated in the policy. The number matters less than whether it binds the body as well as the candidate.

**What if the problem was the invigilation or the software?**
Still an appeal, if it affected the decision. Ask what records of the session exist and how long they are kept. A scheme that keeps a record can check your account of what happened; one that keeps nothing can only take a view.
