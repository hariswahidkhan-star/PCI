# Judge a new certifying body on what it will not claim

*LinkedIn text post — 1,855 characters. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post body:**

A new certifying body cannot show you a track record. It does not have one.

So how to judge a new certifying body comes down to three things it can show you today: what it publishes before you pay, which decisions it has separated, and what it openly refuses to claim.

That third one does most of the work, and almost nobody looks at it.

An organisation in year one has no pass rate worth quoting, no holder population, no employer survey and no salary data. There is nothing to compute them from. So any of those numbers, published early, has been manufactured.

Read for absence instead. No pass rate. No recognition claimed that has not been granted. A plain statement that it is not accredited. That is a body that has made itself checkable.

Then test the syllabus with a pen. Pick a line and try to write an examinable question from it.

Try it on this. Contract £30.0m. Instructed variations £2.4m, not yet enforceable. Job 45% complete.

Delivery measures against £32.4m: 0.45 × 32.4 = £14.58m earned
The accounts recognise against £30.0m: 0.45 × 30.0 = £13.50m

£1.08m apart, and neither system is wrong.

A syllabus line saying "variations" cannot examine that. One that asks you to determine what may be recognised and reconcile it to the reported progress position can. You can tell which document you are holding in a page.

And the honest other side. If a client's pre-qualification, a framework agreement or your employer's policy requires an accredited credential, take the accredited route. A scope argument does not beat a procurement condition, and you will lose a year finding out.
