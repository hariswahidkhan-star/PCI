# Chartered standing and examined credentials: the split

Chartered routes in surveying and engineering assess whether you have practised competently: structured experience over years, an evidence portfolio, a professional review by peers against a competence framework, and a code of conduct. An examined credential asks a narrower question — can you do the work on a published syllabus, on the day. If you need chartered standing, take the chartered route; nothing examined substitutes for it.

That includes the credentials described below, and saying so first is the only way the rest of this is worth reading.

This article is written for LinkedIn and is original to it. It draws on work published on credentialfinder.org, where [the routes are compared at category level](https://credentialfinder.org/best-certification-for-planning-engineers). credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials named here.

## What do chartered routes assess?

Practised competence, evidenced and peer-judged. The candidate accumulates responsible experience, maps it to a published competence framework, assembles evidence that the work was theirs, writes it up, and defends it in front of assessors who have done the same work.

The instrument is a review rather than a paper. It looks backwards at what you have actually done, and its central question is whether a professional peer would trust your judgement on similar work tomorrow. [What assessors look for in an evidence portfolio](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/professional-fellowship-application) is consistent enough across schemes to prepare for.

It also carries obligations beyond the assessment: a code of conduct, a duty to practise within your competence, and continuing development. Those obligations are part of what chartered standing means, not an add-on to it.

## What does an examined credential assess?

Applied knowledge against a published scope, marked against published objectives, on a date. The syllabus is declared in advance, the same paper reaches every candidate, and the result says what was examined.

That is a genuinely different instrument. It cannot tell you whether somebody has run a difficult job well for six years. What it can do is state precisely which body of material a holder has been tested on, which a portfolio review does not set out to do.

The PCI credentials are of this second kind: PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI, examining 13 / 16 / 16 domains and 61 / 61 / 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge whose proportions are 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI.

## Two instruments, side by side

| Axis | Chartered routes in surveying and engineering | Examined credentials such as the PCI suite |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Have you practised competently? | Can you do the work on this syllabus? |
| Instrument | Evidence portfolio and professional review by peers | Written examination against published objectives |
| Direction of view | Backwards, over years of responsible work | Forwards, from a declared scope to a date |
| Basis of judgement | A competence framework, applied by assessors | Domains and knowledge areas, published in advance |
| What it confers | Chartered standing, asked for by name in some markets and on some contracts | A statement of the scope a holder has been examined on |
| Where the subject sits | The discipline's own competence framework | The join between the money and the delivery |
| Time shape | Logged experience first, assessment after | Study, then examination, at a time you choose |

The rows do not compete. Some employers want both, and holding one says nothing against the other.

## A month with three right answers

The reason the join needs somebody's syllabus is easiest to see in arithmetic. Every figure here can be checked in your head.

Fixed price contract, £24.0m. Total expected cost £20.0m, so expected margin £4.0m. Costs incurred to date: £11.6m.

On a cost-to-cost measure, 11.6 ÷ 20.0 = 58% complete, so revenue is 0.58 × 24.0 = **£13.92m** and margin recognised to date is 0.58 × 4.0 = £2.32m.

The certified valuation for the same month is **£12.60m**. Retention at 5% withholds £0.63m, so the payable amount is **£11.97m**.

Three numbers for one month's work: £13.92m recognised, £12.60m certified, £11.97m collectible. The £1.32m between the first two is a contract asset — measured into revenue but not yet an unconditional right to payment. The £1.95m between the first and the third is the same gap plus retention that will sit on the balance sheet long after the work is finished.

Each number is right in its own frame. Measurement produces the valuation. Progress towards satisfying the obligation produces the revenue. The payment terms produce the cash.

## Why the join belongs to somebody's syllabus

A chartered route in a measurement discipline examines measurement competence to a professional standard, and is built to. A finance qualification examines when revenue may be recognised and what a provision must satisfy, and is built to. Both do their own subject thoroughly.

The three-number month above sits between them. Somebody has to be able to say, in one sentence a director can act on, why the recognised figure exceeds the certified one and why the cash trails both. That sentence is not hard once you know both rulebooks. It is very hard when you know one.

The PCI suite is aimed at that sentence, which is why its Body of Knowledge carries finance and reporting alongside delivery rather than after it.

## When a chartered route is the answer and this is not

Take the chartered route, not an examined credential, when a client, a contract or an employer's grade structure asks for chartered standing; when you want peer-assessed professional status in your own discipline; when you are close enough to submission that your hours are better spent on the portfolio; or when you need a credential from an accredited body today.

PCI does not offer chartered standing, does not assess a portfolio in place of an examination, and does not claim either. Those are different things to be, and pretending otherwise would be the fastest way to deserve nobody's trust.

## What PCI does not claim

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

No pass rates, no salary outcomes, no holder numbers — none of them published, because none of them can yet be evidenced. What is published, in full and before payment: the Body of Knowledge, 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes.

## How to sequence them

If chartered standing is your objective, that is the work. Log the experience, build the evidence, sit the review, and treat everything else as optional until it is done.

If you are already chartered, or on a route where you never will be, the useful question is which body of material you have never been formally tested on. For most people who own project numbers, the honest answer is the finance side of their own reports.

## FAQ

**Does an examined credential count towards chartered standing?**
Treat it as no unless the assessing body says otherwise in writing. Chartered routes are built on evidenced experience judged against a competence framework, and study is normally supporting material rather than credit. Ask the body that will assess you, and take the written answer over anyone's marketing.

**Is chartered standing better than a certification?**
They answer different questions, so "better" depends entirely on what you are being asked for. Chartered standing says peers judged your practice competent. An examination says you were tested on a declared scope. A hiring manager who wants one and is offered the other usually notices.

**I am chartered already. What would an examined credential add?**
A tested scope you can point at, in an area your competence framework may not cover. If your reports feed revenue, forecast and provision decisions, being examined on that crossing point is the gap most commonly left open — and the three-number month above is what it looks like when it stays open.

**Is PCI accredited?**
No. PCI is new and independent, is not accredited by any ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and states it on every page rather than in small print. The Body of Knowledge is published in full so the syllabus can be judged before anyone pays, which is the only honest basis on which a new body can ask to be taken seriously.
