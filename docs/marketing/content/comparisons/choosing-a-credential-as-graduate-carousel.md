# Your first credential, and what it can prove

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

A graduate who can produce five numbers in front of somebody is worth more than a graduate holding a certificate and unable to do it.
Here are the five numbers, and then the honest version of the certification question — including the case for waiting.

Twelve slides.

---

**Slide 1 — The test that gets you hired**
Nobody hires a graduate for a certificate. They hire the person who can produce the numbers in the room, explain what they mean physically, and say what would change the answer. Everything else on these slides follows from that.

**Slide 2 — The figures**
A package with a budget at completion of £4.0m. At cut-off, the plan says you should be 50% complete and site says you are 40%. Cost to date, accruals included, is £1.8m.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Planned value = 50% × 4.0 = **£2.0m**
Earned value = 40% × 4.0 = **£1.6m**
SPI = 1.6 ÷ 2.0 = **0.80**
CPI = 1.6 ÷ 1.8 = **0.889**
EAC = 4.0 × 1.8 ÷ 1.6 = **£4.5m**, so **£500k over budget**

**Five lines. Learn to produce them without hesitating.**

**Slide 4 — What the two indices actually say**
SPI 0.80 says the work is behind the plan. CPI 0.889 says each pound is buying less work than budgeted. They are different failures with different fixes, and confusing them is the commonest mistake in a first project review.

**Slide 5 — Why most credentials assume experience**
They examine judgement formed on real projects: which earning rule fits this work, when a forecast should be rebuilt rather than escalated, what a variance means physically. That is deliberate design, and it is why one sat too early proves less than the same one sat later.

**Slide 6 — Cost and scheduling credentials**
Examine estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis and the economics of the asset life cycle. The right category if your desk is narrow and technical: you are given planning work or estimating work, and you want method rather than habit.

**Slide 7 — The established project controls certifications**
Examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. The right category once you have spent time inside a working control cycle and your seat covers more than one of its parts.

**Slide 8 — Project management certifications**
Examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment. Built for somebody who has held responsibility for running projects, which is rarely a graduate's first job.

**Slide 9 — Chartered routes in surveying and engineering**
Examine assessed professional competence and chartered standing over several years of logged practice. Multi-year by design. If you are on one of these routes, that is your first credential and it has already started.

**Slide 10 — When an early credential does earn its money**
Your employer funds it and protects the study time. Or your role is narrow and technical and one category examines exactly it. Or you are moving sideways from a related discipline and need to show a deliberate choice. Outside those three, the money often buys more as training.

**Slide 11 — Where PCI stands**
PCL-AI covers the control cycle and how its numbers become reported numbers: 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, published in full before anybody pays. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**Slide 12 — The order, and when to wait**
Take what your employer funds. Match the category to the desk you actually have. If a framework or a visa route needs accreditation now, go where accreditation already exists. And consider waiting six months, because real month-ends make every one of these examinations easier. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the three credentials named here.
