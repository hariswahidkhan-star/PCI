# Finance qualification or project finance credential?

Take the accountancy route if your work is the entity's numbers: preparing or signing accounts, financial control across a business, or a career in practice. Take a project finance credential if your work is one contract at a time and the argument you keep having is about a forecast. They examine different subjects, and both examine their own thoroughly.

## What each kind of qualification is examined on

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial analysis and control. They are deep on when revenue may be taken, what a provision must satisfy, and how an entity's reporting holds together.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. They are deep on how work is measured, what a critical path does, and how a forecast is built from the bottom up.

Neither category sets out to examine the join between them, and that is a design decision rather than an oversight. A syllabus that tried to cover both without dropping something would serve its own candidates badly.

## The join, with the arithmetic

A control account is surveyed on the 26th because that is when the site can be walked. The ledger closes on the 30th.

Earned value measured to the 26th: **£2.86m**. Actual cost to the 30th: **£3.10m**.
Cost performance index = 2.86 ÷ 3.10 = **0.923**.

Four days of production at £48,000 a day is £192,000 of work done and not yet earned. Accrue it and earned value becomes £3.052m.
Cost performance index = 3.052 ÷ 3.10 = **0.985**.

Same crew, same month, same money. The reported performance problem was a calendar. On a large control account that difference walks straight into the forecast, and from the forecast into recognised revenue.

Nobody in that chain did anything wrong. The gap is that the delivery side is rarely examined on cut-off, and the finance side is rarely examined on how a quantity got measured.

## How to choose, honestly

Choose the accountancy route if you need a recognised professional qualification for a finance role, if you might work in practice, or if you need an accredited credential now. That last point matters and is worth stating plainly.

Choose a project finance credential if you own a cost report that has to survive a month-end and a question from someone senior, and if the arguments you lose are about accruals, measured progress and forecasts turning into margin.
