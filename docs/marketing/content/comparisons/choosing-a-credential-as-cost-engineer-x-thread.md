# Choosing a credential as a cost engineer

*X / Threads. Nine posts, each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply, not in
post 1.*

---

**1/**
A £3.0m exposure held by £0.6m of contingency, and nobody in the room said it out loud.

Here is the arithmetic, and the credential question a cost engineer should take from it.

**2/**
Sanction estimate £20.0m, stated accuracy ±15%.

Band = 20.0 × 0.15 = ±£3.0m

So the honest statement is a range: £17.0m to £23.0m.

**3/**
The contingency approved alongside it: £0.6m. Three per cent.

Both numbers are defensible on their own. Side by side they describe a risk appetite nobody
chose.

**4/**
Now escalation. £8.0m of spend remains, indexation at 4%.

8.0 × 0.04 = £0.32m

More than half the contingency, before a single risk event. Draw it down and £0.28m is left.

**5/**
None of that is an estimating error.

It is what happens when a range, a contingency and an escalation allowance are set in three
conversations and never put on one page.

**6/**
Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis and
the economics of an asset's life.

That is your subject, examined in depth and by design.

**7/**
The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
control of delivery.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement and disclosure — where
your out-turn estimate lands.

**8/**
The reconciliation of those three numbers belongs to none of them in particular. That is why it
is so rarely challenged.

PCL-AI examines the controls frame around it: 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas.

**9/**
If you estimate for a living, take the depth of a cost-focused credential. If your employer names
one, take that one.

credentialfinder.org is run by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PCL-AI. PCI is new
and not accredited — read the syllabus.
