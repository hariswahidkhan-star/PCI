# The month reported margin moved and nobody was wrong

**Post body (1,889 characters):**

A delivery team revised an estimate for delivery reasons. Reported margin fell by £0.57m in the same month. Nobody did anything wrong.

Here is the arithmetic, and you can check every line of it.

Contract price £26.0m. Costs incurred to date £9.0m. Estimated total cost at the last approved review £24.0m.

Progress = 9.0 ÷ 24.0 = 37.5%
Revenue = 0.375 × 26.0 = £9.75m
Margin = 9.75 − 9.0 = £0.75m

Now the estimate moves to £25.5m: a subcontractor replaced, a rate agreed, three weeks of weather.

Progress = 9.0 ÷ 25.5 = 35.3%
Revenue = 0.353 × 26.0 = £9.18m
Margin = 9.18 − 9.0 = £0.18m

Same month, same site, same work in the ground. A number owned by delivery moved, and a reported result followed it through a mechanism neither profession was examined on.

That is the finance and delivery overlap, and four artefacts live in it.

The cut-off calendar: progress measured to one date, costs booked to another, and an index that moves for reasons that are not performance.

The accrual list: work done and not yet invoiced. Left out, the cost report flatters itself and every forecast built on it inherits the flattery.

The earning rule: a 50/50 rule and a 0/100 rule on the same activity produce different earned value in the same month.

The change register: instructed and not priced, priced and not approved, approved and not in the budget. Three states, three treatments.

None of the four is difficult. All four need somebody who knows what the other side does with them.

Delivery credentials examine the forecast thoroughly. Accountancy qualifications examine the reported number thoroughly. The join belongs to both subjects and sits at the centre of neither, which is exactly why it goes unowned.
