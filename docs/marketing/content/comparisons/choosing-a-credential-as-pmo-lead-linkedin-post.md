# The portfolio number that answers no question at all

*LinkedIn text post — 1,889 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

A portfolio of 12 projects reports a combined earned value of £84.0m against a combined actual cost of £88.0m.

Cost performance index = 84.0 ÷ 88.0 = **0.955**. Unremarkable, and it will pass a board meeting.

Now take out one project, which alone has earned £6.0m against £9.0m of cost.

That project: 6.0 ÷ 9.0 = **0.667**.
The other eleven: 78.0 ÷ 79.0 = **0.987**.

Eleven projects are running to plan. One is in serious difficulty. **The portfolio number describes neither of them.**

If a board asks one question about that £4.0m gap, "the portfolio is at 0.955" is not an answer to it.

Defending an aggregate means decomposing it in the room: which components move it, how each was measured, what earning rule produced them. That is a controls competence, not a governance one.

Which is why the credential question for a PMO lead turns on what you personally defend.

Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment. For method, governance, assurance cadence and stakeholder work, that is the matching subject and it is examined properly.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery — the layer sitting underneath the portfolio numbers.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process — closer if your PMO's centre of gravity is assurance.

If your PMO is a method and governance function, take the project management route. If your grading matrix names a certification, take the named one. The exception is narrow: you are alone in front of a board with the portfolio forecast.
