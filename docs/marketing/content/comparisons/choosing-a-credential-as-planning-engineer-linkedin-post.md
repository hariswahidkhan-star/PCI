# Float looks free until somebody prices it in pounds

**Post body (1,883 characters):**

Float looks free until somebody prices it.

A package carries 20 days of total float to a sectional completion date. Time-related preliminaries on that package run at £8,500 a day.

Priced: 20 × 8,500 = £170,000.

Say 12 of those days go to instructed change and 8 to a subcontractor's own performance. The entitlement worth pursuing is 12 × 8,500 = £102,000, and whether any of it is recoverable depends on records made in the month each event happened, not the analysis you run at the end.

The other £68,000 flows into outturn cost. That moves the estimate at completion, which moves percentage complete, which moves reported margin.

Now the credential question. Does the examination you are weighing follow that number past the programme, or stop where the programme stops?

Both answers are legitimate. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis, in depth. The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. Each publishes a boundary and examines its own subject thoroughly.

So sort the choice by what you sign, not your job title.

If your signature stops at the programme — logic, baseline, status, delay analysis — a credential examining schedule development, maintenance and control examines your job. Take that one.

If your dates and progress drive a cost report, an entitlement position or a forecast somebody books, half the consequences of your work land outside a scheduling examination. That is the argument for reading across the boundary.

And if a client's prequalification names a certification, take the named one. A requirement is not a comparison.
