# Read the Body of Knowledge before you pay for the exam

*LinkedIn text post — 1,722 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Same package. Same survey date. A cost performance index of 0.88 or 1.06, depending on a rule somebody chose before the job started.

Work it and see.

Budget £6.00m across 400 units. At cut-off, 150 units complete and 60 started but unfinished. Actual cost £2.55m.

Units-complete rule: 150 ÷ 400 = 37.5%. Earned value £2.25m. Index = 2.25 ÷ 2.55 = 0.88.

Half credit at start: (150 + 30) ÷ 400 = 45%. Earned value £2.70m. Index = 2.70 ÷ 2.55 = 1.06.

£0.45m apart, one package, one month, one survey.

That is the reason to read a syllabus before paying for the examination behind it. A Body of Knowledge worth the money makes you choose the rule and defend the choice. One that lists "progress measurement" as a topic leaves you to find out in the month it matters.

Ninety minutes, seven passes, in this order:

Read the last domain first, because introductions are written to persuade and the back of the book is where the real scope boundary shows.
Count the verbs, not the pages.
Find one worked calculation that reaches an answer.
Find the exclusions, which are the hardest paragraph to write and the most useful to read.
Trace one topic you already know and see whether it deepens or repeats.
Read what the body holds itself to, not only what it expects of you.
Read one case study to its decision.

If most verbs are "understand" and "appreciate", it is a reading list, and the standard is being set somewhere you cannot see.
