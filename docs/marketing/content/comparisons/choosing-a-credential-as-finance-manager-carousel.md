# Finance on capital projects: eleven slides upstream

*LinkedIn document carousel. 11 slides at 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the
arithmetic, because slide 3 is the one people screenshot.*

---

### Slide 1 — cover
**£2.4m of working capital tied up.**
Not one line of it was a finance decision.
*Alt text: title slide reading "£2.4m of working capital tied up, and not one line of it was a
finance decision".*

### Slide 2 — the terms
Monthly certified value: **£1.5m**

Client pays **60 days** after certification.
Subcontractors are paid at **30 days**.
*Alt text: slide setting out monthly certified value of £1.5m with 60-day client terms and 30-day
subcontract terms.*

### Slide 3 — the arithmetic
Payment gap = one month of spend ≈ **£1.5m** outstanding

Retention 5% of £18.0m of certifications = **£0.9m**

1.5 + 0.9 = **£2.4m**, before a single dispute
*Alt text: calculation slide showing £1.5m of certification lag plus £0.9m of retention giving
£2.4m of working capital tied up.*

### Slide 4 — the delivery variable
Certification slips two weeks over an unresolved measurement disagreement.

Exposure grows by half a month of spend — about **£0.75m**.

Nobody spent more. A valuation meeting ran late.
*Alt text: slide showing a two-week certification delay adding roughly £0.75m of working capital
exposure.*

### Slide 5 — the same problem in the P&L
Contract £30m, expected costs £24m, costs to date £12m.

50% complete → revenue **£15m**, margin **£3m**

Forecast costs rise to £26m → 12 ÷ 26 = 46.2% → revenue **£13.85m**, margin **£1.85m**
*Alt text: calculation slide showing a forecast cost movement from £24m to £26m reducing
recognised revenue and margin by £1.15m each.*

### Slide 6 — where each number is born
You sign reported revenue. Delivery owns the percentage complete.

You sign the provision. A project team owns the forecast.

You sign the cash profile. A certification calendar owns the dates.
*Alt text: slide pairing what a finance lead signs with where each underlying number is built.*

### Slide 7 — what the finance category examines
Recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control.

Examined at a depth no project credential attempts — and the exposure above sits upstream of all
four.
*Alt text: slide listing what accountancy and finance qualifications set out to examine.*

### Slide 8 — the five questions
When was the forecast last rebuilt, not escalated?
Which earning rule produced this progress?
What is instructed but not yet priced?
Which valuations rest on an open negotiation?
What does next quarter's certification calendar look like?
*Alt text: slide listing five factual questions a finance lead can ask a project team.*

### Slide 9 — where PFL-AI sits
**16 domains. 61 knowledge areas.**

The reporting boundary, and the delivery inputs that arrive at it.
*Alt text: slide summarising PFL-AI as 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas covering the reporting
boundary.*

### Slide 10 — when this is not for you
Your path runs through the finance function → finish that qualification.

Projects are short and low-variance → little upstream risk to fix.

A lender or a regulated role names a qualification → take that one.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body, not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC
17024 accreditation body, and not claiming to be.
*Alt text: slide listing three situations in which an existing finance qualification is the right
answer, with PCI's accreditation position stated plainly.*
