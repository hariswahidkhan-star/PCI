# Cost and scheduling credentials: where PCL-AI sits

*X / Threads. Eight posts, each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply, not in
post 1.*

---

**1/**
A £190k forecast overrun that was five days of calendar, not five days of work.

Here is the arithmetic, and what it says about which kind of credential examines which side of
the line.

**2/**
1,000 m of pipe at £1,000/m. Budget £1.0m.

The planner cuts the schedule on the 25th: 800 m in the ground, so earned value is £800k.

Finance cuts the ledger on the 30th: £950k booked.

**3/**
CPI = 800 ÷ 950 = 0.84
EAC = 1,000 ÷ 0.84 = £1.19m

A £190k overrun goes into the monthly pack, and three people spend a week explaining it.

**4/**
Recut both sides to the 30th.

950 m in the ground. £950k earned against £950k spent. CPI 1.00. EAC £1.0m.

Nothing on that site changed. A date changed.

**5/**
Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule
analysis, thoroughly and by design.

The reporting handover sits outside their scope, as it does for every credential in that
category.

**6/**
That handover is what PCL-AI examines: 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas, covering the control
cycle and how its numbers become reported numbers.

**7/**
If your work is estimating, take the depth of the cost and scheduling route.

If you need an accredited credential today, go where accreditation already exists.

Neither answer is PCL-AI. We would rather say so than sell you the wrong thing.
