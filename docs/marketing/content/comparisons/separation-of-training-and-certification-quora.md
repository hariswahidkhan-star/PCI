# Worth checking who signs the decision, not who taught

It can be worth something, and the thing to check is not whether the same company does both. It is whether the two decisions are kept apart, and whether the body will show you in writing how.

A trainer's job is to get you ready. An examiner's job is to record accurately whether you are. Those jobs pull in different directions, and when one organisation holds both, the second is the one under commercial pressure. Some bodies handle that well and say exactly how. Others do not, and the way to tell is the same in both cases.

Five checks, all of which you can run before paying anything:

**Can you read the whole syllabus first?** Domains, knowledge areas, the depth expected in each — not a marketing outline. If the syllabus only exists inside the course, the course is the syllabus and you cannot judge either one independently.

**Is a named course required before you may sit?** In a regulated trade where supervised practice is part of the competence, a mandated course makes sense. It is harder to defend when the body selling the mandated course is the body awarding the credential.

**Does the body suggest its own preparation improves your odds?** Rarely written that bluntly. It usually appears as "official", or as one route described as the complete preparation.

**Who reviews a decision you dispute?** A useful answer names a person or a panel that did not make the original decision. Anything vaguer is a promise to be reasonable, not a process.

**Is there a published impartiality policy?** It should name the conflicts the body actually has, the commercial ones included, and say what it does about each.

Why this is workable at all in a technical subject: the questions have defensible answers. Budget at completion £24.0m, earned value £4.8m, actual cost £5.6m. CPI is 4.8 ÷ 5.6 = 0.857, and the estimate at completion on the assumption that performance continues is 24.0 ÷ 0.857 = £28.0m. That is the answer, or the answer is wrong. Nobody is talked round on it by who taught the candidate. Where a subject cannot be assessed that way, the identity of the marker starts to matter a great deal more.

One thing that overrides all of the above: if your employer or your client mandates a specific certification, the mandate decides. Take that route, and keep these checks for the next decision.
