# Scope or prestige: the two axes, worked in numbers

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Recognition gets you the interview. Scope decides the year that follows.
Twelve slides on running both axes yourself, including the arithmetic that shows what a scope gap actually costs.

Disclosure before you read: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.

---

**Slide 1 — Two axes, one budget**

Scope decides whether the examination covers the decisions you sign. Recognition decides how fast a stranger reads your CV.

Where both point at the same credential there is no dilemma. Where they conflict, you need to know which one is actually blocking you.

**Slide 2 — What each axis buys**

| | Scope | Recognition |
|---|---|---|
| Question it settles | Does this cover what I sign? | Will a stranger know what it is? |
| Gives a reader | Detail of what was assessed | A category, placed in seconds |
| Gives you | Whether you can do the work | Whether you get the conversation |
| Cannot do | Get you past a keyword filter | Say what you were examined on |
| How to check it | Read the syllabus | Read live job advertisements |

**Slide 3 — What a scope gap costs**

Progress measured to the **25th**. Costs booked to the **30th**.

Earned value **£4.20m**. Actual cost **£4.65m**, of which **£0.35m** is spend on work that will be earned next month.

As reported: CPI = 4.20 ÷ 4.65 = **0.903**
Aligned to one date: CPI = 4.20 ÷ 4.30 = **0.977**

On a **£12.0m** budget at completion: **£13.29m** forecast against **£12.28m**.

**£1.01m** of difference, produced by a calendar rather than by performance.

**Slide 4 — Nobody in that example did anything wrong**

The delivery cut-off and the finance cut-off are different dates. That is normal, and both are defensible on their own terms.

Closing the gap needs somebody who has been examined on both sides of it. That is a scope question, and recognition has no answer to it.

**Slide 5 — Measuring recognition, part one**

Open ten current advertisements for the exact role you want, in the exact market you want it in.

Write down every credential each advertisement names. Note whether it appears under essential or under desirable.

**Slide 6 — Measuring recognition, part two**

Now do the same for ten profiles of people already holding that job.

Two counts, one hour. The result is specific to your market and current, which beats any general claim about employer preference — including a claim a certifying body makes about itself.

**Slide 7 — The scope audit, done properly**

List the decisions you personally sign: a forecast at completion, a valuation, a percentage complete, an accrual, a change position, a schedule submission.

Open the syllabus. Mark each one **examined**, **assumed** or **excluded**.

Count the rows, not the pages. Four hundred topics you will never touch is not scope.

**Slide 8 — What each category is examined on**

Cost and scheduling credentials: estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis.

Accountancy and finance qualifications: recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial control.

Project management certifications: managing projects across people, process and the business environment.

Chartered routes in surveying and engineering: assessed professional competence over years of supervised practice.

Each examines its own subject thoroughly and is built to.

**Slide 9 — When recognition should win**

A client's prequalification names a certification. Your employer's grading matrix lists one against the band you want. You are moving country and your CV gets thirty seconds from somebody with no context.

In all three, recognition is the axis that is blocking you. Take the recognised route. A scope argument you never get to make is worth nothing.

**Slide 10 — When scope should win**

You already get interviews and the difficulty is the work: a forecast you cannot defend, a month-end where numbers move for reasons nobody can explain, a client asking why the cost report and the accounts disagree.

Also when your role has moved and your credential has not. Plenty of people are certified against the job they held four years ago.

**Slide 11 — Where this body sits, on both axes**

On scope: each PCI Body of Knowledge is proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed AI. PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI run to 13, 16 and 16 domains and 61, 61 and 63 knowledge areas, published in full before anybody pays. Those proportions describe the Body of Knowledge, not any examination's weighting.

On recognition: PCI is new, and new means what it says. Run the twenty-advertisement count and record what you find, including if what you find is nothing.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**Slide 12 — When neither axis is the question**

If your employer mandates a certification, that settles it before either axis matters.

If you want assessed chartered standing, that is a professional route with years of supervised practice behind it, and no examination substitutes for it.

And if you cannot tell which category you should be shopping in, that is the prior question. Answer it first, then run both axes.
