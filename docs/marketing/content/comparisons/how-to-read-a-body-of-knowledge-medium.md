# How to read a Body of Knowledge before you pay for it

A published Body of Knowledge is a prospectus, and you can check it before any money moves. Seven passes in a fixed order take about ninety minutes and tell you whether the credential examines the work you actually do: read the last domain first, count the verbs, find one calculation, find the exclusions, trace one topic you know, read the scheme documents, read one case study to its decision.

The order matters. Front-to-back reading is how a document persuades you; this order is how you assess it.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. The method below is written so that it works when you turn it on PCI, which is the only kind of method worth publishing from this side of the table.

## The seven passes at a glance

| Pass | Minutes | What you are looking for | What a weak answer looks like |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 Last domain first | 10 | The real edge of the scope | A bin for leftovers |
| 2 Count the verbs | 15 | Observable actions | Understand, appreciate, be familiar with |
| 3 Find one number | 10 | A worked calculation reaching an answer | Formulas in a box, never used |
| 4 Find the exclusions | 10 | A stated boundary | No exclusions anywhere |
| 5 Trace one topic you know | 20 | Depth that increases | The same words at two levels |
| 6 Scheme documents | 15 | What the body holds itself to | Described but not published |
| 7 One case study | 10 | A decision and a number | A narrative with a moral |

## Pass one: read the last domain first

An introduction is written to be persuasive. The last domain is where a scheme puts whatever would not fit elsewhere, so it shows you the true edge of the syllabus faster than the contents page.

Ask two questions of it. Does it hold something the body plainly cares about? And has the scope promised on the cover survived to the back of the document?

## Pass two: count the verbs, not the pages

Highlight the verb attached to every knowledge area. That is the whole pass, and it needs no subject knowledge, because you are reading grammar rather than content.

*Understand*, *appreciate* and *be familiar with* describe an internal state, and nobody can mark an internal state consistently. *Calculate*, *reconcile*, *classify*, *select and justify* describe something that leaves evidence on a page.

A document dominated by the first kind is a reading list. The examination standard behind it is being set somewhere a candidate cannot see.

## Pass three: find one number

Any credential touching money should show a worked calculation in its own materials — figures going in, an answer coming out, an answer that could be wrong.

If the document produces no numbers, the examination will not either. That can still be the right purchase for a role built on vocabulary and process. You should simply know which one you are buying.

## Pass four: find the exclusions

The hardest paragraph to write in any syllabus is the one saying what it does not cover, and it is the most useful one to read.

Every category has a boundary, drawn deliberately. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. Product certifications for scheduling software examine operating a specific tool rather than the discipline behind it. Each examines its own subject thoroughly, because that is what it was designed to do.

What a candidate needs is to know where the line runs, since the work itself does not respect it.

## Pass five: trace one topic you already know

This is the pass that decides the purchase. Choose a subject you have run under pressure, find everywhere the document touches it, and read whether the treatment deepens or simply repeats.

Take progress measurement, and see why the wording of one line matters.

A work package carries a budget of £6.00m across 400 units. At cut-off, 150 units are complete and 60 more are started but unfinished. Actual cost is £2.55m.

Under a units-complete rule, only finished units earn. 150 ÷ 400 = 37.5%, so earned value is 0.375 × 6.00 = **£2.25m**, and the cost performance index is 2.25 ÷ 2.55 = **0.88**.

Under a rule crediting half the value at start, the same survey gives (150 + 0.5 × 60) ÷ 400 = 45%, so earned value is 0.45 × 6.00 = **£2.70m**, and the index is 2.70 ÷ 2.55 = **1.06**.

**£0.45m of difference from one field survey, and an index that lands either side of 1.00.** No one miscounted. The rule was fixed before the work started.

A Body of Knowledge worth paying for makes a candidate choose that rule and defend the choice against the evidence available. One that lists "progress measurement" as a topic leaves the discovery for the month it matters.

## Pass six: read what the body holds itself to

A syllabus states what the body expects of a candidate. The scheme documents state what it expects of itself: how questions are written and reviewed, who makes the certification decision, who hears an appeal, on what grounds a credential is suspended or withdrawn, how long records are kept.

Publishing both gives a candidate two sides of a contract. Publishing only the syllabus gives them a prospectus and a hope.

## Pass seven: read one case study to its decision

Read a case study to the end. Does it name a decision and a number, or does it narrate a project and conclude that communication matters?

A case study that will not commit to an answer cannot be examined, and it usually tells you how the rest of the document was written.

## How to decide once the ninety minutes are up

If the verbs are observable, the exclusions are stated and the topic you know deepens as you trace it, the body has told you what it will hold you to. That is what a syllabus is for.

If it does not, that is not proof of a poor credential. It is a set of questions to put to the body directly, and the answers carry more information than the document did. The standing list is in [questions to ask a certifying body](https://credentialfinder.org/questions-to-ask-a-certifying-body).

And if an employer mandates a specific certification, or a framework you bid into scores an accredited one, none of this decides anything. Take the credential you are required to hold. Reading a syllabus is for a choice that is genuinely yours to make.

## FAQ

**Ninety minutes seems short for a document that size. Is it enough?**
It is enough to assess how the document was made, which is the decision in front of you. The seven passes sample the structure deliberately rather than covering the content. Reading a syllabus end to end is study, and study comes after the purchase, not before it.

**What if the syllabus is only available after payment?**
Ask for it, and treat the reply as evidence. Some bodies publish a detailed outline instead of the full text, which is a defensible middle position — the test is whether that outline carries observable verbs or only subject headings. A refusal to show anything is itself an answer.

**Does this method work outside project controls?**
Yes. Passes one to four and six to seven read structure rather than content, so they transfer to any published syllabus in any discipline. Pass five is the exception: it needs a topic you can already argue about, so use the part of the subject where you have done the work.

**Is a bigger Body of Knowledge a better one?**
There is no relationship. Length usually tracks how many topics a scheme wished to appear to cover. A short document with observable verbs and honest exclusions prepares a candidate better than a long one listing headings, and it takes a third of the time to check.

**How should a new body be judged on this?**
More strictly, not less. A body without a track record has the published documents and little else, so those documents carry the whole argument — see [judging a certifying body that is new](https://credentialfinder.org/judging-a-certifying-body-that-is-new). The corollary is fair: a new scheme publishing examinable outcomes and its own process requirements has put more on the table than a long-established one publishing a topic list.
