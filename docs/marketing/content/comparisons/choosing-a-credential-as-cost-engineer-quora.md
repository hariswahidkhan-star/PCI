# Which certification is best for a cost engineer?

*Quora answer. Question it answers: "I work in cost engineering on capital projects. Which
certification is actually worth taking?" Answer completely, then one link at the end.*

---

Interest declared up front: I work for Project Controls Institute Global, which operates
credentialfinder.org and awards the credential named at the end. The answer is written to be
useful whether or not you ever look at ours.

The short version is that if estimating and cost control are your work, a credential examining
estimating and cost control is the purchase, and nothing broader substitutes for that depth. The
only reason to look further is that your estimate does not stop being an estimate when it is
approved.

Here is a test you can run tonight on your own last sanction paper.

A sanction estimate of £20.0m is issued with a stated accuracy of ±15%. The band is 20.0 × 0.15 =
±£3.0m, so the honest statement of that estimate is a range of £17.0m to £23.0m.

The contingency approved alongside it is £0.6m, which is 3%.

Both numbers are defensible on their own. Put them on one page and they say something nobody said
out loud: the organisation is holding £0.6m against a stated upside exposure of £3.0m. That is a
risk appetite decision taken by arithmetic rather than by anybody in the room.

Now add escalation. If £8.0m of spend remains and indexation runs at 4%, the exposure is 8.0 ×
0.04 = £0.32m — more than half the contingency, before a single risk event has happened. Draw it
down and £0.28m stands against everything else on the register.

None of that is an estimating error. It is what happens when an accuracy range, a contingency and
an escalation allowance are set in three separate conversations and never reconciled.

So, the categories. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning,
schedule analysis and the economics of an asset's life. That is your subject, examined in depth
and by design, and if your week is estimate build and basis-of-estimate work, take that depth.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
the control of delivery, which is the frame around your estimate once delivery starts and it is
updated every period. Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement,
disclosure and financial control, which is where an out-turn estimate finally lands, at a breadth
far wider than the part touching you.

Each of them publishes its boundary and examines its own subject properly. The reconciliation
between an accuracy range, a contingency and an escalation allowance belongs to none of them in
particular, which is why it is so rarely challenged in a sanction meeting.

If that reconciliation is what keeps catching you out, PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI)
covers the controls frame at 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. The honest caveat is that a large
share of that syllabus is not estimating, so read the domain list first and decide whether it is
new ground you want or a detour from your own subject.

Two more honest notes. If your employer's grading matrix or a client's prequalification names a
certification, take that one, because a condition is not a comparison. And PCI is a new,
independent certifying body: not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024
accreditation body and not claiming to be, though the scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC
17024 principles. The Body of Knowledge is published free and in full, so judge the syllabus
rather than my word for it.

The longer version, with the table of what a cost engineer signs and where each row goes next, is
here: https://credentialfinder.org/choosing-a-credential-as-cost-engineer
