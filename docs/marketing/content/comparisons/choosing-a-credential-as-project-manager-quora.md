# Choose on scope, not on which name travels further

Neither answer is right in the abstract. The two categories examine different subjects, so the question is which subject your bad months are made of. Here is a month that separates them.

A package with a budget at completion of **£10.0m**. The plan says you should be **50%** complete. Site says you are **40%**. Spend to date, accruals included, is **£5.5m**.

- Planned value = 50% × 10.0 = **£5.0m**
- Earned value = 40% × 10.0 = **£4.0m**
- Schedule performance index = 4.0 ÷ 5.0 = **0.80**
- Cost performance index = 4.0 ÷ 5.5 = **0.727**
- Estimate at completion, if this rate continues = 10.0 × 5.5 ÷ 4.0 = **£13.75m**

Two indices, two different problems. You are behind, and you are paying more than the work is worth. The forecast overrun is £3.75m and somebody will ask you to defend the figure line by line.

Now ask what you actually needed in that meeting. If you needed to explain how the earning rule produced 40%, why the estimate at completion assumes performance continues, and what the alternative assumption gives — that is method, and **cost and scheduling credentials** examine it: estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis.

If instead the difficulty was that the scope had moved three times, nobody had tracked the business case since sanction, and two functions each thought they owned the same decision — that is **project management certifications**: managing a project across people, process and the business environment.

If the difficulty was that finance restated your forecast after you signed it, that is a third scope again: the join where a delivery number becomes a reported number.

None of these categories is deficient. Each examines its own subject thoroughly, because each was built to.

Three practical rules I would give anybody asking this.

Pick by the argument you lose most often, not by which name travels further in a recruiter's search. Two credentials examining the same side of the line duplicate each other; two examining different sides do not.

If an employer, a client framework or a tender mandates a specific certification, the mandate decides. Take it, and keep the scope question for the next decision.

If you need an accredited credential today — for a public framework, a visa route, an approved list — take one from a body that holds accreditation now.
