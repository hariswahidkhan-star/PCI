# Accountancy qualifications and PFL-AI: the overlap

*X / Threads thread — 7 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link sits in the final post. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters, so the live figure runs lower.*

**Post 1/7 — the hook** (224 characters)
The planner said 58% complete. The accounts recognised 55.88%. Same contract, same month, £0.64m apart.

Neither of them was wrong. The difference was one rule about material sitting on site, and it is nobody's home subject.

**Post 2/7 — the delivery number** (239 characters)
Fixed price £30.0m. Costs incurred to date £14.5m. Total expected cost £25.0m.

Cost-to-cost progress = 14.5 ÷ 25.0 = 58.0%.
Revenue implied = 0.58 × 30.0 = £17.40m.

That is the number the cost report supports, and it is properly derived.

**Post 3/7 — the adjustment** (247 characters)
Of the £14.5m, £1.2m is cable delivered and not yet installed.

Cost incurred that is not proportionate to progress comes out of the measure:
13.3 ÷ 23.8 = 55.88%. Revenue = £16.76m.

The cable is carried at cost, with no margin, until it goes in.

**Post 4/7 — what one category examines** (258 characters)
Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. They examine it thoroughly and are built to.

How a quantity was measured on site, and against which rule of credit, sits outside that scope by design.

**Post 5/7 — what the other examines** (262 characters)
Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Also thoroughly, also by design.

The join — cut-off, accrual evidence, measured progress becoming revenue — belongs squarely to neither, and the money moves there.
