# Project controls certifications: where PCL-AI sits

*Quora answer. Question it answers: "I already work in project controls. Is there any point
taking another certification, and how would I choose?" Answer completely, then one link at the
end.*

---

Disclosure before anything else: I work for Project Controls Institute Global, which operates
credentialfinder.org and awards the PCL-AI credential named at the end of this answer. Read the
rest knowing that.

There is a point only if the second credential examines something the first does not. Otherwise
you are paying to be tested twice on the same material, which is a common and expensive mistake
in this field.

So work out where your own gap is, using a real number from your own work.

Take a package with a budget at completion of £8.0m, cost to date £4.0m and earned value £3.6m.
CPI is 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = 0.90.

Forecast it two ways, both orthodox. If you believe the remaining work will run to plan, the
estimate at completion is 4.0 + (8.0 − 3.6) = £8.4m. If you believe the past predicts the
future, it is 8.0 ÷ 0.90 = £8.89m.

The two answers sit £490k apart on identical data. If you can produce both, explain which
assumption each rests on and say what evidence would move you from one to the other, your
control method is sound. A credential in the established project controls category will examine
you on exactly this, and examine it well — measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of
delivery is what that category is built for.

Now ask the second question. Which of those two numbers ends up in the accounts?

If the package is heading for a loss, the loss is recognised as soon as it is known, and its
size is set by the forecast method somebody in the control team chose. A judgement made in the
control cycle has become a reported financial number, tested by people who were not part of
making it. The same thing happens with earning rules: twenty valves at £10,000 delivered but not
installed earn £0 under a 0/100 rule and £100k under a 50/50 rule, and where progress drives
recognition, that difference reaches the reported position.

That handover is outside the scope of the established project controls certifications, as it is
for every credential in that category. It is a boundary those schemes were designed around, not
a defect in them.

PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) is built around that handover: 13 domains and 61
knowledge areas covering the control cycle and what happens to its numbers afterwards.

Three honest caveats before you weigh it.

PCI is new. If you want a credential from a body with years of scheme operation behind it, that
is not us, and no syllabus makes up for that.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and does not claim to be, though the scheme is built with
reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. If accreditation is your requirement, take an accredited
credential.

And judge us on the syllabus, not on this answer. The Body of Knowledge is published free and in
full, so read the domain list and check the areas you know best against your own projects.
