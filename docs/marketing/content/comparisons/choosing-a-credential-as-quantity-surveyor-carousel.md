# Valued, recognised, received: three numbers, one month

*LinkedIn document post — 11 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Two margins for the same month on the same job: plus £0.22m and minus £0.13m.
The gap is £0.35m of instructed work nobody has agreed, and it is the one line in your application that nobody assessed you on.

Eleven slides on what a valuation becomes after it leaves your desk.

---

**Slide 1 — One month, three numbers**
Valued, recognised, received. A surveyor produces the first. The other two are produced by rules that sit outside the profession's own subject, and they are the two a board actually discusses.

**Slide 2 — Who is telling you this**
credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials named on slide 9. That is an interest, declared up front so you can discount for it.

**Slide 3 — The figures**
Valuation of work executed this month: **£2.4m**, including **£0.35m** of instructed variation with no agreed price. Cost incurred in the month: **£2.18m**, of which **£0.30m** was spent doing that variation work.

**Slide 4 — The arithmetic**
Margin as valued: 2.40 − 2.18 = **+£0.22m**.

Amounts depending on a negotiation are recognised only when it is highly probable they will not reverse. Constrain the £0.35m and revenue is £2.05m.

Margin as recognised: 2.05 − 2.18 = **−£0.13m**.

**A £0.35m swing on one line, with nothing in dispute on site.**

**Slide 5 — And then the cash**
Retention at 5% on the £2.05m is £0.10m, so about **£1.95m** is due, a month or two later. Valued £2.4m, recognised £2.05m, received £1.95m. Three descriptions of one month, and you own the first.

**Slide 6 — What the chartered route assesses**
Chartered routes in surveying and engineering assess professional competence and standing through years of supervised practice, tested by submission and interview rather than by a paper. Measurement, valuation and entitlement are their own subject, and no examination substitutes for that assessment.

**Slide 7 — What the finance route examines**
Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control — the whole downstream subject, at a depth far beyond the part touching your valuation. That is a sound design decision for a syllabus about reporting.

**Slide 8 — Where the join sits**
Neither category sets out to examine the handshake between a valuation and a recognised number. That is not a failing in either. It is a boundary, and it is one line in your monthly application.

**Slide 9 — What PCI puts against it**
The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) aims at that boundary: 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas. The Body of Knowledge is proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20% governed AI — the proportions of the syllabus, not an examination weighting — and it is free to read in full before anybody pays.

**Slide 10 — What PCI does not claim**
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

**Slide 11 — When to take the chartered route, which is often**
If what you need is assessed professional standing in surveying, take the chartered route, and take it without hesitating. Same answer if a framework or a client panel names professional standing as a condition. The narrow case for anything else: you already have standing, and the gap between valued and reported is the part of your month that costs you.
