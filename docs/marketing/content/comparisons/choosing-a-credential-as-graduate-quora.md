# Usually not yet, and here is the honest reason

Usually not, unless your employer is paying for it. Most credentials worth holding assume working experience and examine judgement formed on real projects. Sitting one early does not make it count for more; it makes it prove less, because the part that matters is the part you cannot yet demonstrate.

What does travel at graduate level is arithmetic you can produce in front of somebody.

A package with a budget at completion of **£1.6m**. Site says **25% complete**. Cost to date is **£500k**.

- Earned value = 25% × 1.6 = **£400k**
- CPI = 400 ÷ 500 = **0.80**
- Estimate at completion = 1.6 × 500 ÷ 400 = **£2.0m**
- Variance at completion = **£400k over budget**

Four lines. Now the part that separates candidates: what does CPI 0.80 mean physically, and what happens to all four numbers if the earning rule changes from 0/100 to 50/50? A graduate who can argue that is more useful than one holding a certificate and unable to do it.

If you are going to take something, match the category to the desk you actually have.

**Cost and scheduling credentials** examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Right if your role is narrow and technical.

**The established project controls certifications** examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. Right once you have spent time inside a working control cycle.

**Project management certifications** examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment. Built for somebody already responsible for running projects, which is rarely a first job.

**Chartered routes in surveying and engineering** examine assessed professional competence over several years of logged practice. Multi-year by design, so if you are on one, that is your first credential and it has already started.

Three cases where an early credential does earn its money: your employer funds it and protects the study time; your role is narrow and technical and one category examines exactly it; or you are moving sideways from a related discipline and need to show a deliberate choice.

One case that overrides everything: if a government framework, a visa route or an employer's approved list requires an accredited credential, take one from a body that holds accreditation today.

Disclosure, because it belongs in the answer rather than under it: I write for Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials and operates credentialfinder.org, the comparison site linked below. PCL-AI runs to 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas covering the control cycle and how its numbers become reported numbers, published in full before anybody pays. If most of those domains describe work you have never seen, a year on a live project will get you more than an examination now.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything, including deciding against it.
