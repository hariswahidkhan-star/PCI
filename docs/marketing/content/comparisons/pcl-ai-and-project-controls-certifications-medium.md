# Project controls certifications: where PCL-AI sits

*Republished from credentialfinder.org. Set the Medium canonical to
https://credentialfinder.org/pcl-ai-and-project-controls-certifications before publishing.*

---

If your gap is control method — measurement, progress, forecasting, the discipline of running a
control cycle — the established project controls certifications examine that subject and examine
it properly. If your gap is what happens to those numbers after they leave the control cycle,
that sits outside the category by design, and PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) is built
around it. This is the narrowest comparison in the set: same discipline, different span.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI,
PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. Read the rest knowing who wrote it.

## What the established project controls certifications examine

Measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. In practice that means the
mechanics of a working control cycle: baselines and change, how physical progress is turned into
a number, variance analysis, the forecasting methods and when each is appropriate, and the
reporting rhythm that holds a project to account month after month.

The category is deep and it is coherent. Somebody examined on it can pick up an unfamiliar
project, find the baseline, test the progress claim and produce a forecast they can defend.
That is not a small thing, and no broad credential replaces it.

## What PCL-AI examines

PCL-AI covers 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. The control cycle is in there, and then the
part that usually has no owner: how a control number becomes a reported number, what evidence
has to support it, who may change it and how the change is recorded.

The Body of Knowledge's proportions are 40 finance and reporting, 40 project management and 20
governed AI. Those are the Body of Knowledge's proportions, not an examination weighting. Behind
the three volumes sit 92 sector case studies, and 113 PCI Standards carrying 532 process
requirements.

## Where the overlap sits

Two pieces of arithmetic, both checkable without a spreadsheet.

**The forecast with two right answers.** A package with a budget at completion of £8.0m. Cost to
date £4.0m. Earned value £3.6m, so CPI is 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = 0.90.

- Remaining work at budget: EAC = 4.0 + (8.0 − 3.6) = **£8.4m**
- Remaining work at the achieved rate: EAC = 8.0 ÷ 0.90 = **£8.89m**

**£490k apart**, on identical data. Both methods are orthodox and both are taught. The gap is the
assumption about the remaining work.

Then the question that decides which number matters: if the package is heading for a loss, that
loss is recognised as soon as it is known, and its size follows the forecast method somebody in
the control team selected. A control judgement has become a reported financial figure, and the
person who made it is usually not in the room where it is signed.

**The earning rule.** Forty valves at £10,000 each, twenty delivered and none installed. A 0/100
rule earns £0. A 50/50 rule, crediting half on delivery, earns £100k. Same steel, same day, and
where measured progress drives what is recognised, £100k of difference travels through to the
reported position.

The control side of both examples is examined thoroughly in the established category. The
reporting consequence is not, and it was never meant to be. Revenue recognition sits outside the
scope of a delivery-side credential, as it does for every credential in that category — a fact
about design, not a criticism.

## The accreditation statement

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to
ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

Nor do we publish a pass rate, a salary figure or a holder count. We have no honest way to
produce them yet. A body that tells you what it cannot claim is telling you the truth about what
it can.

## How to decide

**Take an established project controls certification** if control method is your gap, or if you
want a credential from a body with years of scheme operation behind it. PCI does not have that
record and cannot manufacture one.

**Take an accredited credential** if accreditation is itself the requirement — a public
framework, a client's approved list, an immigration route.

**Take what your employer mandates or funds.** A mandate is a business requirement, not a scope
decision.

**Look at PCL-AI** if you can already run the control cycle and keep losing the argument at the
handover — the forecast finance restates, the earning rule the auditor tests, the accrual that
lands after the pack is signed.

## What is not on this page, and why

No fees. No examination lengths or question counts. No pass marks, pass rates, difficulty
rankings or salary outcomes — not for the established project controls certifications, and not
for PCL-AI either.

Those numbers belong to whoever sets them, they move on that body's timetable, and a comparison
resting on them is stale by the next revision. Difficulty and prestige rankings are worse: they
are opinions presented as measurements, and they push people towards the credential with the
loudest reputation rather than the one that examines their problem.

Scope survives all of that. What a category sets out to examine does not change quietly, it can
be checked against any syllabus a reader opens, and it is the only axis on which a new body can
make an argument that does not depend on being believed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is PCL-AI a replacement for an established project controls certification?**
No. The established category examines control method in more depth than PCL-AI does, and that
depth is the reason to hold one. PCL-AI examines the same cycle plus the reporting handover. If
your method is already sound and the handover is where you lose, the second scope is the one
worth paying for.

**Is PCL-AI accredited?**
No. PCI is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and
does not claim to be, though the scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. If
you need an accredited credential today, take one from a body that already holds accreditation.

**Why should anyone take a credential from a new body?**
Only if the syllabus earns it. Read the 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas free before paying,
check the treatment of three areas you know well from your own projects, and see whether it
matches what actually happens. If it does not, do not buy it. That test costs nothing and it is
the one we would apply ourselves.

**Does PCL-AI examine AI tooling?**
It examines the governed use of AI inside the control cycle — where a model may assist, what a
human must decide, and what has to be recorded. It is one of the three strands of the Body of
Knowledge, alongside finance and reporting, and project management.

**What if I already hold a project controls certification?**
Then you have the method. The question is whether the handover is costing you arguments. Look at
your last three months of forecast restatements: if finance changed your number and you could
not say why in their vocabulary, that is the gap PCL-AI examines.
