# Project controls certifications: where PCL-AI sits

*LinkedIn post. No link in the body — it goes in the first comment.*

---

Two forecasts from the same month-end data, £490k apart.

The package: budget at completion £8.0m, cost to date £4.0m, earned value £3.6m.

CPI = 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = 0.90

Method one — assume the remaining work runs at budget:
EAC = 4.0 + (8.0 − 3.6) = £8.4m

Method two — assume the remaining work runs at the rate you have achieved:
EAC = 8.0 ÷ 0.90 = £8.89m

Same data. £490k apart. The difference is the assumption, and both assumptions are defensible.

Here is the part that decides which one matters. If that package is heading for a loss, the loss reaches the accounts as soon as it is known, and the forecast method you chose sets its size. A control-cycle judgement becomes a reported number, and the person who made it is usually not in the meeting where it lands.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery. They examine that thoroughly and are built to. What happens to the number after it leaves the control cycle sits outside their scope, as it does for every credential in that category.

PCL-AI examines both sides: 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas, the control cycle and the handover into reporting.

If what you need is a credential from a body with a long record behind it, that is not us. PCI is new, and we would rather say so than imply otherwise.
