# Judging a certifying body that has no track record

*X / Threads thread — 9 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/9 — the hook** (226 characters)
A body founded last year has no track record to show you. That is a fact, not a defence. It can still show you three things today: what it publishes before you pay, which decisions it keeps apart, and what it refuses to claim.

**Post 2/9 — the syllabus** (234 characters)
Check one. Is the syllabus published in full, free, before payment? Download it and read the boundary statements rather than the topic list. A body that will not show you what it examines until you have paid has answered the question.

**Post 3/9 — the pass standard** (208 characters)
Check two. Ask how the pass standard is set: against a description of competence, or against the cohort. One means your result depends on you. The other means it depends on whoever happened to sit that month.

**Post 4/9 — separation** (210 characters)
Check three. Ask who writes the paper, who sets the standard, and whether any of them sells the preparation. Preparation deciding the result is a structural failure, not a question of anybody's good intentions.

**Post 5/9 — the appeal route** (226 characters)
Check four. The appeal route: published grounds, a timescale, a named decision-maker, and someone who was not part of the original decision. Read it before you need it. It is short, and it shows how the worst day was designed.

**Post 6/9 — the record** (202 characters)
Check five. A credential is worth what a stranger can confirm in thirty seconds, years later, without your help. Try verifying somebody else's before you enrol, and see what the record actually returns.

**Post 7/9 — read for absence** (211 characters)
Check six. In year one there are no pass rates, no holder numbers and no salary data to compute from. A new body publishing all three has manufactured them. What a body declines to claim is the informative part.

**Post 8/9 — what time buys** (214 characters)
Check seven, the one that goes against us. An older body has heard appeals, revised a syllabus under pressure and survived a bad cohort. A new one has done none of that and cannot claim to have. Only time fixes it.

**Post 9/9 — our own answer** (234 characters)
Ours, plainly. PCI publishes its Body of Knowledge in full, and publishes no pass rates, holder numbers or salary figures. credentialfinder.org is operated by PCI Global. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it.
