# Ninety minutes with a Body of Knowledge, before you pay

*LinkedIn document post — 12 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Same package, same survey date, and a cost performance index of either 0.88 or 1.06.
The difference is a rule somebody chose before the job started, and it is the reason to read a syllabus before paying for the examination behind it.

Twelve slides on the ninety-minute check, in the order that works.

---

**Slide 1 — Ninety minutes**
Reading a published Body of Knowledge properly takes about ninety minutes and costs nothing. It is the cheapest due diligence available before a certification decision, and most candidates skip it because they read front to back, which is how a document persuades rather than how you assess one.

**Slide 2 — Two rules, one survey**
A work package. Budget £6.00m across 400 units. At cut-off, 150 units are complete and 60 more are started but unfinished. Actual cost is £2.55m. Nothing is in dispute. The only open question is which units earn.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Units-complete rule: 150 ÷ 400 = 37.5%. Earned value = 0.375 × 6.00 = **£2.25m**. Cost performance index = 2.25 ÷ 2.55 = **0.88**.

Half credit at start: (150 + 30) ÷ 400 = 45%. Earned value = 0.45 × 6.00 = **£2.70m**. Index = 2.70 ÷ 2.55 = **1.06**.

**£0.45m apart on one package, in one month, from one survey — and the index lands either side of 1.00.**

**Slide 4 — Pass one: read the last domain first**
Ten minutes. An introduction is written to persuade. The last domain is where a scheme puts whatever would not fit elsewhere, so it shows the real edge of the scope faster than the contents page. Ask whether the scope promised on the cover has survived to the back of the book.

**Slide 5 — Pass two: count the verbs, not the pages**
Fifteen minutes, and no subject knowledge required, because you are reading grammar. Highlight the verb on every knowledge area. Understand, appreciate and be familiar with describe an internal state nobody can mark. Calculate, reconcile, classify, select and justify describe something that leaves evidence on a page.

**Slide 6 — Pass three: find one number**
Ten minutes. Any credential that touches money should show a worked calculation in its own materials: figures in, an answer out, an answer that could be wrong. If the document produces no numbers, the examination will not either. That can still be the right purchase — you should simply know which one you are buying.

**Slide 7 — Pass four: find the exclusions**
Ten minutes. The hardest paragraph to write in a syllabus is the one saying what it does not cover, and it is the most useful to read. Every category draws its boundary deliberately and examines its own subject thoroughly. What you need is to know where the line runs, because the work does not respect it.

**Slide 8 — Pass five: trace a topic you already know**
Twenty minutes, and the pass that decides the purchase. Take a subject you have run under pressure and find everywhere the document touches it. Does the treatment deepen, or repeat the same words at a second level? Slide 3 is what a strong treatment of progress measurement makes you able to do.

**Slide 9 — Pass six: what the body holds itself to**
Fifteen minutes. The syllabus states what the body expects of you. The scheme documents state what it expects of itself: how questions are developed, who makes the certification decision, who hears an appeal, how records are kept. Publishing both gives you two sides of a contract.

**Slide 10 — Pass seven: one case study, to its decision**
Ten minutes. Read a case study to the end. Does it name a decision and a number, or narrate a project and conclude that communication matters? A case study that will not commit to an answer cannot be examined, and it usually tells you how the rest was written.

**Slide 11 — What ninety minutes cannot tell you**
Not difficulty, not standing, not whether a particular employer will accept the credential. And if your employer mandates a specific certification, or a framework you bid into scores an accredited one, none of this decides anything. Take the credential you are required to hold.

**Slide 12 — Where this comes from, and what it does not claim**
credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials. PCI publishes the Body of Knowledge in full before anyone pays — 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas for PCL-AI, 16 and 61 for PFL-AI, 16 and 63 for PML-AI, with 92 sector case studies — and holds itself to 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements. PCI is new and not accredited. Read the syllabus and judge it.
