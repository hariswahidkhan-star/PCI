# Seventy per cent of what? How a pass mark is really set

*X / Threads thread — 9 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/9 — the hook** (166 characters)
A pass mark on its own tells you nothing. Seventy per cent of what? A cut score only means something once you know how the paper was built and how the cut was chosen.

**Post 2/9 — two families** (223 characters)
Two families. Criterion-referenced: the cut comes from a judgement about what a just-competent candidate should be able to do. Norm-referenced: a fixed share of the cohort passes, so your result depends on who sat with you.

**Post 3/9 — the norm-referenced trap, in numbers** (232 characters)
The norm-referenced problem, in numbers. A scheme passes the top 30%.

Sitting A: the 70th percentile is 66. You score 68 and pass.
Sitting B: a stronger cohort puts it at 71. You score 68 and fail.

Same knowledge, different month.

**Post 4/9 — the panel method, in numbers** (222 characters)
Criterion-referenced, in numbers. A panel judges, item by item, the share of just-competent candidates who would answer it correctly. Average the judgements: 0.62 across the paper means a cut of 62% of the marks available.

**Post 5/9 — why percentages do not compare** (153 characters)
So a cut of 62 on a hard paper is a higher standard than 70 on an easy one. Comparing two schemes by their published percentages compares nothing at all.

**Post 6/9 — the border** (251 characters)
Measurement has error. Suppose the standard error is 3 marks: 61 against a cut of 62 sits inside it.

The question is not whether the border is fuzzy. It is whether the rule for the border was published before you sat, or decided afterwards about you.

**Post 7/9 — two more to ask** (184 characters)
Two more worth asking. Is the cut re-set when the paper changes? And where several versions of a paper exist, are they equated, so a candidate is not failed by drawing the harder form?

**Post 8/9 — the tell** (170 characters)
A body that moves the cut after seeing results, with no published rule, is managing its pass rate rather than its standard. Ask when the cut was set, not only what it is.

**Post 9/9 — our own answer** (267 characters)
PCI publishes its Body of Knowledge in full and publishes no pass rates. 40/40/20 describes the Body of Knowledge, not an exam weighting.
