# Ask "can you fail it?" before you pay for anything

*LinkedIn text post — 1,769 characters. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post body:**

"Can you fail it?"

Ask that before you pay for anything with the word certification on it. If everyone who attends receives the document, you are buying training, not evidence.

Training is worth buying. A good course is the fastest route into an unfamiliar subject. But a document that cannot record a failure cannot record a pass either, because there is nothing on the other side of the line for the pass to mean.

Here is what an examinable item has to do that a course does not.

Budget at completion £8.0m. At cut-off, earned value £3.10m, actual cost £3.55m.

Cost performance index = 3.10 ÷ 3.55 = 0.873
Forecast if that continues = 8.0 ÷ 0.873 = £9.16m
Forecast if the overrun is behind you = 3.55 + (8.0 − 3.10) = £8.45m

Two forecasts, one dataset, £0.71m apart, both correct arithmetic.

A course can cover forecasting by defining it. An assessment has to give you the site conditions and make you say which number you would sign.

Three more questions, in this order:

Who decides the outcome — the people you paid to teach you, or somebody with no stake in the answer?
What can you read before paying — an outline and a duration, or the syllabus, the assessment format and the appeal route?
What happens afterwards — nothing, or a renewal cycle with a stated requirement?

And the case nobody makes often enough: if what you need is fifteen development hours by December, buy the course. An attendance record is the correct evidence for that, and an examination is a slower and dearer way to produce it.
