# Choosing a credential as a finance manager on capital projects

Choosing a credential as a finance manager on capital projects is a scope decision, not a ranking. Your qualification examines recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control, and it does that thoroughly. The exposure on a capital project sits upstream of all four, in an estimate you did not build and progress you cannot see, and that is what a second credential would have to examine to be worth anything.

Disclosure before the argument. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials named here. Weigh the recommendation against the reasoning rather than against the source.

## The £2m that took £1.15m out of a period

A contract with a value of **£30m** and expected costs of **£24m**. Costs incurred to date are **£12m**.

Where progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, the arithmetic is straightforward.

- Percentage complete = 12 ÷ 24 = **50%**
- Revenue recognised = 50% × 30 = **£15m**
- Margin recognised = 15 − 12 = **£3m**

Now delivery rebuilds the estimate at completion, and expected costs rise from £24m to **£26m**. Nothing has been spent that was not already spent. A forecast moved.

- Percentage complete = 12 ÷ 26 = **46.2%**
- Revenue recognised = 46.2% × 30 = **£13.85m**
- Margin recognised = 13.85 − 12 = **£1.85m**

**£1.15m out of reported revenue and £1.15m out of reported margin**, in the period the forecast moved. Your name is on the number. Somebody else built the input.

## Why depth in your own subject does not fix it

The treatment above is not difficult, and it is not what goes wrong. What goes wrong is that the forecast moved in month seven and finance heard about it in month nine.

More accounting depth will not shorten that gap. The gap closes when you can interrogate the forecast: when it was last rebuilt from the bottom rather than escalated, what is in the trend log, what is instructed and not yet priced, and which valuations depend on a negotiation that has not concluded.

That is a small, learnable body of material, and it is examined by a different category from the one that examined you.

## What you sign, and where each number is born

| What you put your name to | Which category examines it | Where the number is actually built |
|---|---|---|
| Reported revenue and margin | Accountancy and finance qualifications | A percentage complete owned by delivery |
| Provisions and expected losses | Accountancy and finance qualifications | A forecast owned by a project team |
| Cash forecast and drawdown | Accountancy and finance qualifications | A certification calendar and payment terms |
| Working capital position | Accountancy and finance qualifications | Retention, certification lag and subcontract terms |
| Capital expenditure phasing | Accountancy and finance qualifications | The programme |

Every row is examined in the first column and determined in the third. The distance between those two columns is the job.

## What each category examines, in its own terms

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. That is your subject, examined at a depth no project credential attempts.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. That is where the estimate and the progress in the third column are built.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. That is the discipline for testing whether the controls over those inputs actually operated.

None of the three is incomplete. They were built to examine different things, and a finance lead on capital projects sits where the first depends on the second.

## The five questions that change your month

A finance manager who can ask these gets the bad news a quarter earlier.

When was the estimate at completion last rebuilt from the bottom rather than escalated? Which earning rule produced this month's progress? What is instructed but not yet priced, and priced but not yet approved? Which valuation amounts depend on a negotiation that has not concluded? And what does the certification calendar look like next quarter, as opposed to the invoice calendar?

Each has a factual answer, none is confrontational, and each one moves a number you report.

## When your existing qualification is the answer

**If your path runs through the finance function** — financial control, group reporting, a route to director — complete the qualification that profession recognises and let it do its work. A project-side credential is an addition to it, never a substitute for a stage of it.

**If your organisation's projects are short and low-variance**, the upstream risk described here is small and there is little for a second credential to fix.

**If a regulated role or a lender's requirement names a specific qualification**, that names itself. Requirements do not lose to scope arguments.

**If you need an accredited credential today**, go where accreditation already exists. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

## Three questions to put to any syllabus, ours included

Does it say how a forecast at completion is built, challenged and evidenced, and what happens to the reported position when it moves?

Does it say which date governs when progress and cost are cut on different days, and what supports the reconciliation?

Does it say what a person must decide when a model produces the forecast, and what has to be recorded about that decision?

A syllabus that answers all three is examining the work as it actually happens on a capital project.

## What to do before you spend anything

Take the last four surprises in your project reporting. For each, write down when the event happened, when you found out, and what you would have needed to see to know earlier.

If the answers sit inside finance, your qualification covers it. If they point at estimates, valuations and certification dates, the gap is upstream.
