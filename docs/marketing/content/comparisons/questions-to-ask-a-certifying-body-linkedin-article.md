# The ten questions any certifying body should welcome

The ten questions any certifying body should welcome are these: can I read the syllabus first, who writes the examination, how was the pass mark set, what will the certificate say I was examined on, can a third party verify it, who hears an appeal, what does recertification check, are you accredited, what will you not claim, and where does my record live if you close.

The questions are the easy part. What follows is what a good answer sounds like and what a weak one sounds like, because the difference is audible and most candidates never get to hear it.

Disclosure first: credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials, so I am one of the bodies you would be asking. Every question below is one I expect to be asked and answer in the same terms.

## Before you pay: questions 1 to 3

**1. Can I read the whole syllabus before I pay anything?**

A good answer is a link. Not an outline, not a marketing summary of the domains, the actual document that the examination is written from. Ours is [the Body of Knowledge in full](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge), published before anybody pays anything, and you should hold every other body to the same test.

A weak answer is that the syllabus is provided on enrolment. That arrangement asks you to buy the description of what you are buying.

**2. Who writes the examination, and are they separate from whoever sells the preparation?**

A good answer names the separation as a policy and explains how it is enforced: who may see live items, who is excluded from item writing because they teach, who signs the certification decision.

A weak answer is that the same team does both because it keeps things aligned. Alignment is exactly the problem. If the people who take your training fee also decide your result, the decision has an interest in it.

**3. How was the pass mark set, and by whom?**

A good answer describes a method and a panel: a criterion-referenced standard, set by subject experts judging what a just-competent candidate should be able to do, fixed before the sitting and re-examined when the paper changes.

A weak answer is a number with no method attached. Seventy per cent of what? A cut score is only meaningful next to the difficulty of the paper it sits on.

## What you actually receive: questions 4 and 5

**4. What will the certificate say I was examined on?**

This is the question candidates skip and the one that decides the most, because scope is where credentials genuinely differ.

| Category | What it sets out to examine |
|---|---|
| Cost and scheduling credentials | Estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis |
| Accountancy and finance qualifications | Recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial control |
| Audit credentials | Governance, risk, control and the assurance process |
| Project management certifications | Managing projects across people, process and the business environment |
| Product certifications for scheduling software | Operating a specific tool rather than the discipline behind it |

Each examines its own subject thoroughly and is built to. A scope boundary is a design decision, not a shortcoming. But scope is what you are buying, so read the designation and the syllabus together and ask which of them your job actually needs.

**5. Can a third party verify it without going through me?**

A good answer is a register a hiring manager can query with an ID, returning a state rather than a yes: active, expired, suspended, revoked, with expiry computed at the moment of the query.

A weak answer is a downloadable certificate. A document is a picture of a claim.

## The arithmetic behind question 4

Here is why scope is not an abstraction.

A fixed-price contract worth £60.0m. Budget at completion £48.0m. Cost incurred to date £21.6m. Control transfers over time and progress is measured on a cost-to-cost basis.

Hold the forecast at £48.0m: progress is 21.6 ÷ 48.0 = **45.00%**, and revenue recognised is 0.45 × 60.0 = **£27.00m**.

Move the forecast to £51.7m: progress is 21.6 ÷ 51.7 = **41.78%**, and revenue recognised is **£25.07m**.

A £3.7m move in a delivery forecast moved the reported number by **£1.93m**. The person who moved the forecast was a planner. The person who explains the movement is a financial controller. Ask which side of that arithmetic your credential examines, and whether anything in your syllabus examines the crossing itself.

## When it goes wrong: questions 6 and 7

**6. Who hears an appeal, and are they independent of the people who marked me?**

A good answer is a published procedure with named stages, a time limit, and a decision-maker who was not involved in the original decision.

A weak answer is that appeals are handled case by case. Case by case means there is no rule, and a process invented after your result is a process shaped by your result.

**7. What does recertification actually check?**

A good answer says what it is for: that the holder is still practising and has kept up with what changed. It states what evidence counts and what happens if you cannot produce it.

A weak answer is a renewal fee with a form attached. That is a subscription, and calling it recertification does not make it a check.

## What the body claims: questions 8 to 10

**8. Are you accredited, by whom, to what standard, and if not, do you say so plainly?**

Answered for this body, in the form it should always take: PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

A weak answer is a phrase engineered to sound like accreditation without being it. If you cannot tell from the sentence whether a body has been assessed by somebody else, that is the answer.

**9. What will you not claim?**

A good answer is specific about its own limits: no promised salary, no promised role, no employer preference, no published pass rates, no holder counts. PCI publishes none of those, and says so as a position rather than leaving the gap for you to notice.

A body that promises an outcome is claiming something it does not control. The strongest signal in this whole list is a body that volunteers what it cannot yet prove.

**10. If you closed tomorrow, where would the record of my examination live?**

A good answer covers retention: how long records are kept, in what form, and what would happen to the register. It is an unglamorous question and the one that most exposes whether a body has thought past its launch.

## Where the honest answer points elsewhere

If your employer mandates a specific certification, question 8 settles it before the other nine. Take the mandated one and ask the other questions afterwards, out of interest rather than for the decision.

If a regulator or a client's procurement rule requires an accredited credential, the same applies. An unaccredited scheme can answer nine questions beautifully and still not meet a requirement written in a contract.

If your work sits entirely on one side of the finance and delivery line, a credential built for that side is the better buy. The overlap argument only earns its keep if you are the person standing in the overlap. There is [an honest comparison of the project controls credentials available](https://credentialfinder.org/best-project-controls-certification), ours included, on a site this Institute operates — read it knowing that.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it rude to ask a certifying body all ten?**
No, and the reaction is itself data. A body that publishes examinable requirements expects to be asked how it examines them. If a question about pass-mark method or appeals produces evasion rather than a document, you have learned more than the answer would have told you.

**Which one should I ask if I only ask one?**
Question 4. Scope decides whether the credential answers the question your job actually asks, and it is the only one where a good answer from the wrong category is still the wrong credential for you.

**Does an unaccredited body automatically fail question 8?**
No. It fails if it obscures the position. Accreditation is a third-party assessment of the certifying process, and the absence of it is a fact to weigh against everything else you have learned, not an automatic disqualification. What should disqualify a body is a sentence built to blur the difference.

**What if the body will not show me the syllabus?**
Treat it as an answer rather than an obstacle. The syllabus is the thing being sold, and a body confident in it has no reason to withhold it. Some publish an outline and reserve the detail; ask specifically whether the document the examination is written from is public.

**My employer is paying. Do these still matter?**
More, if anything. You spend the study hours whoever pays the invoice, and you carry the designation on your profile for years afterwards. Scope, verifiability and what the body refuses to claim all outlive the transaction.
