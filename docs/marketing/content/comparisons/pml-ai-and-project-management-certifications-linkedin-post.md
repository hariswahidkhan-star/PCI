# 71% complete, 64% recognised, and £1.82m in between

*LinkedIn text post — 1735 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

The project manager reported 71% complete on Monday. The accounts recognised 64% on Thursday. £1.82m sat between the two, and both numbers were properly derived.

A £26.0m fixed-price contract. Expected cost at completion £23.4m. Costs incurred £14.976m.

Cost-to-cost progress: 14.976 ÷ 23.4 = 64.0%. Revenue = 0.640 × 26.0 = £16.64m.
Physical progress, measured against installed quantities: 71%. On the same price that implies £18.46m.
Difference: £1.82m.

Physical progress and cost-based progress answer different questions. One says how much of the work is standing. The other says how much of the expected cost has been consumed, and it is the second one that was chosen as the measure of transfer.

When the two diverge by seven points, somebody has to be able to say why — productivity better than tender, a rate error, uninstalled material, or a measure that no longer depicts the work faithfully. "The system says 64%" is not that answer.

Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment. That is a large subject, examined thoroughly, and built to be.

The sentence that connects a progress figure to a recognised revenue figure is not in that subject, and there is no reason it should be.

The PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) is examined there: 16 domains, 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge weighted 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI.
