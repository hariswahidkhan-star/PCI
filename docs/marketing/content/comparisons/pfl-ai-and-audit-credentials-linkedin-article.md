# Audit credentials and PFL-AI: two different questions

If your job is to give assurance — to test whether a control existed, operated and left evidence, and to do it independently — an audit credential is the right thing to hold, and the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) is not a substitute for it. If your job is to produce the numbers that assurance is given over, PFL-AI is aimed at you. The two examine different questions about the same month-end.

This is a LinkedIn original, written under the Institute's certification and careers pillar. It is not a copy of the comparison published on credentialfinder.org, and it uses a different worked example.

**Disclosure.** credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. An interested party wrote this, so check the syllabus rather than the tone.

## The two questions, side by side

| Axis | Audit credentials | PFL-AI |
|---|---|---|
| The question asked | Did the control exist, operate as designed, and leave evidence? | Is this the right measure, and what does it do to revenue and margin? |
| Independence | Central to the credential and to the role | Not applicable; the holder usually sits inside the reporting chain |
| Evidence | How to obtain it, test it, document it and conclude on it | How to produce it at source: measurement rules, goods received, applications |
| Population and sampling | Design, selection and inference | Outside the scope; the discipline is the control account and the cost ledger |
| Risk | Governance, risk and control across the organisation | Contract-level exposure: forecast, provision, contract asset, retention |
| Output | An opinion or a report, with a defined standard behind it | A cost report, a forecast and a set of numbers somebody else will test |
| Accreditation status | Established routes, many of them accredited | New, independent, not accredited |

Neither column is a criticism of the other. A credential that examined both properly would examine neither well, which is why the categories are drawn where they are.

## What audit credentials examine

Audit credentials examine governance, risk and control, and the assurance process itself: how an engagement is planned, how evidence is obtained and evaluated, how independence is preserved, and how a conclusion is reached and reported.

Holders come out able to look at a process and say whether it does what it claims to do, and to document that in a form that survives challenge. It is a demanding subject and the credentials go a long way into it.

What sits outside that scope, by design, is the construction of the project measure itself — which rule of credit turns hours into earned value, whether a survey date and a ledger date are the same date, and what a forecast movement does to recognised revenue.

## What PFL-AI examines

PFL-AI is examined on the chain that turns delivery data into financial data on a contract: measurement rules, accrual evidence, cut-off between the site and the ledger, the estimate at completion, and the accounting consequences that follow from it.

It is 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and the Body of Knowledge is published in full before anyone pays anything, so the scope can be checked rather than believed.

It confers no right to give an opinion, no independence, and no standing in an assurance engagement. It is not trying to.

## Worked: the control operated perfectly

A £15.0m contract. Total expected cost £12.0m. Progress measured cost-to-cost.

In month 6 the cost team raised an accrual of **£450k** for goods received and not invoiced, supported by delivery records. Good work, properly evidenced.

In months 7 and 8 the same £450k was carried forward, on the basis that it was "about the same as last month". The control operated. There was a policy, an owner, a review and a signature every month.

At the month 7 cut-off the real position was **£610k**. Cost incurred was understated by **£160k**.

£160k on £12.0m of expected cost is **1.33 percentage points** of measured progress. On a £15.0m price, that is **£0.20m of revenue** in the wrong period, from a number nobody in the process got wrong on their own terms.

The month 8 version of the same habit runs the other way: the real position was £330k, so cost was overstated by £120k and revenue was pulled forward. Two periods, two errors, one repeated figure, and a control that would test as effective in both months.

## Why both questions are needed

"Did the control operate?" and "is the number right?" are genuinely different questions, and the second one does not become true because the first one did.

An accrual repeated without evidence has a review, an owner and a signature. What it does not have is a delivery note behind this month's figure. The person best placed to notice that is the person who knows what was delivered to site — which is a project controls role, not an assurance role.

This runs the other way too. Completeness of cost is approached by working from the goods received log and the site measurement back into the ledger, and that log is a project record. Where the project's records are thin, everything resting on them is thinner, and that is a project controls problem before it is anybody else's.

## The accreditation position

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

If accreditation is a condition of your role, your client's requirements or your own judgement, that settles it, and it settles it against PCI today.

## How to decide

**Take an audit credential** if you work in an assurance function, if independence is the point of your job, if you audit systems or processes rather than build the numbers inside them, or if your progression runs through an audit team. That is a clear-cut answer and it deserves to be said plainly.

**Sit PFL-AI** if you are on the other side of the table: the person who produces the cost report, defends the forecast, and has to explain why the accrual moved. The parts of the month-end that get challenged are the parts it examines.

**Both, in sequence,** if you audit capital projects and keep finding that the hard part is not the control framework but the measurement underneath it. Read [the Body of Knowledge for the PFL-AI credential](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/certifications/pfl-ai) and decide from the scope, not from a claim about it.

## FAQ

**Does PFL-AI qualify me to audit anything?**
No. It confers no assurance standing, no independence and no right to give an opinion. It examines how project financial information is produced, which is the subject matter an auditor tests rather than the testing itself.

**I am an internal auditor covering capital projects. Would this add anything?**
Possibly. The areas holders most often say they were never examined on are rules of credit, cut-off between site survey and ledger close, accrual evidence and how a forecast movement lands in revenue. If your work already covers those in depth, it would not.

**Is PFL-AI accredited?**
No. PCI is a new, independent certifying body, not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. The syllabus is published in full so you can judge the scope yourself.

**Is this saying audit credentials miss things?**
No. A scope boundary is a design decision. Audit credentials examine assurance thoroughly and are built to do exactly that. The point is only that the design of a project measure is decided somewhere else, and somebody should be examined on it.
