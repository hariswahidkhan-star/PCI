# Choosing a credential as a project manager: scope first

Choosing a credential as a project manager who owns the numbers is a scope decision, not a
ranking decision. Project management certifications examine managing projects across people,
process and the business environment. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost
control, planning and schedule analysis. PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) examines the
delivery side, the reporting side, and the join between them.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI,
PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. You should know that before you weigh anything
below.

## What each kind of credential sets out to examine

Read that table as three different jobs, not three grades of the same job.

## What project management certifications examine

Project management certifications examine how a project is run: initiating and planning work,
managing scope and change, leading a team, dealing with stakeholders, and working inside the
business environment the project sits in.

That is a wide brief and these credentials cover it properly. If your difficulty is that the
sponsor keeps moving the scope, or that three functions each think they own the same decision,
this is the category built for your problem.

## What cost and scheduling credentials examine

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis
and the economics of the asset life cycle. They go deep on method: how an estimate is built and
classed, how a network behaves, how progress is measured, how a forecast is produced.

If you are the person defending a number in a project review, that depth is worth having. A
project manager who has been through this category argues from method rather than from
confidence.

## What accountancy and finance qualifications examine

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial
analysis and control. They answer when revenue may be recognised, what a provision has to
satisfy, and how a set of accounts has to hold together.

They are not built to examine a critical path, and there is no reason they should be. The scope
boundary is a design decision, not a shortfall.

## Where the overlap sits, and what it costs

The overlap is the point where a delivery number becomes a reported number. Progress becomes
revenue. A commitment becomes an accrual. A forecast at completion becomes the margin somebody
reports to a board.

Here is the arithmetic, on a single package.

You run a £6.0m package. Earned value to date is £3.30m. The ledger shows £3.00m of cost.

- CPI = 3.30 ÷ 3.00 = **1.10**
- EAC = 6.00 ÷ 1.10 = **£5.45m** — £550k better than budget

Then finance posts £250k of goods received but not yet invoiced. The steel arrived, the invoice
has not. Cost to date is £3.25m.

- CPI = 3.30 ÷ 3.25 = **1.015**
- EAC = 6.00 ÷ 1.015 = **£5.91m**

The forecast moved £460k. Nothing changed on site. One accrual moved it, and the person who
knew it was coming was not in the room when the report was signed.

A project manager who owns the numbers is the person who has to explain that swing. Explaining
it needs both vocabularies at once: what earned value is measuring, and what the ledger is
recognising.

## The accreditation position, stated plainly

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference
to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

We publish no pass rate, no salary figure and no holder count, because we have no honest way to
produce them yet. What we can show is the syllabus. PCL-AI runs to 13 domains and 61 knowledge
areas, and every one of them is published before you pay anything. There are 92 sector case
studies across the three Bodies of Knowledge, and 113 PCI Standards carrying 532 process
requirements behind them.

## How to decide

**Take a project management certification** if the work in front of you is running the project:
scope, change, people, governance, the business case. That is the category examined for it.

**Take a cost and scheduling credential** if your day is estimating, planning or cost control
and you want depth in the method itself. Nothing here is a substitute for that depth.

**Take an accredited credential from an established body** if you need accreditation now — for
a public-sector framework, a visa route, an employer's approved list. PCI cannot give you that
today and will not pretend otherwise.

**Take the one your employer will pay for** if the choice is between a funded route and an
unfunded one. A credential you actually sit beats a better-matched one you never start.

**Look at PCL-AI** if the argument you keep losing is at the join: the forecast that finance
restates, the progress figure the auditor queries, the accrual nobody warned you about. That is
the scope PCL-AI was built to examine.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a project management certification and a project controls one?**
Most people do not. Two credentials that examine the same side of the line duplicate each other.
Two that examine different sides — delivery method and the reporting join — do not. Decide by
looking at which argument you lose most often, then read the syllabus for that side before you
commit money.

**My employer mandates a specific certification. Does scope still matter?**
The mandate settles it. Take the certification your employer requires, because a credential you
need for a bid or a framework is a business requirement rather than a scope question. Scope
matters for the second credential, if you ever take one, and for what you read in between.

**Is PCL-AI a substitute for an accredited credential?**
No. PCI is not accredited by any ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and does not claim to be. If
your requirement is accreditation itself, an established accredited scheme is the right answer
today. If your requirement is scope across the finance-and-delivery join, read the PCL-AI Body
of Knowledge and judge it on what it examines.

**I have never worked on the finance side. Is PCL-AI too far a jump?**
No accountancy background is assumed. The syllabus builds the reporting side from the delivery
side you already know: how progress becomes revenue, how a commitment becomes an accrual, how a
forecast becomes a reported margin. If you have run a month-end handover, you have met the
problem already.

**What can I read before paying anything?**
The whole Body of Knowledge — every domain and knowledge area, for each of the three
credentials. Read the domain list, find the three areas closest to work you have actually done,
and check whether the treatment matches what you know. That test tells you more than any
marketing page, including this one.
