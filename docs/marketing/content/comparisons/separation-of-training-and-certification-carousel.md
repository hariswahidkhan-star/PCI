# Five checks before you pay a certifying body anything

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the arithmetic and is the one people screenshot. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

The organisation that sold you the course should not be the organisation that decides whether you passed.
Five checks you can run on any scheme in ten minutes, before you pay it anything.

Ten slides, with the arithmetic on slide 3 and the honest cost of the separation on slide 8.

---

**Slide 1 — The principle, in one line**
Buy preparation from whoever teaches it best. Take the decision on whether you passed from somebody with no stake in the answer. Where one organisation does both, ask in writing how it keeps the two apart.

**Slide 2 — This is not an argument against training**
Most people need preparation and good preparation is worth paying for. A trainer's job is to get you ready. An examiner's job is to record accurately whether you are. Different jobs, different pressure.

**Slide 3 — What an examinable item looks like**
BAC **£24.0m**. EV **£4.8m**. AC **£5.6m**, accruals in.
CPI = 4.8 ÷ 5.6 = **0.857**
EAC = BAC ÷ CPI = 24.0 ÷ 0.857 = **£28.0m**
VAC = 24.0 − 28.0 = **−£4.0m**
86p of budgeted work per £1 spent. £28.0m, or the answer is wrong — whoever taught the candidate.

**Slide 4 — Check 1: is the syllabus readable before you pay?**
Not a marketing outline. Domains, knowledge areas, the depth expected in each. If the syllabus only exists inside the course, the course is the syllabus and you cannot judge either one first.

**Slide 5 — Check 2: is a named course required before you may sit?**
A mandated course is defensible in a regulated trade where supervised practice is part of the competence. It is harder to defend when the body selling the mandate is the body awarding the credential.

**Slide 6 — Check 3: does the body claim its route improves your odds?**
It is rarely written as bluntly as odds. Look for "official", or for one route described as the complete preparation. Any claim of that shape means the exam and the product are on the same balance sheet.

**Slide 7 — Checks 4 and 5: the review and the policy**
Who looks again at a decision you dispute, and did that person make it? And is there an impartiality policy that names the commercial conflict and says what happens? A promise to be fair is not a process.

**Slide 8 — What the separation costs the body doing it**
A syllabus published in full is read by competitors on day one and can be taught by anyone without asking. Preparation sells to every candidate; an examination sells once. The body that separates them gives up the larger half of its own market.

**Slide 9 — When this is not your deciding factor**
If your employer mandates a specific certification, the mandate decides. If a tender or a regulator requires an accredited scheme today, take the accredited route — transparency about a syllabus is not a substitute for accreditation.
