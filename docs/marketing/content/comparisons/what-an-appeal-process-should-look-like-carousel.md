# Ten slides on how to appeal a certification decision

*LinkedIn document post — 10 slides, 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the arithmetic and is the one people screenshot. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

Read the appeals policy before you book the exam, not after you fail it.
The fastest test: find the deadline that binds the body rather than the candidate.

Ten slides on what a working appeals process contains, with the difference between a ground and a grievance on slide 3.

---

**Slide 1 — Appeal or complaint**
An appeal challenges a decision made about you: a result, an eligibility refusal, an integrity finding, an invalidation, a refused accommodation. A complaint is about conduct or service. Different routes, different people, different deadlines.

**Slide 2 — Published before you sit**
An appeals policy that arrives "on request" is not a control. It should be on the site, readable before you apply, in the same place as the syllabus and the eligibility rules.

**Slide 3 — A ground, worked**
An item gives EV **£26.6m** and AC **£30.0m** and asks for CPI to two decimal places.
26.6 ÷ 30.0 = **0.886666…**
Two decimal places: **0.89**. Three: **0.887**.
A candidate who wrote 0.89 answered the question asked. If only 0.887 scored, the scheme contradicted its own instruction — and that hit everyone who sat the paper.

**Slide 4 — Ground or grievance**
Checkable: a disrupted session in the record, a marking scheme that contradicts the paper, an eligibility rule applied that is not the published rule, an agreed accommodation not provided.
Not a ground alone: disagreeing with the syllabus or the pass standard.

**Slide 5 — The deadline that binds the body**
Most policies give the candidate a window and give themselves none. Search the page for a limit that applies to the body. A window on one side only is a queue, not a process.

**Slide 6 — Who hears it**
A useful policy names a role outside the original decision. "The relevant team will review" names nobody. The person reviewing should not be the person defending.

**Slide 7 — What your record shows meanwhile**
A result under review should show no score and no pass or fail. Held, and labelled held. A provisional number that is later withdrawn cannot be recalled from everyone who already saw it.

**Slide 8 — Remedies, listed**
"Upheld" is not a remedy. Look for the list: re-mark, re-sit at no further charge, record corrected, credential reinstated, item fixed for everyone who sat it. Fixing it for one candidate is a settlement, not a remedy.

**Slide 9 — Where PCI is weak, said plainly**
PCI's appeals route ends inside PCI. An accredited scheme adds a body above the scheme that can be told it is not following its own rules. If you need that layer today, take the accredited route.

**Slide 10 — The disclosure and the standing offer**
credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it. The appeals policy, the impartiality policy and the full syllabus are all public before anyone pays.
