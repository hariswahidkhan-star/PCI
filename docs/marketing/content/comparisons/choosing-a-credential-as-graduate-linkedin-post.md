# Five lines that beat a certificate at graduate level

*LinkedIn text post — 1,756 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

At graduate level, five lines of arithmetic will get you further than a first certificate will.

Here they are. A package with a budget at completion of £2.0m. The plan says you should be 30% complete. Site says 25%. Spend to date is £600k.

Planned value = 30% × 2.0 = £600k
Earned value = 25% × 2.0 = £500k
SPI = 500 ÷ 600 = 0.833
CPI = 500 ÷ 600 = 0.833
EAC = 2.0 × 600 ÷ 500 = £2.4m, so £400k over budget

Both indices land on the same number because you have spent exactly what you should have earned by now. Being able to say that out loud, and then explain what would change if the earning rule changed, is what gets a graduate trusted with a report.

Most credentials worth holding assume you have already done the work. They examine judgement formed on real projects, which is deliberate, and a credential sat too early proves less than the same one sat later.

So the order I would suggest.

Ask what your employer funds, and take that if the category is anywhere near your desk. Ask what your desk actually is: planning and estimating point to cost and scheduling credentials, a mixed controls seat points to the established project controls certifications, running whole projects points to project management certifications.

Ask whether you need accreditation now — a government framework, a visa route, an approved list. If you do, go where accreditation already exists today.

Then consider waiting. Six months of real month-ends makes any of these exams easier and your answers better.
