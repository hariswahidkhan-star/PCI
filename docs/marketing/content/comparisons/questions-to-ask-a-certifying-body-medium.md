# Ten questions worth asking any certifying body you pay

Ten questions worth asking any certifying body before you pay it: can I read the whole syllabus first, who writes the examination, how was the pass mark set, what will the certificate say I was examined on, can a third party verify it, who hears an appeal, what does recertification check, are you accredited, what will you not claim, and where does my record live if you close.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. So we are one of the bodies you would be asking, and every question below is answered about us further down.

## The ten, with the answers you are listening for

| # | The question | A good answer sounds like | A weak answer sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Can I read the whole syllabus before I pay? | A link to the document the examination is written from | "You receive it on enrolment" |
| 2 | Who writes the examination, and are they separate from whoever sells preparation? | A stated separation, and how it is enforced | "The same team, so it stays aligned" |
| 3 | How was the pass mark set, and by whom? | A method and a panel, fixed before the sitting | A percentage with nothing attached |
| 4 | What will the certificate say I was examined on? | The designation, matched to the published syllabus | A domain list that appears nowhere else |
| 5 | Can a third party verify it without going through me? | A register queried by ID, returning a state | A downloadable certificate |
| 6 | Who hears an appeal? | A published procedure, stages, time limits, an independent decision-maker | "Case by case" |
| 7 | What does recertification check? | A stated purpose and the evidence that counts | A renewal fee with a form attached |
| 8 | Are you accredited, by whom, to what standard? | A plain yes with the body named, or a plain no | A sentence engineered to sound like a yes |
| 9 | What will you not claim? | A specific list of refusals | Salary figures and employer-preference claims |
| 10 | Where does my record live if you close? | A retention policy, in writing | Silence |

## Question 4 is the one that decides most

Scope is what you are actually buying, and scope is where the categories genuinely differ.

| Category | What it sets out to examine |
|---|---|
| Cost and scheduling credentials | Estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis and the economics of the asset life cycle |
| The established project controls certifications | Measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery |
| Accountancy and finance qualifications | Recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial analysis and control |
| Audit credentials | Governance, risk, control and the assurance process |
| Project management certifications | Managing projects across people, process and the business environment |
| Product certifications for scheduling software | Operating a specific tool rather than the discipline behind it |
| Chartered routes in surveying and engineering | Assessed professional competence and chartered standing |

Every one of these examines its own subject thoroughly and is built to. A scope boundary is a design decision, not a shortcoming, and a reader who holds one of them should recognise the description as fair.

The risk is not a poor credential. It is a good credential answering a question your job does not ask.

## The arithmetic, so scope is not an abstraction

A fixed-price contract worth £60.0m. Budget at completion £48.0m. Cost incurred to date £21.6m. Control transfers over time, and progress is measured on a cost-to-cost basis.

Hold the forecast at £48.0m: progress is 21.6 ÷ 48.0 = **45.00%**, so revenue recognised is 0.4500 × 60.0 = **£27.00m**.

Move the forecast to £51.7m: progress is 21.6 ÷ 51.7 = **41.78%**, so revenue recognised is **£25.07m**.

A **£3.7m** move in a delivery forecast moved the reported number by **£1.93m**, and expected margin with it. The person who moved the forecast was a planner. The person who has to explain the movement is a financial controller. Ask which side of that crossing your credential examines, and whether anything in the syllabus examines the crossing itself.

## Question 3, expanded, because most candidates accept the number

A pass mark is not a fact about a candidate until you know how the paper was built.

A criterion-referenced cut comes from a judgement about what a just-competent candidate should be able to do, made by subject experts before the sitting. A norm-referenced cut passes a fixed share of the cohort, which means your result depends partly on who sat with you.

The number that results depends on the difficulty of the paper. A cut of 62 on a hard paper can be a higher standard than 70 on an easy one, which is why comparing two schemes by their published percentages compares nothing at all.

Ask when the cut was set, not only what it is. A body that moves it after seeing the results, with no published rule, is managing its pass rate rather than its standard.

## Our answers to all ten

**On question 1.** The Body of Knowledge is published in full and can be read before anybody pays anything. That is the check that matters most and the one fewest bodies make easy.

**On questions 6 and 7.** PCI publishes its appeals route and its certification decision policy, and you should read them rather than take this sentence for it.

**On question 8.** PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

**On question 9.** No promised salary, role or outcome. No published pass rates. No holder counts. No employer-preference claim. The 40/40/20 proportions describe the Body of Knowledge — finance and reporting, project management, governed AI — and are not an examination weighting.

**What sits behind the syllabus.** 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes. The PCI Standards are certification requirements established by the Institute; they are not law, and nothing PCI publishes is legal, tax or accounting advice.

## When the honest answer is to take a different route

If your employer mandates a specific certification, question 8 settles it before the other nine. Take the mandated one, and ask the rest afterwards out of interest.

If a regulator or a client's procurement rule requires an accredited credential, an unaccredited scheme can answer nine questions well and still fail a requirement written into a contract. Settle that first.

If your work sits wholly on one side of the finance and delivery line, a credential built for that side is the better buy. The overlap argument only earns its keep if you are the person standing in the overlap, and plenty of good careers are not.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it rude to ask all ten?**
No, and the reaction is data in itself. A body that publishes examinable requirements expects to be asked how it examines them. Evasion in place of a document is more informative than any answer would have been, and it costs you nothing to find out early.

**Which question should I ask if I only ask one?**
Question 4. Scope decides whether the credential answers the question your job actually asks, and it is the only one where an excellent answer from the wrong category still leaves you with the wrong credential.

**Does an unaccredited body automatically fail question 8?**
No. It fails if it obscures the position. Accreditation is a third-party assessment of the certifying process, and its absence is a fact to weigh against everything else you have learned rather than an automatic disqualification. A sentence built to blur the difference should disqualify a body.

**How do I ask question 10 without sounding cynical?**
Ask it as a records question, because that is what it is. How long are examination records kept, in what form, and what happens to the register if the body winds up? Any body that has taken advice on running a register will have an answer ready.

**Do these questions work outside project controls?**
Yes. Nothing in the list depends on the subject being examined. The categories change, the questions do not, and a body in any field that answers all ten without flinching has told you a great deal about how it will treat your result.
