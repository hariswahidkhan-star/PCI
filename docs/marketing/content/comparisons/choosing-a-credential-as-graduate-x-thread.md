# Four lines a graduate should be able to do: 7 posts

*X / Threads thread — 7 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. No link in the thread itself; the link goes in the first reply. X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/7 — the hook** (160 characters)
At graduate level, four lines of arithmetic get you further than a first certificate does. Here are the four lines, and then the honest answer on certification.

**Post 2/7 — the figures** (115 characters)
A package with a budget at completion of £900k. Site says 50% complete. Spend to date, accruals included, is £600k.

**Post 3/7 — the arithmetic** (157 characters)
Earned value = 50% × 900 = £450k
CPI = 450 ÷ 600 = 0.75
EAC = 900 × 600 ÷ 450 = £1.2m
VAC = £300k over budget

Four lines. Say them out loud in an interview.

**Post 4/7 — what it proves** (215 characters)
Producing the numbers is half of it. The other half is explaining what CPI 0.75 means physically, and what changes if the earning rule changes. That second half is what a first credential cannot show on your behalf.

**Post 5/7 — why the timing matters** (212 characters)
Most credentials worth holding assume working experience and examine judgement formed on real projects. That is deliberate. One sat too early proves less than the same one sat after six months of real month-ends.

**Post 6/7 — the order** (249 characters)
1. Take what your employer funds if the category is near your desk.
2. Match the category to the desk you actually have.
3. If you need accreditation now, go where it already exists.
4. If a body will not show the syllabus before payment, note that.
