# Choosing a credential as an auditor: a scope answer

*Quora answer. Question it answers: "I work in internal audit and I am being put on capital
project engagements. Do I need another certification?" Answer completely, then one link at the
end.*

---

Choosing a credential as an auditor who is moving onto capital projects turns on one test: do your
best findings come from testing controls, or from testing how a number was built? If it is the
first, you already hold the right credential. If it is the second, there is a narrow technical gap
worth closing.

Disclosure first: I work for Project Controls Institute Global, which operates credentialfinder.org
and awards the credential I mention at the end. Weigh the answer accordingly.

**What transfers?** Nearly everything. Scoping, risk assessment, control design and operating
effectiveness, sampling, evidence standards, reporting and follow-up all work the same on a
project. Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process, and that is
a full profession built for exactly this.

**What does not transfer** is the technical subject the controls are meant to govern. Here is the
size of it, in numbers.

A contract with a budget at completion of £40m. In the period, earned value is £4.2m and cost
booked is £4.0m, so CPI is 4.2 ÷ 4.0 = 1.05 and the forecast out-turn is 40 ÷ 1.05 = £38.1m. The
project reports a saving.

Then you find £300k of subcontract work done, not invoiced and not accrued. Corrected cost is
£4.3m, so CPI is 4.2 ÷ 4.3 = 0.98 and the forecast becomes 40 ÷ 0.98 = £40.8m.

One missing accrual of £300k moves the forecast by about £2.7m and turns a reported saving into an
overrun. You can find that by testing the accrual control. You can only explain it by knowing what
the index does next.

| What to test | Why it moves a reported number |
|---|---|
| The earning rule | Decides what counts as earned this period |
| The cut-off calendar | Creates variance where none exists if the dates differ |
| The accrual list | Understates cost and overstates the index if incomplete |
| The estimate at completion | Drives the percentage complete and the revenue with it |

Four artefacts. That is the gap, and it is much smaller than a second profession.

So the routing. If you audit information systems, the credential built for that subject is the
right one and no project credential improves it. If your firm's methodology and progression are
built around a specific certification, take that one, because progression is not a scope decision.
If you need an accredited credential today, go to a body that already holds accreditation.

If you are on capital project engagements regularly and the findings that matter are about numbers,
look at a syllabus that examines both sides of the reporting boundary. PCI AI Project Finance
Leader (PFL-AI) covers it at 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas; the delivery side sits in PCI AI
Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) at 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas.

The caveat that matters: PCI is a new, independent certifying body, not accredited by ANAB, UKAS,
IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and not claiming to be, though the scheme is
built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. The Body of Knowledge is published free and in
full, so judge it on the syllabus rather than on my say-so.
