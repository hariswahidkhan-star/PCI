# Tool certificates and controls credentials: the real split

A tool certificate and a controls credential answer different questions. If you need to operate a planning package well — build the network, set the calendars, load resources, cut a baseline, produce the reports — the product certificate for that software is the right thing to hold, and no method credential substitutes for it. If you need to defend the number the package produces, that is a separate subject with its own syllabus. Most careers need both, in that order.

This piece first appeared on credentialfinder.org, where [the certification categories are set out side by side](https://credentialfinder.org/best-project-controls-certification). credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. Read the rest knowing that.

## The two scopes, on the axes that matter

| Axis | Product certifications for scheduling software | A project controls credential such as PCL-AI |
|---|---|---|
| Primary object | The package: fields, settings, calculation options, outputs | The discipline: measurement, forecasting, control, reporting |
| Question answered | Can this person operate the tool correctly? | Can this person decide what the tool should be told? |
| Where the method sits | Outside the assessment | It **is** the assessment |
| Evidence of competence | You produced the model and it behaves | You can defend the number the model produced |
| Portability | Follows the product and its release cycle | Follows the published Body of Knowledge |
| Finance interface | Outside scope | Inside scope: cut-off, accrual, the tie to the ledger |
| Typical trigger | An advert names a package and wants it driven now | A role holds you to the forecast rather than the file |

Neither column is stronger than the other. They answer different questions, and the question is usually set by the role rather than by the candidate.

## What product certifications for scheduling software examine

They examine the product, and they examine it in depth: coding structures, calendars and their exceptions, relationship types and lags, constraints, resource and cost loading, baselining, the progress fields, layouts, filters and the reports the package can generate.

That is a large body of material and the assessments are built to test it seriously. Someone who holds one can take over a live model and be useful inside a week, which is precisely why employers ask for them by name.

The scope stops at the product because that is what the certification is for. It does not set out to settle what the model ought to contain, and does not claim to.

## What a project controls credential examines instead

The method the file is an expression of. How scope is broken down and budgeted, how progress is defined before it is ever measured, how earned value ties to cost at a shared cut-off, how a forecast is built, evidenced and defended, and how the result reaches a report that somebody signs.

The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) sits in that category: 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge whose proportions are 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI.

Method knowledge moves between packages and employers. Product knowledge moves with the product. Both statements are descriptions, not criticisms.

## Where the overlap sits, and why it costs money

The overlap is the moment the file stops being a planning artefact and becomes an input to money. That happens at measurement, and again at month end.

Here is one control account, and every figure can be checked in your head.

Budget at completion is £4.0m. Actual cost to date is £2.10m. The activity is 100 days long and 60 days have elapsed, so the duration-based progress field reads 60%. On site, 4,500m of 10,000m is installed, so physical progress is 45%.

Earn on the duration field: EV = 0.60 × 4.0 = **£2.40m**, so CPI = 2.40 ÷ 2.10 = **1.14**.
Earn on units installed: EV = 0.45 × 4.0 = **£1.80m**, so CPI = 1.80 ÷ 2.10 = **0.86**.

Take EAC = BAC ÷ CPI and the same £4.0m budget forecasts at **£3.50m** or **£4.67m**. The gap is £1.17m and it was produced by a field selection in a file where every entry is correct.

Knowing the package holds several progress fields, and how to switch between them, is product knowledge and a product certification covers it. Deciding that installed quantity earns and elapsed time does not — writing that rule down before the first measurement, evidencing it every period, and holding it when a subcontractor disagrees — is rules of credit, and the decision is taken away from the keyboard.

The forecast then travels. Where control transfers over time, revenue is measured by progress towards satisfying the obligation, and a cost-to-cost input method — [how progress becomes revenue](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/ifrs-15-for-construction) — puts an estimate at completion in the denominator. Move the EAC, move somebody's revenue. That is why the join between the delivery numbers and the reported ones has to belong to a syllabus rather than to whoever is free on day four of close.

## The five days that no float column shows

The same pattern shows up in the schedule, smaller and more common.

Three activities in sequence: 10 days, then 20, then 25. The forward pass finishes on day 55, and the package reports zero total float on all three, because with no project finish date entered the backward pass starts from the calculated finish. Late dates equal early dates. The arithmetic is right.

The contract date is day 50. The programme is five days late and the float column is silent, because the contract date was never in the file.

Entering a must-finish-by date takes two minutes and minus five appears down the chain. Any trained operator can do it. Knowing that the file must be told the contract date, that the date comes from a document with consequences attached, and that float ownership is usually settled in that same document, is discipline knowledge rather than product knowledge.

## What PCI does not claim

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

PCI publishes no pass rates, no salary outcomes and no holder numbers. It will not publish them until they exist and can be evidenced, because a certifying body that quotes an unverifiable number about itself has told you something about the rest of its numbers.

What it does publish, in full and before anyone pays: the Body of Knowledge, 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes. The calculation suites behind PFL-AI and PML-AI run 15,613 machine checks, all passing; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

## How to decide, honestly

**Take the product certification, not this one**, if a job advert names the package and your hands are not fast in it; if you are being handed a live model to run next month; if your employer's framework requires a specific certification for a grade; or if you need a credential from an accredited body today. Four honest cases, and a comparison that hid them would not be worth your time.

**Take a method credential** when the thing you are held to has stopped being the file. The step from producing a programme to owning a forecast is a change of subject, not a change of typing speed, and it is where most people find their preparation was thinner than they thought.

**Take both** if your career runs the way most do: the package first, because it gets you into the work, then the method, because it keeps you there when the questions get harder.

## How to judge either kind of assessment before you pay

Ask the same four questions of any provider, this one included. Can you read the whole syllabus before you pay? Is there an assessment, marked against published objectives, rather than a certificate of attendance? Is the pass standard set by a stated procedure? Can a third party verify a certificate without asking the holder?

A body that answers those four in writing is telling you what it is. A body that answers with adjectives is telling you something as well.

## FAQ

**Does a product certification for scheduling software cover earned value?**
It covers the package's earned value fields and how the calculations behave. The earning rule itself — what counts as progress, what evidence supports it, which cut-off it shares with actual cost — is a method decision taken before the software is opened. That rule is what moves a forecast, as the £1.17m example above shows.

**Will PCL-AI teach me to use a planning package?**
No, and it is not built to. It examines the discipline the file expresses, across 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. If your market runs on a particular package, learn that package and certify on it. The two assessments sit alongside each other rather than in competition, and holding one says nothing against the other.

**I already hold a tool certification. What does a method credential add?**
It changes what you can defend. Operating the model answers "is the file right"; the method answers "is the file measuring the right thing, against the right cut-off, in a way finance can use". Those are different meetings, and the second one is usually more senior.

**Is a method credential useful if I never touch the software?**
Yes, and that describes a large part of the audience: cost managers, commercial staff, PMO leads and finance business partners who read schedules and forecasts rather than build them. The arithmetic in this piece is exactly what they are asked to interpret.

**Is PCI accredited?**
No. PCI is new and independent, is not accredited by any ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and states that on every page rather than in a footnote. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles, and the Body of Knowledge is published in full so the syllabus can be judged before anyone pays.

**Why does this comparison name no credentials?**
Because the argument does not need them. What a category of certification sets out to examine is a fact about the category, checkable against any syllabus a reader cares to open, and it does not go stale when somebody else revises their scheme. Naming would add heat and no information.
