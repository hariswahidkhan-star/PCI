# Twenty days of float, priced: a planning engineer's choice

*X / Threads thread — 9 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/9 — the hook** (196 characters)
Float looks free until somebody prices it.

A package carries 20 days of total float to a sectional completion date. Time-related preliminaries run at £8,500 a day.

Priced: 20 × 8,500 = £170,000.

**Post 2/9 — split the consumption** (216 characters)
Say 12 of those days go to instructed change and 8 to a subcontractor's own performance.

Entitlement worth pursuing: 12 × 8,500 = £102,000. Recoverable only if the records were made in the month each event happened.

**Post 3/9 — the eight days nobody pays for** (193 characters)
The other 8 days are £68,000 of outturn cost with no payer attached.

That is not a scheduling number any more. It is a cost forecast number, and it keeps moving after it leaves your programme.

**Post 4/9 — one step further** (193 characters)
Follow it. Package value £24.0m, cost forecast £21.0m, so forecast margin £3.0m.

Add the £68,000: margin £2.932m.

Three tenths of a point of margin, from eight days of float that looked free.

**Post 5/9 — the credential question** (210 characters)
So here is the actual question behind a planner's credential choice.

Does the examination you are weighing follow that number past the programme, or stop where the programme stops? Both are legitimate designs.

**Post 6/9 — the categories** (246 characters)
Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis.

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery.

Each publishes a boundary.

**Post 7/9 — the sorting rule** (187 characters)
Sort by what you sign, not your job title.

Programme, logic, status, delay analysis and nothing downstream? A scheduling-focused credential examines your job, and fewer evenings buys it.

**Post 8/9 — when to stop reasoning** (217 characters)
Two cases end this early. A client's prequalification or a grading matrix names a certification: take the named one. A procurement rule requires an accredited credential: that settles it, and it settles it against us.

**Post 9/9 — ours, plainly** (258 characters)
PCI publishes its Bodies of Knowledge in full and free before anybody pays, and publishes no pass rates.
