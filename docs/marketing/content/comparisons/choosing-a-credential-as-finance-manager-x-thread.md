# Capital projects: where your reported numbers are born

*X / Threads. Eight posts, each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply, not in
post 1.*

---

**1/**
£2.4m of working capital tied up on one contract, and not a line of it was a finance decision.

Here is where it comes from.

**2/**
Monthly certified value: £1.5m.

The client pays 60 days after certification. Subcontractors are paid at 30.

One month of spend funded from your own balance sheet — about £1.5m outstanding at any time.

**3/**
Retention is 5% of certified value.

A year of certifications totalling £18.0m → 18.0 × 0.05 = £0.9m held back, released long after the work.

1.5 + 0.9 = £2.4m, before a single dispute.

**4/**
Now the part finance does not control.

Certification slips two weeks because a measurement disagreement is unresolved. The receipt slips two weeks. Exposure grows by half a month of spend — about £0.75m.

A valuation meeting ran late.

**5/**
Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control — thoroughly, and by design.

The exposure above sits upstream of all four: the estimate, the progress, the certification calendar.

**6/**
Five questions that move your month:

When was the forecast last rebuilt, not escalated?
Which earning rule made this progress?
What is instructed but not priced?
Which valuations rest on an open negotiation?
Certification calendar next quarter?

**7/**
When your own qualification is the answer:

Your path runs through the finance function → finish that one.
Projects are short and low-variance → little upstream risk.
A lender or regulated role names a qualification → take it.
