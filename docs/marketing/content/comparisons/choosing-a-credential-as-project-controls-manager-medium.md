# The credential for a project controls manager, by scope

*Republished from credentialfinder.org. Set the Medium canonical to the credentialfinder.org original linked at the foot of this piece before publishing.*

---

The credential for a project controls manager should be chosen on what you defend, not on what
you supervise. You already hold the discipline. The question is whether the next one examines the
same ground again, or the ground your signature has moved onto: a forecast a finance function
books, a contingency a board challenges, an integrated report that has to survive both.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI,
PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. The reasoning is checkable against any syllabus;
the recommendation is interested.

## What a controls manager signs, and where it travels

| What you put your name to | Which category examines it | Where it travels next |
|---|---|---|
| The integrated cost and schedule baseline | The established project controls certifications | Change control and the budget |
| The estimate at completion each period | The established project controls certifications | Reported margin and any provision |
| Contingency and risk drawdown | Cost and scheduling credentials | Board reporting and the funding position |
| Earning rules and the measurement method | Cost and scheduling credentials | Every index and forecast downstream |
| The cut-off calendar across cost and progress | Rarely examined in any single category | The accounts, directly |
| Assurance responses on controls maturity | Audit credentials, in part | Client and sponsor confidence |

Row five is the one to notice. It is examined systematically in no single category, and it is the
row that produces the arguments nobody can settle at month-end.

## Four defensible forecasts, and why the spread is the job

Budget at completion **£40.0m**. Actual cost **£18.0m**. Earned value **£16.4m**.

Cost performance index = 16.4 ÷ 18.0 = **0.911**. Take a schedule performance index of **0.95**.

| Method | Arithmetic | Forecast | What it assumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overrun was a one-off | 18.0 + (40.0 − 16.4) | **£41.6m** | Remaining work runs to budget |
| Performance to date holds | 40.0 ÷ 0.911 | **£43.9m** | The past predicts the rest |
| Cost and schedule pressure both persist | 18.0 + 23.6 ÷ (0.911 × 0.95) | **£45.3m** | Both trends continue to completion |
| Bottom-up re-estimate | 18.0 + re-priced remainder | Yours to build | The plan has been rebuilt line by line |

A spread of **£3.7m** across the first three, from one dataset, with nothing separating them but
an assumption a person chooses and defends. Selecting among them is the competence; reciting them
is not.

## Why that spread reaches the accounts

Where progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, the forecast at
completion is the denominator in that fraction.

Move the denominator and the measured percentage moves. Move the percentage and recognised
revenue moves with it. So a £3.7m forecast spread is also a spread in reported margin.

When you present £43.9m and a finance director asks what happens at £45.3m, the question is not
about arithmetic. It is about whether the difference has already been provided for.

## What each category examines, in its own terms

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
the control of delivery. That is the core of the role and those examinations treat it seriously.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis,
and the economics of an asset's life. For a manager who came up through one side, this category
is where the other side is examined.

Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the
business environment. For a controls manager moving towards a project manager's seat, that is the
shift the category is built for.

Each of the three examines its own subject thoroughly and publishes what it covers. The cut-off
calendar in row five belongs to none of them, and that is where the practical failures happen.

## What PCL-AI examines

PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) is the one aimed at this seat: 13 domains and 61 knowledge
areas, with the Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management
and 20% governed AI. That proportion describes the syllabus, not an examination weighting, which
remains a separate decision and is not published.

For a manager who already forecasts confidently, the finance and reporting share is the part
worth reading first. It is the material that decides whether you can defend the forecast in front
of the people who book it.

Across the three volumes there are 92 sector case studies, and 113 mandatory PCI Standards
carrying 532 process requirements sit underneath them.

## When another route is the right one

**If your organisation is built around a single project management method** and promotion runs
through the certification attached to it, take that certification. Fit arguments lose to internal
grading matrices, every time.

**If you are heading for a project manager or delivery director role** rather than deeper into
controls, a project management certification is the honest answer. The breadth is the point, and
depth on forecasting is not what the next job will test.

**If a client's prequalification names a credential**, that is a requirement rather than a
comparison, and there is nothing to weigh.

**If you need an accredited credential today**, go to a body that already holds accreditation.
PCI cannot meet that requirement now.

## How to decide

Take your last four forecasts and, for each, write down the assumption you chose, the challenge
you received and where the challenge came from. Three patterns usually emerge.

Challenges about method — earning rules, measurement, the estimate's build — mean the gap is
deeper controls material.

Challenges about consequence — margin, provision, cash, disclosure — mean the gap is the reporting
boundary, and more depth in method will not close it.

Challenges about credibility mean the gap is recognition rather than scope, and that is a
different kind of purchase.

## Frequently asked questions

**I already hold a controls credential. Is a second one duplication?**
It is if the second examines the same subject at a similar depth, which is a common and expensive
mistake. It is not if it examines the boundary your role has moved onto. Count the decisions you
sign that your current credential did not examine, and let that count decide.

**How much finance does a controls manager actually need?**
The handshake, not the profession: what a percentage complete becomes, when an expected loss must
be recognised in full, what an accrual is for, what a contract asset is. That is a small body of
material with a high return, and it is the part your signature reaches.

**Does moving into management change the credential I need?**
It changes who challenges you. Managing a controls team is examined by very little; defending the
team's output is examined by a good deal.

**Should I get my team certified instead of myself?**
Often, yes, and it is the better return in many organisations. A team examined against a common
standard argues less about method and more about the job. Decide it as a capability question
rather than a training-budget one.

**What if my organisation has no defined earning rules at all?**
Then that is the project to do before any credential. Write them, get them agreed with commercial
and finance, and apply them consistently for a quarter. The credential will make more sense
afterwards, and the arguments will have halved by then.
