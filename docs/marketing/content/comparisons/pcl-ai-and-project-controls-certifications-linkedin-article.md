# Where PCL-AI sits against project controls certifications

The established project controls certifications examine measurement, progress, forecasting and
the control of delivery. That is the closest category to PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI)
and the honest comparison is narrow: same discipline, different span. PCL-AI examines the same
control cycle and then keeps going, into what happens to the numbers after they leave it.

Disclosure first. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which
awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. I work for the body that awards
them. Treat this as an argument to test, not a verdict.

## The forecast that has two right answers

Take a package with a budget at completion of £8.0m. Cost to date is £4.0m. Earned value is
£3.6m.

- CPI = 3.6 ÷ 4.0 = **0.90**

Now forecast it two ways, both of them orthodox.

**Remaining work at budget.** You believe the overrun was a one-off — a bad weather month, a late
permit — and the rest will run to plan.
EAC = 4.0 + (8.0 − 3.6) = **£8.4m**

**Remaining work at the achieved rate.** You believe the past is the best guide to the future.
EAC = 8.0 ÷ 0.90 = **£8.89m**

The two answers are **£490k apart** on identical data. Nothing separates them except which
assumption about the remaining work you are willing to defend.

Every credential in the established project controls category will teach you both methods and
examine you on the arithmetic. That is exactly what the category is for.

## The question that comes next

Which of those two numbers goes into the accounts?

If the package is heading for a loss, the expected loss is recognised as soon as it is known,
and the size of it is set by the forecast method somebody in the control team picked. The
control-cycle judgement has become a reported financial number with an auditor's name on it.

That transition is what PCL-AI examines. Not because forecasting method is a finance subject —
it is not — but because the handover has rules of its own: which forecast is used for reporting
and why, what evidence supports it, who may change it, and how the change is recorded when the
number moves between the two cycles.

## The same problem in one line

Forty valves at £10,000 each. Twenty delivered, none installed.

- 0/100 earning rule → earned value **£0**
- 50/50 rule, half credited on delivery → earned value **£100k**

Same steel, same day, £100k of difference in what the project claims to have earned. Where
measured progress drives what is recognised, that difference travels straight through to the
reported position.

Somebody owns that rule. Somebody must document it, apply it consistently across packages, and
defend it when it is tested. The control side of that is examined in the established
certifications. The reporting consequence is not, and it does not need to be — the boundary is
a design decision, not a gap in the workmanship.

## What each side actually covers

| Axis | The established project controls certifications | PCL-AI |

| What it sets out to examine | Measurement, progress, forecasting and the control of delivery | The same control cycle, plus how its outputs become reported financial numbers |

## Where PCL-AI is the wrong choice

**If you want a credential from a body with a long record**, take one from the established
category. PCI does not have that record. We are new, and no amount of syllabus quality
substitutes for years of scheme operation. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

**If you need accreditation now**, go where it exists. PCI is a new, independent certifying
body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and
does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles.

**If your employer mandates a specific certification**, that settles it. A mandate is a business
requirement rather than a scope question.

**If you are already deep in control method** and your gap is elsewhere entirely — commercial
management, contract administration, systems — no credential in this comparison is your answer.

## What PCL-AI is, in the figures we can stand behind

13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. The Body of Knowledge's proportions are 40 finance and
reporting, 40 project management and 20 governed AI — the Body of Knowledge's proportions, not
an examination weighting. Across the three volumes there are 92 sector case studies, and 113 PCI
Standards carrying 532 process requirements sit behind the scheme.

You will notice what is not in that paragraph. No pass rate. No salary figure. No holder count.
No accreditation claim. We have no honest way to produce those numbers yet, so we do not print
them. Judge a new body partly by the claims it declines to make.

You will also notice there are no fees, examination lengths or difficulty rankings anywhere in
this article, for us or for anybody else. Those specifics belong to whoever sets them and change
on their timetable, and a difficulty ranking is an opinion presented as a measurement. Scope is
the axis that survives, and it is the one a reader can check against any syllabus they open.

## Who this is actually for

The controls lead whose forecast keeps being restated after it leaves them, and who cannot say
in finance's own vocabulary why the restatement happened.

The project controls manager who is asked, twice a year, to explain to an auditor why measured
progress and recognised value differ, and who assembles that answer from scratch each time.

The planner or cost engineer moving into a role where the numbers are signed rather than
submitted, and who has worked out that the second half of the job is a different subject from
the first.

If none of those is you, the established category examines control method more deeply than we
do, and depth in your own subject is worth more than breadth across somebody else's. That is
not modesty. It is the same scope argument applied honestly in the other direction.

## A test you can run this week

Take your last month-end pack. Find the estimate at completion and work out which method
produced it. Then produce the other one. If the two are within a rounding error, your project is
behaving; if they are hundreds of thousands apart, ask who chose, on what evidence, and where
that choice was written down.

Then read the syllabus of whatever credential you are considering — ours included — and check
whether it examines the answer you just found. That is a better test than any comparison
article, this one included.
