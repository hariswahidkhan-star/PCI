# Two credentials, no ranking, and the grid you fill in

*X / Threads thread — 9 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link goes in the first reply. Character counts are for production; X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/9 — the hook** (188 characters)
Nobody can rank two credentials for you. Two people can weigh the same pair honestly and reach opposite answers, because the weights belong to whoever signs the work. Build a grid instead.

**Post 2/9 — the six rows** (198 characters)
Six rows, two columns, one evening. What is examined, and how deep. What it assumes you know. Who decides the result. What it costs in hours. What changes when you hold it. What happens if you fail.

**Post 3/9 — row one, done properly** (236 characters)
Write out every decision you sign in a normal month: a forecast, a valuation, a progress percentage, an accrual. Eight to twelve lines.

Mark each one against both syllabuses — examined, assumed, excluded — and count the examined lines.

**Post 4/9 — the hours, in numbers** (226 characters)
Row four, in numbers. A extends what you already do daily: 60 hours. B is half new to you: 140 hours.

At four hours a week that is 15 weeks against 35.

Twenty weeks of difference, and no comparison table anywhere carries it.

**Post 5/9 — compare like with like** (253 characters)
Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment.

Different subjects. Not different grades.

**Post 6/9 — the first tie-break** (192 characters)
If the count ties, ask who decides the result. Where the organisation selling the preparation also awards it, the evidence is weaker whatever the syllabus says. Ask how the two are held apart.

**Post 7/9 — the second tie-break** (165 characters)
Then try to verify a stranger's credential in that scheme today, without an account. If you cannot do it in five minutes, neither can a hiring manager in four years.

**Post 8/9 — when the grid does not apply** (228 characters)
Three cases end the exercise early. A client's prequalification names one. An employer's grading matrix names one. A procurement rule requires an accredited credential.

Take the named route and stop. A requirement outranks fit.

**Post 9/9 — our own answer** (273 characters)
