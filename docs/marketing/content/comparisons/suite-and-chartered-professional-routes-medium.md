# Chartered routes and examined credentials, compared

If a client, a contract or an employer's grade structure asks for chartered standing, take the chartered route: it assesses practised competence, evidenced over years and judged by peers, and no examined credential substitutes for it. An examination answers a narrower question — can you do the work on a published syllabus, on the day. Most people who need both take them in that order.

This piece first appeared on credentialfinder.org, where [the routes are compared at category level](https://credentialfinder.org/best-certification-for-planning-engineers). credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. That ownership should be visible before you weigh anything below, not discovered afterwards.

## Two instruments, on the axes that matter

| Axis | Chartered routes in surveying and engineering | Examined credentials such as the PCI suite |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Have you practised competently? | Can you do the work on this syllabus? |
| Instrument | Evidence portfolio and professional review by peers | Written examination against published objectives |
| Direction of view | Backwards, across years of responsible work | Forwards, from a declared scope to a date |
| Basis of judgement | A competence framework applied by assessors | Domains and knowledge areas published in advance |
| Evidence it rests on | Work you did, attributed and defended | Answers you gave, marked consistently |
| What it confers | Chartered standing, asked for by name in some markets and on some contracts | A statement of the scope a holder was examined on |
| Where the subject sits | The discipline's own competence framework | The join between the money and the delivery |
| Time shape | Logged experience first, assessment after | Study, then examination, at a time you choose |

Neither column outranks the other. They are answers to different questions, and reading the row that matters to your next role is more useful than ranking the columns.

## What chartered routes assess

Practised competence, evidenced and peer-judged. A candidate accumulates responsible experience, maps it against a published competence framework, assembles evidence that the work was genuinely theirs, writes it up, and defends it in front of assessors who have done comparable work.

The instrument is a review, not a paper. Its central question is whether a professional peer would trust the candidate's judgement on similar work tomorrow, and the answer rests on what the candidate has actually done.

Chartered standing carries obligations that outlast the assessment: a code of conduct, a duty to practise within your competence, and continuing development. Those obligations are part of what the standing means. A body that assesses in this way is making a statement about a person's practice, which is a different kind of statement from "was tested on the following material".

## What an examined credential assesses

A declared scope, marked against published objectives, on a date. The syllabus is published in advance, every candidate meets the same paper, and the result states what was examined.

The strength of the instrument is precision. A reader of your profile knows exactly which body of material you have been tested on, which a portfolio review does not set out to convey. The limit is equally plain: a paper cannot tell anyone whether you ran a difficult job well for six years.

The PCI credentials are examined credentials. PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI cover 13 / 16 / 16 domains and 61 / 61 / 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge whose proportions are 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI. They do not confer chartered standing and are not offered in place of it.

## Where the overlap sits: a month with three right answers

The reason an examined scope can add something to a chartered professional is easiest to see in arithmetic. Every figure here can be checked in your head.

Fixed price contract £24.0m. Total expected cost £20.0m, so expected margin £4.0m. Costs incurred to date £11.6m.

Cost-to-cost progress: 11.6 ÷ 20.0 = **58%**.
Revenue: 0.58 × 24.0 = **£13.92m**. Margin recognised to date: 0.58 × 4.0 = £2.32m.
Certified valuation for the same month: **£12.60m**.
Retention at 5%: £0.63m withheld, so **£11.97m** is payable.

Three numbers for one month's work. The £1.32m between revenue and certification is a contract asset: measured into revenue, not yet an unconditional right to payment. The £1.95m between revenue and cash is that contract asset plus retention.

Each number is right in its own frame. Measurement produces the valuation. Progress towards satisfying the obligation produces the revenue. The payment terms produce the cash. Nobody in the chain has made an error, and somebody still has to explain all three to a director in one sentence.

## The tail nobody puts on the slide

Carry the same job to completion and the retention rate does something worth noticing. At 5% of £24.0m, £1.20m is withheld across the contract. Expected margin is £4.0m.

**Thirty per cent of the job's entire expected profit sits in retention**, released on a schedule that has nothing to do with the progress curve and everything to do with practical completion and the defects period. A project can be complete, profitable and reported as such while a third of its margin is still somebody else's cash.

Measuring the work correctly does not produce that insight. Neither does knowing the recognition rules alone. It comes from holding both at once, which is a scope question rather than a talent question — and [an S-curve that holds up](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/project-cash-flow-forecasting) is where it becomes visible early enough to matter.

## What PCI does not claim

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

It does not confer chartered standing. It does not assess a portfolio in place of an examination. It publishes no pass rates, no salary outcomes and no holder numbers, because none of those can yet be evidenced and a certifying body that quotes an unverifiable figure about itself has told you something about the rest of its figures.

What is published in full, before anyone pays: the Body of Knowledge, 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes. The calculation suites behind PFL-AI and PML-AI run 15,613 machine checks, all passing; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

## How to decide, honestly

**Take the chartered route, not this**, if chartered standing is asked for anywhere in your market; if you want peer-assessed professional status in your own discipline; if you are close enough to submission that your hours belong to the portfolio; or if you need a credential from an accredited body today.

**Take an examined credential** when the gap is material rather than status: when your reports feed revenue, forecast and provision decisions and you have never been formally tested on that side of them.

**Take both** if your career runs the way many do — chartered in the discipline you practise, examined on the crossing point where your numbers turn into someone else's accounts.

## How to judge either assessment before you pay

The same four questions work on any provider, this one included.

Can you read the whole syllabus or competence framework before you commit? Is there an assessment marked against published criteria, rather than a certificate for attending? Is the standard for passing set by a stated procedure rather than a number somebody chose? Can a third party verify a certificate without asking the holder to send a scan?

Bodies that answer those in writing are telling you what they are. Bodies that answer with adjectives are also telling you something.

## FAQ

**Does an examined credential count towards chartered standing?**
Assume not unless the assessing body says otherwise in writing. Chartered routes rest on evidenced, responsible experience judged against a competence framework, and study is normally supporting material rather than credit. Ask the body that will assess you and keep the written answer, because marketing from anywhere else is not evidence.

**Is chartered standing better than a certification?**
They answer different questions, so "better" depends entirely on what is being asked for. Chartered standing says peers judged your practice competent. An examination says you were tested on a declared scope. Someone who wants one and is offered the other tends to notice within a minute of the conversation starting.

**I am already chartered. Why would I sit an examination?**
For a tested scope in material your framework was not built to cover. If your reports feed revenue, forecast and provision decisions, the crossing point is the gap most often left open, and the three-number month above is what an open gap looks like on a real job.

**Does PCI offer chartered standing?**
No. PCI awards examined credentials and says so plainly. It is a new, independent body, not accredited by any ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and it publishes its Body of Knowledge in full so the syllabus can be judged before anyone pays. Anyone who needs chartered standing should pursue a chartered route.

**Which of the three PCI credentials fits a quantity surveyor or commercial manager?**
The finance-facing one, usually: PFL-AI covers 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas around the money side of delivery. Someone running programme and cost together tends towards PCL-AI. The Body of Knowledge is published, so the honest answer is to read the domain list rather than take a recommendation from the body selling it.

**Why does this comparison name no credentials?**
Because the argument does not need them. What a category of assessment sets out to do is a fact about the category, checkable by any reader against any scheme they care to open, and it stays true when somebody revises their syllabus next year. Naming would add heat and no information.
