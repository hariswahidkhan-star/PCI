# Picking the credential for a project controls manager

Picking the credential for a project controls manager is a scope decision rather than a prestige
one. You already hold the discipline. The only question worth asking is whether the next
certificate examines the ground you already cover, or the ground your signature has quietly moved
onto — the forecast a finance function books, the contingency a board challenges.

Disclosure first. credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which
awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials mentioned later. I write for the body that
awards them. Discount this accordingly and go and read a syllabus yourself.

## The month I stopped arguing about the forecast

The argument I remember was not about method. Every method in the room was correct.

Budget at completion **£40.0m**. Actual cost to date **£18.0m**. Earned value **£16.4m**.

Cost performance index = 16.4 ÷ 18.0 = **0.911**. Schedule performance index, **0.95**.

| Method | Arithmetic | Forecast | What it assumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overrun was a one-off | 18.0 + (40.0 − 16.4) | **£41.6m** | Remaining work runs to budget |
| Performance to date holds | 40.0 ÷ 0.911 | **£43.9m** | The past predicts the rest |
| Cost and schedule pressure persist | 18.0 + 23.6 ÷ (0.911 × 0.95) | **£45.3m** | Both trends run to completion |
| Bottom-up re-estimate | 18.0 + re-priced remainder | Yours to build | The plan has been rebuilt line by line |

A spread of **£3.7m** across the first three, from one dataset, with nothing between them but an
assumption a person chooses and then defends.

Choosing among them is the competence. Reciting the four formulas is not, and every controls
manager I know worked that out within a year of taking the job.

## Why the argument was really a reporting argument

Here is the part I had not been examined on.

Where progress is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, the forecast at
completion is the denominator in that fraction. Move the denominator and the measured percentage
moves. Move the percentage and recognised revenue moves with it.

So the £3.7m spread was never only a forecast spread. It was a spread in reported margin, and the
finance director in that meeting knew it before I did.

The question I got was not "how did you calculate £43.9m". It was "what happens if it is £45.3m,
and has that been provided for". Those are different questions and only one of them is answered
by controls method.

## What each category sets out to examine

**The established project controls certifications** examine measurement, progress, forecasting
and the control of delivery. That is the core of the role, and those examinations treat it
seriously.

**Cost and scheduling credentials** examine estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis
and the economics of an asset's life. For a manager who came up through one side of that, this is
where the other side gets examined.

**Project management certifications** examine managing projects across people, process and the
business environment. For a controls manager heading towards a delivery seat, that is exactly the
shift the category was built for.

Each of the three examines its own subject thoroughly and publishes what it covers. None of them
is deficient for leaving out somebody else's subject. That is what a scope boundary is.

## The row that belongs to nobody

The cut-off calendar sits in none of those three.

Which date the progress is cut on. Which date the ledger is cut on. Whether they are the same
date. What happens to an index when they are five days apart, and who is allowed to restate it
afterwards.

That is not a scheduling question or an accounting question. It is the join, and I have watched
three functions spend a week on it every month-end because nobody in the room had ever been
examined on the handover itself.

## What PCL-AI examines, and what it does not claim

PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) is aimed at this seat: 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas,
with the Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20%
governed AI. Those proportions describe the syllabus. They are not an examination weighting, and
the blueprint remains a separate and unpublished decision.

For a manager who already forecasts confidently, the finance and reporting share is the part
worth opening first. It is the material that decides whether you can hold the number in front of
the people who book it.

Behind the three volumes sit 92 sector case studies and 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532
process requirements.

Now the part that matters more. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by
ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The
scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge
before you decide anything.

We publish no pass rate, no salary figure and no holder count. There is no honest way to produce
them yet, and inventing them is the one thing that would finish a certifying body.

## When the answer is a different category

**If your employer's grading matrix names a certification for the band you want, take that one.**
Fit arguments lose to internal matrices, every time, and the person who wrote the matrix is not
in your reporting line.

**If a client's prequalification lists a credential, that is a requirement rather than a
comparison.** There is nothing to weigh.

**If you need an accredited credential today**, go to a body that already holds accreditation.
PCI cannot meet that requirement now, and saying otherwise would be the kind of claim this
article exists to avoid.

**If you are heading for a delivery director's seat rather than deeper into controls**, a project
management certification is the honest answer. The breadth is the point, and forecasting depth is
not what the next job will test.

## How to decide in one evening

Take your last four forecasts. For each one, write down the assumption you chose, the challenge
you received, and where the challenge came from.

Challenges about method — earning rules, measurement, how the estimate was built — mean the gap is
deeper controls material.

Challenges about consequence — margin, provision, cash, disclosure — mean the gap is at the
reporting boundary, and no amount of extra depth in method will close it.

Challenges about credibility mean the gap is recognition rather than scope, which is a different
purchase entirely and worth being honest with yourself about.

## Frequently asked questions

**I already hold a controls credential. Is a second one duplication?**
It is, if the second examines the same subject at a similar depth. That is a common and expensive
mistake. It is not duplication if it examines the boundary your role has moved onto. Count the
decisions you sign that your current credential never examined, and let the count decide.

**How much finance does a controls manager actually need?**
The handshake, not the profession. What a percentage complete becomes, when an expected loss must
be recognised in full, what an accrual is for, what a contract asset is. That is a small body of
material with a high return, and it is the part your signature reaches.

**Does moving into management change the credential I need?**
It changes who challenges you. Managing a controls team is examined by very little. Defending the
team's output is examined by a good deal, and that is the part the step up actually adds.

**Should I certify my team instead of myself?**
Often, yes. A team examined against a common standard argues less about method and more about the
job. Treat it as a capability decision rather than a training-budget one.

**What if we have no defined earning rules at all?**
Then that is the project to do first. Write them, agree them with commercial and finance, and
apply them consistently for a quarter. Any syllabus will make more sense afterwards, and half the
arguments will have gone.
