# Seven questions to ask before you pay for an examination

*X / Threads thread — 8 posts, each under 280 characters and each able to stand alone. The link sits in the first reply, not in the thread. X counts any URL as 23 characters.*

**Post 1/8 — the hook** (204 characters)
A 95% pass rate and a 28.5% pass rate can describe the same cohort.
400 enrol. 120 sit the exam. 114 pass.
114 ÷ 120 = 95%.
114 ÷ 400 = 28.5%.
Both true. One gets printed, and rarely with its denominator.

**Post 2/8 — three words people merge** (240 characters)
Examinable: there is a declared scope and an assessment against it.
Accredited: a third party oversees the certifying body against a standard.
Recognised: someone says so — ask who, in what document, and what follows.
Three claims, not one.

**Post 3/8 — questions 1 and 2** (235 characters)
1. Can I read the whole syllabus before paying? Domains and knowledge areas, not a topic list.
2. Is there an assessment, or attendance?
A certificate that proves you were in the room proves you were in the room. That is all it claims.

**Post 4/8 — questions 3 and 4** (224 characters)
3. Is marking criterion-referenced — answers judged against published objectives, same paper for every candidate?
4. How is the pass standard set, by what procedure, and by whom?
Ask for the method. You do not need the mark.

**Post 5/8 — questions 5 and 6** (215 characters)
5. How are candidates identified and sittings supervised, in writing?
6. Are appeals and retakes published, with a time limit and a named route?
"Contact us" is not a policy. It is a hope that you will not need one.

**Post 6/8 — question 7** (227 characters)
7. Can a third party verify a certificate without asking the holder for a scan?
The value of a credential is what somebody else can check. If verification depends on the holder emailing a PDF, forgery is undetectable by design.

**Post 7/8 — when to walk away** (251 characters)
If a client contract, an employer framework or a regulator requires an accredited credential, examinability is not enough. Take the accredited route.
And if nobody will put the answers in writing, you are being asked to trust what they will not state.

**Post 8/8 — where this Institute stands** (260 characters)
PCI publishes its Body of Knowledge in full before anyone pays: 113 Standards, 532 process requirements, 92 sector case studies.
No pass rate, no salary claims, no holder numbers.
PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it.
