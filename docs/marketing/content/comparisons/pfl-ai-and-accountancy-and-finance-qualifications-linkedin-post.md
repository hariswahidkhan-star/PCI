# Accountancy and finance qualifications: where PFL-AI sits

*LinkedIn post. No link in the body — it goes in the first comment.*

---

£100k of reported margin turned on one delivery note last month. Nobody did anything wrong.

The contract: £10m of revenue, £8m of budgeted cost, £2m of margin. Progress measured by cost incurred against total expected cost.

Cost to date £4.0m → 50% complete → revenue £5.0m → margin recognised £1.0m.

Then £400k of switchgear arrives on site and lands in cost. It is not installed.

Include it and progress is 4.4 ÷ 8.0 = 55%, so revenue is £5.5m and margin recognised is £1.1m.

Treat those materials as not depicting progress, and the honest answer is revenue of £5.4m against cost of £4.4m — margin still £1.0m.

Same site. Same steel. £100k of difference in reported margin, decided by whether anyone checked what "delivered" meant.

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure, financial analysis and control. They examine the treatment question properly, and are built to. What they do not set out to examine is whether the site figure feeding the calculation reflects the site — that comes out of the control cycle.

PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) is built on that join: 16 domains, 61 knowledge areas.

To be plain about what it is not. PFL-AI is not an accountancy qualification, is not a route into practice, and confers no right to sign anything. If that is what you need, take an accountancy qualification and do not let anyone talk you out of it.
