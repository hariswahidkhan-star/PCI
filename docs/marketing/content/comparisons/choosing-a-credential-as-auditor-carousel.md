# Auditing capital projects: eleven slides on the gap

*LinkedIn document carousel. 11 slides at 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the
arithmetic, because slide 3 is the one people screenshot.*

---

### Slide 1 — cover
**Same evidence. Two write-ups.**
One goes in the appendix. One goes in the executive summary.
*Alt text: title slide reading "Same evidence, two write-ups — one goes in the appendix, one goes
in the executive summary".*

### Slide 2 — the sample
£6.0m of progress claimed in the period.

You sample **30** progress claims.

**Four** have no measurement record behind them.
*Alt text: slide describing a sample of thirty progress claims of which four have no measurement
record.*

### Slide 3 — the arithmetic
4 ÷ 30 = **13.3%**

6.0 × 0.133 ≈ **£0.80m** of progress claimed without evidence underneath it.
*Alt text: calculation slide showing a 13.3 per cent exception rate extrapolated to roughly
£0.80m of unevidenced progress.*

### Slide 4 — write-up A
"Records were missing."

Accurate. Closed with a revised template.

No forecast moves.
*Alt text: slide showing the weaker version of the finding, expressed as missing records.*

### Slide 5 — write-up B, the chain
Unsupported progress → inflated earned value → higher index → lower forecast out-turn → higher
percentage complete → more revenue recognised.

Same evidence. Different finding.
*Alt text: slide tracing unevidenced progress through the index and the forecast into recognised
revenue.*

### Slide 6 — what audit credentials examine
Governance, risk, control and the assurance process.

A full profession with its own standards, and it transfers to capital projects intact.
*Alt text: slide listing what audit credentials set out to examine.*

### Slide 7 — what does not transfer
The technical subject those controls govern: how progress is measured, how a forecast is built,
which numbers can be tested against evidence.

A scope boundary is a design decision.
*Alt text: slide stating that the technical project subject sits outside the audit category by
design.*

### Slide 8 — the four artefacts
**The earning rule.** One per work type, applied consistently.
**The cut-off calendar.** Progress and cost measured to the same date.
**The accrual list.** Work done and uninvoiced, captured and supportable.
**The estimate at completion.** Rebuilt or escalated, and when last challenged.
*Alt text: slide listing four project artefacts an auditor can test with evidence.*

### Slide 9 — where the PCI credentials sit
**PFL-AI:** 16 domains, 61 knowledge areas, the reporting boundary.
**PCL-AI:** 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas, the delivery side.
*Alt text: slide summarising PFL-AI and PCL-AI by their domain and knowledge-area counts.*

### Slide 10 — when this is not for you
You audit information systems → the credential built for that subject is the right one.

Your firm's progression names a credential → take that one.

You need an accredited credential today → go where accreditation exists. PCI is new, independent,
not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and not claiming
to be.
*Alt text: slide listing three situations in which an existing audit credential is the right
answer, with PCI's accreditation position stated plainly.*

### Slide 11 — what to do, and the disclosure
Take your last three project engagements. For each, mark whether the best finding came from
control testing or from understanding how the number was built.

Then read the syllabus — ours is free, in full, before anyone pays.

*credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PFL-AI and
PCL-AI.*
*Alt text: closing slide with the ownership disclosure and an exercise on the reader's last three
engagements.*
