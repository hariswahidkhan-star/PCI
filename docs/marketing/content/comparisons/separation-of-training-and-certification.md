# Separation of training and certification, and why it exists

If the organisation that took your training fee also decides whether you passed, the certificate is evidence about the course rather than about you. Separation of training and certification is what makes a third party willing to rely on the result. It is also the single easiest thing to check before paying.

> **Who publishes this page.** credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. The test below applies to PCI, and the answer is set out plainly further down.

## The conflict, stated without drama

A training organisation is paid to teach and is judged on satisfaction. A certifying body is paid to assess and is judged on whether its decisions hold up.

Put both roles in one commercial unit and the incentives point one way. Failing a candidate produces a complaint, a refund request and a worse review. Passing one produces a testimonial. Nobody has to be dishonest for that pressure to shape outcomes over a few hundred candidates.

This is not an argument against training. Training is where competence is built, and a good course is worth more to most candidates than the examination fee. It is an argument about who signs the decision at the end.

## What separation of training and certification looks like

| Signal | Separated | Not separated |
|---|---|---|
| Who marks or decides | A function that does not deliver preparation | "Your tutor assesses your progress" |
| Fee structure | Examination fee distinct from any course fee | One price covering "course and certification" |
| Examiner relationship | The person deciding has not taught you | The assessor is the trainer |
| Failure | Documented resit rules, waiting period, feedback | Not addressed anywhere on the site |
| Preparation providers | Anyone may prepare candidates; the body does not require its own course | The body's own course is compulsory |
| Guarantees | None, because the outcome is not the body's to promise | "Pass guarantee" |

The last row deserves a sentence of its own. A pass guarantee is a refund policy dressed as an outcome. If the body can guarantee the result, the result was never in question, and a credential nobody can fail is a receipt.

## The test that takes one email

Ask two questions. **Who decides whether I passed, and do they report to the people who sold me the preparation?** Then: **what are the resit rules?**

A body that has genuinely failed candidates has written resit rules, because it needed them. A body that has never failed anybody has nothing to send you.

That is not a trick. It is a request for documents any certifying function will already have. Eight further questions worth asking in the same email are set out in [ten questions to ask before you pay](https://credentialfinder.org/questions-to-ask-a-certifying-body).

## Why it matters to the person reading your CV

Certification exists so that somebody who has never worked with you can rely on a claim about your competence. That reliance is only rational if the assessment was made by a party with nothing to gain from the answer.

Where the trainer decides, the reader of your CV is effectively being asked to trust a supplier's report on its own delivery. Most experienced hiring managers know this, which is why the question "who awarded it?" is asked more often than candidates expect.

The distinction between a credential and a record of attendance turns on the same point, and is set out in [how to tell a credential from a certificate of attendance](https://credentialfinder.org/credential-or-certificate-of-attendance).

## What separation costs, honestly

It costs candidates certainty. A separated body cannot tell you that you will pass, cannot smooth a marginal result, and will not adjust a decision because you bought preparation from an approved provider.

It costs the body revenue. Compulsory courses are a reliable income line, and giving them up in favour of an open preparation market means competitors may prepare your candidates better than you do.

It also costs the body a marketing claim. "97% of our candidates pass" is unavailable to an organisation that has separated the two functions and refuses to publish figures it cannot yet stand behind.

Where preparation is genuinely open, the question becomes which preparation is any good. The hub sets out [what an online course actually delivers](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/project-controls-course-online), including how to judge one when the certifying body is not selling it.

## PCI's position, stated plainly

The people who prepare candidates do not decide whether they passed. Preparation is not compulsory, no provider is required, and buying preparation from anybody does not affect a certification decision.

The standard is criterion-referenced, so a candidate is measured against a defined level of competence rather than against other candidates, and no outcome is guaranteed to anyone.

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

The Body of Knowledge is published in full and free before payment, which is the other half of an open preparation market: candidates cannot prepare independently if the syllabus is behind the till.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it wrong for a certifying body to publish study material?**
No. Publishing a syllabus, sample items and guidance is part of running a fair examination, and hiding them would advantage candidates who can afford a course. The line is between publishing material everybody can use and selling preparation whose purchase influences the decision.

**What if the only preparation available is from the certifying body?**
That happens with new or small schemes and is not automatically a conflict, provided the decision function is separate and preparation is not compulsory. Ask whether independent providers are permitted, and whether a candidate who prepares alone is at any disadvantage in the process.

**Does separation mean the examination is harder?**
It means the result is not negotiable. Difficulty comes from the syllabus and the standard-setting method rather than from the org chart. What separation removes is the possibility that a marginal decision is settled by the commercial relationship.

**How do I check this for a credential I already hold?**
Look at who signed the decision and whether that function is distinct from the training arm. If it is not, the credential still records what you learnt; it is simply weaker as independent evidence, and it is worth pairing with something assessed at arm's length.

**Why do so many schemes bundle course and certificate?**
Because bundling sells well and removes the candidate's fear of failure. It is a coherent commercial model and it produces a document. It does not produce independent evidence, and the difference matters most at the moment somebody else has to rely on it.
