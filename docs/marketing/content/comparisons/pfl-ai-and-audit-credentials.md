# Audit credentials: where PFL-AI sits alongside them

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process, and they are subject-agnostic on purpose. If your career is assurance, that is your credential. PFL-AI examines the project numbers an assurance process is pointed at: the earning rule, the forecast, the cut-off. They fit together more often than they compete.

> **Who publishes this page.** credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. Read it as a scope comparison from an interested party, which is what it is.

Categories are described by what they examine. No awarding body is named, and none is linked.

## What audit credentials examine

Audit credentials examine governance, risk and control, and the process by which assurance is obtained: planning an engagement, assessing risk, designing tests, sampling, evidence, reporting, and the independence that makes the opinion worth anything.

The category is built to be portable. The same method applies to a payroll cycle, a data centre, a treasury function or a construction contract, and that portability is the point rather than a limitation.

What the category does not set out to examine is the technical subject matter of any one domain. An auditor testing a progress claim is examining whether a control operated, not deriving what the correct percentage of completion should have been. Those are different questions, and the second one has its own literature.

| Scope axis | Audit credentials | PFL-AI |
|---|---|---|
| Core subject | Governance, risk, control, the assurance process | The project financial numbers and the controls that produce them |
| Relationship to the subject matter | Independent of it, by design | Inside it |
| Portability | Applies across every function and industry | Capital projects and contract delivery |
| Evidence of competence | Written examination plus supervised experience in most routes | Scenario examination, single best answer, remotely proctored |
| Deliberately out of scope | Deriving the technically correct figure in a specialist domain | Forming or signing an assurance opinion |
| Where they meet | The control over how progress is claimed and approved | The rule that decides what progress means |

## What PFL-AI examines

The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) has 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, with a Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20% governed AI. Those are syllabus proportions and not an examination weighting.

PFL-AI does not qualify anybody to give an opinion, and holding it is not an assurance qualification. It examines what an assurance engagement on a capital project is looking at: how the earning rule was chosen, how the forecast was built, what happened at the cut-off, who approved the claim, and what evidence exists for a number a model helped produce.

The PCI Standards behind the scheme run to 113 mandatory Standards carrying 532 process requirements, which is the level of specificity an assurance programme can actually test against.

## Where the overlap sits, with the arithmetic

An assurance test on progress claims, worked through.

A contractor books **£18.0m** of progress claims across **240** claims in a year. The reviewer samples **30 claims** by value, worth **£3.0m**, and tests each against physical evidence.

Three claims, worth **£0.60m** between them, are supported by evidence that shows progress overstated by an average of **8 percentage points**.

Overstatement found in the sample = 0.08 × 0.60 = **£0.048m**.
Projected to the population by value = 0.048 × (18.0 ÷ 3.0) = **£0.29m**.

That £0.29m is the projected misstatement, and deciding whether it matters is an assurance judgement made against materiality and the rest of the evidence. The method is exactly what an audit credential examines, and it is examined well.

Now the second question, which the test cannot answer. **What should the percentage have been?**

Answering it requires the contract's earning rule, the treatment of material delivered but not installed, and the measurement basis being applied consistently across all 240 claims. If the rule is cost-to-cost and **£0.40m** of a **£3.60m** cost base is uninstalled steel, the correct progress on that package is 3.20 ÷ 7.60 = **42.1%**, not 3.60 ÷ 8.00 = **45%**, and a claim at 45% is overstated by 2.9 points before anybody looks at the site.

| Question | Whose examination covers it |
|---|---|
| Did the approval control operate, and can I evidence that? | Audit credentials, thoroughly |
| Is £0.29m projected misstatement material here? | Audit credentials, thoroughly |
| What is the defensible percentage under this contract's earning rule? | Project financial and controls credentials |
| Who signed the number a model drafted, and on what evidence? | Sits between the two, and is examined by neither by tradition |

Nobody in that table is doing their job badly. The last row is simply new, and a project needs somebody who can answer all four. ## Five controls on a capital project that are worth testing

Whatever credential you hold, these five are where project reporting usually fails, and each one is testable with evidence that already exists.

**The earning rule is documented, contract by contract, and applied consistently.** Where it changed mid-contract, there is a written reason and the comparatives were restated.

**Progress is approved by somebody who did not claim it.** Segregation here is ordinary practice everywhere else and is routinely absent on site.

**Cut-off instructions were issued, dated, and evidenced.** Goods received and not invoiced were identified from receipt records rather than from memory.

**The forecast was challenged by somebody other than its author,** and the challenge is on file. A forecast nobody argued with has not been reviewed.

**Machine-assisted outputs are logged.** Where a model drafted a commentary or proposed a figure, the file shows what it produced, who reviewed it and what changed.

None of those five is exotic, and the evidence for each is a document rather than an opinion. A programme built on them tests the reporting that management actually relies on, rather than the reporting that is easiest to sample.

## What PCI does not claim

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

No pass rates, no salary figures, no holder numbers are published on this site. A body at founding stage would be inventing them, and the first invented number is the last one anybody believes.

## How to decide

**Take an audit credential** if your career is assurance, internal audit, risk or control. It is the qualification for that work, it is portable across every industry, and nothing in a project-specific syllabus replaces it.

**Take an audit credential** if the systems you examine are technology, treasury, payroll or compliance rather than capital projects. A project-specific credential would be the wrong tool, and saying so is more useful than a sales page.

**Satisfy the mandate first** if your professional body or employer requires a particular qualification to hold your role. Add scope afterwards.

**Consider PFL-AI** if you audit or assure capital projects and keep meeting the technical question behind the control question: what the percentage should have been, whether the forecast is credible, what the cut-off did to the period. The role-level version is in [choosing a credential as an auditor moving into projects](https://credentialfinder.org/choosing-a-credential-as-auditor), and the recognition side of the same seam is covered in [the accountancy scope comparison](https://credentialfinder.org/pfl-ai-and-accountancy-and-finance-qualifications).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does PFL-AI let me sign an assurance opinion?**
No. It is not an assurance qualification, confers no opinion-signing rights and does not claim any. It examines the project financial content that an assurance engagement is pointed at, which is subject knowledge rather than assurance method.

**I audit capital projects already. What would be new?**
The technical derivation rather than the control test: how an earning rule is selected against a contract, how uninstalled material is treated, how a forecast is constructed and challenged, and what governance a machine-assisted figure needs. Read the domain list against your last three engagements and see how many rows you would have wanted.

**Is sampling enough to find a forecast problem?**
Sampling finds control failures and projects them. A forecast problem is usually not a control failure at all: every claim can be properly approved and the forecast still be wrong, because the earning rule or the estimate to complete is unsound. Those are technical questions rather than evidence questions.

**Should an auditor hold a project credential to be credible on site?**
Not necessarily. Independence and method are the basis of an audit opinion, not domain seniority. Domain knowledge changes the quality of the questions rather than the standing of the opinion, and plenty of excellent project assurance is done without it.

**What does the governed AI content add for an assurance reader?**
A testable position. If a model drafted a variance commentary or proposed a forecast, an assurance programme needs to know what the model was permitted to decide, what data it saw, who reviewed the output and what evidence remains. Those are ordinary control questions applied to a new input.
