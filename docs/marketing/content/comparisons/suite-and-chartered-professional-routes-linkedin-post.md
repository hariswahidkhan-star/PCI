# Three right answers to how much work has been done

*LinkedIn text post — 1,891 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Three people gave three different answers to how much work had been done last month.
All three were right, and the gaps between them ran to £1.95m.

Worked case. Fixed price £24.0m. Total expected cost £20.0m, so margin £4.0m.

Costs incurred to date: £11.6m.
11.6 ÷ 20.0 = 58% complete on a cost-to-cost measure.
Revenue: 0.58 × 24.0 = **£13.92m**.

The certified valuation for the same month is **£12.60m**. Retention at 5% holds back £0.63m, so **£11.97m** is payable.

Recognised £13.92m. Certified £12.60m. Collectible £11.97m. The £1.32m between the first two is a contract asset: work measured into revenue that is not yet an unconditional right to payment.

Three rulebooks, three questions, one month.

Chartered routes in surveying and engineering assess whether you practise competently: structured experience over years, a portfolio of evidence, a professional review by peers against a competence framework, and a code of conduct you sign up to. That is assessed competence. If a client, a role or a grade asks for chartered standing, nothing examined substitutes for it — including anything below.

An examination answers a narrower question: can you do the work on a published syllabus, on the day, marked against published objectives.

The PCI credentials are the second kind. PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI examine 13 / 16 / 16 domains and 61 / 61 / 63 knowledge areas, on a Body of Knowledge weighted 40 / 40 / 20 across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI. The £1.32m above lives in that join.

credentialfinder.org, where the fuller comparison sits, is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards those three credentials. PCI is new and not accredited — read the syllabus and judge it. And if you are going for chartered standing this year, put the hours into the portfolio first.
