# Cost and scheduling credentials: where PCL-AI sits

*Republished from credentialfinder.org. Set the Medium canonical to
https://credentialfinder.org/pcl-ai-and-cost-and-scheduling-credentials before publishing.*

---

If your work is estimating, planning or cost control, a cost and scheduling credential examines
that subject in more depth than any broad credential will. If your recurring difficulty is the
point where a delivery number becomes a reported number, that sits outside the category by
design, and PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) is built around it. Decide on scope, not on
reputation.

credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI,
PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. Read the rest knowing who wrote it.

## What cost and scheduling credentials examine

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning, schedule analysis
and the economics of the asset life cycle.

That means the method itself: how an estimate is built and classed, how contingency is set, how
a network behaves when the logic changes, how progress is measured, how a forecast is produced
and then defended in a room that does not want to hear it.

It is a deep technical body of material. Somebody who has been examined on it can do things that
a general project manager cannot, and the category is built to examine it properly.

## What PCL-AI examines

PCL-AI covers 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. It examines the same control cycle, and then
the handover: how progress becomes revenue, how a commitment becomes an accrual, how a forecast
at completion becomes a reported margin, and what governs the use of AI inside that cycle.

The Body of Knowledge's proportions are 40 finance and reporting, 40 project management and 20
governed AI. Those are the Body of Knowledge's proportions, not an examination weighting. Behind
the three volumes sit 92 sector case studies and 113 PCI Standards carrying 532 process
requirements.

## Where the overlap sits

Here is the join, with arithmetic you can check without a spreadsheet.

A pipeline package. 1,000 metres at £1,000 a metre. Budget at completion £1.0m.

The planner cuts the schedule on the 25th, because the progress sheets come back then and the
report has to be drafted. 800 metres are in the ground, so earned value is £800k.

Finance cuts the ledger on the 30th, because that is the accounting period. £950k of cost is
booked, including the five days of work that went in after the planner stopped counting.

Side by side, as the monthly pack shows them:

- CPI = 800 ÷ 950 = **0.84**
- EAC = 1,000 ÷ 0.84 = **£1.19m**
- Forecast overrun: **£190k**

Recut both to the 30th. 950 metres in the ground, £950k earned against £950k spent:

- CPI = **1.00**
- EAC = **£1.0m**

Nothing on that site changed. Two functions each did their own job correctly and the pack
reported a £190k problem that did not exist.

Now the second version. Forty valves at £10,000 each sit in the same package. Twenty are
delivered, none installed. Under a 0/100 earning rule they earn £0. Under a 50/50 rule, with
half credited on delivery, they earn £100k. Same steel, same day, £100k of difference — and
where measured progress drives what gets recognised, that difference reaches the reported
position.

Neither problem is a scheduling problem or a cost engineering problem. Revenue recognition sits
outside the scope of a scheduling credential, as it does for every scheduling credential. That
is a fact about design. These credentials examine their own subject thoroughly and were built to.

## The accreditation statement

PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to
ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

We also publish no pass rate, no salary uplift and no holder count. We have no honest way to
produce those figures yet, and a body that tells you what it cannot claim is telling you the
truth about what it can.

## How to decide

**Take a cost and scheduling credential** if estimating or planning is your subject and you want
depth in the method. Breadth is not a substitute for depth in your own discipline.

**Take an accredited credential from an established body** if accreditation is itself the
requirement — a public framework, a client's approved list, an immigration route. PCI cannot
meet that requirement today.

**Take whatever your employer mandates or funds**, if either applies. A mandate is a business
requirement, and a funded credential you actually sit beats a better-matched one you never
start.

**Look at PCL-AI** if the arguments you lose are at the join: the forecast finance restates, the
earning rule the auditor tests, the accrual that arrives after the pack is signed.

## What this comparison leaves out, deliberately

There are no fees on this page, no examination lengths, no question counts, no pass marks and no
difficulty rankings — for PCI or for the cost and scheduling category.

Two reasons. Those specifics belong to whoever sets them and they change on that body's
schedule, so a comparison built on them is wrong within a year and misleads readers in the
meantime. And a ranking of difficulty or prestige is an opinion dressed as a measurement, which
is exactly what somebody choosing a credential does not need.

What is left is scope: what a kind of credential sets out to examine, and which side of the
finance-and-delivery line that puts it on. Scope is checkable against any syllabus you open,
including this one's. If a comparison page cannot survive without a league table, it was not
comparing anything.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does PCL-AI replace a cost and scheduling credential?**
No. They examine different spans. A cost and scheduling credential goes deeper into estimating
and schedule method than PCL-AI does; PCL-AI covers the reporting handover, which that category
does not set out to examine. If estimating method is your whole job, the depth is worth more
than the breadth.

**Is PCL-AI accredited?**
No. PCI is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and
does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. If you
need an accredited credential now, take one from a body that already holds accreditation. That
answer will not change until ours does.

**Do I need an accountancy background for PCL-AI?**
No. The syllabus assumes the delivery side and builds the reporting side from it — how progress
becomes revenue, how a commitment becomes an accrual, how a forecast becomes reported margin.
If you have argued with finance about a month-end cut-off, you have met the material already.

**How do I judge a certifying body I have not heard of?**
Read the whole syllabus before paying. Find the three knowledge areas closest to work you have
personally done and check the treatment against what you know. Ask what the body will not claim
— accreditation, pass rates, salary figures — and treat plain answers as a better sign than
confident ones.

**What can I read for free?**
All three Bodies of Knowledge, in full, before any payment: 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas
for PCL-AI, with 92 sector case studies across the volumes. Start with the domain list and stop
reading if the treatment does not match your experience.
