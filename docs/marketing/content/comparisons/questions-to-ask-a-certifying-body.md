# Ten questions to ask any certifying body before you pay

Ten questions sort a certification from a course, and every one of them can be asked by email before you spend anything. The pattern to watch for is not a wrong answer. It is an answer that describes benefits when you asked about process.

> **Who publishes this page.** credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials described here. PCI's own answers to several of these questions are set out below, including the ones that are not flattering.

## The ten questions to ask any certifying body

| # | Ask this | A good answer sounds like | A weak answer sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | May I read the full syllabus before I pay? | "Here it is, free, in full." | "Detailed content is provided on enrolment." |
| 2 | Who decides whether I passed? | A named function, separate from the people who teach | "Our expert trainers assess you" |
| 3 | How is the pass mark set? | A defined method, reviewed, applied to the paper | "You need 70%", with nothing behind it |
| 4 | What happens if I fail? | A resit route, a waiting period, feedback at domain level | Silence, or "our candidates pass" |
| 5 | Can I appeal, and how? | Grounds, a timescale, a panel independent of the original decision | "Contact us and we'll look into it" |
| 6 | Are you accredited, and on what scope? | The accreditation body, the scope schedule, the dates — or a plain no | Logos with no register entry behind them |
| 7 | How do you keep the examination current? | Item review, retirement, a revision cycle with dates | "Our content is always up to date" |
| 8 | What does renewal require, and what does it cost over ten years? | Every recurring line, stated | One headline figure at the checkout |
| 9 | How can an employer verify my credential? | A register the employer can search themselves | "We issue a certificate you can show" |
| 10 | What do you not claim? | A straight list: no accreditation, no pass rates, no salary promises | "Recognised internationally by industry leaders" |

Question 10 is the one that reveals the most, and almost nobody is asked it. A body that can tell you what it does not claim has thought about the boundary of its own evidence.

## Why question 2 does most of the work

If the organisation that takes your training fee also decides whether you passed, you have bought a course. A course is a legitimate purchase and it is an input; it is not evidence about you, because the party assessing you is paid by the outcome.

The separation is the thing that makes a certification decision worth anything to a third party. It is also the easiest thing to check: ask who signs the decision, and whether that function reports to the people who sold you the preparation. Why the two are kept apart, and what a genuine separation looks like from the inside, is set out in [why preparation and the certification decision are separated](https://credentialfinder.org/separation-of-training-and-certification).

## Why question 3 catches so many

"You need 70%" is not a standard-setting method. It is a number chosen before anybody saw the items, applied to papers of different difficulty, and it means a candidate can pass an easy form and fail a hard one at the same level of competence.

A body with a defensible answer will describe how the standard was judged against the items, who the judges were and when the standard is reviewed. [How a pass mark is set](https://credentialfinder.org/how-a-pass-mark-is-set) works through the arithmetic of a criterion-referenced cut score, including why a fixed percentage across every form is a problem rather than a policy.

## Why question 8 costs the most money

Every credential has recurring lines: renewal on a cycle, any membership required to remain listed, and continuing development that takes your time whether or not it takes your money.

Ask for the recurring lines rather than the headline. Then ask whether membership must run alongside the credential or is optional, because that single answer changes a ten-year total more than any discount at the checkout.

Add your own preparation time to the total. Hours are the largest line in almost every case, and they are the one nobody quotes. What kind of preparation actually earns those hours back is covered on the hub in [what preparation genuinely works](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/project-controls-training).

## PCI's answers to the uncomfortable ones

**Are you accredited?** No. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

**What is your pass rate?** Not published, because at founding stage the number would be based on too few candidates to mean anything, and a figure like that is quoted forever once it is published.

**Will this raise my salary?** PCI makes no claim about pay, employment or promotion, and treats any provider that does as making a claim it cannot support.

**How many people hold your credentials?** Not published, for the same reason as the pass rate.

**May I read the syllabus first?** Yes. The Body of Knowledge is published in full, free, before anybody pays. PCL-AI has 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, PFL-AI has 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and PML-AI has 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

A body that answers these four questions with a claim rather than a refusal is telling you what its other numbers are worth.

## How to send the questions

One email, numbered, no preamble. Ask for written answers rather than a call, because a call produces reassurance and an email produces a record.

Give a reply-by date. A body that certifies people has an administrative function; if ten questions cannot be answered in a week, that is data about how an appeal or a verification request will be handled later.

Then read the reply for the shape rather than the content. Answers that address the numbered questions in order are a different organisation from answers that arrive as a brochure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it reasonable to ask all ten before paying?**
Yes, and the reaction is part of the answer. You are asking a certifying body to document how it certifies, which is the least a candidate is entitled to. A body that treats the questions as an obstacle has told you how it will treat a query about your own result.

**What if the answers are good but the body is new?**
Then you have a well-run new body, which is a real thing. Age is evidence of survival rather than of quality. Weigh the syllabus, the process answers and the plain statement of what is not claimed, and accept that you are taking a position earlier than other people will.

**Which question do most providers fail?**
In practice, the second and the tenth. Separation of preparation from the decision is structurally awkward for organisations built as training companies, and the discipline of stating what is not claimed is unusual in any marketing function.

**Should I ask my employer these questions too?**
If your employer is mandating a specific credential, ask which requirement it satisfies and how the choice was made. Sometimes the answer is a policy with a reason behind it, and sometimes it is a decision nobody has revisited in a decade. Both are useful to know before you commit your evenings.
