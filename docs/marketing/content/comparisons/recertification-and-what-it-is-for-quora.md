# Is recertification just a way to charge you again?

Sometimes it is. The test is what the cycle asks you to do rather than what it costs, and you can apply that test in ten minutes, before you pay anybody anything.

**What recertification is honestly for.** Passing an examination establishes competence against a syllabus on a date. That is a precise claim and a narrow one. Everything after that date is inference, and a renewal cycle is the mechanism that keeps the claim attached to the present rather than to the year you sat.

**Three shapes, and what each one proves.** A development-based cycle asks for evidence of continuing professional development relevant to the scope you were certified in, and it shows you have kept working at the subject. A reassessment-based cycle asks you to sit something again, usually shorter, and it shows you still meet the standard today. A declaration-based cycle asks for a fee and a statement that you still abide by the code, and it shows you paid and signed. The third is not worthless where the body genuinely enforces its code. On its own it is an annuity with a certificate attached.

**The arithmetic that decides whether you will actually keep it.** Read the requirement as hours a month, never as a headline. An illustrative cycle asking for 30 hours across two years is 15 hours a year, which is about 75 minutes a month. If those minutes can be filled with work you already do — a standard read properly, a procedure you rewrote, a session delivered to your team — the body is asking you to record your work rather than invent extra. If they can only be filled by attending that body's own paid events, the hours are not the real cost of the cycle.

**Three questions settle whether a cycle is serious.** Does activity that is not the body's own count? Does work-based learning count, with evidence? Is the evidence sampled, or must every hour be submitted? Yes, yes and sampled describes a cycle designed around practitioners. No, no and everything describes a revenue model with a diary attached.

**Ask about membership separately.** Plenty of bodies run membership and certification and present them as one line on an invoice. Ask in writing whether membership must be maintained for the certification to stay valid, or whether the two are independent. Over a decade that answer moves the cost of holding a credential more than any discount at the point of purchase.

**Find out what a lapse costs before you need to know.** Three facts: the length of the grace period, what reinstatement requires, and the point at which reinstatement stops being available and a full re-examination is the only route back. Then diarise the renewal two months early. That is the entire risk strategy and it works.

One case where none of this is your decision: if your employer's framework or a client's pre-qualification recognises a particular scheme's cycle, work to that cycle and read the rest for interest.
