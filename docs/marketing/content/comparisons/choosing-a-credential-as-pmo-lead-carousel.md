# Choosing a credential as a PMO lead

*LinkedIn document carousel. 11 slides at 1080 × 1350. One idea per slide. Slide 3 carries the
arithmetic, because slide 3 is the one people screenshot.*

---

### Slide 1 — cover
**A portfolio at 0.955.**
**One project inside it at 0.667.**
Which credential examines the difference?
*Alt text: title slide showing a portfolio cost performance index of 0.955 beside a single
project at 0.667.*

### Slide 2 — the set-up
A PMO reports 12 projects on one page.

Combined earned value **£84.0m**.
Combined actual cost **£88.0m**.

One number for the board.
*Alt text: slide describing a portfolio of twelve projects with combined earned value of £84.0m
against combined actual cost of £88.0m.*

### Slide 3 — the arithmetic
Portfolio CPI = 84.0 ÷ 88.0 = **0.955**

Take one project out. It earned **£6.0m** against **£9.0m** of cost.

That project = 6.0 ÷ 9.0 = **0.667**
The other eleven = 78.0 ÷ 79.0 = **0.987**
*Alt text: calculation slide showing a portfolio index of 0.955 splitting into 0.667 for one
project and 0.987 for the remaining eleven.*

### Slide 4 — what the aggregate did
Eleven projects running to plan.
One in real difficulty.

The portfolio number describes neither of them.

A £4.0m gap sits inside a figure that reads as unremarkable.
*Alt text: slide stating that the aggregate index describes neither the eleven healthy projects
nor the one in difficulty.*

### Slide 5 — the question you get asked
Boards do not ask about aggregates. They ask about the one project somebody mentioned in a
corridor.

Answering means decomposing the number in the room: which components move it, how each was
measured, what earning rule produced them.
*Alt text: slide explaining that defending a portfolio number means decomposing it live in the
meeting.*

### Slide 6 — what project management certifications examine
Managing projects across people, process and the business environment.

For method, governance and stakeholder work, that is the matching subject and it is examined
properly.
*Alt text: slide listing what project management certifications set out to examine.*

### Slide 7 — what the other two examine
**The established project controls certifications**: measurement, progress, forecasting and the
control of delivery. The layer under the portfolio numbers.

**Audit credentials**: governance, risk, control and the assurance process.
*Alt text: slide summarising what project controls certifications and audit credentials each set
out to examine.*

### Slide 8 — the row nobody was examined on
Who wrote your rules for what a model may draft in a status report, and what it may never
decide?

Reporting is being drafted by models in a great many PMOs. That policy is being signed by
somebody almost no scheme has examined.
*Alt text: slide asking who wrote the PMO's rules on the use of AI in reporting.*

### Slide 9 — what PML-AI examines
**16 domains. 63 knowledge areas.**

Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed
AI. Syllabus proportions, not examination weightings.
*Alt text: slide summarising PML-AI as 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas with a syllabus split of
40, 40 and 20.*

### Slide 10 — when this is not for you
Your PMO is method and governance → take a project management certification.

Your PMO is an assurance function → the audit category is a serious fit.

Your grading matrix names one → take that one.
*Alt text: slide listing three situations in which a different category of credential is the
right answer for a PMO lead.*

### Slide 11 — the position, and the disclosure
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. Not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other
ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and not claiming to be. Built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024
principles.

*credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards PML-AI.*
*Alt text: closing slide stating that PCI is not accredited and disclosing that it operates
credentialfinder.org.*
