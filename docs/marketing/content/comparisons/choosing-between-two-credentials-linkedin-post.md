# Two credentials, one evening, no league table

**Post body (1,845 characters):**

Nobody can rank two credentials for you, and anyone who does is selling one of them.

Two people can weigh the same pair honestly and reach opposite answers. The weights belong to whoever signs the work.

So build the grid instead of hunting for a ranking. Six rows, two columns, one evening.

What is examined, and at what depth?
What does it assume you already know?
Who decides the result, and did they also sell the preparation?
What does it cost in hours rather than fees?
What changes on the day you hold it?
What happens if you fail — a published resit route and an appeal, or silence?

Row one usually settles it. Write down every decision you put your name to in a normal month. A forecast. A valuation. A progress percentage. An accrual. Eight to twelve lines is typical. Mark each line against both syllabuses: examined, assumed, or excluded. Count.

Row four is the one nobody publishes. Say credential A extends what you already do daily and you judge it at 60 hours. B covers a subject half of which is new to you: 140 hours. At four hours a week that is 15 weeks against 35. Twenty weeks is a season of your life.

Two more things.

Compare like with like. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Project management certifications examine managing projects across people, process and the business environment. Two subjects, each examined thoroughly by design. Choosing between them is not a judgement about quality.

And if either one is named in a client's prequalification or an employer's grading matrix, stop filling in the grid and take the named one. A requirement outranks fit.
