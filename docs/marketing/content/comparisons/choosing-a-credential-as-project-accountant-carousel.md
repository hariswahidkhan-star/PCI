# The margin a project accountant did not build

*LinkedIn document post — 11 slides, 1080 × 1350. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

**Post caption (the first two lines carry the post):**

£0.63m came off the reported margin this month, and nobody made an accounting error.
An estimate the finance team did not build moved by £1.5m, and the whole swing followed it into the accounts.

Eleven slides on where the risk actually sits in a project reporting role.

---

**Slide 1 — The accounting is not the hard part**
If you are qualified, recognition, measurement and disclosure are examined and you can do them. What you cannot examine from a ledger is whether the number you divided by is current.

**Slide 2 — Who is telling you this**
credentialfinder.org is operated by Project Controls Institute Global, which awards the PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI credentials named on slide 9. Declared here so you can discount for it.

**Slide 3 — The arithmetic**
Contract price **£33.0m**. Cost incurred to date **£12.0m**. Estimate of total cost **£30.0m**.

Progress = 12.0 ÷ 30.0 = **40.0%**.
Revenue = 0.40 × 33.0 = **£13.20m**.
Margin = 13.20 − 12.00 = **£1.20m**.

**Slide 4 — Then delivery reforecasts**
Expected total cost becomes **£31.5m**: a rate agreed above allowance, a subcontractor replaced, three weeks of rework.

Progress = 12.0 ÷ 31.5 = **38.1%**. Revenue = **£12.57m**. Margin = **£0.57m**.

**£0.63m off the reported margin, decided by a number you did not build.**

**Slide 5 — And it lands on the balance sheet too**
Invoiced £11.0m, so the contract asset is 12.57 − 11.00 = **£1.57m**. That figure then ages, and its ageing is a conversation with a commercial team about entitlement rather than one with a ledger.

**Slide 6 — What each category examines**
Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process.

**Slide 7 — None of them is deficient**
Each was designed to examine a different subject, and each examines it thoroughly. A project accountant simply happens to sit where the first meets the second, on a boundary neither was built to cover.

**Slide 8 — The five questions**
When was the estimate last rebuilt bottom-up rather than escalated? What earning rule produced this month's progress? Which costs are accrued on an estimate rather than a valuation? What is in the change register but not the budget? Which amounts depend on an unconcluded negotiation?

**Slide 9 — What PCI puts against it**
The PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI): 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, with the Body of Knowledge proportioned 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management and 20% governed AI — the proportions of the syllabus, not an examination weighting. For a qualified accountant, the project management half is the only reason to look.

**Slide 10 — What PCI does not claim**
PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

**Slide 11 — When your qualification is the whole answer**
Progressing inside a finance function? Finish the qualification and let it do its job. Short, repeatable, low-variance projects? The upstream risk here barely exists. A role that names a specific qualification? Take the named one. The case for looking further is narrow: long contracts, movable estimates, contested variations, and you in front of the board.
