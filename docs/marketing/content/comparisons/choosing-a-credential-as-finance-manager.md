# Choosing a credential as a finance manager on capital projects

Your qualification examines recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control, and it does that thoroughly. On capital projects the exposure is upstream of all four: an estimate you did not build, progress you cannot see, and a cash position driven by certification dates rather than by invoices. The credential worth considering is one that examines those inputs, not one that re-examines the reporting.

|---|---|---|
| Reported revenue and margin | Accountancy and finance qualifications | A percentage complete owned by delivery |
| Provisions and expected losses | Accountancy and finance qualifications | A forecast owned by a project team |
| Cash forecast and funding drawdown | Accountancy and finance qualifications | A certification calendar and payment terms |
| Working capital position | Accountancy and finance qualifications | Retention, certification lag and subcontract terms |
| Capital expenditure phasing | Accountancy and finance qualifications | The programme |
| Business case tracking after sanction | Split across two categories | Delivery performance against the sanction estimate |

Every row is examined in the first column. Every row is determined in the third. That distance is the job.

## The working capital arithmetic, which is a delivery calendar in disguise

Monthly certified value on a contract is **£1.5m**. The client pays **60 days** after certification. Subcontractors are paid at **30 days**.

The gap is one month of spend funded from your own balance sheet, or roughly **£1.5m** outstanding at any time.

Retention is **5%** of certified value. Over a year of certifications totalling **£18.0m**, retention held = 18.0 × 0.05 = **£0.9m**, released long after the work and often in two tranches.

Working capital tied up = 1.5 + 0.9 = **£2.4m**, before a single dispute.

Now the part driven by delivery rather than by finance. If certification slips by two weeks because a measurement disagreement is unresolved, the receipt slips by two weeks, and the exposure grows by half a month of spend, about **£0.75m**. Nobody has spent more money. A valuation meeting ran late.

Forecasting that properly needs the certification calendar, the measurement dispute log and the programme, none of which arrive with the ledger. The mechanics of building the profile are set out in [project cash flow forecasting that holds up](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/project-cash-flow-forecasting).

## What each category examines, in its own terms

Accountancy and finance qualifications examine recognition, measurement, disclosure and financial control. That is your subject, examined at a depth no project credential attempts.

Cost and scheduling credentials examine estimating, cost control, planning and schedule analysis. That is where the estimate and the progress in your third column are built.

Audit credentials examine governance, risk, control and the assurance process. That is the discipline for testing whether the controls over those inputs actually operated.

None of the three is incomplete. They were built to examine different things, and a finance lead on capital projects sits at the point where the first depends on the second.

## The five questions that change your month

A finance manager who can ask these gets the bad news a quarter earlier.

When was the estimate at completion last rebuilt from the bottom rather than escalated? Which earning rule produced this month's progress? What is instructed but not yet priced, and priced but not yet approved? Which valuation amounts depend on a negotiation that has not concluded? And what does the certification calendar look like for the next quarter, as opposed to the invoice calendar?

Each question has a factual answer, none is confrontational, and each one moves a number you report. That is the whole case for learning the delivery side, and it is a much narrower body of material than a second profession.

## When your existing qualification is the answer

If your path runs through the finance function — financial control, group reporting, a route to director — complete the qualification that profession recognises and let it do its work. A project-side credential is an addition to it, never a substitute for a stage of it.

If your organisation's projects are short and low-variance, the upstream risk described here is small, and there is little for a second credential to fix.

If a regulated role or a lender's requirement names a specific qualification, that names itself. Requirements do not lose to scope arguments.

## How to decide

Take the last four surprises in your project reporting. For each, write down when the event happened, when you found out, and what you would have needed to see to know earlier.

If the answers are all inside finance, your qualification covers it. If they point at estimates, valuations and certification dates, the gap is upstream, and no amount of additional accounting depth will close it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should a qualified accountant take a project credential?**
Only for the delivery half, and only if a large part of the syllabus is genuinely new to you. If it repeats recognition, measurement and disclosure, you would be paying to be examined on what you already hold. Read the domain list and count the new material first.

**Is this not the project controls team's job?**
It is their job to build the numbers and yours to report them, and the reporting carries your name. Knowing enough to interrogate an estimate is not doing their work; it is the difference between being told in month seven and finding out in month nine.

**What if there is no controls function at all?**
Then somebody in finance is doing it implicitly and probably without a method. That is the case where learning the delivery side has the highest return, because there is nobody to ask, and where the first fix is usually written earning rules rather than a new report.

**Does an audit credential help instead?**
If you are moving into assurance, yes; it is a distinct profession examined properly. If you are staying in finance, it teaches you to test controls rather than to build or interrogate the numbers, which is a different need. The comparison is in [where PFL-AI sits against audit credentials](https://credentialfinder.org/pfl-ai-and-audit-credentials).

**How do I get delivery data earlier without becoming unpopular?**
Ask for the artefacts rather than the answers: the trend log, the change register, the certification calendar. They exist already, they are factual, and requesting them monthly is a routine that costs nobody an argument. The interpretation is then yours to do.
