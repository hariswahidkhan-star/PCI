# What recertification is actually for, and what it is not

Recertification exists so that the date on a certificate keeps meaning something. It says the holder is current, not that they once were. It is not a subscription, not a loyalty scheme and not a second examination for its own sake — and where a scheme cannot say which of those it is, that is worth knowing before you sit rather than three years later.

## Why currency is a real question in this discipline

A credential records a judgement made against a syllabus on a date. Underneath it, the reporting requirements, the contract forms, the measurement conventions and now the governance expectations around automated tooling all move.

Take one that any practitioner can check. A fixed-price contract of £42.0m. Costs to date £22.8m, expected total cost £38.0m.

- Cost-to-cost progress = 22.8 ÷ 38.0 = **60%**
- Revenue recognised on that basis = 0.60 × 42.0 = **£25.2m**

Now measure the same month by units of output, where 48% of the work is physically complete.

- 0.48 × 42.0 = **£20.16m**

Same contract, same data, **£5.04m** apart. Which method faithfully depicts the transfer of control, what evidence supports it, and how the tooling reports it are questions with answers that move over a working life. A certificate issued years ago is silent on whether the holder moved with them.

That is the case for recertification, and it is the only case that survives contact with a sceptical candidate. Anything else is a renewal notice with a professional vocabulary.

## The two mechanisms, and what each actually measures

| | Recorded CPD | Re-examination |
|---|---|---|
| What it measures | Continued contact with the subject | Current command of the syllabus |
| Strength | Continuous, spread across a cycle, fits around work | Assessed to the same standard as everybody else |
| Failure mode | Counting hours without checking relevance | Testing memory if the paper never changes |
| Cost to the holder | Time in small pieces, plus record-keeping | Concentrated preparation and a sitting |
| What a body must do | Define what counts and verify claims | Keep the item bank current and calibrated |

Neither is better in the abstract. A scheme that records CPD and never samples it is measuring attendance; a scheme that re-examines against a stale paper is measuring recall. Both designs work when the body does the work behind them, and the published policy is where you find out whether it does.

## What good CPD looks like on paper

Mapped, not counted. A record that shows which knowledge areas the activity relates to is worth several times one that shows hours. PCI's syllabus runs to 13, 16 and 16 domains and 61, 61 and 63 knowledge areas across PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI, and a CPD entry that names the area it touched can be judged for relevance by somebody who was not there.

Evidenced, not asserted. Keep the artefact — the paper you wrote, the procedure you rewrote, the review you sat in — rather than a line in a spreadsheet.

Sampled, not audited to death. A body that verifies every claim is spending its money in the wrong place. A body that verifies none is running an honesty box and calling it assurance.

And it should include the parts of the subject you avoid. The easiest CPD to accumulate is more of what you already do well, which is exactly the CPD that changes nothing.

## Five things to check in any recertification policy

**Is the validity period published before you apply?** If you have to ask, ask, and keep the reply in writing.

**What counts, and against which parts of the syllabus?** A policy that accepts anything vaguely professional is not maintaining a standard, it is maintaining a mailing list.

**Who verifies?** Look for sampling, and for what happens when a sampled claim does not hold up.

**What happens if you lapse?** Renewal with conditions, a full re-sit, or a permanent loss. All three are defensible designs and the difference matters to you.

**Does verification show status on the date it is checked?** This is the one employers actually use. A public check should show current, expired, suspended or withdrawn as at today, not merely that a person once passed. A register of everyone who ever qualified is a list, not a verification.

## Where a different route is the right answer

If your employer's framework or a client's pre-qualification recognises a particular scheme's cycle, that cycle is the one that matters to you. Work to it and treat everything else as background.

If you need a credential from an accredited scheme today, take the accredited route. PCI is a new, independent certifying body. It is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles. Read the published Body of Knowledge before you decide anything.

If you are two years from retiring from delivery work, a recertification cycle may be a cost with no return, and there is no version of that sentence a certifying body enjoys writing. Judge it on what the next few years of your work actually needs.

## FAQ

**Is recertification just a way of charging holders again?**
It can be, and the test is what the body does with the cycle. A scheme that defines what counts, maps it to the syllabus and samples claims is maintaining a standard. A scheme that takes a declaration and issues a new date is selling a renewal, and the published policy usually makes clear which one you are looking at.

**Is CPD or re-examination better?**
They measure different things. CPD measures continued contact with the subject across a cycle; re-examination measures current command of the syllabus at a point. The stronger schemes use CPD for the cycle and re-examination where the subject has changed enough that contact is not evidence of currency.

**What happens to my credential while I am mid-cycle?**
It should show as current until the validity date, and the public check should say so with the date attached. If a scheme cannot show a status as at today, an employer cannot rely on it, and neither can you when a client asks.

**Does anything I do at work count as CPD?**
Only if you can say which part of the syllabus it touched and show something for it. Rewriting a cost control procedure counts, and the procedure is the evidence. Attending a briefing you cannot summarise a week later does not, whatever the certificate says.

**What if I let it lapse?**
Find out before you need to know. Some schemes allow renewal with conditions inside a grace period, some require a full re-sit, and the difference is a fortnight against a season. It is one line in a policy and nobody reads it until the month it applies to them.
