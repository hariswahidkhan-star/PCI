# The capability gap project controls training leaves open

*Two curricula, both sound, meeting at a number neither examines. Here is how to find that seam on your capability map, and the calculation that shows a project team why it matters.*

A finance curriculum rarely teaches float. A delivery curriculum rarely teaches cut-off. Both are taught well. The gap project controls training leaves open is not inside either course: it sits on the join between them, where a single number is assembled from two sources and nobody has been examined on the handover.

That is why a well-reviewed course changes so little. The course was not weak. The map that decided which courses exist was drawn from the organisation chart, and no chart has a box for a seam.

## What the gap looks like in one calculation

The figures are arithmetic: no client, no sector, no currency, and no claim about how often this happens.

Earned value is 2,200,000. Invoiced cost is 1,850,000.

> 2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19

The cost performance index reads 1.19. Every unit of cost has bought 1.19 units of work, so the report says the job is running under budget, and on the evidence in the system it is right.

Now the entry that never reached the system. There is 240,000 of work performed and not yet invoiced at the reporting date. Accrue it.

> 1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000
>
> 2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05

Fourteen points moved on one missing accrual. Nothing changed on site, and the month closed twice: once for the ledger, once for progress. The error is accounting. The damage is delivery. Neither training alone catches it.

Use it as an exercise. Show the first division, ask the room whether they would sign, then show the second. Watch which side of the room answers. That split is the map you have been trying to draw with a survey, and it takes ninety seconds.

## Why a training needs analysis cannot see it

A needs analysis asks each function what it needs, and each function answers accurately about its own work. The seam has no respondent: it belongs to no job description, so it is never reported as a gap and never reaches a development plan.

Competency frameworks inherit the same shape. They are built from roles, roles come from the structure, and a handover between two structures is a task with no owner. The framework is not wrong about any role in it; it is silent about the space between them, and silence in a framework reads as coverage.

The problem is not confined to project work. Wherever one reported figure is assembled by two functions, whether a margin, a provision or a completion date given to a customer, the same blind spot exists for the same structural reason.

## Where each capability sits today

| Capability | In a finance curriculum | In a delivery curriculum | Examined as a joined task |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cut-off: what may be posted to a period | Typically yes | Rarely | Rarely |
| Accruing work performed and not yet invoiced | Typically yes | Rarely | Rarely |
| Float and the critical path | Rarely | Typically yes | Rarely |
| Measuring physical progress on site | Rarely | Typically yes | Rarely |
| Reconciling earned value to the ledger at the same date | Rarely | Rarely | Nowhere, and the report divides one by the other |

Check those rows against your own catalogue before you accept them; the shape is the point. If your catalogue genuinely covers the bottom row, you should be able to name the person who owns it without looking.

## Three fixes for a project controls training plan

1. **Name the join as a capability.** "Reconcile earned value to the cost ledger at a common date, and evidence the accrual" is a capability, not an interface issue. Put it on the map, give it one named owner, and teach it with both functions in the same room, because two functions taught separately will each learn their own half again.

2. **Teach it against a live report, not a case study.** Bring last month's real cost report into the session and ask for two dates: when the ledger closed, and when progress was measured. If they differ and no accrual bridges them, the session has paid for itself before the content starts.

3. **When you fund a credential, read what it examines rather than what it promises.** A syllabus that will not show you its domains is telling you something, and half an hour spent reading domains beats an afternoon reading brochures. [A like-for-like comparison of what the main controls credentials examine](https://credentialfinder.org/best-project-controls-certification) is one way in; PCI publishes it and PCI is in it, so use it to find the questions and take the answers from each awarding body direct.

## The training that machine output depreciates first

Put that cost report in front of a model and ask it for the commentary. It reads the same field the report reads, explains a cost performance index of 1.19 correctly, cites the formula, and writes it up more cleanly than whoever it replaced. The one thing it cannot do is notice the accrual that was never there to read.

That gives a curriculum a useful test. Training in producing and explaining a figure is what machine output substitutes for fastest. Training in interrogating one — which date it closed on, what is missing from it, who touched it before it reached you — becomes more valuable as explanation gets cheaper.

The governance question runs along the same line and is cheaper to settle in writing than after a board paper has gone out: [what an AI policy for project reporting has to decide](https://pciai.org/ai-policy-for-project-controls) is which figures a model may originate and which it may only restate.

## What examination adds to training

Training changes what someone can do. An examination is how a third party knows it without taking anyone's word for it. For a capability that appears only at a handover, the second matters as much as the first: the call gets made with both halves in view and nobody to hand it to.

That is the argument the Project Controls Institute was built around, and it is worth reading rather than taking on trust. Its three credentials divide the ground: the PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) examines 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and the PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

Behind them sit 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, which are the Institute's certification requirements and not law, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes.

15,613 machine calculation checks run against PFL-AI and PML-AI, all passing; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite. That is a statement about the material in two of the three sets. It is not a statement about anybody who has sat anything.

The PCL-AI Body of Knowledge is 40 per cent finance and reporting, 40 per cent project management, 20 per cent governed use of machine output. That is the shape of the syllabus and not an examination weighting: the syllabus is settled, the exam blueprint is still an open decision, and anyone quoting you an exam weighting is reading you a marketing document.

## Four questions to ask before you fund a course

**Which handovers does this course examine, rather than describe?**

Description is cheap, and any syllabus will cover a handover in a slide. Ask which handovers a delegate is assessed on, with both functions present, and what that assessment requires them to produce. If the answer is a knowledge check, the course teaches recognition. The seam needs somebody who can reconcile two sources and defend the result under challenge.

**What does a delegate bring back that a colleague can use on Monday?**

Learning transfer on this subject is concrete: a stated cost cut-off date, a stated progress date, an accrual with evidence behind it, and one line under the report naming all three. If a provider cannot name the artefact a delegate produces, the course will review well and change nothing, which is the failure this article is about.

**Do the finance and delivery cohorts ever sit in the same room?**

Two functions taught separately each learn their own half again, more thoroughly. The join is the one capability that cannot be taught to one side at a time, because the exercise needs the person who owns the ledger and the person who owns the progress measurement to disagree in front of each other and settle it.

**What is examined by a third party here, and what is only attended?**

Attendance evidences exposure. Examination evidences a capability somebody outside your organisation is prepared to stand behind. For a capability that appears only at a handover, that difference decides whether a manager can be told the call was made competently, or only that a course was completed on time and within budget.

## The part that belongs to you

Someone in your organisation signs a forecast this month. If the accrual is missing, the number they defended was wrong before they saw it, and they will find out in a meeting where they are the only person accountable for it. The reason they could not see it will be a gap nobody taught them.

The map that decided what they were taught is yours.

So read [the PCL-AI Body of Knowledge, domain by domain](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge), and test it against your own capability framework rather than against anyone's marketing. Then take your project team's last cost report and find the accrual nobody asked about. Both exercises take an afternoon, and neither needs a budget.
