# Fourteen CPI points, lost in an accrual not a schedule

*LinkedIn Company Page post — 1,703 characters including hashtags. No link in the body; the link goes in the first comment.*

Your CPI reads 1.19. The report says the job is running under budget.
The accrual nobody posted says 1.05.

Earned value: 2,200,000.
Invoiced cost: 1,850,000.
2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19.

Now add the 240,000 of work that is done and not yet invoiced.

1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000.
2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05.

Fourteen points moved on one missing accrual, and nobody was careless.

Work done and not yet invoiced is the single figure that sits outside both systems. Earned value has already counted it. The ledger has not seen it. Somebody has to know it exists before they can ask for it.

The error is accounting. The damage is delivery. Neither training alone catches it.

Accountancy exams rarely test float. Engineering exams rarely test cut-off. A project lives in the overlap, and the overlap is where the money is lost.

You sign the forecast. Miss the accrual and the number you defended was wrong before you saw it. Seniority means owning both ledgers, and a credential that examines only one half leaves you accountable for a gap nobody taught you.
