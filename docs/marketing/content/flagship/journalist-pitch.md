# Same project, two CPIs: 1.19 and 1.05

*Journalist pitch email. Everything below the rule is the message exactly as it is sent: plain text, one named recipient, no attachment.*

---

**Subject:** Same project, two CPIs: 1.19 and 1.05

[First name],

One of those numbers was signed off. The other one was true.

Earned value 2,200,000. Invoiced cost 1,850,000. CPI 1.19, so the job reads under budget, and that is the number that reaches the board. Add 240,000 of work done and not yet invoiced: cost 2,090,000, CPI 1.05. Illustrative arithmetic, not a case study.

Why now: Project Controls Institute Global has published the Bodies of Knowledge for its three credentials in full before anyone has sat an examination. PCL-AI examines 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. The examination blueprint is still an open decision.

I can send the published syllabuses and the 113 mandatory Standards they sit on, and put [NAME], [TITLE], on the phone about what the Institute has not decided. That is the part most launches will not discuss. No accreditation is claimed. There is no pass rate, because nobody has sat an examination.

Shall I send it over? A one-word no is as useful to me as a yes.

[SENDER NAME]
[TITLE], Project Controls Institute
[TELEPHONE]
https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge

---

**Linking note.** One link, one domain, and it is not in the body. [The PCL-AI Body of Knowledge](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge) on `projectcontrolsinstitute.org` sits in the signature, spelled out in full, and answers the question the third paragraph raises: "you say you have published it, so where is it". Nothing else is linked, because nothing else is claimed. If the correspondent asks how a credential is checked rather than what it examines, [credentialfinder.org](https://credentialfinder.org/how-to-verify-a-certification) answers that — in the reply, once, and only if they ask. No `pciai.org`, `pciworld.org` or `pciglobal.ai` link belongs anywhere near a pitch: a media email carrying a set of company URLs is a campaign, and a correspondent who receives one files it accordingly. Once the wire release is live, the follow-up email points at the newsroom URL rather than the wire copy, so any citation the correspondent writes lands on the canonical page.
