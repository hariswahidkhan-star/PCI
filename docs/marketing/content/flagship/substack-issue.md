# Issue one: fourteen points and work done not yet invoiced

*The Overlap is a monthly letter about the places where project finance and project delivery report the same month differently. It arrives in the week after most month-ends close, while the answer still matters.*

Accountancy exams rarely test float. Engineering exams rarely test cut-off. Projects need both. The gap between them has a name: work done not yet invoiced, sitting in neither system on the day the cost report is signed.

I have wanted to write that down properly for a long time, and email turned out to be the only place to do it. Long enough to show the working, short enough that you might actually check it.

So: issue one. No paywall, nothing to buy at the end, and no exam sitting to sell you. Just the number that got me here.

## The arithmetic

Round figures, belonging to no project and no client. That is deliberate. You can test them against your own last close rather than admire someone else's.

Earned value: 2,200,000
Invoiced cost: 1,850,000

2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19

A cost performance index of 1.19. The job is running under budget, the cost report says so, and the cost report is accurate.

Now add the 240,000 of work that is done and not yet invoiced.

1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000
2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05

Fourteen points, on one accrual.

At one end of that distance you release contingency. At the other you have a difficult conversation with a sponsor. Same month, same job, and nobody had to do anything careless to get from one to the other.

Earned value counted the work, because the work happened. The ledger did not, because no invoice had arrived. The 240,000 sits outside both systems until somebody thinks to ring the supplier and ask what they have done and not yet billed for.

The error is accounting. The damage is delivery. Neither training alone catches it.

## Why the room was full of competent people

A chartered accountant is examined on when revenue may be recognised and what a provision has to satisfy. Almost never on a critical path or an earning rule.

An engineer is examined on float and progress measurement. Almost never on cut-off or a contract asset.

Both were examined properly. Neither was examined on the other one's half, and a project lives in the half that neither of them sat.

I want to be exact about that, because the lazy version of this argument insults somebody. It is not that accountants cannot read a schedule or that engineers cannot read a balance sheet. Plenty do both, and they usually learned it the expensive way, on a job where it had already gone wrong. The gap is in what each profession is examined on. Syllabuses are fixable. People do not need fixing.

You sign the forecast. Miss the accrual and the number you defended was wrong before you saw it. Seniority means owning both ledgers, and a credential that examines only one half leaves you accountable for a gap nobody taught you.

Beneath all three sit 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements. Those are certification requirements set by the Institute. They are not law, and nothing in this letter is accounting, tax or legal advice.

The Body of Knowledge is 40 per cent finance and reporting, 40 per cent project management, 20 per cent governed AI. That is the Body of Knowledge's shape. It is not the examination's. The exam blueprint is an open decision, you will not read a weighting for it here until it is settled, and if somebody quotes you one before then they have read the wrong document.

Governed AI sits in there for an unglamorous reason. A model will produce a forecast faster than you can check it, and it will produce one whether or not the accrual was ever posted.

Automation changes how quickly a wrong number reaches the board. It does not move whose name is on it, which is why the syllabus treats [what a model may propose and what it may never decide by itself](https://pciai.org/ai-policy-for-project-controls) as a governance question rather than a tooling one.

## The rules this letter writes under

Four of them. They are the reason to subscribe, and they are the reason some issues will be shorter than you would like.

**Every number carries its scope, or it does not run.** Here is one: 15,613 machine calculation checks run against PFL-AI and PML-AI, all passing. PCL-AI has no equivalent suite. The shorter version of that sentence is more impressive and it is false. A number stripped of its scope is not a smaller claim, it is a wrong one.

**The worked example is arithmetic, not a case study.** Round figures, no client, no sector, no date. And I will not tell you how often the missing accrual happens, because I have no researched frequency to cite. Neither does anyone who quotes you one.

**No pass rates, no student numbers, no salary uplift, no accreditation or endorsement claims.** Not held back. They do not exist. A certifying body that publishes a number it cannot source has already failed the only examination that matters, and the readers it would attract are not the ones I am writing for.

**Corrections go at the top of the next letter**, at the same length as the error, not into a quiet edit of the archive. This issue has nothing to correct yet. Give it time.

## What arrives, and when

Monthly, in the week after most closes. One number that two systems report differently, worked in full with the division shown. One question short enough to ask out loud in a review without a slide behind it. One piece of syllabus that examines the thing.

There is no shortage of material. The three Bodies of Knowledge carry 92 sector case studies between them, 26, 33 and 33 across the volumes, and each one is a month in which two systems could disagree.

Issue two takes estimate at completion. Four accepted methods, four different assumptions about how the rest of the job will behave, and at least three of them defensible in the same meeting while disagreeing with each other. Most forecast arguments are really an argument about which of the four everyone thinks they are using.

## Four questions this issue will get

**Is this not just a matter of chasing the accruals harder?**

No, and that is the clerical version of the answer. The structural version is that the two halves of the ratio sit on different bases. Earned value accrues itself the moment work is performed. Invoiced cost waits for a supplier's billing run. Divide one by the other and you have measured billing habits rather than performance, which is why capable teams reproduce it every month.

**Does it not wash out over a full year?**

It does. The timing difference reverses, and by the annual accounts the effect has gone. Nothing gets decided annually, though. Re-baselining, contingency release, descoping and a board forming a view on whether the job is fine all happen on a month, and the month is where the fourteen points sit.

**How often does the missing accrual actually happen?**

I do not know, and neither does anyone who quotes you a figure. There is no researched frequency I can point at, so there is no number for it in this letter and there will not be one. What I can tell you is the condition: the arithmetic bites wherever the cost cut-off and the progress date differ and nobody accrues the gap between them. Whether those two dates match on your job is a question you can settle this week.

**What does 40/40/20 mean for the examination?**

Nothing yet. Those are the Body of Knowledge's proportions — 40 per cent finance and reporting, 40 project management, 20 governed AI — and they describe the syllabus rather than the paper. The exam blueprint is still an open decision. Anyone quoting you an examination weighting, for this credential or any other, is reading from a marketing document rather than a published one.

## Before then

Two things, and the first one is free.

Take your last month-end and find the work that was done and not yet invoiced. Then check whether the number in your cost report knew about it. If it did, you have a good process and I would like to hear how you run it.

Then read [the 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge) of the PCI AI Project Controls Leader against what you actually needed to know that month. Not against a job description. Against the month. If you already hold a letter or two and want to know what this adds, the honest comparison is [set side by side on what each one examines](https://credentialfinder.org/best-project-controls-certification), never on who awards it.

That is issue one. If you want issue two, subscribe below and it will find you in about a month.

And if the arithmetic here has ever cost you a quarter, hit reply and tell me what it looked like from your side. No names, no client, nothing I would print without asking. It comes to a person, and it makes the next letter better than anything I had planned.
