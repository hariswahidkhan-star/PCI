Your CPI reads 1.19. The missing accrual says 1.05. Which number reaches the board?

One hour on one cost report. The whole session is a single division done twice, and the second answer is the one you would have had to defend.

**The report as it was signed.** Earned value is 2,200,000. Invoiced cost is 1,850,000. 2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19, so the job is running under budget and the commentary writes itself.

**The report after cut-off.** 240,000 of work is done and not yet invoiced. Accrue it: 1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000, and 2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05.

| | Invoiced cost basis | Incurred cost basis |
|---|---|---|
| Earned value | 2,200,000 | 2,200,000 |
| Cost | 1,850,000 | 2,090,000 |
| **CPI** | **1.19** | **1.05** |

*Plain-text fallback where the field will not take a table:*
*2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19 · 1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000 · 2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05*

Fourteen points moved and nobody was careless. The earned value was right. The invoice register was right. The 240,000 was finished work that had not yet reached the ledger, and it sat in the gap between two systems that were each doing their job. The error is accounting. The damage is delivery. Neither training alone catches it.

These are illustrative figures, not a case study. There is no client, no sector and no currency behind them. They were chosen so that every division can be checked by hand while the session is running, and you are invited to check them.

### What you will be able to do afterwards

- Say what your own CPI's denominator holds, invoiced cost or incurred cost, and name the report you would look in to find out.
- Identify the cut-off interval between a ledger close and a data date, and put a number on the work standing in it.
- Recalculate a cost performance index with an accrual applied, and quantify the movement in points.
- Explain why neither schedule variance nor the schedule performance index would have caught this, neither of them carrying a cost term.
- Name which of the four estimate-at-completion methods divide by CPI, and therefore which forecasts inherit the error before anyone has misapplied anything.
- Run three checks on a cost forecast before you sign it.

No software is needed and there is nothing to install. Bring the last cost report you put your name to.

### Who this hour is for

Planners, cost engineers, project accountants, quantity surveyors, commercial managers, PMO leads and finance business partners on capital projects. If you sign a cost forecast, review one or have to argue with one, the session is aimed at you.

It is not for anyone hoping to watch a tool demonstrated: no product appears in it.

### What the session does not do

The 40/40/20 proportions describe the Body of Knowledge, and no examination weighting is published or discussed in the hour, because the exam blueprint is an open decision. Nor is any comparison drawn that places another body's credential below PCI's. PCI holds no accreditation, recognition or endorsement from any body and claims none; if that matters to you, and it should, check it before you spend an hour with us. PCI publishes [a comparison of what each certifying body puts on its examination, ours included](https://credentialfinder.org/best-project-controls-certification), which is a starting point and not an independent audit — for that, read each body's own scheme documents and ask an accreditation body directly.

### Before you come

Read the PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) [Body of Knowledge](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge): 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas, 40 per cent finance and reporting, 40 project management, 20 governed AI. Then take your last forecast and find the accrual nobody asked about. Bring what you find, and the hour becomes yours rather than ours.

### Questions asked in advance

**Is this a sales pitch?** No, and the reason is structural rather than a promise: there is no cohort, no course, no exam date and no price list, so there is nothing this hour could sell you. You are sent the recording afterwards. Nothing else follows it.

**Our ERP posts accruals automatically. Does this apply to us?** Then the process caught it, and this hour is about the month it does not: a subcontractor claim assessed after cut-off, a milestone certified late, a variation agreed in principle and not yet valued. Worth asking what your accrual is based on, and who signs it.

**What is the exam weighted at?** 40/40/20 describes the Body of Knowledge. The exam blueprint is an open decision and no weighting is published. That answer will not change during the session.

**Do I need earned value experience?** You need the terms. The arithmetic is deliberately simple, which is the point: this is not a hard sum that someone got wrong, it is an easy sum built on the wrong cost basis.

**Does it cover AI?** One segment of it. A forecasting model that reads the invoice register learns that this project's CPI is 1.19, and it will say so faster and more confidently than the spreadsheet did. What a model may and may not decide inside a forecast is [a governance question long before it is a tooling one](https://pciai.org/ai-policy-for-project-controls).

**Will it be recorded?** Yes. Everyone who registers is sent the recording, whether they attend or not.
