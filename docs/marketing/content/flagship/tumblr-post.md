# Tumblr flagship — the accrual that moves CPI

## 1. The post, exactly as it goes up

No title. Image first. No read-more break.

---

[IMAGE — build spec in section 3]

**Nobody here does anything wrong. That is the whole problem.**

A cost report can be arithmetically correct and still be false.

Someone runs earned value on a job. Earned value is the budgeted cost of the work that has actually been finished, not the money that has gone out of the door. Say it comes to 2,200,000. Invoiced cost is 1,850,000.

Cost performance index is earned value divided by cost. Anything above 1.00 means you are getting more finished work per unit of money than you planned for.

**2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19**

1.19 is a good month. It goes in the report, and nobody has any reason to argue with it.

Except 240,000 of work is finished and has not been invoiced. A subcontractor did it. The invoice has not been raised, or it is sitting in somebody's outbox, or it is in dispute. The work is real and the cost is real. It is just not in the ledger yet.

Accrue it, which means putting the cost in the period the work happened in rather than the period the paperwork arrived in, and cost becomes 2,090,000.

**2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05**

Fourteen points. Nothing slipped on site. The entire move happened inside a ledger, in a line item that had not turned up.

**The error is accounting. The damage is delivery.**

The reason this keeps happening is structural.

Accountancy exams rarely test float. Engineering exams rarely test cut-off. Both professions are examined hard, and they are examined on different halves of the same project. The accrual sits precisely on the join. The person who signs the forecast is standing over it.

So you sign, and the number you defended was already wrong when it reached you. Seniority means owning both ledgers. A credential that examines one half of the job leaves you carrying a gap nobody ever taught you.

That gap is the reason the Project Controls Institute exists. The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) examines 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas across both sides of it, and it sits on 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements. Those are the Institute's certification requirements rather than law, and nothing here is accounting advice.

There is nothing to book and nothing to buy. The [PCL-AI Body of Knowledge](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge) is published in full, so read it against your own last month-end. If there is no question in it you would struggle with, ignore us with our blessing.

## 2. Why it is built this way

**The first line is not the hook, the image is.** On a dashboard the image lands before any word of the body, so the opening sentence continues the card rather than repeating it. The card says both numbers are correct; the body says nobody did anything wrong. Reverse them and the reader has been told the same thing twice before reaching the arithmetic.

**Every term is defined in the sentence that uses it.** Earned value, cost performance index and accrual all get a plain-English gloss inline. Most of this dashboard has never costed a project, and a post that assumes the vocabulary loses them at the second paragraph. A post that explains it well is the kind Tumblr reblogs with "I don't work in this field and I read the whole thing."

**The divisions are printed, not summarised.** Anyone can check both on a phone. A number a stranger can verify is the only reason a stranger stays, and it is also the block a language model can lift whole.

**The turn is bolded and alone on its line.** "The error is accounting. The damage is delivery." is the sentence that gets pulled into a reblog with nothing else attached, so it is given the room to be pulled.

**The syllabus argument arrives after the arithmetic, never before.** Lead with the credential and the post is an advert with sums in it. Lead with the sums and the credential is the answer to a question the reader has just been made to ask.

## 3. The image — 1280 × 1600 build spec

Tumblr renders a dashboard image at roughly 540 px wide, so this canvas displays at about 42 per cent. Everything below is sized for that. **Nothing on this canvas goes below 30 px**, because 30 px becomes 12.7 px on the dashboard and anything smaller is decoration rather than information.

All coordinates are pixels from the top-left. Type positions are **baselines**, not box tops. Build at 1×; never build small and scale up.

### 3.1 Canvas and export

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Artboard | 1280 × 1600 px, 4:5 — tall enough to hold the vertical narrative, short enough to survive being scaled to 540 |
| Colour space | sRGB, no transparency |
| Delivery file | PNG-24, `pci-cpi-gap-1280x1600.png`, target under 2 MB — the platform ceiling is far higher, but a heavy image stalls a fast dashboard |
| Never | animated, transparent, watermarked across the arithmetic, or padded to a square |

Tumblr does not crop a tall image in the dashboard, which is this platform's one real format advantage over Instagram. Use it: the story reads top to bottom, and the gap between the two panels is doing the explaining.

### 3.7 Two tests before it ships

**The 540 test.** Export, scale the file to 540 px wide and look at it at arm's length. Four things must survive: the two-line headline, 1.19, 1.05 and the gold 240,000. If they do not, the fault is the leading in the panels, not the point size.

**The stripped-caption test.** Cover the body text and read the image alone. It must still state nothing false. It does, because everything on it is either arithmetic or a fact from the register, and there is no examination weighting on it to be misread.

## 4. Working the post after it goes up

Reblog with commentary once, from the same blog, about a week later, adding the second half of the arithmetic nobody asks about: what happens to the schedule variance in the same month. Do not add a new claim and do not restate the ask. A self-reblog with something in it is native; a bare bump is not.

Do not queue this behind twenty other posts. Tumblr blogs are read by their last few posts, and a certifying body publishing daily on this dashboard would look like it was farming it.

If the post travels, the reblog chain is the asset, not the note count. Read what people add. The additions that correct the arithmetic are worth more than the ones that praise it.
