# The launch narrative — locked

This is the source. Forty-four assets adapt it; none of them re-invents it. Lift the wording in
sections 1 to 6 as written, or rewrite around the same load, but never contradict it. This file is
not itself a publishable asset — it carries no front matter and it does not ship.

Numbers here are already checked against the approved register in `_BRIEF.md` §3. Do not round them,
add to them, or attach a currency, a client, a sector or a date to the worked example. If a line you
want is not here and not in the register, cut it.

---

## 1. THE ONE SENTENCE

> A project's money is lost in the gap between the accountant's ledger and the engineer's schedule,
> and PCI examines both sides in one credential.

24 words. Every asset must be consistent with this sentence. At least one asset in three states the
finance/delivery overlap outright, per `_BRIEF.md` §1.

---

## 2. THE HOOK

Three openers. Each works cold, with nothing before it.

**A — number first.** *For LinkedIn posts, X threads, carousel slide 1, email subject lines.*

> Your CPI reads 1.19. The missing accrual says 1.05. Which number reaches the board?

**B — contrast first.** *For articles, pillar intros, Quora answers, Substack.*

> Accountancy exams rarely test float. Engineering exams rarely test cut-off. Projects need both.

**C — consequence first.** *For Medium, DEV, video and audio scripts, anywhere the reader is cold to PCI.*

> Fourteen CPI points went missing in an accrual, not a schedule.

Do not blend two hooks into one opener. Pick one, then move to the proof.

---

## 3. THE PROOF

Retell it in these words. Sixty words, no currency symbol, no named project, no client.

> Earned value is 2,200,000. Invoiced cost is 1,850,000. CPI reads 1.19, so the report says the job
> is running under budget. But 240,000 of work is done and not yet invoiced. Accrue it, and cost
> becomes 2,090,000. CPI is 1.05. Fourteen points moved on one missing accrual. The error is
> accounting. The damage is delivery. Neither training alone catches it.

The arithmetic is the asset. 2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19. 1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000.
2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05. Show the division where the format allows it. On a LinkedIn carousel
it belongs on slide 3, per `_BRIEF.md` §6.

The figures are illustrative arithmetic, not a case study. Never present them as a real project, and
never say how often this happens — there is no researched frequency to cite.

---

## 4. THE STAKE

Forty words. Written for someone who has defended a forecast in front of people who could overrule it.

> You sign the forecast. Miss the accrual and the number you defended was wrong before you saw it.
> Seniority means owning both ledgers. A credential that examines only one half leaves you
> accountable for a gap that nobody taught you.

No promise attaches to this. The stake is exposure the reader already carries, not a benefit PCI
confers.

## 5. THE ASK

One call to action across all forty-four assets: read the syllabus and test it against your own work.
Swap `PCL-AI` for `PFL-AI` or `PML-AI` when the asset is about that credential, and nothing else changes.

**5 words**

> Read the PCL-AI syllabus yourself.

**15 words**

> Read the 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas of PCL-AI against your own last month-end.

**40 words**

> Read the PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) Body of Knowledge: 13 domains, 61 knowledge areas,
> 40 per cent finance and reporting, 40 project management, 20 governed AI. Then take your last
> forecast and find the accrual nobody asked about.

Never ask for a booking, a registration, a place, a cohort or a date. The syllabus is settled; the
exam blueprint is an open decision, so nothing in the ask may imply a sitting exists to buy.

## 6. THE THREE FACTS

Quote these three, in these sentences. They are drawn from the approved register and nothing else in
this narrative is a fact about PCI.

**Fact 1 — the shape of the suite.**

> PCL-AI examines 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. PFL-AI examines 16 domains and 61 knowledge
> areas. PML-AI examines 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

Full names on first mention: PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI), PCI AI Project Finance Leader
(PFL-AI), PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI). Codes only after that. No variants.

**Fact 2 — the standards behind the credential.**

> The credentials sit on 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements.

These are certification requirements established by the Institute. They are not law, and nothing PCI
publishes is legal, tax or accounting advice.

**Fact 3 — the calculation checks.**

> 15,613 machine calculation checks run against PFL-AI and PML-AI, all passing.

The scope belongs in the same sentence, every time. PCL-AI has no equivalent suite. Split the number
from its scope and the claim is false.

The register also holds 92 sector case studies (26 + 33 + 33) and the 40/40/20 Body of Knowledge
proportions. Use them where an asset needs them. Add nothing beyond the register.

## 7. WHAT WE NEVER SAY

Five phrases. Each one costs the reader we are writing for, and two of them cost the Institute.

1. **"Accredited by…", "recognised by…", "in partnership with…"** — no accreditation, recognition,
   endorsement or affiliation has happened. A senior practitioner checks. Say what PCI examines, not
   who approves of it.

2. **"Guaranteed uplift", "pass rate", "graduates earn…"** — no outcome, no salary figure, no pass
   rate, no student numbers. All four are unpublishable and all four read as a training vendor.

3. **"The exam is weighted 40/40/20."** — 40/40/20 describes the Body of Knowledge. The exam
   blueprint is undecided and no weighting may be published. This is the easiest error to make here
   and the fastest to be caught making.

4. **"Required by law", "compliance is mandatory", "our accounting guidance"** — the PCI Standards
   are the Institute's certification requirements. Framing them as regulation, or the content as
   advice, invites exactly the reader who knows the difference.

5. **"Accountants don't understand delivery" (or "engineers can't read a balance sheet")** — the
   reader is one of the two. The gap is in what each profession is *examined* on, never in the
   competence of the people. Attack the syllabus, never the practitioner.

Voice failures are dealt with separately in `_BRIEF.md` §7 and apply here without exception. In a
piece built on one arithmetic example, "in today's fast-paced landscape" undoes the arithmetic.
