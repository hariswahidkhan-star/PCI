# Fourteen CPI points went missing in an accrual, not a schedule

*Pitch email to Construction Week Middle East. Everything below the rule is the message exactly as it is sent: plain text, one named recipient, no attachment.*

---

**Subject:** Fourteen CPI points went missing in an accrual, not a schedule

[First name],

Earned value 2,200,000. Invoiced cost 1,850,000. CPI 1.19, so the job reads under budget. Add 240,000 of work done and not yet invoiced: cost 2,090,000, CPI 1.05. Illustrative arithmetic, not a case study.

On a certification-based payment cycle that gap is not an oversight. Work is executed, the application goes in, the engineer certifies, and only then is there an invoice to book. The ledger trails the works by design, and every CPI built on it inherits the lag.

Scale multiplies it. Each package carries its own gap between work in place and cost booked, and the programme figure is the sum of them. It is a commercial-desk story, not a planning one.

Project Controls Institute Global has published the Bodies of Knowledge for its three credentials in full before anyone has sat an examination. PCL-AI examines 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, on 113 mandatory PCI Standards. The examination blueprint is still an open decision.

I can put [NAME], [TITLE], on the phone about what has not been decided. Most launches will not discuss that part. No accreditation is claimed. There is no pass rate, because nobody has sat an examination.

Or I write it: 700 words for the commercial and cost side, arithmetic shown. Which is more use? If neither, one word back and I will leave it there.
