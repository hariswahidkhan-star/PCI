> FORMULAS USED
> CPI = EV ÷ AC
> EAC = AC + (BAC − EV), remaining work at the budgeted rate
> EAC = BAC ÷ CPI, remaining work at the cost performance achieved so far
> EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) ÷ (CPI × SPI), remaining work against both cost and schedule performance
> EAC = AC + a bottom-up estimate to complete, when the original basis no longer holds

## Chapters, and why these ones

YouTube needs the first chapter at 00:00, at least three of them, and none shorter than ten seconds. That is the mechanical floor. The reason to care is that Google lifts chapter titles into key moments, so a chapter list is seventeen more chances to be the result someone clicks, and a list reading "Part 3 — Analysis" throws all seventeen away.

Three rules were applied to the list above.

**Each title is a thing somebody would type.** "Cut-off, in plain words" and "What an accrual is, and what it is not" are search behaviour. "Context and background" is not.

**The two exam chapters are a matched pair.** "Accountancy exams rarely test float" sits directly above "Engineering exams rarely test cut-off". Both professions are in this audience, and in a scanned list each one finds its own line and neither finds an accusation. Separating them, or keeping only one, turns the argument into a side.

**The last chapter says what the credential does not claim.** For a certifying body publishing its first long-form video, the chapter a sceptical viewer jumps to is the one about the credential. Naming the limit in the chapter title is the cheapest credibility in the whole asset.
