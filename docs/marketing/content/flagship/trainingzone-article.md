# Assessing professional competence: the 'understands' trap

*Most competency frameworks contain statements that cannot be marked. The fix starts with the verb.*

Assessing professional competence requires three things in writing: an act the person must be able to perform, evidence that would show they performed it, and a rule separating a pass from a fail before anyone sits down. A framework that says someone "understands" a subject has none of the three. Nobody has ever observed an understanding.

## Why "understands" cannot be marked

"Understands." "Is aware of." "Appreciates the importance of." Each names a state of mind, and no assessor has ever seen one. Instead the assessor infers it from something the candidate did, then marks the inference: an opinion with a form attached. Give the same candidate to two assessors and both marks will be defensible, which describes a standard that does not exist.

The verb is where the standard lives. Calculates. Reconciles. Identifies. Refuses. Each names an act that either happened or did not, and the pass mark then writes itself.

## What assessing professional competence actually requires

| As it is usually written | Why it cannot be marked | Written so it can be |
|---|---|---|
| Understands revenue recognition | No act is named; the assessor marks an inference | Determines which of a period's costs belong to it, and evidences the cut-off |
| Is aware of earned value principles | "Aware of" has no failure condition | Calculates a cost performance index and names what is absent from it |
| Appreciates the importance of accruals | An importance cannot be wrong | Identifies and quantifies work performed but not yet invoiced at a reporting date |
| Has sound commercial judgement | Two assessors will disagree, both defensibly | Refuses to sign a forecast where ledger and progress closed on different dates |

The middle column is the test. If you cannot say what failing looks like, you have written an aspiration, and aspirations are marked generously.

## The competence no framework catches

Assessing what happens inside a function is the easy half. Accountants are examined on cut-off, engineers on float and progress, and both examinations are rigorous.

The competence that defeats frameworks sits where two functions hand a number to each other. It has two assessors, each qualified for half of it, and a competence with two assessors has none.

Here is one, as arithmetic. No client, no sector, no currency, no frequency claim.

Earned value is 2,200,000. Invoiced cost is 1,850,000.

> 2,200,000 ÷ 1,850,000 = 1.19

The cost performance index reads 1.19: on the evidence in the system, the job is under budget.

Now the figure nobody was asked for. At the reporting date, 240,000 of work is performed and not yet invoiced. Accrue it.

> 1,850,000 + 240,000 = 2,090,000
>
> 2,200,000 ÷ 2,090,000 = 1.05

Fourteen index points moved, and no work changed hands to move them. The error is accounting, the damage is delivery, and neither training alone catches it.

Ask who assesses that. The finance assessor marks the accrual and never sees the index. The delivery assessor marks the index and takes the cost figure as given. Both mark correctly, and together they certify the person who signs the report.

None of which criticises either profession. The gap is in what each is examined on, and a syllabus is a document, not a person.

## Any figure with two authors has this shape

A delivery date promised to a customer is co-authored by sales and operations. A headcount forecast is co-authored by HR and finance. Wherever a reported number has two authors, the framework holds a competence for each and nothing for the join.

## What is worth assessing once explanation is free

A model handed that report will explain a cost performance index of 1.19 accurately and cite the right formula. It cannot notice the missing accrual, because the accrual was never a record.

Competence in producing a figure is what machine output substitutes for first. Competence in interrogating one gets more valuable as explanation gets cheaper. Settle [what a model may supply when it writes the monthly commentary](https://pciai.org/generative-ai-project-reporting) before the tool is switched on.

## What examination adds to a development plan

Training changes what a person can do. An examination is the part a third party can rely on without knowing the person, the trainer or the course.

That matters most for a competence that surfaces only at a handover. Everywhere else there is somebody downstream to catch the mistake. At the handover, the person holding it is the last one who sees it.

That is the argument the Project Controls Institute was built on, and it is a claim to check rather than accept. Three credentials carry it. PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) is examined across 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas; PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) across 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas; PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) across 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

Behind them sit 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements. Those are the Institute's certification requirements, not law.

15,613 machine calculation checks run against PFL-AI and PML-AI, all passing; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite. For anyone who has moderated a paper, that is the interesting sort of number, because it is a check on the document rather than on the candidates.

PCL-AI's Body of Knowledge runs 40 per cent finance and reporting, 40 per cent project management and 20 per cent governed use of machine output. That is the syllabus's shape, not an examination weighting. The syllabus is settled; the blueprint for the paper is not, and a provider quoting you a weighting for a paper nobody has built has made it up.

## The part that belongs to L&D

A forecast gets signed in your organisation this month, by somebody your framework says is competent to sign it. If the accrual is missing, the number was wrong before they saw it, and they will find that out in a room where they are the only person accountable for it.

The competence that would have caught it was never written down, so nobody was ever assessed on it.

So read [the PCL-AI Body of Knowledge set out as things a holder must be able to do](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/body-of-knowledge) and mark it against your own framework. Then open that framework and count the statements beginning with "understands".

## Questions this raises

**Can professional competence be assessed by a written examination at all?**

Part of it. A written examination can hand a candidate a report where the ledger and progress dates differ and ask what they would do, which is a decision with a right answer. It cannot assess whether they say so with the client in the room.

**What is actually wrong with "understands" in a competency framework?**

It names a state of mind, and no assessor can observe one. The assessor infers it from something the candidate did and marks the inference, so two assessors reach different marks and both can defend theirs. Use a verb that names an act.

**How do you assess a competence that sits between two functions?**

Write it as one statement with one owner, marked by someone who can mark both halves. Split between two assessors it stops being a competence, because each marks their own side correctly and neither sees the join. If nobody can mark both, that is the finding.

**Does a credential prove competence, or only knowledge?**

It depends on what the examination requires the candidate to do, which you may ask before funding anyone. Ask a certifying body for its domains and knowledge areas, then ask what a candidate must produce to pass. A body that shows the first and not the second is selling attendance.
