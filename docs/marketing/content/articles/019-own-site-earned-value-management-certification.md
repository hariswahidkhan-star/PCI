# Earned value management certification: who it suits

An earned value management certification examines whether you can build an earned value position from source data, read it correctly, and defend a forecast made from it. It suits planners, cost engineers and controls leads who already produce or challenge monthly performance numbers. It suits nobody who has never set an earning rule.

The formulas are the easy half. The examinable half is which forecast you sign.

## What does an earned value management certification examine?

Six competencies, and they are examined in roughly this order of difficulty.

| Competency | What a candidate must be able to do | Why it fails people |
|---|---|---|
| Earning rules | Choose a progress rule per work type and justify it | Most candidates have only ever used the rule they inherited |
| Data integrity | Measure EV and AC to the same cut-off, including accruals | Invoice-based actuals look right and are not |
| The arithmetic | CV, SV, CPI, SPI, VAC, TCPI without a spreadsheet | Speed under pressure, not concept |
| Forecasting | Compute several EAC values and defend one | Requires an argument, not a formula |
| Variance analysis | Explain a variance by cause, not by restating the number | "Cost is over because we spent more" scores nothing |
| System and reporting | Baseline control, thresholds, change discipline, audit trail | Rarely taught outside government-facing programmes |

A credential that only tests the third row is a numeracy test. The competencies either side of it are where the discipline lives.

## The exam-style problem it comes down to

A rail signalling package. Budget at completion is £8.40m over 52 weeks. At the week 26 cut-off, planned value is £4.20m, earned value is £3.75m and actual cost is £4.35m.

**The position.** CV = EV − AC = 3.75 − 4.35 = **−£0.60m**. SV = EV − PV = 3.75 − 4.20 = **−£0.45m**.

CPI = EV / AC = 3.75 / 4.35 = **0.862**. SPI = EV / PV = 3.75 / 4.20 = **0.893**.

You are getting 86 pence of budgeted work for every pound spent, and by value you are around a tenth behind plan at the halfway mark.

**The forecast, four ways.**

| Method | Formula | Assumes | This package |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remaining work at budget | EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) | The overrun was a one-off and is now behind you | 4.35 + 4.65 = **£9.00m** |
| Remaining work at current CPI | EAC = BAC / CPI | Performance so far is representative and continues | 8.40 / 0.862 = **£9.74m** |
| Remaining work at CPI and SPI | EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) / (CPI × SPI) | Schedule recovery will keep costing money | 4.35 + 4.65 / 0.770 = **£10.39m** |
| Bottom-up re-estimate | EAC = AC + a fresh estimate to complete | The team can re-estimate the remaining scope honestly | 4.35 + a defended ETC |

A spread of £9.00m to £10.39m on one dataset. The examination is not asking which is correct; it is asking which assumption you are prepared to defend to a sponsor who will hold you to it.

**The number that ends the argument.** TCPI to finish on budget = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) = 4.65 / 4.05 = **1.148**.

The team has run at 0.862 for 26 weeks. Finishing on budget now requires 1.148, which is 1.148 / 0.862 = a **33% step change** in efficiency, from the same crew on the same site, in the back half of a signalling job.

Writing "recovery plan in place" against that is how forecasts lose credibility. The full method set, including earned schedule and where each forecast breaks, sits in [the earned value management pillar](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/earned-value-management).

## Which credentials cover earned value?

There is no single EVM licence. Five families touch the subject and they differ in what they will actually test.

| Family | EVM coverage | Assessment style | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| General project management credentials | A component within a wider syllabus | Knowledge and situational judgement | Delivery managers who need the vocabulary |
| Cost engineering and controls bodies | Substantial, alongside estimating and claims | Technical examination, experience-gated | Cost engineers and estimators |
| Scheduling specialisms | Schedule performance and progress, less on cost | Technical examination | Planners and forensic schedulers |
| Software and system training certificates | Tool operation and system set-up | Attendance or product test | Teams standing up an EVM system |
| PCI AI-era suite (PCL-AI, PFL-AI, PML-AI) | Integrated: earned value inside project accounting and governed AI | Scenario-based, single best answer | Controls, finance and delivery leads |

PCI claims no equivalence with, endorsement by or affiliation to any other body. Naming these families is positioning, and you should read each issuer's own published requirements before paying anything.

The fifth row is the one to understand before choosing. PCI does not sell an earned value certification on its own, because a forecast that stops at CPI has answered only half the question. Earned value sits inside the wider credential, next to the accounting it feeds.

## Who it suits, and who should skip it

It suits a planner or cost engineer whose monthly pack already carries indices someone questions, and who wants the challenge answered by method rather than seniority.

It suits a controls lead moving to a government-facing or contractually mandated EVM programme, where the system requirements are examinable in their own right.

It suits a finance analyst reviewing contractor performance reports, because the fastest way to stop being told a comfortable story is to be able to recompute it.

Skip it if you have never owned a progress measurement rule. Learn the cycle first, since earned value on top of guessed progress is arithmetic performed on fiction.

Skip it too if what you actually need is the integrated credential. Holding a narrow EVM certificate and no view of how the forecast lands in the accounts leaves the seam uncovered, which is where projects lose money.

## What it costs, and what to ask before paying

Price varies enormously and correlates weakly with rigour. Ask four questions of any provider: who decides the result, whether they also sell the preparation, whether the standard is published in enough detail to study from, and what a retake costs.

PCI publishes its own figures rather than quoting on request: the PCL-AI examination at USD 500 with a current 30% discount applied, taking it to USD 350, and student membership at USD 99 a year discounted to USD 49.50. Fees change, and checkout is what binds.

The full breakdown, including refund windows and the five-year total, is in [what certification actually costs](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/project-controls-certification-cost).

## A six-week preparation plan

Week 1: earning rules. Write down the rule applied to every work type on your current project and whether it can be gamed.

Week 2: data integrity. Trace one month's actual cost from ledger to report and find the accrual that is missing or double-counted.

Week 3: the arithmetic, by hand. Twenty positions, no spreadsheet, computing all six measures until it takes under two minutes each.

Week 4: forecasting. Take three real packages, compute four EAC values for each, and write a paragraph defending one. That paragraph is the examinable skill.

Week 5: variance analysis by cause. Decompose a cost variance into rate, quantity and scope, and stop when the explanation would satisfy an auditor.

Week 6: system and governance. Baseline change control, reporting thresholds and the audit trail, then a full timed mock.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is an earned value management certification worth it?**
It is worth it if your work is questioned monthly and you currently answer with seniority rather than method. It is poor value if you do not yet own progress measurement, or if the credential you actually need is the integrated one covering the accounting your forecast feeds. Decide from job advertisements you want, not from the syllabus.

**Do I need to know earned value for a project controls certification?**
Yes, in any credible scheme. Earned value is how cost and schedule performance are made comparable, and PCI's scenario-based examination places it inside realistic project positions rather than asking for definitions. Expect to be asked what you would tell a sponsor, not what CPI stands for.

**Which EAC method do examiners expect?**
None by default, which is the point. The expected answer names the cause of the variance and matches it to the assumption behind a method: a finished one-off event supports remaining work at budget, a rate or productivity error supports the CPI method, and schedule pressure bought with overtime supports the combined CPI and SPI method.

**Can I pass without site experience?**
It is possible and it is harder. The arithmetic is learnable from a book; the judgement questions reward anyone who has argued about a progress claim at a cut-off. Candidates from finance usually need float, earning rules and progress measurement, and candidates from site usually need the accounting side.

**Does earned value still matter with AI forecasting tools?**
Yes, and more so. A model that predicts outturn still needs an honest earned value position as its input, and someone who can say why the model disagrees with the CPI method. PCI examines the governed use of AI as a domain in its own right, which includes refusing a forecast you cannot explain.
