# PMI-SP vs AACE PSP: which scheduling credential fits

PMI-SP vs AACE PSP is a choice between two scheduling credentials with different assessment philosophies. PMI-SP examines scheduling inside PMI's project management framework through objective questions. PSP examines planning and scheduling as a production and forensic discipline, with practical exercises as well as objective ones.

Pick by two things: which acronym your market names, and whether you want an assessment that makes you produce work rather than recognise answers. If PMP and the tool certificates are still on your list, [the wider shortlist for planners](https://credentialfinder.org/best-certification-for-planning-engineers) sets all five against each other first.

## PMI-SP vs AACE PSP on the axes that matter

| | PMI-SP | AACE PSP |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | PMI | AACE International |
| Framing | Scheduling as a knowledge area within PMI's project management framework | Planning and scheduling as a discipline in its own right, within total cost management |
| Emphasis | Process, terminology, framework alignment | Development, maintenance, analysis and forensic use of the programme |
| Assessment shape | Objective questions against a published exam content outline | Objective questions plus practical exercises requiring worked answers |
| Experience gate | Documented scheduling months, plus formal scheduling education hours | Substantial documented experience, with academic study substituting for part |
| Renewal | Professional development units on a fixed cycle, in the credential's subject area | Recertification cycle with continuing education |
| Where it is named most | Organisations standardised on PMI, IT and mixed-portfolio environments | Engineering, construction, energy, mining, and claims and forensic work |
| Main criticism | Framework-led rather than site-led | Study load and breadth surprise candidates |

Eligibility rules, question counts and fees are revised periodically. Confirm each against the current PMI and AACE handbooks before you commit a study calendar to them.

## The practical difference, with the arithmetic

PSP's practical exercises ask you to produce an answer a claims consultant could check. Here is the kind of problem that separates the two papers.

An update is cut at data date **day 60**. The remaining network runs mechanical installation M for 30 days, then testing N for 15 days, then handover. Forecast completion falls on **day 105**. The contract completion date is **day 110**, so the programme carries **5 days** of terminal float.

An employer-caused event delays the permit that releases M by **14 days**. Insert that delay into the update current at the time and rerun the network: M now starts 14 days later, so forecast completion moves from 105 to **day 119**.

Against the contract date of 110, the project is now **9 days** late. Fourteen days of delay produced nine days of entitlement, because five days were absorbed by float that existed before the event.

That single step is where most disputes actually live. If float belongs to the project rather than to either party, the employer's event may consume it and the contractor recovers nine days, not fourteen.

If the contract says otherwise, the answer changes. Naming the method is easy; showing which days went where is the examinable skill, and [which delay analysis method suits which dispute](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/delay-analysis-techniques) is a separate decision the examination expects you to defend.

Add a concurrent contractor-caused delay of **6 days** in the same window and the answer stops being arithmetic and becomes contract interpretation. A good candidate says exactly that, then states what records would be needed to argue it.

## Reporting schedule performance without misleading anyone

Both credentials expect you to explain what an index means, and this is the one most often quoted wrongly.

A programme has a budget at completion of **£12.0m**. At month 8, planned value is **£5.60m** and earned value is **£5.04m**.

Schedule performance index = EV ÷ PV = 5.04 ÷ 5.60 = **0.90**.

That index is measured in money, and it drifts back towards 1.00 as a project completes, because earned value must eventually equal planned value even on a late job. Reported alone at month 20, it hides the problem it is meant to reveal.

Earned schedule fixes it by asking when the plan expected this much value. Planned value reached £4.60m at month 7 and £5.60m at month 8, so:

Earned schedule = 7 + (5.04 − 4.60) ÷ (5.60 − 4.60) = 7 + 0.44 = **7.44 months**.
Time-based index = 7.44 ÷ 8 = **0.93**.
Schedule variance in time = 7.44 − 8 = **−0.56 months**, about **17 days** behind.

"Seventeen days behind" is a sentence a project board can act on. "SPI 0.90" is a sentence they will nod at. Quote both, and quote the driving path alongside them, because neither index names the activity causing the slip.

## What both credentials assume you already do properly

Neither examination rescues a programme with bad hygiene, and both will probe it.

Fix the data date and do not move it. Collect actual starts, actual finishes and remaining durations rather than percentages, then let the network recalculate before you touch anything.

Know whether your project runs retained logic or progress override for out-of-sequence work. Retained logic keeps the original relationships and is more conservative.

Progress override ignores the relationship for the started activity and usually shortens the programme. Changing that setting moves the completion date without anybody changing the plan, which is why an examiner asks about it.

Treat constraints as debts. A start-no-earlier-than on a real access date is defensible with a written reason. A finish-no-later-than that manufactures float is the defect that surfaces at claim time and damages everything else in the programme.

## What neither credential examines

Neither PMI-SP nor PSP examines what your programme does to the cost report once progress becomes earned value.

Progress measurement is the join. A units-installed rule and a milestone rule applied to the same package on the same day produce different earned value, different indices and, downstream, a different view of what may be recognised as revenue.

An engineer is examined on float and progress measurement. A chartered accountant is examined on recognition and provisions. Nobody is routinely examined on the handover between them, which is where forecasts quietly become misstatements. PCI's credentials exist to examine that boundary rather than either side of it alone.

## Where PCI fits

The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) covers 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas. Its companions are the PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI), with 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and the PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI), with 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

Every Body of Knowledge holds the same proportions: 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed AI. Behind them sit 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes (26 + 33 + 33).

Entry is around three years of professional experience in any field, counted full-time-equivalent, with no degree requirement. The examination is scenario-based multiple choice with a single best answer, sat under remote proctoring, and the fee opens a 12-month scheduling window from payment.

PCI is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and does not claim to be. The scheme is designed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 principles, including a criterion-referenced cut score. PCI claims no recognition, endorsement, affiliation or partnership with PMI, AACE or any other body, and publishes no pass rates or holder numbers.

## How to choose in ten minutes

Search live job advertisements for the roles you want in your region and count which of the two acronyms appears. In Gulf and international EPC work PSP is named far more often; in PMI-standardised organisations PMI-SP is.

Then ask what you need to prove. If your weakness is vocabulary and framework alignment, PMI-SP's objective paper matches it. If your weakness is producing defensible analysis under time pressure, PSP's practical exercises are the better training, and the harder paper; [the PSP's scope, fees and practical component](https://credentialfinder.org/aace-psp-certification-guide) set out what that commitment involves.

Finally, ask which renewal you will actually keep up. A lapsed credential is worse than none, because it dates your last serious study on your own CV.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is PSP harder than PMI-SP?**
Most candidates who have taken both describe PSP as the heavier paper, largely because of the practical exercises and the breadth of AACE's total cost management framing. PMI-SP is demanding in a different way, since its items are written against PMI's vocabulary and a fluent site planner can lose marks by answering from experience rather than from the framework.

**Can I hold both?**
Yes, though the overlap is large and the second one adds less than candidates hope. A better second credential is usually one that covers a different subject, such as cost engineering or the finance boundary, rather than a second view of scheduling. Both carry independent renewal obligations that run for as long as you hold them.

**Do I need one to work as a planner?**
No. Plenty of senior planners hold neither and are hired on their programmes. A credential matters most when you are being assessed by someone who has never seen your work: a new market, a new employer, or a role where your analysis will be read by lawyers. That is when independent evidence pays for itself.

**Which is better for delay claims work?**
PSP is the closer fit, since AACE's material treats forensic analysis of the programme directly and the practical exercises reward showing your working. Claims work is still won by records rather than credentials, so the credential opens the door and the contemporaneous evidence decides the outcome.

**How much preparation should I plan?**
Around eighty hours over five or six months suits a working planner for either paper, and more for PSP if the practical format is unfamiliar. Sit a timed mock early; [a week-by-week PMI-SP study plan](https://credentialfinder.org/pmi-sp-exam-prep) shows how those hours spread around a working month-end. Study only the domains it exposes, and practise at least one full delay analysis by hand, because that is the skill that decays fastest between projects.
