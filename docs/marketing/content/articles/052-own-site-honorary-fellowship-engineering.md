# Honorary fellowship in engineering: what it recognises

An honorary fellowship in engineering recognises distinguished contribution, conferred by a board or council at its discretion. It involves no examination and certifies no competence. It is a recognition of what someone gave to the discipline, not a qualification, a licence or an accreditation, and writing it as though it were any of those is the mistake that matters.

That distinction sounds pedantic until a client, a regulator or a tribunal reads your credentials. Then it is the whole point.

## What an honorary fellowship in engineering is

An honorary fellowship is a title conferred by an institution's governing body on a person whose contribution to the field it wishes to mark publicly. Conferral is discretionary, unexamined, and usually revocable.

It differs from every other thing the word "fellow" attaches to. Four separate ideas share the label, and conflating them is how honest people end up making claims they cannot support.

| What it is | How it is conferred | Examination? | What it evidences | What it must never be presented as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Honorary fellowship | Board or council decision, at discretion | No | Contribution the body chose to mark | A qualification, licence or certification |
| Elected fellowship grade | Nomination and assessment against published criteria | No, but evidence is assessed | Sustained contribution above competent practice | Proof of technical competence |
| Examined credential | Passing a published examination | Yes | Assessed knowledge against a syllabus, on a date | A statement about your whole career |
| Chartered or licensed status | Statutory or chartered process, usually with peer review | Usually | Fitness to practise under that jurisdiction's rules | Anything the register does not say |

Read down the third column. Only two of the four involve an assessment of what you can do, and honorary fellowship is not one of them.

The second row is the one most readers actually want. An elected fellowship grade is applied for against published criteria with a proposer and referees behind it, and [how an elected fellowship grade is won](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/how-to-become-a-fellow-of-a-professional-institution) is a separate process from anything a board confers at its discretion.

## Why institutions confer honorary fellowships

Three reasons, and they are all reputational rather than technical.

**To mark contribution the certification system cannot see.** Someone who spent fifteen years mentoring, writing or building a chapter has changed the discipline in ways no examination measures.

**To align the body with the standard it wants set.** Institutions confer recognition on people whose conduct they want treated as normal, which is why conduct standing is checked before anything is announced.

**To bring senior people into the institution's work.** Recognition is often the start of committee, standards or review involvement rather than a full stop.

None of these reasons is about the recipient's technical assessment, and a body that pretends otherwise is misleading its own members.

## What "distinguished contribution" is usually taken to mean

The phrase appears in almost every honorary policy and is rarely defined. In practice, assessors look at four strands, and no single one is required.

**Developing others.** Years of mentoring, teaching and sponsoring practitioners. The quiet work, and usually the strand with the most corroborating witnesses.

**Advancing knowledge.** Methods, writing, tools or input to a body of knowledge that other professionals now use. Contribution that outlasted the project it was invented on.

**Community leadership.** Building the institutions of the profession — chapters, committees, events, standards groups — and doing the administrative work that keeps them alive.

**Ethical leadership.** Holding the standard under pressure: reporting honestly when the number was unwelcome, and governing new technology carefully when the shortcut was available.

The pattern matters more than any single item. A title held is not a contribution; something that changed because of you is.

## How PCI's honorary route works

PCI publishes Honorary Fellow (PCI) as a board-conferred recognition of distinguished contribution. It involves no examination and is separate from the examined PCI credentials — PCL-AI, PFL-AI and PML-AI.

The published eligibility is at least eight years of professional experience in project controls, cost control, finance, project management or a closely related field; of which at least three years at a managerial level leading teams, functions, programmes or budgets; a demonstrable record of distinguished contribution supported by a CV and, where available, academic and professional evidence; and a complete, accurate application with the required declarations.

PCI states plainly that meeting the criteria entitles nobody to recognition. Conferral is at the board's sole discretion on the merits of each application, further information may be requested, and any application may be declined without reasons being given.

On fees, PCI's published position is that it charges no nomination, assessment or credential fee for honorary recognition, and that optional charges may apply only for printed certificates, international delivery, replacement documents or ceremony attendance — none of which influences the decision or creates any entitlement. Any indicated review time, such as 15 to 30 days, is described as an estimate rather than a commitment.

Identity documents are requested only if the board shortlists you. Shortlisted candidates receive a secure, single-use personal link, valid for fourteen days, to provide a recent passport-style photograph and one government-issued ID; those documents are seen only by the board and deleted after the decision.

Set against the examined routes, the shape is simple:

| Route | Fee | Examination | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | Paid | Yes | Your PCI credential, earned |
| Founding | Free | Yes | Your PCI credential, earned |
| Honorary | Free | No | Honorary Fellow (PCI) |

An examined PCI credential is always earned by passing the examination, on every route. The honorary route produces a recognition and never a credential, and PCI's own pages say so in those words. Details of what the examined route covers are in [the PCL-AI certification guide](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/pcl-ai-certification).

PCI makes no claim of accreditation, recognition, endorsement, affiliation or partnership beyond what is true today, and nothing here is legal advice.

## How to write it after your name, honestly

Use the exact form the conferring body publishes, and nothing else.

PCI publishes the honorary recognition as "Honorary Fellow (PCI)" and the Fellow membership grade with the post-nominal FPCI. Those are different things conferred by different processes, and inventing a hybrid of the two is a misrepresentation even when it is accidental. Which of the two a planner, cost engineer or controls lead should be working towards is set out in [the fellowship routes open to project professionals](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/fellowship-for-project-professionals).

On a CV, put honorary recognition under honours or professional recognition, never under qualifications or certifications. If a form asks for professional qualifications and all you hold is an honorary fellowship, the correct answer to that question is none.

## The red flags

Fellowship has a commercial imitation industry, and it is easy to spot once you know the pattern.

**A fee to be nominated or assessed.** Legitimate recognition may charge for a printed certificate or an event. It does not charge for the decision.

**Unsolicited approaches offering the title.** Recognition arrives from a body you have some connection with, on the basis of a record it can check. It is not sold by email.

**Criteria that are impossible to fail.** If everything is a qualifying contribution, nothing is being recognised.

**No conduct check and no revocation clause.** A body that cannot withdraw recognition has not really conferred any.

**Language that blurs recognition and certification.** Any offer that describes itself as both an honour and a professional qualification is describing something that cannot exist.

The practical test is simple: ask whether the body would decline you, and how you would find out that it had.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is an honorary fellowship a qualification?**
No. It is a recognition of contribution conferred by a board, with no examination behind it. A holder who has passed no examination holds no certification, and should never present the recognition as evidence of assessed competence. On forms, it belongs under honours or recognition, not under qualifications.

**Can I apply for one, or must I be nominated?**
It varies by body. Many institutions confer honorary titles only on nomination by members or council. PCI publishes an open application route for Honorary Fellow (PCI), in which anyone may submit their record for the board's consideration, while making clear that conferral remains entirely at the board's discretion.

**Does an honorary fellowship expire?**
Usually not on a fixed cycle, but it is typically revocable. Bodies reserve the right to withdraw recognition obtained through inaccurate information, or where continued association would be inappropriate. Treat it as a standing relationship with conduct obligations rather than a document you own outright.

**Should I get an honorary fellowship or a certification first?**
Certification, without much hesitation. An examined credential evidences what you can do now and is portable to employers and clients who have never heard of you. Recognition evidences what you have given, usually arrives later in a career, and rests on a record that certification and delivery work help build in the first place.

**Do honorary fellows have to keep up CPD?**
For the honorary recognition itself, generally no, because there is no competence claim to maintain. Where the same person also holds an examined credential or a membership grade, the CPD rules for those apply unchanged. Recognition never relaxes an obligation attached to something you earned separately.
