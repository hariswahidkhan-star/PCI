# Planning engineer salary: a free tool and how to use it

A planning engineer salary is a range set by five things: sector, country and its tax treatment, employment basis, the scope you actually own, and the evidence you can produce for it. A single quoted figure is not comparable to anything until you normalise it. The worksheet below does that in about an hour.

The figures below are placeholders. Replace each with a number you collected yourself.

## Why a single planning engineer salary figure is always wrong

Two people with the same job title can be doing different jobs. One updates progress against a baseline somebody else built; the other owns the baseline, the forecast and the argument with the client about both, and [the route from a first site role to owning a baseline](https://pciworld.org/how-to-become-a-planning-engineer) is how the second one got there.

They are priced differently because they carry different risk. A number quoted without the scope attached tells you nothing you can use in a negotiation.

## The tool: six fields that make any offer comparable

Fill this in for every offer or advert you are weighing, one row each.

| Field | What you record | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Guaranteed cash | Base salary or day rate, nothing conditional | The only part you can plan on |
| Conditional cash | Bonus target, multiplied by the proportion actually paid in the last three years | A target bonus is a marketing number until you know the payout history |
| Deferred and statutory value | Employer pension, end-of-service entitlement, notice period, redundancy terms | These are real money and they are invisible on the headline |
| Benefits you would otherwise buy | Healthcare, car or allowance, schooling, flights, life cover | Count only what you would genuinely pay for yourself |
| Cost of holding the job | Commute, relocation not reimbursed, professional insurance, unpaid gaps | Subtract it, because you cannot spend it |
| Scope owned | One sentence: what you would be accountable for | Sets which band the number should sit in at all |

The output is one figure per opportunity on the same basis. That is the only thing you should compare.

Gulf packages need one extra line, because the end-of-service gratuity accrues on the basic wage alone and the split decides what it is worth: [what a planning engineer salary in the UAE is made of](https://pciworld.org/planning-engineer-salary-uae) works that arithmetic through.

## Step one: normalise a staff offer

Take a staff offer with a base of 62,000. Employer pension at 8% adds 4,960.

The bonus target is 10%, but the scheme has paid out at about 60% of target for three years running, so count 62,000 × 0.10 × 0.60 = **3,720**, not 6,200.

Car allowance is 5,400 and healthcare you would otherwise buy is worth 1,800. Gross comparable value is 62,000 + 4,960 + 3,720 + 5,400 + 1,800 = **77,880**.

Now subtract the cost of holding it. The commute is 3,200 a year you would not otherwise spend, so the comparable figure is 77,880 − 3,200 = **74,680**.

## Step two: convert a day rate to the same basis

A day rate is not an annual salary divided by 250. Start from days you will actually invoice.

There are about 260 weekdays. Remove 8 public holidays and the 25 days you will take as leave or lose to illness, which leaves 227. Assume 15 days of gap between assignments, which is optimistic in a slow market, and you have **212 billable days**.

At 420 a day, gross fee income is 420 × 212 = **89,040**.

Then fund yourself. Self-funded pension 5,000, accountancy and professional indemnity 1,800, and a reserve of 2,500 for the sick pay nobody will give you. Comparable figure: 89,040 − 5,000 − 1,800 − 2,500 = **79,740**.

Against the staff offer at 74,680, the contract pays 6.8% more. The break-even day rate is (74,680 + 5,000 + 1,800 + 2,500) ÷ 212 = 83,980 ÷ 212 = **396 a day**.

So the 420 rate buys about 24 a day for carrying termination risk, gaps, and every cost the employer used to absorb. That is the trade, stated as a number instead of a feeling.

## Step three: read the percentiles properly

Say you collect seven verified data points from offers and direct conversations: 52, 58, 61, 64, 66, 71 and 88.

The mean is 460 ÷ 7 = **65.7**. The median is the fourth value, **64**. One point has dragged the mean 1.7 above the middle of the market, and that point is a programme lead doing a different job.

Quartiles: the lower half is 52, 58, 61, so Q1 is **58**. The upper half is 66, 71, 88, so Q3 is **71**. The interquartile range is 71 − 58 = **13**.

That gives you a sentence you can defend: half of what I found sits between 58 and 71, the midpoint is 64, and the outlier is not this role.

This matters when reading a published guide, because the band a recruiter prints is usually the interquartile range. A quarter of that market is paid below the bottom of the printed band and a quarter above the top, and the guide rarely says so.

## Where the numbers come from, ranked by honesty

| Source | Distance from a real transaction | Good for | How it misleads |
|---|---|---|---|
| An offer you or a named colleague actually received | It is the transaction | Anchoring a negotiation | A sample of one, with conditions you may not know |
| A recruiter's annual guide for your discipline and city | One step: built from placements that firm made | Bands by seniority and location | Reflects that firm's client mix; printed bands are usually the interquartile range |
| An advert that states a figure | One step: an employer's opening position | Current, live, checkable | Adverts without a figure are excluded, and those are often the highest payers |
| Job-board self-reported averages | Several steps | A rough sanity check | Self-selected, unverified, stale, and it mixes job titles that share a word |
| A professional body's member survey | Depends entirely on the response rate | Discipline-specific detail | Respondents differ from the population |
| A training provider's "salary uplift" claim | Not a transaction at all | Nothing | No control group, survivor bias, and the number exists to sell the course |

Two verified offers beat twenty averages. Collect the two.

## What actually moves a planner's number

| Lever | Bottom of the band | Top of the band | Evidence an employer will accept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope owned | Statusing a schedule somebody else built | Owning the baseline and the forecast on a contract | A baseline you set and a variance you defended in writing |
| Sector | Fit-out and small building works | Energy, rail, aviation, water, process plant | Contract names and values you worked on |
| Contract exposure | One employer, one form of contract | Client, contractor and consultant sides of the same job | Roles across at least two of the three |
| Finance fluency | Reports float and percentage complete only | Explains what the forecast does to revenue, cash and the accrual | Being the person the commercial lead calls first |
| Verifiable competence | Tool certificates and attendance records | Examined knowledge against a published syllabus | A credential somebody else can verify |

The fourth row is the one that reprices people. Planners who can carry a forecast into a finance conversation stop being interchangeable, because most cannot.

Scope moves in steps rather than smoothly, and [the project controls salary bands](https://pciworld.org/project-controls-salary) set out where each step happens and what an employer expects at it.

## What this page will not tell you

We publish no salary table and no figure for what a credential does to pay. Nobody can isolate that effect honestly without a control group, and anyone showing you a percentage is selling something.

What a credential does is narrower and checkable. It is independent evidence that somebody examined your knowledge against a published syllabus, which shortens the part of a hiring conversation where you are asked to prove what you know. Which of them are examined that way, and on what, is set out in [the planning credentials compared on what each examines](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/planning-engineer-certification).

PCI examines the overlap where projects lose money: PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) at 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) at 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) at 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

The Bodies of Knowledge are weighted **40 / 40 / 20** across finance and reporting, project management and governed AI, and rest on **113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements**. The calculation content behind PFL-AI and PML-AI has been verified by **15,613 machine calculation checks, all passing**, a suite covering PFL-AI and PML-AI only, with no equivalent for PCL-AI.

That is a statement about the material being arithmetically sound. It is not a statement about anybody's pay.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should I ask for?**
Ask for a figure you can defend with the scope sentence from the worksheet, not a percentage above your current package. Put your normalised comparable figure against the two closest verified data points you collected, then state the number and the reason in the same breath. A number with a reason attached survives the second meeting; a number alone gets negotiated down.

**Is contracting worth it?**
Only above the break-even rate, and only if you can carry the gaps. Run the day-rate arithmetic above with your own honest estimate of billable days, then add the pension, insurance and sick pay the employer used to fund. Most people who feel underpaid on a day rate have been comparing gross fee income against a net salary.

**Do salary guides tell the truth?**
They tell the truth about the placements that particular firm made, which is not the same as the market. Use them for the shape of the bands by seniority and city, not for the absolute figure, and remember the printed range is usually the middle half of their data rather than its full spread.

**Does a certification change what I am paid?**
It changes what you can evidence, and evidence is what a hiring manager is short of. We do not publish an uplift figure because there is no honest way to isolate one, and a provider who quotes you a percentage is quoting you their marketing rather than their data.

**How often should I redo this?**
Once a year, and again before any conversation about promotion or a move. Rates move with the pipeline in your sector rather than with the calendar, so a worksheet built two years ago in a busy market will make you ask for the wrong number.
