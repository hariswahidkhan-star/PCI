# Construction contract WIP journal entries, month by month

Construction contract WIP journal entries post four things each month: the cost incurred, the amount billed, the revenue earned at measured progress, and the net position between them. That net is a contract asset when revenue exceeds billings, and a contract liability when billings run ahead.

Everything else in the month-end file is detail hanging off those four postings.

This is a LinkedIn original, written under the Institute's cost control and estimating pillar for the people who produce the numbers rather than the people who post them.

## What do construction contract WIP journal entries have to record?

**One: cost incurred.** Debit cost of sales, credit trade payables and accruals. Incurred, not invoiced, so goods received and not invoiced sit in the accrual.

**Two: the certified application.** Debit trade receivables for the amount due, debit retention receivable for the amount held, credit a billings control account with the gross.

**Three: revenue at measured progress.** Debit the billings control account, credit revenue with the cumulative figure less what has already been recognised.

**Four: presentation.** Whatever is left in the billings control account is the contract position. A debit balance is a contract asset; a credit balance is a contract liability.

The control account is the whole trick. Billings go in, revenue comes out, and the residue is the number people call WIP.

## The contract

Fixed price **£24.0m**. Forecast cost at completion **£19.2m**, so an expected margin of **£4.8m**, or 20%. Progress measured cost-to-cost. Retention 5%, released at practical completion.

## Month one: billed ahead of the work

Costs incurred **£2.40m**, of which £0.40m is goods received and not invoiced. Application certified at **£3.60m** gross, so £0.18m retained and £3.42m due.

Progress = 2.40 / 19.20 = **12.50%**. Cumulative revenue = 0.125 × 24.00 = **£3.00m**.

| Posting | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of sales | £2.40m | |
| Trade payables | | £2.00m |
| Accruals | | £0.40m |
| Trade receivables | £3.42m | |
| Retention receivable | £0.18m | |
| Billings control | | £3.60m |
| Billings control | £3.00m | |
| Revenue | | £3.00m |

The billings control account holds a credit of 3.60 − 3.00 = **£0.60m**. That is a contract liability: the client has been billed for work not yet performed.

Month one margin is 3.00 − 2.40 = **£0.60m**, which is 20% of revenue, exactly as expected.

## Month two: the position flips

Costs in the month **£4.80m**, cumulative **£7.20m**. Application certified **£3.00m** gross, cumulative **£6.60m**, with £0.15m retained in the month.

Progress = 7.20 / 19.20 = **37.50%**. Cumulative revenue = 0.375 × 24.00 = **£9.00m**. Revenue for the month = 9.00 − 3.00 = **£6.00m**.

The billings control account now runs 0.60 credit, plus 3.00 credit for the application, less 6.00 debit for revenue, leaving a debit of **£2.40m**.

That is a contract asset. Cross-check it directly: cumulative revenue 9.00 less cumulative billings 6.60 = **£2.40m**.

A contract asset growing every month is a commercial signal, not an accounting one. It means work is being performed faster than it is being certified, and someone should be asking why the applications are behind the progress.

## Month three: the forecast moves and the month goes red

Costs in the month **£4.80m**, cumulative **£12.00m**. Application certified **£3.40m**, cumulative **£10.00m**. No unusual transaction, no dispute, nothing written off.

The cost engineer revises the forecast cost at completion from £19.20m to **£21.60m**.

Progress = 12.00 / 21.60 = **55.56%**. Cumulative revenue = 0.5556 × 24.00 = **£13.33m**. Revenue for the month = 13.33 − 9.00 = **£4.33m**.

Against a cost of £4.80m, the month reports a margin of **−£0.47m**.

| Posting | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cost of sales | £4.80m | |
| Trade payables and accruals | | £4.80m |
| Trade receivables | £3.23m | |
| Retention receivable | £0.17m | |
| Billings control | | £3.40m |
| Billings control | £4.33m | |
| Revenue | | £4.33m |

The billings control account moves from £2.40m debit to **£3.33m** debit, which ties to cumulative revenue 13.33 less cumulative billings 10.00.

Check the margin two ways. Cumulative margin is 13.33 − 12.00 = **£1.33m**. Total expected margin is now 24.00 − 21.60 = £2.40m, and 55.56% of £2.40m is **£1.33m**. The prior cumulative margin was £1.80m, so the movement is **−£0.47m**.

A £2.40m forecast movement removed £0.47m of profit in the month it was recognised, and it did so through the revenue line, not through cost. That is the point most project teams have never been shown: the forecast is not management information, it is a posting.

## The materials adjustment changes the revenue figure

Where a cost is incurred that is not proportionate to progress, it comes out of the measure and the item is recognised at cost with no margin until it is installed.

Suppose **£3.00m** of the £12.00m cumulative cost is switchgear delivered to site and not installed.

Adjusted cost incurred = 12.00 − 3.00 = £9.00m. Adjusted forecast = 21.60 − 3.00 = £18.60m. Progress = 9.00 / 18.60 = **48.39%**.

Adjusted price = 24.00 − 3.00 = £21.00m. Revenue from progress = 0.4839 × 21.00 = £10.16m, plus the switchgear at cost £3.00m, giving **£13.16m**.

Cumulative margin becomes 13.16 − 12.00 = **£1.16m**, against £1.33m unadjusted. Cross-check: 48.39% of the £2.40m expected margin is £1.16m.

The £0.17m difference is margin that would otherwise have been taken on a delivery note.

## Four balances that are not the same thing

| Balance | Arises when | What it means commercially |
|---|---|---|
| Contract asset | Cumulative revenue exceeds cumulative billings | Work done and not yet certified. Applications lag progress |
| Contract liability | Cumulative billings exceed cumulative revenue | Billed ahead. The client is funding the margin |
| Trade receivable | The right to payment is unconditional | Certified and due. Only time stands between this and cash |
| Retention receivable | Cash withheld pending completion | A collection risk, not a measurement one. It never reduces revenue |

Contract asset and trade receivable are often lumped together in a management pack, and they should not be. One is exposed to a commercial argument about entitlement, the other only to the client's payment run.

## Getting the cut-off right, or the rest is decoration

Every figure above depends on cost, progress and billing being measured to the same date.

Accrue for goods received and not invoiced, or the cost is understated and progress is understated with it, and the margin flatters itself. Recognise progress to a later date than cost and the margin flatters itself the other way.

The discipline that makes this hold together is set out in [month-end close for projects](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/month-end-close-for-projects), and it is worth more than any accounting policy decision.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should contract costs go through a WIP asset first?**
Under IFRS 15 the normal treatment is to expense contract costs as incurred and recognise revenue by reference to progress, so the balancing figure sits in the contract asset or liability rather than in an inventory-style WIP account. Costs to fulfil a contract, such as mobilisation, are a separate case: they are capitalised where they relate directly to the contract and are expected to be recovered, then amortised in line with progress.

**Why does the billings control account net to the contract position?**
Because billings are credited into it and revenue is debited out of it. If more has been billed than earned, a credit remains and the entity owes work. If more has been earned than billed, a debit remains and the entity is owed certification. The account is doing the comparison for you every month.

**Does retention reduce revenue?**
No. Retention is a payment timing matter, so the full certified value is billed and the retained portion sits as a separate receivable until release. The effect lands in working capital rather than in margin, which is how a contract can report a healthy margin and still leave the business short of cash.

**How is an unapproved variation posted?**
Only to the extent that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue is highly unlikely. That constrained amount joins the transaction price and flows through the same progress calculation, so it is recognised proportionally rather than in full. An optimistic quantum on a disputed head of claim is not revenue, and booking it creates a reversal in a later period.

**Who should prepare these entries, finance or the project?**
Finance posts them; the project supplies every input that matters. Costs incurred and accrued, the split of costs that do and do not measure progress, the forecast at completion and the claim position all originate on site.
