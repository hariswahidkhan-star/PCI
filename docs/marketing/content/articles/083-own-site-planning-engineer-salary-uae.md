# Planning engineer salary UAE: what the market really pays

In the UAE, the headline number is the least informative part of the offer. What the market really pays is a package: a basic wage plus allowances, no personal income tax, and a statutory end-of-service gratuity calculated on the basic alone. Two offers with identical totals can differ by tens of thousands over five years.

This piece is about how the package works and how to price one, because that arithmetic is checkable. The figures used are placeholders for your own.

## Planning engineer salary UAE offers: what the package is made of

An offer here almost always arrives as a split. There is a basic wage, then a housing allowance, a transport allowance and usually an annual flight, with medical cover provided as a policy rather than as cash.

The split is not cosmetic. The basic wage is the base for the end-of-service gratuity and for several other entitlements, so the ratio between basic and allowances changes the real value of an identical total.

| Component | Usual basis | Counts towards gratuity? | Guaranteed? | What to check before signing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic wage | Monthly, stated in the contract | Yes | Yes | The percentage of total. A low basic quietly reduces your exit value |
| Housing allowance | Monthly cash, or company accommodation | No | Yes, if in the contract | Whether it is cash or in kind, and what happens if you leave mid-tenancy |
| Transport allowance | Monthly cash | No | Yes, if in the contract | Whether a company vehicle replaces it and who pays fines and fuel |
| Annual air ticket | One economy ticket, sometimes for family | No | Usually contractual | Family entitlement, class, and whether it is paid as cash |
| Medical insurance | Employer policy, mandatory cover | No | Yes | The tier, family cover, and the excess you would pay |
| Schooling | Rare below senior levels | No | Only if written in | The cap per child and the number of children covered |
| Bonus | Discretionary in most contractors | No | No | The payout history, not the target |

Anything not written into the contract is a conversation, not an entitlement. Offer letters here are often warmer than the contract that follows them, so compare the two documents line by line.

## Why the basic wage split decides thousands

The end-of-service gratuity is a statutory entitlement under UAE labour law. As a general framework, an employee who completes a year of service accrues 21 days of basic wage for each of the first five years and 30 days of basic wage for each year after that, with the total capped at two years' pay.

Rules, exclusions and the treatment of resignation change over time, and the current text of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 and your own contract govern your position. Nothing here is legal advice, and you should confirm your entitlement with the employer and, where the sum is material, with a qualified adviser.

Now the arithmetic. Take a total package of AED 25,000 a month, offered two ways.

**Split A** puts basic at AED 12,500, which is 50% of the total. **Split B** puts basic at AED 15,000, which is 60%. The monthly cash is identical.

Daily basic wage is the monthly basic divided by 30. Split A gives AED 416.67 a day; Split B gives AED 500.

At five years, the entitlement is 21 × 5 = **105 days**. Split A gives 105 × 416.67 = **AED 43,750**; Split B gives 105 × 500 = **AED 52,500**.

That is a difference of AED 8,750 for identical monthly pay.

At eight years, it is 105 days plus 30 × 3 = **195 days**. Split A gives 195 × 416.67 = **AED 81,250**; Split B gives 195 × 500 = **AED 97,500**.

The gap has widened to AED 16,250.

Ask for the basic percentage before you negotiate the total. Most candidates negotiate the number on the front page and accept whatever split arrives underneath it.

## How to compare a UAE offer with one at home

Compare net to net, and count what each side is no longer paying for. A gross-to-gross comparison across a tax boundary is meaningless.

Work an example with your own inputs. Suppose the offer at home is 62,000 gross and your all-in effective deduction rate is 29%, which you take from your own payslip rather than from a table. Net is 62,000 × 0.71 = **44,020**.

The UAE offer is AED 25,000 a month, so AED 300,000 a year. At an assumed conversion of 4.60 to the pound, checked on the day, that is **65,217** with no personal income tax deducted.

The gap looks like 65,217 − 44,020 = **21,197**. It is not that simple, because the two packages carry different obligations.

Deduct the retirement saving nobody is funding for you. At 8% of the UAE figure that is 5,217, so the gap narrows to **15,980**.

Add back the gratuity accrual. Split B accrues AED 52,500 over five years, which is 11,413 at the same rate, or **2,283 a year**, taking the comparable advantage to **18,263**.

Then subtract what the package does not cover but your life requires: school fees above any cap, visa and medical costs for dependants, and the trips home beyond the contractual ticket. For a family, school fees alone can absorb most of that advantage, which is why single and family versions of the same offer are different offers.

## What moves a planner's number in this market

| Factor | Toward the bottom of the band | Toward the top |
|---|---|---|
| Employer type | Subcontractor or small consultancy | Client, PMC, or a main contractor on a landmark programme |
| Sector | Fit-out, low-rise residential | Aviation, rail, power and water, oil and gas, data centres |
| Contract exposure | Statusing one package | Owning the programme against a FIDIC-based form, with claims exposure |
| Reporting audience | An internal manager | A client's board, a lender or an audit |
| Nationality-linked banding | Common in parts of the market | Reduced where the employer pays for scope |

That last row is uncomfortable and real. Parts of this market still price by passport rather than by capability, and the practical defence is evidence: a credential somebody else can verify, and a scope statement that names what you owned.

## Where to get real figures

We publish no UAE salary table, because PCI holds no primary dataset it can stand behind. What follows is how to build a defensible one in an afternoon.

Collect verified points first: any offer you or a named colleague received in the last twelve months, with the split and the sector attached. Three of these are worth more than any published average.

Then read two or three recruitment firms' annual guides for the Gulf, taking the band shape by seniority rather than the absolute figure, and remembering that a printed band is usually the middle half of their placements.

Then sample live adverts. Record the figures where they are stated, and note that the adverts carrying none are disproportionately the senior roles.

Discount every self-reported job-board average, because the sample selects itself and the titles are not comparable.

Take the median of your verified points, not the mean. One senior outlier will otherwise set your expectation for a job you were not offered.

## What UAE employers actually test at interview

Tool depth gets your CV opened. Primavera P6 is close to mandatory on the contractor and consultant side, and increasingly the enterprise version rather than the standalone.

What decides the offer is different. Expect to be asked how you built a baseline that a client accepted, how you handled a delay event and what records supported it, and what your progress figure did to the payment application.

The candidates who struggle are the ones who can drive the software and cannot defend the number it produced. That gap is the whole reason PCI examines finance and delivery together rather than separately, and it is worth more here than anywhere because the contract forms are claim-intensive.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a UAE salary really tax-free?**
There is no personal income tax on employment income in the UAE, but that does not make the package free of obligation. You may still have a tax position in your home country depending on residence rules, there is no state pension accruing, and the gratuity is not a substitute for one. Treat the absence of tax as a reason to save deliberately, not as a raise.

**What percentage should my basic wage be?**
There is no statutory minimum split, and practice varies widely between employers. Because the gratuity is calculated on basic alone, a higher basic is worth more to you at exit and less to the employer at accrual, which is exactly why the split is negotiable. Ask for the percentage in writing before you discuss the total.

**Do I need my degree attested?**
Employers routinely require attestation of the degree certificate for a work permit and for client approval, and the process runs through the issuing country and the UAE authorities. Start it before you resign, because it takes longer than any notice period you are likely to have and cannot be rushed from inside the country.

**Is contracting common in the UAE?**
Less common than in the UK or Australia, and usually structured through an agency that becomes your sponsor. The visa is tied to that sponsorship, so the risk profile differs from contracting at home. Price it accordingly, and read the notice provisions before the rate.

**How do I compare two UAE offers quickly?**
Put both on the same basis: annual guaranteed cash, plus the value of anything you would otherwise buy, plus the gratuity accrual on the stated basic, minus anything you must pay yourself. Then compare the scope sentence for each role, because the one that puts you closer to owning a forecast will be worth more within two years regardless of which pays more today.
