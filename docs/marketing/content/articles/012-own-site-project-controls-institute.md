# Project controls institute: what it is and how to start

The Project Controls Institute is an independent certifying body for the integrated discipline of project controls, cost engineering and project finance, with governed AI examined throughout. It publishes three Bodies of Knowledge, sets the examinations, and makes certification decisions separately from any preparation provider. Entry needs around three years of professional experience in any field.

## What the Project Controls Institute is

The full legal name is Project Controls Institute Global, Inc. It is a Delaware Non-Stock Corporation, and it exists to certify people rather than to sell training.

Three credentials sit at the centre of it, each with its own Body of Knowledge and its own examination.

| Code | Full name | Domains | Knowledge areas | Centre of gravity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PCL-AI | PCI AI Project Controls Leader | 13 | 61 | Integrated controls: cost, schedule, earned value, forecasting, risk |
| PFL-AI | PCI AI Project Finance Leader | 16 | 61 | Appraisal, modelling, capital structure, coverage, bankability |
| PML-AI | PCI Project Management Leader – AI | 16 | 63 | End-to-end delivery, governance, adaptive methods, benefits |

Each Body of Knowledge is built to the same proportions: 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed AI. That split is the Institute's whole argument in one line.

## Why an institute for this, and not another PM body

A chartered accountant is examined on when revenue may be recognised. An engineer is examined on float and progress measurement. Neither examination covers the point where one output becomes the other's input.

Consider a fixed-price contract of £100m with a forecast cost of £80m and £40m spent. Under a cost-to-cost measure, progress is 40 / 80 = 50%, so cumulative revenue is £50m and reported margin is £10m.

Now the cost engineer revises the forecast to £90m. Progress becomes 40 / 90 = 44.4%, revenue becomes £44.4m, and **reported margin falls from £10m to £4.4m** — a £5.6m movement caused by a forecast, not by a transaction.

Very few of the people who move that number have ever been examined on what it does. That is the gap the Institute was formed to close, and it is why its credentials examine both sides rather than one.

## Who the credentials are for

The intended population is people who are asked to defend a number in front of someone senior.

Cost engineers, cost controllers and project accountants sit squarely inside it, as do planners and schedulers who are increasingly asked what their revised dates do to reported performance.

Project and programme managers who own the whole result sit inside it too, usually arriving via PML-AI. So do finance business partners embedded in capital delivery, who typically arrive via PFL-AI.

There is no degree requirement and no requirement to have worked in project controls specifically. The entry rule is around three years of professional experience in any field, measured full-time-equivalent, and capability is then demonstrated through the examination rather than assumed from a CV.

## What the Institute publishes

The three Bodies of Knowledge are the primary publication, and they are the syllabus the examinations map to.

Underneath them sit **113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements**. These are certification requirements established by the Institute. They are not law, and nothing PCI publishes is legal, tax or accounting advice.

Applied material runs alongside the standards: **92 sector case studies across the three volumes**, split 26, 33 and 33.

The calculation content has been machine-verified. **15,613 machine calculation checks run and pass across the PFL-AI and PML-AI material, and PCL-AI has no equivalent suite today** — the figure is only true with that scope attached.

## What the Institute does not claim

Any certifying body's credibility rests on what it refuses to say. The position is published rather than implied.

| Question | The position today |
|---|---|
| Accreditation | Not accredited by ANAB, IAS or any ISO/IEC accreditation body |
| Standards reference | Designed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles |
| Tax status | Intends to seek 501(c)(3) recognition; not granted, and donations are not represented as tax-deductible |
| Pass rates and holder numbers | Not published, because the scheme is at founding stage |
| Exam blueprint | Item counts and pass mark follow a job-task analysis and a modified-Angoff standard-setting study, and are not published yet |
| Outcome promises | None. No course, no membership and no preparation guarantees a pass |

The examination is criterion-referenced rather than graded on a curve. Everyone who demonstrates the required standard passes, whatever anyone else scored, which is the only defensible way to score a competence credential.

## How preparation stays separate from the decision

PCI owns the standard, the Bodies of Knowledge and the examinations. Certuvo is PCI's official platform for preparation and study.

That separation is deliberate. A body that both trains you and decides whether you passed has an obvious conflict, and employers discount the credential accordingly.

Candidates may prepare however they wish. Self-study, employer training, real project experience and Certuvo are all legitimate routes, and none of them changes the certification decision.

## How to start, in five steps

**1. Check eligibility.** Around three years of professional experience in any field, counted full-time-equivalent. Part-time work counts pro rata, concurrent roles count once, and a career break does not reset the clock.

**2. Read the Body of Knowledge and mark it honestly.** Go domain by domain and label each area confident, patchy or new. Most experienced practitioners find two or three genuine gaps rather than twelve.

**3. Prepare against that list, not against a generic syllabus.** Planners usually need time on project accounting and forecasting; cost engineers on schedule logic and risk; almost everyone on governed AI.

**4. Book and sit the examination.** It is scenario-based multiple choice with a single best answer, sat online under remote proctoring or at a test centre. Paying the examination fee opens a 12-month scheduling window from the payment date.

**5. Maintain the credential.** Certification is kept current through a CPD cycle and recertification, including currency in the governed use of AI.

## What it costs to reach the credential

Fees are published on the enrolment pages and change, so no figure is quoted here. The structure is worth knowing before you budget.

There is a membership element, an examination fee that opens the 12-month scheduling window, optional preparation, and a recurring recertification cost across the CPD cycle.

The larger cost is usually your own time. Decide the number of study hours you will commit, then divide by the weeks available: 80 hours over 26 weeks is roughly three hours a week, which is a plan rather than a hope.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Project Controls Institute accredited?**
No. PCI is not accredited by ANAB, IAS or any ISO/IEC accreditation body, and does not claim to be. The scheme is built with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles: a criterion-referenced cut score, an independent certification decision, and preparation kept strictly apart from that decision. The current status is published and maintained.

**Do I need a degree to certify?**
No. The entry requirement is around three years of professional experience in any field, measured full-time-equivalent, and no specific degree is required. That is deliberate, because a degree is a poor proxy for project-controls capability. Eligibility is confirmed at application and may be verified on a sample or risk basis.

**What is the examination actually like?**
Scenario-based multiple choice with a single best answer. Each item places you in a realistic project situation — reading a cost and schedule position, interpreting earned value indices, judging whether an AI-generated forecast can be trusted — and asks what a competent professional would do next. It tests applied judgement rather than recall.

**Which credential should I choose?**
Choose by what you are accountable for. Defending dates, float and progress points to PCL-AI. Defending a forecast, a margin or a cash position points to PFL-AI. Defending delivery of the whole scope points to PML-AI. All three Bodies of Knowledge share the same 40/40/20 proportions, so a second credential covers ground you have already met.

**How long does certification take?**
It depends on preparation, because the pathway is online and self-paced. The fixed constraint is the 12-month scheduling window that opens when the examination fee is paid. Working practitioners typically plan around a small number of weak domains rather than the whole syllabus, which shortens the calendar considerably.

**Is PCI a training provider?**
No. PCI sets the standard, publishes the Bodies of Knowledge, owns the examinations and makes certification decisions. Certuvo is PCI's official platform for preparation and study, and candidates are free to prepare elsewhere or not at all. Nothing about how you prepared affects how the examination is scored.
