# Project controls qualifications: the gap PCI AI fills

Most project controls qualifications examine one half of the job. Cost engineering credentials examine measurement. Project management credentials examine delivery. Accountancy examines reporting, and none of the three examines the handover between them, which is where a forecast becomes reported profit. That handover is what PCI AI was built to certify.

## What project controls qualifications already exist

The market is not empty. It is segmented, and each segment examines the part of the discipline its founding profession owned.

| Credential family | What it examines | How it assesses | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| General project management (for example PMP, PRINCE2) | Scope, delivery method, teams, stakeholders | Knowledge and situational judgement | Project and programme managers |
| Cost engineering and controls (the AACE International family) | Estimating, cost control, earned value, claims | Technical examination, experience-gated | Cost engineers, estimators, planners |
| Scheduling specialisms (for example PMI-SP, AACE PSP) | Network logic, progress, delay analysis | Technical examination | Planners and schedulers |
| Professional accountancy (ACA, ACCA, CPA and equivalents) | Recognition, measurement, disclosure, audit | Multi-stage examination plus training contract | Finance professionals |
| PCI AI-era suite (PCL-AI, PFL-AI, PML-AI) | The integrated discipline, including project accounting and governed AI | Scenario-based, single best answer | Controls, finance and delivery leads |

PCI claims no equivalence with, endorsement by, or affiliation to PMI, AACE International, any accountancy body or any other organisation. They are independent bodies, they set their own standards, and naming them here says nothing about their view of PCI.

Read each body's published requirements before you pay anything. The table is a map of what gets examined, not a ranking.

## What none of these qualifications examines

A chartered accountant is examined on when revenue may be recognised and what a provision must satisfy. They are almost never examined on a critical path or on a rule of credit.

An engineer is examined on float, logic and progress measurement. They are almost never examined on cut-off or on a contract asset.

The project lives in the overlap. Every month, a number produced under one set of assumptions is consumed, unchanged, under another, and nobody in the chain has been examined on the crossing.

## The arithmetic that shows the gap

Take a fixed-price contract worth £60m with an authorised budget at completion (BAC) of £48m. At the data date, the numbers are these.

- Planned value (PV): £24.0m
- Earned value (EV): £21.6m
- Actual cost (AC): £24.0m

Cost variance is EV − AC = 21.6 − 24.0 = **−£2.4m**. Schedule variance is EV − PV = 21.6 − 24.0 = **−£2.4m**.

The indices follow. CPI = EV / AC = 21.6 / 24.0 = **0.90**, and SPI = EV / PV = 21.6 / 24.0 = **0.90**.

So far this is one profession's territory, and a competent cost engineer produces it in an afternoon. The difficulty starts at the next question, which is what the project will finally cost.

## Four EAC methods, four different answers

Estimate at completion is not one calculation. It is four, and each encodes a different belief about what happens next.

| Method | Formula | What it assumes | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remaining work at budget | EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) | The overrun was a one-off and will not repeat | £50.4m |
| Performance continues | EAC = BAC / CPI | Cost performance to date is the best predictor | £53.3m |
| Cost and schedule pressure both persist | EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) / (CPI × SPI) | Recovering the schedule will cost money too | £56.6m |
| Bottom-up re-estimate | EAC = AC + a fresh ETC | The historic index tells you nothing useful about the work left | £52.8m |

The arithmetic, worked:

- Method 1: 24.0 + (48.0 − 21.6) = 24.0 + 26.4 = **£50.4m**
- Method 2: 48.0 / 0.90 = **£53.3m**
- Method 3: CPI × SPI = 0.90 × 0.90 = 0.81, so 24.0 + (26.4 / 0.81) = 24.0 + 32.6 = **£56.6m**
- Method 4: the discipline leads re-price the remaining work at £28.8m, so 24.0 + 28.8 = **£52.8m**

The spread between the cheapest and the dearest is **£6.2m on identical inputs**. Nothing separates them except an assumption, and the assumption is a judgement, not a calculation.

One more number decides whether any of this is recoverable. The to-complete performance index needed to land on budget is (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) = 26.4 / 24.0 = **1.10**.

A team running at 0.90 must now run at 1.10, which is a 22% improvement in cost efficiency on the work that remains. Teams promise that in steering meetings roughly every month. They deliver it rarely.

## What the forecast does to the reported number

Here is the part the engineering qualifications do not cover and the finance qualifications never connect back.

Under a cost-to-cost input measure, progress is costs incurred divided by total expected costs — in other words, AC divided by EAC. That single ratio drives revenue on a fixed-price contract.

Take method 1 first. Progress is 24.0 / 50.4 = 47.6%, so cumulative revenue is 0.476 × 60 = **£28.6m**, and reported margin is 28.6 − 24.0 = **£4.6m**.

Now take method 3. Progress is 24.0 / 56.6 = 42.4%, cumulative revenue is 0.424 × 60 = **£25.4m**, and reported margin is 25.4 − 24.0 = **£1.4m**.

The same project, the same costs incurred, the same invoices issued. **Reported margin differs by £3.1m according to which EAC method the cost engineer used.**

Push the EAC past £60m and the effect changes character entirely. The contract becomes onerous, and the whole expected loss is recognised at once rather than spread across the remaining work.

That is the mechanism behind the pattern every finance director recognises. A project reports acceptable margin for three quarters, then corrects violently in one month. The deterioration took nine months; the accounting event took one.

## Why AI widened the gap instead of closing it

Model output is already inside forecasts, schedules and risk registers, usually arriving with a software licence rather than through a decision anyone recorded.

A model that predicts activity slippage, codes commitments or drafts a variance narrative is making an assertion that ends up in a report someone signs. The person signing has to be able to say how good the assertion is.

That is a specific literacy, and neither founding profession was examined on it. A headline accuracy figure of 91% means nothing until you know the base rate, the precision and the recall behind it.

Governed AI is therefore not a chapter bolted onto the end of a syllabus. It is a competence that has to be examined inside cost, schedule, risk and reporting, because that is where the output lands.

## What PCI examines instead

PCI is an independent certifying body with three AI-era credentials, each with its own Body of Knowledge and its own examination.

| Code | Full name | Domains | Knowledge areas | What a holder can defend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PCL-AI | PCI AI Project Controls Leader | 13 | 61 | The integrated controls position: cost, schedule, earned value, forecasting, risk, governed AI |
| PFL-AI | PCI AI Project Finance Leader | 16 | 61 | The financial position: appraisal, modelling, capital structure, coverage, bankability |
| PML-AI | PCI Project Management Leader – AI | 16 | 63 | End-to-end delivery: initiation, governance, execution, adaptive delivery, benefits |

The Bodies of Knowledge are built in a deliberate proportion: **40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed AI**. No holder can pass by knowing only the side they came from.

Underneath the three volumes sit **113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements**, and **92 sector case studies across the three volumes** (26, 33 and 33 respectively).

The calculation content has been machine-verified. **15,613 machine calculation checks run and pass across the PFL-AI and PML-AI material; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite today**, and stating the figure without that scope would be false.

| Exam blueprint | Item counts and the pass mark are set by job-task analysis and a modified-Angoff standard-setting study before go-live, and are not published yet |

The PCI Standards are certification requirements established by the Institute. They are not law, and nothing PCI publishes is legal, tax or accounting advice.

## Which credential fits which accountability

Choose by the question you are asked to answer in a meeting, not by your job title.

If you are asked to defend a date, a float position or a progress figure, PCL-AI is the closest fit. Measurement sits at the centre, and the finance content exists so you can say what your measurement did to the reported number.

If you are asked to defend a forecast, a margin or a cash position, PFL-AI fits. Reporting sits at the centre, and the delivery content exists so you can interrogate a forecast rather than receive it.

If you are asked to defend delivery of the whole thing, PML-AI fits. Contract, scope and integration sit at the centre, with enough of both other disciplines to stop two reports telling you two different truths.

## What actually changes for a holder

The month-end conversation changes first. A cost engineer who can state what a revised EAC does to recognised revenue is no longer handing over a number and hoping.

The forecast basis improves next. Somebody who knows the number will be consumed under a revenue standard writes down why it moved, and that written basis is the difference between a forecast that survives an audit and one that survives a meeting.

And the AI question becomes answerable in the room. Someone can say what precision and recall the model achieved on this project's data, and whether that is good enough for the decision being taken.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are PCI qualifications accredited?**
No. PCI is not accredited by ANAB, IAS or any ISO/IEC accreditation body today, and it does not claim to be. The certification scheme is designed with reference to ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification principles, including a criterion-referenced cut score and strict separation between preparation and the certification decision. Accreditation status is published and kept current.

**How is this different from a cost engineering certification?**
Scope. A cost engineering examination goes deep on estimating, cost control and earned value, and stops at the boundary of the finance function. PCI examines that same measurement work and then what it does downstream: progress into revenue, forecast into provision, and machine output into a signed report. The overlap is the subject, not an appendix.

**Do I need an accounting background to sit one?**
No. The entry requirement is around three years of professional experience in any field, with no degree requirement. The Bodies of Knowledge are written for practitioners crossing into unfamiliar territory, which is why the finance content starts from the accounting foundations rather than assuming them.

**Why examine AI separately rather than mentioning it throughout?**
Both, in fact. Governed AI is a domain in its own right in each Body of Knowledge, and it also appears inside the other domains, because that is where model output lands in practice. Reading precision and recall matters in the risk chapter and the forecasting chapter, not only in an AI chapter.

**How many domains does each credential cover?**
PCL-AI covers 13 domains across 61 knowledge areas. PFL-AI covers 16 domains across 61 knowledge areas. PML-AI covers 16 domains across 63 knowledge areas. All three follow the same 40/40/20 proportions of finance and reporting, project management and governed AI in their Bodies of Knowledge.

**Which one should I take first?**
Take the one matching what you are accountable for now, not where you would like to be. The three are designed to be read across, so the second is substantially easier than the first, and starting with material you use daily makes the crossing into the other discipline concrete rather than theoretical.
