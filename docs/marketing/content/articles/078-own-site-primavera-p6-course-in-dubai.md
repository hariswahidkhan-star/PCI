# Primavera P6 course in Dubai: choosing the right one

A Primavera P6 course in Dubai earns its fee when it makes you build a network, status it at a data date and defend the result using your own project's data on a current version. Courses that walk through menus produce operators who can populate a schedule and cannot explain their own float.

The tool is the easy half. The scheduling judgement is the half that gets tested when a programme slips and somebody asks why the dates moved.

## What does a Primavera P6 course in Dubai cover?

Three levels are sold, and providers do not always label them clearly.

**Fundamentals.** Enterprise structures, work breakdown, activity types, relationships, calendars, and a baseline that schedules without error. This is where most short courses stop.

**Updating and progressing.** The data date, actual dates, remaining duration, physical percent complete against activity percent complete, and how the tool recalculates. This is where most real work happens.

**Advanced.** Resource and cost loading, levelling, multiple calendars, code structures for reporting, layouts and global change, and reflection or what-if schedules. This is where a planner stops being a data-entry clerk.

Ask which level you are buying. A two-day fundamentals course sold as "advanced planning" is common and the certificate will not say which one you attended.

The most useful question to a provider is unglamorous. Do I build a schedule with my own project's data, or watch a demonstration file being built?

## The settings that change your dates

Two schedules with identical logic and identical progress can finish weeks apart because of options set in a dialogue box. A course that skips this teaches you to trust an answer you cannot reproduce.

Take two activities. A has a ten-day duration; B has a ten-day duration and follows A finish-to-start. The data date is day 5. A is progressed with six days remaining. B has started out of sequence and has seven days remaining.

Under **retained logic**, B's remaining work waits for A. A finishes at 5 + 6 = day 11, and B runs 11 to 18, so B finishes on **day 18**.

Under **progress override**, the relationship is ignored for the started activity. B's remaining seven days run from the data date, so B finishes on **day 12**.

Six days of difference on one activity, from a setting nobody mentions in the report. Multiply that across a network with hundreds of out-of-sequence starts and the finish date becomes an artefact of a preference rather than a plan.

Constraints do the same thing in reverse. Put a "finish on or before day 90" constraint on a milestone whose calculated finish is day 97 and the tool reports total float of 90 − 97 = **−7 days** along the whole path feeding it. Everything upstream is now shown as critical, which is true arithmetically and useless as management information, because the negative float came from a date somebody typed rather than from the work.

Calendars are the third trap, and in the UAE they are not the default. The local working week, public holidays and reduced working hours during Ramadan all change duration in calendar days, and a schedule imported with a default calendar will produce dates that cannot happen on site.

## What the tool will not tell you

Driving relationships matter more than the critical path label. The path that is actually pushing your milestone can include activities with small positive float, and a layout filtered to zero-float activities will hide every one of them.

The second blind spot is float a setting produced rather than the work. Negative float that came from a typed constraint date rather than from logic or progress is arithmetic, not criticality, and a report that presents it as criticality is describing somebody's preference as a fact about the job.

None of this is a P6 feature. It is scheduling method, and it is what separates a planner from a person who owns the licence.

## How to check a provider in Dubai

Ask which version of P6 is taught, and whether it matches what your employer runs. Ask whether you get a licence to practise on during and after the course.

Ask for the certificate wording in advance. "Attended" and "assessed" are different words and employers read them differently, and if you want the second word on paper, [the examined routes available across the UAE](https://pciglobal.ai/project-controls-training-uae) are where it comes from.

Ask who the trainer is and what they have planned. A trainer who has never defended a programme in a client review will teach you the buttons and none of the judgement.

Ask about the licensing authority for the training provider, the total price including any applicable tax, and the cost of a re-sit or a repeat attendance. ## How does PCI examine scheduling?

PCI does not sell software training and does not certify tool operation; [what a Primavera P6 certification covers](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/primavera-p6-certification), and what it stops short of, is set out separately. The PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) credential has 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas and examines scheduling as part of a controls role that also carries cost, risk, change and reporting.

The Body of Knowledge is proportioned 40/40/20 across finance and reporting, project management, and governed AI. Behind the syllabus sit 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes (26 + 33 + 33). The calculation content of the PFL-AI and PML-AI volumes is verified by 15,613 machine calculation checks, all passing; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

The sensible combination for a planner in the UAE is both: tool training for the software, an examined credential for the method. PCI is an independent certifying body, examinations are delivered online under proctoring, and nothing here is legal, tax or accounting advice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a Primavera P6 certificate enough to get a planning job in Dubai?**
It helps you past a screening filter and no further. Employers on major programmes ask candidates to explain a float report, a delay position or an out-of-sequence update, and those questions are about method. Pair the tool certificate with evidence that you were examined on scheduling itself.

**How long does a P6 course take?**
Fundamentals is commonly a few days, updating and progressing a few more, advanced longer. The number that matters is practice hours afterwards, not classroom days. Build and status a real schedule for a month and you will learn more than any additional course adds.

**Should I learn P6 or Microsoft Project?**
Learn the one your sector uses, which in Gulf capital projects is usually P6. The method transfers between them because critical path method is not a product feature. If you plan to work across contractors and clients here, P6 is the safer default.

**Is an online P6 course as good as classroom?**
For content, yes, provided you get hands-on software access and somebody reviews the schedule you build. Live online cohorts with small numbers work well, and recordings help when site work overruns. Self-paced video is the weakest option, because nobody ever tells you your logic is wrong.

**What does a P6 course not teach?**
Whether your schedule is honest. Earning rules, progress measurement, the difference between activity percent complete and physical percent complete, delay analysis, and the cost consequences of time are method subjects. Tool courses touch them in passing, and they are what a client's controls team will question.
