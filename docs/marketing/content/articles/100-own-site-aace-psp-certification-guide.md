# AACE PSP certification guide: scope, cost and who it fits

The PSP is AACE International's Planning and Scheduling Professional credential, and this AACE PSP certification guide sets out what it examines: schedule development, maintenance and control across the project life cycle, including the forensic use of a programme, assessed by written examination with practical exercises rather than recall alone. It suits planners with real site experience and a market that names AACE.

It is not an entry-level certificate, and it is not a software qualification.

## What an AACE PSP certification guide should tell you first

Where the current facts live. AACE publishes the scope, the eligibility gate, the examination structure and the fees on its own certification pages and in the certification handbook, and revises them.

Anything on this page that could go stale is described rather than quoted, so that you check the source instead of trusting a summary. The parts that do not go stale — what the discipline requires, and the arithmetic — are worked in full below.

## What the examination covers

Described in PCI's own words, the ground falls into four areas.

| Area | What it asks of you | Where candidates come unstuck |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule development | Turning scope into a network with defensible durations, logic and calendars | Treating durations as given and logic as decoration |
| Schedule maintenance and control | Statusing, progressing, updating and reporting against a baseline | Statusing to a rule nobody wrote down before the work started |
| Forensic and claims use | Reconstructing what actually drove a date, with evidence | Choosing an analysis method after seeing which answer it produces |
| Communication | Presenting schedule information to people who did not build it | Producing a Gantt chart when a recommendation was asked for |

The practical exercises are what make PSP different from a multiple-choice credential. You have to produce an answer under time pressure, which is a separate skill from recognising one.

## What it costs

Five lines, and only the first is visible at the checkout: membership, application or eligibility review, the examination sitting, any retake, and recertification each cycle. Member and non-member pricing differ. Read them from AACE's fee page on the day you decide, and see [the five fee lines totalled over a ten-year hold](https://credentialfinder.org/aace-certification-cost) for what they come to once renewals repeat.

For contrast, PCI publishes its own figures in full. The PCL-AI certification examination is listed at **USD 500** with a current 30% discount applied, taking it to **USD 350**; student membership and registration is **USD 99 a year**, discounted 50% to **USD 49.50**; recertification and renewal is **USD 99 per three-year cycle**. Year one is 49.50 + 350 = **USD 399.50**, and ten years is 399.50 + (3 × 99) = **USD 696.50**.

Then the line that dwarfs both. A hundred hours of preparation, valued at USD 40 an hour, is **USD 4,000** of your own time. Choose on scope, not on price.

## Who it fits

| If this is you | PSP or something else | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Planner or scheduler on engineering, construction or energy work | PSP | The examination matches the work, and the market names the credential |
| Scheduler in a PMI-standardised organisation | PMI-SP | Framework language matters more than production in that setting |
| Planning technician, one to two years in | Not yet | The scenarios assume judgement you have not had the chance to build |
| Planner moving into claims and delay work | PSP, then consider forensic specialisation | Forensic use of a programme is already inside the PSP scope |
| Planner who keeps being asked what the programme does to the forecast | PSP plus a finance-side credential | PSP stops at the schedule; the cost report and the accounts do not |
| Someone who mainly operates P6 | A tool certificate first, then PSP | Prove the software, then prove the judgement, and never confuse the two |

Two rows often describe the same planner. Where they do, [the PSP and PMI-SP compared paper by paper](https://credentialfinder.org/pmi-sp-vs-aace-psp) settles which one to sit first.

## Preparation: the calendar and the calculations

Plan for a hundred hours or more, because the practical exercises need practice at producing, not just reading.

### Working days are not days

A network says 38 working days. The contract says a date.

Start on Monday 3 March 2025 on a standard five-day calendar. Thirty-five working days is exactly seven weeks, which lands on Friday 18 April. Three more working days gives **Wednesday 23 April 2025**.

Now apply the holiday calendar. Good Friday falls on 18 April 2025 and Easter Monday on 21 April 2025, so two working days come out of that stretch and the finish moves to **Friday 25 April 2025**.

Two days, from a calendar file nobody reviewed. Multiply that across a programme with several calendars and a shift pattern, and you have the single most common cause of a schedule that is arithmetically correct and contractually wrong.

### Float is not yours until you can say whose it is

Total float is the shared slack along a chain, not an allowance sitting on each activity, and [what total float actually is, with worked examples](https://projectcontrolsinstitute.org/total-float), is the first thing an examiner probes. Free float is the slack an activity can consume without moving its immediate successor.

The distinction decides money. If a contractor consumes float early and an employer change later needs that float, the argument about who owned it is the whole claim, and PSP-level work is where the evidence for it is built.

Concurrency is the other trap. Where an employer delay and a contractor delay overlap, the usual outcome is time without money — an extension of time granted, and each party carrying its own costs.

### What a delay decision does to the forecast

This is where a planning credential meets the cost report, and it is worth the arithmetic.

Site overheads run at **£185,000 a month**, which is **£6,167 a day** across a 30-day month. A 24-day delay is 24 × 6,167 = **£148,000** of extended preliminaries. Under a time-only outcome the contractor carries it.

That £148,000 lands in the cost report as actual cost with no earned value against it. Before the delay, with earned value at **£9.0m** and actual cost at **£9.6m**, the cost performance index was 9.0 ÷ 9.6 = **0.938**. Afterwards actual cost is £9.748m, so the index is 9.0 ÷ 9.748 = **0.923**.

On a budget at completion of **£20.0m**, the index-based forecast moves from 20.0 ÷ 0.938 = **£21.32m** to 20.0 ÷ 0.923 = **£21.67m**. One delay decision, taken on schedule evidence, moved the forecast outturn by **£0.35m**.

And the forecast does not stop at the cost report. Where progress for revenue purposes is measured by costs incurred against total expected costs, the forecast is the denominator, so it moves reported progress, reported revenue and reported margin. If forecast cost passes the contract price, the expected loss is recognised in full as soon as it is known rather than spread across the remaining months.

A chartered accountant is examined on recognition and provisions, almost never on float. An engineer is examined on float, almost never on cut-off.

PSP examines one side of that line properly and stops. PCI AI Project Controls Leader (PCL-AI) examines both in one syllabus across 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and every PCI Body of Knowledge holds the same proportions: 40% finance and reporting, 40% project management, 20% governed AI.

## Where PCI fits, and what it does not claim

The Project Controls Institute is an independent certifying body with three credentials: PCL-AI at 13 domains and 61 knowledge areas, PCI AI Project Finance Leader (PFL-AI) at 16 domains and 61 knowledge areas, and PCI Project Management Leader – AI (PML-AI) at 16 domains and 63 knowledge areas.

Behind them sit 113 mandatory PCI Standards carrying 532 process requirements, and 92 sector case studies across the three volumes (26 + 33 + 33). The calculation content of the PFL-AI and PML-AI volumes is verified by 15,613 machine calculation checks, all passing; PCL-AI has no equivalent suite.

PCI is not accredited by ANAB, UKAS, IAS or any other ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body and does not claim to be. It claims no recognition, endorsement, affiliation or partnership with AACE or any other body, and publishes no pass rates, salary figures or holder numbers.

## Frequently asked questions

**How hard is the PSP compared with a multiple-choice credential?**
Harder to fake, easier to prepare for if you actually plan. The practical exercises reward people who produce schedules and narratives every week and punish those who have only read about them. Set aside a third of your study time for writing answers to a page limit against a clock.

**Do I need to be an AACE member to sit it?**
Membership is not usually the gate; the experience and education requirements are. Membership does normally reduce the examination fee enough to pay for itself in the year you sit, so do that subtraction from AACE's current fee page before deciding.

**How much experience does the PSP assume?**
Substantial, with academic study allowed to substitute for part of it under AACE's published rules. The practical answer is different from the eligibility answer: if you have never defended a programme to a hostile audience, the scenarios will read as abstract and you will lose marks you could have kept by waiting a year.

**Is PSP recognised outside engineering and construction?**
Less so. It carries most weight in engineering, construction, energy and infrastructure markets, and in Gulf and international EPC work where AACE credentials appear by name in job advertisements. In organisations built on PMI's methods, PMI-SP is the acronym on the requisition, and [a twelve-week PMI-SP study plan](https://credentialfinder.org/pmi-sp-exam-prep) is the better use of the same hundred hours.

**Does a Primavera P6 certificate cover the same ground?**
No. A tool certificate proves you can operate software, which is often the entry ticket for a first role. PSP assesses the judgement behind the network, the status and the delay narrative, and no vendor test asks about any of it.

**Should I hold PSP and a finance-side credential?**
If your programme feeds a cost report that someone books, yes. PSP proves you can build and defend the schedule; what that schedule does to the forecast, the revenue and the margin is examined by nobody on the planning side, which is the gap the PCI syllabus was written to close.
